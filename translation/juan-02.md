# Sanming Tonghui · Juan Two
## A Comprehensive Gathering of the Three Fates

**Compiled by Wan Minying of the Ming dynasty.** From the imperially commissioned *Siku Quanshu*.[^01]

*Reading conventions.* The terminology follows juan one: **Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, and Water** are the Five Phases; *qi*, *yin*, *yang*, *nayin*, and **Lu** retain their technical scope. Stem–branch names use unaccented pinyin. Angled brackets **〈 〉** mark the source's small-character commentary; square brackets mark supplied clarification or an explicitly noted repair. Birth, Bathing, Cap and Belt, Arrival at Office, Imperial Prosperity, Decline, Sickness, Death, Tomb, Extinction, Gestation, and Nurturing name technical stages. Their names do not by themselves predict physical events. Endnotes distinguish interpretation, attested variants, and conjecture.[^02]

## 1. On the River Chart and the Hongfan Five Phases
### 論河圖及洪範五行

<!-- source: L14-L22 -->
In antiquity, when Paoxi ruled all beneath Heaven, he took the River Chart as his model in making the Eight Trigrams. Thus he set out the names Qian and Kun, Kan and Li, Zhen and Xun, Gen and Dui, and established the images of Heaven and Earth, sun and moon, wind and thunder, mountain and marsh. The *Appended Statements* says: “Heaven and Earth establish their positions; mountain and marsh communicate their qi; thunder and wind press upon one another; Water and Fire do not assail one another. The Eight Trigrams interlace.” Once the Eight Trigrams stand in their ordered array, the twenty-four positions operate together within them.

Consider the transformations of the Eight Trigrams in the light of yin and yang's waxing and waning. Jia originally belongs to Wood and is assigned to Qian. Qian joins with Kun: Kun's upper and lower lines replace Qian's upper and lower lines, producing the image of Kan. Jia follows Kan's transformation and therefore belongs to Water. Yi originally belongs to Wood and is assigned to Kun. Kun stands opposite Qian: Qian's [upper and lower lines] replace Kun's [upper and lower lines], producing the image of Li. Yi receives Li's transformation and therefore belongs to Fire. Bing originally belongs to Fire and is assigned to Gen. Gen stands opposite Dui: Dui's lower line replaces Gen's lower line, producing the image of Li. Bing receives Li's transformation and therefore belongs to Fire. Ding originally belongs to Fire and is assigned to Dui. Dui stands opposite Gen: Gen's upper line replaces Dui's upper line, producing the image of Qian. Ding receives Qian's transformation and therefore belongs to Metal.[^03]

Geng originally belongs to Metal and is assigned to Zhen. Zhen stands opposite Xun: Xun's lower line replaces Zhen's lower line, producing the image of Kun. Geng receives Kun's transformation and therefore belongs to Earth. Xin originally belongs to Metal and is assigned to Xun. Xun stands opposite Zhen: Zhen's upper line replaces Xun's upper line, producing the image of Kan. Xin receives Kan's transformation and therefore belongs to Water. Ren originally belongs to Water and is assigned to Li. Li stands opposite Kan: Kan's middle line replaces Li's middle line, producing the image of Qian. Receiving Qian's transformation, Ren ought originally to belong to Metal. Yet it is lodged in Li Fire: the Fire blazes and the Metal melts. Unable to withdraw and establish itself elsewhere, it attaches itself to Li Fire and stands there; therefore it belongs to Fire. Gui originally belongs to Water and is assigned to Kan. Kan stands opposite Li: Li's middle line replaces Kan's middle line, producing the image of Kun. Gui receives Kun's transformation and therefore belongs to Earth.

Such are the changes through which the eight stems are assigned to the trigrams. Qian and Kun exchange their upper and lower lines, taking their images from the meaning of Obstruction and Peace; hence the saying, “Heaven and Earth establish their positions.” Zhen and Gen give their upper lines to Xun and Dui, while Xun and Dui give their lower lines to Zhen and Gen, taking their images from Influence, Constancy, Decrease, and Increase; hence, “Thunder and wind press upon one another; mountain and marsh communicate their qi.” Li gives its middle line to Qian and Kun, while Qian and Kun give their lower lines to Kan and Li, taking their images from Already Across and Not Yet Across; hence, “Water and Fire do not assail one another.”[^03]
Some of the Eight Trigrams change; others do not. Qian and Kun, rooted in Metal and Earth, do not change because they are the ancestors of yin and yang, the father and mother of all the trigrams. They withdraw to a place of repose and illumination; mature and exalted, they remain unchanged. Kan, Li, Zhen, and Dui occupy the four cardinal positions and do not change their Metal, Wood, Water, and Fire, because Zi, Wu, Mao, and You each preside over a place of full seasonal strength. They proclaim the commands of the four seasons and carry forward the transformations of qi; therefore they do not change.

Gen and Xun, however, change in their operation. Gen Earth takes its position at the northeastern boundary between Kan and Zhen, standing between Decline at Chou and Sickness at Yin. Seeking succession and replacement, it naturally forms mountain and transforms into Wood. Xun takes its position at the southeastern boundary between Zhen and Li, standing between Decline at Chen and Sickness at Si. Unable to stand independently, it returns to Water. Chen is Water's Tomb; Xun and Chen therefore both become Water.

Hai originally belongs to Water. Because it is generated by Metal, it succeeds to Metal's place; thus Hai belongs to Metal. Yin originally belongs to Wood. Because it is generated by Water, it succeeds to Water's place; thus Yin belongs to Water. Si originally belongs to Fire. Because it is generated by Wood, it takes advantage of Zhen's decline and succeeds to Zhen's position; thus Si belongs to Wood. Shen originally belongs to Metal; Water is born at Shen, and Metal assists Water's force; thus Shen belongs to Water.

Chen, Xu, Chou, and Wei are the spirits of the five directions and the five Earths, distributed among the four seasonal endings. They preside over creative transformation as the potter shapes his clay, and supply the substance that bears all things. Originally they ought not to change. Yet Wood, born through Earth and attached to it, takes away half of Earth to become Water. Water moves; Earth is still. Chen and Xu are yang and moving, and therefore belong to Water; Chou and Wei are yin and still, and therefore belong to Earth. The grounds for assigning the Five Phases of transformed qi are, in broad outline, of this kind.

Heaven and Earth join, and the myriad things communicate; those above and below join, and virtue and achievement are accomplished; man and woman join, and their purposes and qi accord. From antiquity to the present, nothing has fulfilled its creative transformation without conjunction. In decline, sickness, replacement, and succession, nothing has brought the workings of change into motion without transmission and the assumption of another's place. Hence the Greater Five Phases of the Hongfan say:[^04]

> Yi, Bing, Li, and Ren are blazing Fire;  
> Qian, Hai, Dui, and Ding inhabit Metal's yielding-to-change.  
> Gui, Chou, Kun, Geng, and Wei are sowing and reaping;  
> Zhen and Gen—the four positions—take Wood's bending and straightening.  
> Jia, Zi, Yin, Shen, Xun, and Xin,  
> With Chen and Xu, all follow Water's moistening descent.

When examining a person's fate, if the stems Jia, Yi, Ding, Geng, Xin, Ren, or Gui occupy the regions of Qian, Gen, Xun, and Kun, one must also judge them according to their transformations. Compare these with the qi-transformations of the ten stems, the sixty nayin, and the stem assignments to the trigrams. One must not judge a fate solely through the River Chart's ordinary Five Phases and then declare, “Such is the Ziping method.” This is why so many present-day readings of fate fail to accord with events.

## 2. On the Heavenly Stems: Yin and Yang, Birth and Death
### 論天干隂陽生死

<!-- source: L26 -->
Someone asks: “It is said that the ten stems differ in yin and yang, hardness and softness, Birth and Death. Is this so?” The reply is: “Of the ten stems, five are yang and five yin. Yang is hard; yin is soft. The *Changes* says, ‘Distinguishing yin and yang, alternately employing softness and hardness.’ As for their distinction in Birth and Death, it is like a mother bearing a child: when the child matures, the mother grows old and dies. This is the natural course of principle. The rhapsody says, ‘Where yang is born, yin dies; where yang dies, yin is born. In their circuits, forward and backward, transformation becomes apparent.’ This is what it means.”

#### Jia 甲 — Yang Wood
Jia Wood heads the ten stems, governs the four seasons, and gives life and nourishment to the myriad things. In Heaven it is thunder and dragon; on Earth it is beam and ridgepole. It is called yang Wood. Its Lu reaches Yin. At Yin it is Wood separated from [the soil]: its roots have been severed and its branches cut away. This is called dead Wood. Dead Wood is hard Wood; only through the axe and adze can it become a useful implement. Its Birth is at Hai. Hai is the Water of rivers, deep pools, ponds, and marshes, called dead Water. Dead Wood placed in dead Water will not rot even after years of immersion: [fir and chun] timber, for instance, remains firm in water.[^05]

But once removed from the water and brought ashore, it encounters Gui Water. Gui is living Water: the rain and dew between Heaven and Earth. Exposed to sun and rain, with dryness and dampness out of balance, the timber withers and decays. It can then generate Fire, and when Fire flourishes the Wood must burn. Thus it faces the danger of flying ashes and vanishing smoke. Moreover, Wu belongs to Li Fire. Fire depends upon Wood for its birth: Wood is Fire's mother, Fire Wood's child. When the child flourishes and the mother declines, how could she not reach her end? Therefore Jia Wood dies at Wu. The classic says, “Wood does not hasten south.” This is precisely its meaning.

Another account says: “Jia is yang, hard, unworked timber, originally without roots, leaves, branches, or rootlets. To be shaped into a useful vessel, it must depend upon Metal; to remain securely stored without decay, it must depend upon Water. Initially, a fitting conjunction with Fire produces the image of cultivated brilliance. But should Fire become excessive, and the southern region be encountered as well, the timber turns to ash and charcoal, and harm follows instead. Jia Wood does not flourish and wither simply with spring and autumn; its transformations through contact with things have no fixed form. One must examine the condition of Fire, Metal, and Water, and then examine transformation and combination. One must not cling to a single rule.”
<!-- source: L28 -->
#### Yi 乙 — Yin Wood
Yi Wood follows Jia, bringing forth and nourishing the myriad things in unceasing generation. In Heaven it is wind; on Earth it is trees. It is called yin Wood. Its Lu reaches Mao. At Mao the trees have deep roots and abundant branches; this is called living Wood. Living Wood is soft Wood. It fears the injury of cutting and felling by yang Metal, and dreads autumn's arrival, when leaves fall and growth withers. It wants moist Earth to cultivate its roots and benefits from living Water to nourish its branches and leaves. Living Water is Gui: Heaven's rain and dew, Earth's springs and sources. Moist Earth is Ji: the soil that is tilled and weeded and accomplishes the work of sowing and reaping. Ji's Lu is at Wu. At Wu the six yangs have run their course and one yin returns to birth; therefore rice flowers open in the Wu hour, and Yi Wood is born in the Wu position.[^06]

The tenth month establishes Hai. At Hai, pure yin holds command, and Ren, whose Lu reaches Hai, exercises authority. Dead Water overflows; Earth is thin and the roots unsupported, deprived of cultivation. Therefore Yi Wood dies at Hai. The classic says, “Water floods and Wood floats.” This is precisely its meaning. Another account says: “Yi is Wood with flourishing branches and leaves. It greatly delights in the gentle warmth of yang, under which it grows luxuriant; the harshness of yin cold is unfavorable and causes depletion and withering. Too much Water overturns its roots; flourishing Metal strips away its living vigor. If its body is weak and Fire abundant, and its course also goes south, the calamity is especially deep. Living Wood is a root-bearing plant: how could it be compared with beams and ridgepoles?”

<!-- source: L30 -->
#### Bing 丙 — Yang Fire
Bing Fire shines in the midst of Heaven, illuminating all six directions. In Heaven it is the sun and lightning; on Earth it is furnace and smelting hearth. It is called yang Fire. Its Lu is at Si. Si is the Fire of furnaces and smelting, called dead Fire. Dead Fire is hard Fire. It delights in dead Wood to kindle its flame and dislikes Metal and Earth obscuring its light. Dead Wood is Jia. Jia's Lu is at Yin, the enclosure of yang Wood: Wood flourishes and Fire is born. Hidden within wood and stone, Fire cannot arise unless people bring it into use. Thus the five yang stems all issue from nature and belong to the Former Heaven; the five yin stems all depend upon human affairs and belong to the Later Heaven. The principle of Bing Fire's Birth at Yin is perfectly clear.[^07]

The sun's Fire rises in the east and sinks in the west. You belongs to Dui, and Dui is marsh. Ji Earth generates Metal; Metal's qi becomes abundant and obscures Bing Fire's light. Unable to display its radiance, how could it escape dimming? Thus Bing Fire is born at Yin and dies at You. The classic says, “Fire does not face west.” This is precisely its meaning.

Another account says: “Bing Fire is the image of the sun, transforming all above and below with light and leaving nothing unilluminated. Yet it does not take floating Wood as its mother: such Wood cannot generate flaming Fire. Nor does it take water-soaked Earth as its child, for this is not what yang Fire produces. Even if it encounters the dead Water of rivers and lakes, without combination or clash their billows do not surge against it; how, then, could they inflict the harm of Water controlling Fire? What it fears is luxuriant Wood: drenched with Water, this cannot generate Fire, but instead can darken its light. It is like the principle in planetary astrology by which Wood and Qi are adverse influences for the Sun.”[^07]

<!-- source: L32 -->
#### Ding 丁 — Yin Fire
Ding Fire follows Bing. It is the essence of the myriad things and the image of cultivated brilliance. In Heaven it is the stars; on Earth it is lamplight. It is called yin Fire. Its Lu reaches Wu, the beginning of the six yin stages. Within Wu there is Yi Wood, which can generate Ding Fire. Yi is living Wood; Ding is living Fire. Living Fire is soft Fire. Ding delights in Yi Wood as its generator: yin generates yin. This is like the use of vegetable and sesame oils for lamps and candles; oil is the rich substance of Yi Wood.[^08]

At the You hour four yin lines hold authority. Lamps can then shine brightly and the stars gleam brilliantly; therefore Ding is born at You. At Yin, three yangs come together: yang Fire is born and yin Fire withdraws. As the sun rises in the east, the stars conceal their radiance; although a lamp still has a flame, its light is no longer conspicuous. Thus Ding is born at You and dies at Yin. The classic says, “When Fire becomes bright, it is extinguished.” This is precisely its meaning.

Another account says: “Ding Fire is yin and soft. Only when it gains the proper season and a fitting configuration can it shine brilliantly. Then even hard, unyielding Metal falls within its power to refine. If it loses the season and its configuration fails, it conceals its light and leaves no trace, not even smoke. It then cannot control even the slightest Metal. Yet dry Wood, though scarce, can still awaken Fire's potential; wet Wood, though plentiful, can hardly make Fire bright. One must examine the strength and weakness within the configuration and not become fixed upon one side.”

<!-- source: L34 -->
#### Wu 戊 — Yang Earth
Before primordial obscurity was divided, Wu Earth embraced the One and guarded the center. After Heaven and Earth were separated, it bore the myriad things in its depth, gathering in the center and spreading through the four intermediate directions. In Heaven it is mist; on Earth it is mountain. It is called yang Earth. Its Lu is at Si. Si is the Fire of furnaces and smelting: refined into a vessel, Earth gives a sound when struck. Its nature is hard and forceful, not readily assailed. It delights in generation by yang Fire and fears yin Metal stealing its qi. Yang Fire is Bing. Bing is born at Yin; Yin belongs to Gen, Gen is mountain, and mountain is hard Earth—Wu Earth. It depends upon Bing Fire for birth.

At You, which belongs to Dui, Metal drains and steals Wu Earth's qi. Metal flourishes and Earth becomes empty; the mother declines while the child thrives. Moreover, Metal striking stone shatters it: how could its life be prolonged? Thus Wu Earth is born at Yin and dies at You. The classic says, “When Earth is empty, it collapses.” This is precisely its meaning.

Another account says: “Wu Earth is deep and thick, its image a city wall. It needs birth in a season-ending month and roots penetrating the branches below; only then can it hold rivers and seas without leaking. If combinations join it above and below, its form is firm and there is no fear of gaps or seepage. If its body rests upon Water and Wood and is weak, its position becomes precarious, with a danger of collapse. When Earth is out of season, much Metal is especially objectionable because it drains it. Once a city wall is complete, one should not add Wood to loosen and penetrate it. It likes a course toward the southeast; but if it is already strong and possesses Seal, going there again lets Fire transform and generate the body excessively, producing harm through overstepping the mean.”
<!-- source: L36 -->
#### Ji 己 — Yin Earth
Ji Earth follows Wu. It is Heaven's primordial qi and Earth's true soil. Its clear qi rises, blending and harmonizing Heaven and Earth; its turbid qi descends, gathering to generate the myriad things. It is called yin Earth. None of the Three Powers—Heaven, Earth, and humanity—can dispense with this Earth. It is like a matchmaker between Qian and Kun: without it, how could yin and yang form a pair? It is therefore present throughout the other four phases, and borrows a period of flourishing within each season. This is true Earth. It delights in generation by Ding Fire and fears being scorched by yang Fire. Its Lu reaches Wu, whose Ding Fire can generate Ji Earth, while Yi Wood draws away the qi through which Earth nourishes growth. At You, Ding Fire is born; since Ding Fire is born, Ji Earth can also be born. At Yin, Wood and Fire hold command, firing Ji Earth into a stone-hard substance [磁石]; it thereby loses its balanced, harmonious qi. How could it remain unharmed? Thus Ji Earth is born at You and dies at Yin. The classic says, “Fire dries and Earth cracks.” This is precisely its meaning.[^09]

Another account says: “Ji Earth is broad and substantial, its image cultivated fields. Numerous combinations and much generation and support are not what it values; it particularly welcomes punishments and clashes that serve a use. This is the substance that brings forth living things. But if it loses seasonal command, is shallow and thin, and the season is unfavorable, it cannot effectively support the work of the hoe, nor can it bury the Metal of swords and halberds. If its course then also enters places where Metal and Water flourish, the body becomes weaker still, which is especially unfavorable. When Fire and Earth generate and complete it, however, sowing and reaping possess the subtlety of unceasing life.”

<!-- source: L38 -->
#### Geng 庚 — Yang Metal
Geng Metal wields Heaven and Earth's power of stern cutting-down and governs the upheavals of arms and warfare among humankind. In Heaven it is wind and frost; on Earth it is metal and iron. It is called yang Metal. Its Lu reaches Shen. Shen is hard Metal, which delights in generation by Wu Earth and fears drowning in Gui Water. Its Birth is at Si: the Wu Earth within Si can generate Geng Metal. This is yang generating yang. Si is furnace and smelting Fire, which refines Geng into bells and tripods that sound when struck. If submerged and buried in Water and Earth, they give no sound: this is what is meant by “Metal, when filled solid, has no resonance.” At Zi, Water's flourishing region, Metal is chilled and Water cold. The child flourishes while the mother declines, and Metal also faces submersion. How could it be born again? Thus Geng Metal is born at Si and dies at Zi. The classic says, “Metal sinks to the bottom of Water.” This is precisely its meaning.

Another account says: “Geng Metal is stubborn and blunt. Controlled by Fire, it becomes a useful implement; once it is a finished implement, however, entering Fire's region damages it instead. If born in summer without roots, and its course also goes southeast, it melts endlessly and never reaches completion. If born in autumn without Fire, and its course goes northwest, it is clarified, tempered, and sharpened, and its radiance remains free and natural. But if it sinks to the bottom of Water, there is no prospect of bringing it into use: Metal is then injured by Water. Again, using thin iron to fell a dense forest not only fails to cut the Wood; the Wood damages the iron instead. If heavy Earth conceals Metal and no punishment, clash, control, or breaking opens it, the Metal remains buried, and its usefulness cannot be displayed.”

<!-- source: L40 -->
#### Xin 辛 — Yin Metal
Xin Metal follows Geng. It is foremost among the five metals and the source of the eight stones. In Heaven it is the [moon], the essence of Greater Yin; on Earth it is Metal, the ore within mountain stone. It is called yin Metal. Its Lu reaches You. Ji Earth within You can generate Xin Metal: yin generates yin. It is called soft Metal, the essence of Greater Yin. At mid-autumn, Metal and Water are in balance, joining and containing light, round, harmonious, and gleaming white. Master Shao's words, “On the fifteenth of the eighth month, delight in the toad's radiance,” refer to this.[^10]

Its Birth is at Zi, the enclosure of Kan Water. Kan contains one yang line belonging to Metal, with two yin lines outside belonging to Earth. Earth generates Metal: the child is concealed in its mother's womb and has not yet revealed its form. Zi Water washes and swirls, carrying away loose sand; only then can Metal show its luster. This is Water assisting Metal's brilliance, making its color bright and clear. At Si, furnace and smelting Fire refines Xin Metal into a dead implement; the Wu Earth within Si also buries its form. Unable to transform, how could it be born again? Thus Xin Metal is born at Zi and dies at Si. The classic says, “Heavy Earth buries Metal.” This is precisely its meaning.

Another account says: “Xin Metal is moist and lustrous, not a stubborn, blunt, hard, and unyielding substance. If blazing Fire refines it, its nature and substance are injured instead: how could it accomplish its beautiful use? It needs Water and Earth to nourish and support it gently and harmoniously, moistening its substance. If the original chart has excessive Fire, it welcomes a northwestern course, removing Fire and preserving Metal. If Metal is too cold, it also needs Bing and Ding to temper it and dispel cold. When it sits upon Lu and is rooted, it occupies a place where its body is strong; even the addition of heavy Earth cannot bury it. In this it is not comparable to yang Metal.”

<!-- source: L42 -->
#### Ren 壬 — Yang Water
Ren Water welcomes yang Earth as the support of embankments and fears yin Wood stealing its qi. In Heaven it is cloud; on Earth it is marsh. It is called yang Water. Its Lu is at Hai. Hai is Water retained in ponds and marshes, called dead Water. Dead Water is hard Water. It depends upon Geng Metal for generation. Geng's Lu reaches Shen, where it can generate Ren Water: this is the qi by which the Five Phases nourish one another in succession. At Mao are flowers, fruits, and trees. Wood flourishes there and can control Earth; Earth, emptied, collapses. The embankments fall, and Ren Water escapes, spreading through the open countryside and flowing away without returning. Yin Wood also steals its qi: how could it remain alive? Thus Ren Water is born at Shen and dies at Mao. The classic says, “Dead Water runs athwart its banks.” This is precisely its meaning.

Another account says: “Ren Water is vast and sweeping, Water with a source, gathering the hundred streams and spreading beneath Heaven. It depends upon Earth for embankments. If there is no Earth among the stems and branches, it must drift and overflow in every direction. But if the body is weak and meets much Fire and Earth, these instead deplete its source and obstruct its current. Ren also likes going south, since Wei and Wu are the region of Gestation and Nurturing, of Wealth, Lu, and gentle warmth. For Birth and return to Lu, nothing surpasses Shen and Hai: these are the repositories where the lineage is gathered and the origin reunited, and Water finds its place of return. If Wealth is abundant and the body weak, encountering these must gather blessings. If the body is strong and Wealth slight, encountering them brings calamity instead. Even a robust youth cannot withstand this.”

<!-- source: L44 -->
#### Gui 癸 — Yin Water
Gui Water follows Ren, completing one circuit of the heavenly stems. Yin and yang's qi reaches fulfillment at the end and gradually returns to the beginning. Its Water is therefore distinguished into clear and turbid and dispersed through the four directions. It moistens downward and assists Earth, possessing the virtue of nourishing all things. In Heaven it is rain and dew; on Earth it is the veins of springs. It is called yin Water. Its Lu is at Zi, where yin reaches its extreme and yang is born, the enclosure where Xin is born and Geng dies. Gui is living Water; living Water is soft Water. It delights in generation by yin Metal and fears obstruction by yang Metal. It wants yin Wood's roots to penetrate and loosen yin Earth. Once Earth's veins are opened, it can flow freely. The second month establishes Mao, the place of flowers, fruits, and trees: Wood flourishes and Earth loosens, and Gui Water can at last pass through.

At Shen, three yin lines take effect and the hexagram Obstruction holds command. Heaven and Earth do not join; the myriad things do not communicate. Kun Earth and Geng Metal within Shen form an enclosing dike, preventing Gui Water from flowing. Confined in pools and marshes, it has no means of exercising its function: how could it continue to generate things? Thus Gui Water is born at Mao and dies at Shen. The classic says, “Water does not flow west.” This is precisely its meaning.

Another account says: “Gui Water is rain and dew, the moisture of yin's beneficence. If its roots reach Hai and Zi, it fills each hollow and gathers into currents, forming rivers. Without Kan and Kun in the pillars, it loses the foundation of its Birth and flourishing and remains weak in body. Even though Wealth and Officer in the configuration are things it can use, they must not be excessive. If Shen, Zi, and Chen are all present, Water gathers into one family and draws upon the Fire of Yin, Wu, and Xu through an unseen clash; this can constitute a superior configuration. If Yin, Wu, and Xu Fire is openly present as the useful influence, both the manifest and underlying levels must be free of weakness for the result to be good. Or, if born deep in summer, it may use Wealth and Officer without losing the palace on which it depends; this indicates great wealth and distinction. Even if its luck course subsequently goes northwest, this is not objectionable as excess.”[^11]

#### Discussion of the competing accounts
The discussion says: The principle of the Five Phases' Birth is the same as that of the myriad things. When the sun first appears, its light is already beautiful to behold; at noon, in the palace of Li, its brilliance is greater still. When the moon first appears it is delicately curved like a moth's brow; at the full, its light is round and clear. Human life likewise passes from youth to maturity, from old age to death: this is ordinary principle. At birth an infant merely cries and laughs; only at maturity are wisdom and folly distinguished. All things are alike in this.

〈Jia Wood is born at Hai. Hai's command belongs to Water, and Jia Wood dwells there. Wood flourishes in spring; at Yin it arrives at office and returns to Lu. Jia Wood gains its enclosure, and at Wu it dies. Bing Fire is born at Yin. Yin's command belongs to Wood, and Bing Fire dwells there. Fire flourishes in summer; at Si it arrives at office and returns to Lu. Bing Fire gains its enclosure, and at You it dies. Geng Metal is born at Si, where [Wu Earth is present], and Geng Metal dwells there. Metal flourishes in autumn; at Shen it arrives at office and returns to Lu. Geng Metal gains its enclosure, and at Zi it dies. Ren Water is born at Shen. Shen's command belongs to Metal, and Ren Water dwells there. Water flourishes in winter; at Hai it arrives at office and returns to Lu. Ren Water gains its enclosure, and at Mao it dies.〉[^12]

Wu Earth is born at Yin because there is Fire within Yin to generate it. At the time of three yangs, the rich substance of the soil begins to stir and all things spring into life: this is Wu's Birth at Yin. Earth flourishes in the four season-ending months. Fire and Earth generate one another as mother and child; therefore Wu follows Bing in arriving at office and returning to Lu at Si, while Ji follows Ding in arriving at office and returning to Lu at Wu. It is clear, then, that Wu Earth is born at Yin and Ji Earth at You. If Wu were born at Shen and Ji at Mao, why would Ren and Wu not return together to Lu at Hai, and Gui and Ji together to Lu at Zi? Later people recklessly composed a *Song of the Proposed Earth Scheme*, with the line “Wu and Ji should reach Extinction within Si [巳懐].” They make Wu's Birth at Shen, Bathing at You, and Cap and Belt at Xu, separating yin and yang in alternation. This is gravely erroneous [one character is missing in the source].[^12]

Another opinion says: The Five Phases' Birth expresses the principle that the mother precedes the child, and that returning to the mother forms the embryo. Earth alone among the phases is distinguished into substance and function. Its depth of virtue bearing all things, abiding in the center without acting, is Earth's substance. Its distribution through the four intermediate directions, flourishing in each season's final month, is Earth's function. Its substance is born at Si, taking the [parents'] Lu; its function is born at Shen, maintaining the parents' position. Thus Water and Earth being born at Shen is the account of the yin–yang specialists; Earth being born at Si is the account of physicians.[^13]

Examining the books of planetary astrology, we find Shen called the Yin–Yang Palace. Therefore Water and Earth are both born at Shen, the Kun position. Water and Earth were never separate, and the account in which Earth follows Water's source also has reason behind it. Each of the other four phases has a single Birth; Earth alone is born at Yin and also at Shen. One thing has two Births because Kun and Gen are Earth's directional positions. Kun belongs to the southwest; arriving there, Earth gains companions, and hence there is “benefit and success.” Huzhongzi says: “Kun is deep and weighty; accumulated Earth completes its work. It is fitting that Earth is born here.” To say additionally that Wu Earth is born at Yin and lodges its Lu at Si expresses the principle of following the mother to find a home. Earth thus has no single fixed position and generates things in many places. What doubt need remain?

Further, Zhou Shi's *Settled Discourse on Yin and Yang* examines the statements that Yi Wood is born at Wu, Gui Water at Mao, Xin Metal at Zi, and Ding Fire at You, called “yin born where yang dies.” It objects: “This overlooks the fact that the winter solstice is Zi, the time of Water's flourishing; the spring equinox is Yi Wood's flourishing; the summer solstice is Ding Fire's flourishing; the autumn equinox is Xin Metal's flourishing. Kan, Li, Xun, and Dui are the cardinal positions Zi, Wu, Mao, and You. A position is where a time is fixed; a time is a position's subtle functioning. When have these ever found Birth in Death and Extinction?”[^14]

Someone asks: “If what you say is true, how is Yi Wood born? Suppose you say at Hai: Hai contains only Jia. How is Jia Wood born? Suppose you say at Mao: Mao contains only Yi. Please clarify this by the examples of Fire, Earth, Metal, and Water.”

The reply is: “Yin and yang together make one substance. Confucius says, ‘The Great Ultimate generates the Two Modes.’ Master Zhou says, ‘Yang transforms and yin combines, generating Water, Fire, Wood, Metal, and Earth.’ Master Zhu says, ‘Each of the myriad things contains a Great Ultimate.’ These three statements are the pivots of the Five Phases. From the statement that each thing contains a Great Ultimate, we know that Wood, too, contains a Great Ultimate. From the statement that the Great Ultimate generates the Two Modes, we know that Wood divides into Jia and Yi, Jia being yang's movement beforehand and Yi yin's stillness afterward. From the statement that yang transforms and yin combines, we know further that Jia supplies a transformation and Yi a conjunction, and only then is Wood generated. This does not mean that Jia is one Wood and Yi another, separate Wood.”

“Jia and Yi depend upon each other to make one Wood. Jia need not itself flourish at Mao, yet Mao must belong to Yi so that Wood flourishes afterward. Yi need not itself be born at Hai, yet Hai must belong to Jia so that Wood is born beforehand. Extend this to Bing and Ding depending upon each other to make Fire; Wu and Ji, Earth; Geng and Xin, Metal; Ren and Gui, Water. Is it not entirely clear?”

Master Zhu says: “Yin qi in circulation is yang; yang qi in condensation is yin. They are not really two things set against one another.” Master Cai says: “The eastern Wood of Yin and Mao, with Chen Earth, is born at Hai; the southern Fire of Si and Wu, with Wei Earth, is born at Yin; the western Metal of Shen and You, with Xu Earth, is born at Si; the northern Water of Hai and Zi, with Chou Earth, is born at Shen.” He also says: “Metal, Wood, Water, Fire, and Earth each possess a yin and a yang. Thus Jia is Wood's yang and Yi Wood's yin. Yi is spoken of in terms of substance; Jia in terms of qi. Yin governs closing: whatever gathers, contracts, and reaches completion is Yi's work. Yang governs opening: whatever unfolds, expands, and disperses is Jia's work.”

This, too, reveals the argument that Jia need not [itself flourish] and Yi need not [itself be born]. These words are sufficient to break through the partiality of the previous account and agree with the original meaning of the ancients' discussion of the ten stems. Moreover, the *Extensive Record* says: “Jia is Wood's trunk; Yi, Wood's root. Bing is Fire's dwelling; Ding, Fire's light. Wu is Earth's hardness; Ji, Earth's softness. Geng is Metal's substance; Xin, Metal's edge. Ren is Water's source; Gui, Water's current.” Thus Jia and Yi are one Wood distinguished into yin and yang, not dead Wood and living Wood split into two things. Being one Wood, they share Birth and Death. The ancients therefore spoke only of the four great Birth positions. Today's division of yin and yang into two gives rise to the distinction “yin born where yang dies; yin dying where yang is born.”

Yet examine Chen Tuan's account: “Jia is timber and Yi grass; Bing flame and Ding ash; Wu earth and Ji sand; Geng metal and Xin stone; Ren water and Gui spring.” This also divides them into two. If they are not distinguished, how can Officer and Killings, Food God and Hurting Officer, Seal-Ribbon and Owl Spirit, Rob Wealth and Companions arise as two names from one phase, while their good and ill fortune, blessings and disasters, differ so greatly? Those who examine fate should take the earlier account as correct.[^15]
## 3. On the Earthly Branches
### 論地支

<!-- source: L50 -->
The functioning of the earthly branches is not the same as that of the heavenly stems. Movement and stillness differ; circle and square are far apart. Although their Five-Phase affiliations are the same, the positions they occupy are not. A branch in the year must be considered as belonging to the year; in the month, as belonging to the month; in the day or hour, as belonging to the day or hour. How could their yin and yang, weight and lightness, hardness and softness be confined to one unvarying form? Take the month's governing branch as primary. Determine which influence is concealed within it and which is employed; identify what drains it and what it dislikes. Weigh the depth and shallowness of the influences throughout the Four Pillars before bringing them into use.[^16]

**Zi 子** heads the twelve branches. It is the Water of streams, ravines, rivers, and seas, and a flourishing place for Wu Earth. Yet only after the period of Major Snow and the return of one yang can it become fully flourishing. What Xin Metal generates must likewise await returning yang and warming Water before it can be born. Zi clashes with Wu, punishes Mao, and forms a triple combination with Shen and Chen. If Shen, Zi, and Chen are all present and gather into a Water configuration, they become rivers and seas, sounding with waves.[^16]

<!-- source: L52-L58 -->
**Chou 丑**, though deep winter brings fearsome ice and frost, has already reached the season's turn to two yangs. Therefore the warmth of Ji Earth within Chou can generate the myriad things. This is Xin Metal's Nurturing place: how could it serve merely for deep concealment? Chou punishes Xu and clashes with Wei. A Storehouse particularly benefits from punishment and clash; these need not be useless. With Si and You it forms a triple combination, gathering a Metal configuration. If a person is born in the Chou month and much Water and Wood appear in the day and hour, the course must turn toward Xun and Li before Earth can avoid decline.

**Yin 寅** establishes spring, its qi gathering three yangs; Bing Fire is born within it. Yin punishes Si, and Si combines with Shen: flourishing together, they become honored guests. Wood flourishes at Mao and is stored at Wei; those of the same kind form one family. At Wu, Fire becomes radiant, with the beauty of passing beyond the ordinary into the sage's realm. On meeting Shen, however, Yin suffers a clash, with the danger of broken Lu and injury to the month's governing branch. If the Four Pillars already contain much Fire, the course must not enter the southern Fire region. The saying “Wood does not hasten south” means this.[^17]

**Mao 卯 Wood** is mid-spring, endowed with flourishing qi. Although it may employ Metal and Water, these must not be excessive. If Geng and Xin repeatedly appear among the stems, Shen and You must not also appear among the branches, lest cutting and destruction follow. If Hai and Zi repeatedly appear among the branches, Gui and Ren must not also appear among the stems, lest there be injury through drifting away. Meeting You brings a clash: the tree must shed its leaves. Meeting Hai and Wei brings combination: the trees must form a forest. If the day and hour rest upon heavy Metal and the major luck cycle also moves westward, the harm cannot be withstood.

**Chen 辰** establishes late spring. It is the moisture of water and mud, upon which the roots of all things depend for cultivation. Although Jia is in Decline here, Yi's residual qi remains. Although Ren is in Tomb here, Gui restores its spirit. Xu supplies the key that can open the contents of the Storehouse. But if three Xu repeatedly clash and smash its door, this is not auspicious. If much Water and Wood appear in the day and hour, and the luck course also turns northwest, Chen Earth cannot survive.

<!-- source: L60-L66 -->
**Si 巳** falls in early summer, when Fire increases its radiance: it is the extreme of the six yangs. Geng Metal lodges its Birth here, [depending upon] its Wu mother; Wu Earth returns to Lu here, following its Fire mother. Meeting Shen produces punishment, yet within punishment there is combination, so that no harm results instead. Meeting Hai produces a clash, and the clash must break it, thereby causing injury. If the luck course also enters the southeastern region of generation and growth, the resulting blaze has the force to burn the very sky.[^18]

**Wu 午 month** sees blazing Fire rise. At the central qi, one yin is born. Geng arrives here without use; Ji arrives here and returns to its enclosure. Meeting Shen and Zi must bring struggle and control; meeting Yin and Xu increases brightness. A southeastern luck course enters the very region of bodily strength. Entry into the northwest instead brings rest and confinement, and the loss of form.

**Wei 未**, at the end of summer, has deepening yin and gradually declining Fire. It contains Yi Wood and Ding Fire: Officer and Seal are concealed, but Wealth is not. Without Hai and Mao to gather it, its form is difficult to change, and it is judged simply as Fire and Earth. Without Chou and Xu to punish and clash with it, the Storehouse does not open, and the power of Officer and Seal is difficult to obtain. If the pillars lack Fire, Metal and Water luck cycles are feared; if the day and hour are very cold, the regions of Bing and Ding are especially welcome. What the useful influence favors and avoids must be distinguished with utmost clarity; not the slightest error is permissible.

**Shen 申 palace** is the Birthplace of Water and Earth. Entering Si and Wu, it encounters Fire's refinement and becomes sword and halberd; meeting Zi and Chen, it encounters Water's tempering and gains a brighter edge. If Wood is plentiful but there is no Fire, Metal can ultimately prevail. If heavy Earth piles up and buries it, however, Metal suffers harm. Shen is stubborn, blunt Metal, different from gentle pearls and jade.

<!-- source: L68-L72 -->
**You 酉** establishes the eighth month: Metal's color is white and Water's current clear. If the day and hour contain much Fire, an eastward luck course is additionally feared. If Water flourishes in the day and hour, a southward course is also feared. Water and mud in the pillars ought to be useful; how could a northwestern course be without benefit? Yet a triple combination with Si and Chou can also make this Metal hard and keen. How could one insist upon treating yin Metal only as gentle pearls and jade?[^19]

**Xu 戌** is the Storehouse of the great furnace. Blunt iron and stubborn Metal depend upon it for refinement. Meeting the Chen dragon clashes forth Ren Water and produces rain and dew; meeting the Yin tiger gathers Bing Fire and produces literary brilliance. Yet when a Fire fate encounters it, this is entry into Tomb: how could injury be entirely avoided?

**Hai 亥** is the place of six yin lines, with rain and snow covering the roads. Earth reaching it lacks warmth; Metal reaching it becomes cold. Its image resembles the gathering of the Five Lakes; its use lies in a triple combination that has a fitting purpose. Thus, to recognize the place where Heaven and Earth become gently warm, look in the regions of Gen, Zhen, Xun, and Li.

In general, the methods of using the Five Phases possess no single fixed reality. Birth and Death, Decline and flourishing are also borrowed names. Go directly to the source and understand whence they arise. For example, the five yang stems are hard and the five yin stems soft. Yet if a hard stem is out of season, weak in body, unsupported, and repeatedly drained of qi, it loses its hardness. If a soft stem commands the season, is strong in body, and is assisted in its functioning, it is not defeated by softness. A further distinction makes Wood and Fire yang, Metal and Water yin. All welcome generation, support, and assistance; what is most valued is balance and harmony.[^20]
## 4. Assigning the Ten Stems to Celestial Phenomena
### 十干分配天文

<!-- source: L76-L80 -->
**Jia Wood is thunder.** Thunder is yang qi's exhalation. Jia Wood belongs to yang, and so thunder supplies its image. Examine the *Monthly Ordinances*: in the middle month of spring, thunder begins to sound. This is evidence of Jia Wood's flourishing. Moreover, thunder stirs within the earth and Wood is born from the earth; their principles are no different. Master Shao says, “Where thunder meets the earth, one sees Heaven's root.” What Birth of yang Wood is not brought about by the stirring of Heaven's root? Jia Wood thus reaches Extinction at Shen because thunder's sound gradually draws back at Shen. A fate with Jia as the day ruler likes to meet spring. Whether it forms Analogous Images, Approaching Qian, Distant Si, or Arched Noble Person, all are greatly auspicious; its luck course does not favor the west. The classic says, “Wood born in spring lives peacefully in the world and is assured of longevity.”[^21]

**Yi Wood is wind.** Yi Wood's Birth is at Wu and its Defeat at Si. It is born at Wu because Yi is the living Wood of mountains and forests, which grows luxuriantly with summer's arrival. As the poem says, “A thousand great trees, green with summer.” Why its Defeat at Si? Si is Xun's region; Xun is wind. Wood flourishes and wind is born. Wind arises from Wood and then breaks Wood, just as Fire arises from Wood and then burns Wood. It is therefore fitting to assign Defeat here. Calling Yi Wood wind speaks from the origin of its generation. For someone born on an Yi day, autumn command is greatly auspicious. Autumn is Metal's flourishing season; Yi Wood can transform and can follow, and tangled roots and knotted joints cannot be fashioned without a keen implement. Meeting Hai, it must die: is this not the time of falling leaves returning to the root?[^22]

**Bing is the sun.** The *Explanation of the Trigrams* says: “Li is Fire; it is the sun.” Sun and Fire are both images of cultivated brilliance; therefore Bing Fire's designation as sun does not change. The sun emerges in the morning and enters at evening; yang Fire is born at Yin and dies at You. How do these differ? Wan Qi's *Rhapsody of the True Treasure* takes a Bing day with a Chou hour as the configuration **The Sun Rising Above the Earth**. How meaningful! How meaningful! For all six Bing days, winter and summer births are inferior to spring and autumn. The spring sun warms the myriad things; the autumn sun dries them. Winter is dark with yin; summer blazes and steams. Examine this closely.[^23]

<!-- source: L82-L84 -->
**Ding Fire is the stars.** As Bing Fire dies, Ding Fire follows into Birth: in Heaven the sun declines and the stars return. The analogy is of this kind. The stars become brilliant only as night enters; yin Fire becomes radiant only as darkness approaches. What else could Ding be called but the stars? The *True Treasure Rhapsody* says: “Yin Fire with the Hai hour: wealth and distinction extend without end.” Commentators explain this as the three wonders of Wealth, Officer, and Seal, and that is acceptable. But do they recognize that Hai is in the north and is Heaven's Gate, and that there is also the principle of the stars arching toward the north? People born on Ding days like night and autumn, as starlight finds its proper season. They also like a course through places where the body is weak. What lies concealed within stone belongs to Ding Fire: even if the stone has been in water, striking it at once can still produce Fire. The Dingsi day in particular often harms father, elder brothers, wife, and children. Wealth fears Companions and Rob Wealth; the elder brother is subordinated to the younger; and Si contains Wu Earth, Hurting Officer.[^23]

**Wu Earth is rosy glow.** Earth has no qi exclusively its own; it depends upon Fire for birth. Rosy glow has no fixed substance; it borrows the sun to appear. Once one understands Bing Fire as the sun, one understands Wu Earth as rosy glow. Glow is the sun's remainder: when the sun is gone the glow vanishes, and when Fire is extinguished Earth has no living impulse. Therefore it is called rosy glow. Danao, developing the Five-Phase images of nayin, made Wuwu Heavenly Fire; his meaning was of this kind. A Wu Earth day ruler likes Water in the Four Pillars, which makes a superior configuration: glow and Water reflect each other and produce beautiful patterns. It especially likes Gui in the year or month stem. Gui is rain; when the glow appears after rain, cultivated brilliance becomes visible.

<!-- source: L86-L88 -->
**Ji Earth is cloud.** Ji Earth has its Birth at You. You is Dui's direction, whose image is marsh. A former worthy said, “Heaven sends timely rain; mountains and rivers bring forth clouds.” Clouds, then, are the qi of mountains and marshes. Although Ji belongs to Earth, considered in this way it is fitting to call it cloud. Jia and Ji combine and transform into Earth; their qi rises and clouds spread. Cloud and thunder join and make rain; its moisture penetrates downward and Earth is wetted. This is creative transformation at its subtlest—its very subtlest! A person whose body ruler is Ji Earth gains distinction by sitting on You, by spring birth, and by meeting [Jia]. One seated on Hai must not meet Yi Wood. Clouds rise to Heaven, but on meeting wind they are scattered beyond restraint.[^24]

**Geng Metal is the moon.** Geng is western yang Metal. How do we know that it corresponds to the moon? The reply is: “Geng among the Five Phases is like the moon amid the four seasons. Geng does not wait for autumn to have its Birth, yet it needs autumn to attain full strength. The moon does not wait for autumn to exist, yet in autumn it becomes brighter. As to color, the moon is white: their color is the same. As to qi, Metal generates Water and tides answer to the moon: their qi is the same. The ancient stem-cycle name for Geng is Shangzhang, showing its brightness alongside the sun. The classic says, ‘Metal sinks at Zi,’ showing its likeness to the moon sinking into waves. On the third day the moon appears in Geng's direction: the newborn moon shares Geng's position. Therefore Geng Metal is the moon.” For one born on a Geng day, Yi and Si appearing in the Four Pillars are called **White Moon, Clear Breeze**. Autumn is best, winter next; spring and summer have nothing to recommend them here.[^21]

<!-- source: L90-L94 -->
**Xin Metal is frost.** The eighth month is Xin Metal's established Lu. During this month Heaven's qi grows stern, white dew becomes frost, and plants yellow, shed their leaves, and decline. Thus yin Wood of the Five Phases reaches Extinction here. Once Wood has undergone cutting by axe and adze, it cannot live again. Axes and adzes enter mountain forests at the proper season; stern frost kills plants at the proper season. Measured against Heaven's Way and compared with human affairs, Xin Metal is indeed frost. Someone asks: “Frost always avoids the sun. Why, then, do Bing and Xin combine?” The reply is: “This, too, is the principle of mutual control. Fire controls Metal; hence, when they combine, Water transforms forth. Frost avoids the sun; when they meet, ice melts and likewise becomes Water. This is the image being taken.” A Xin person sitting on Mao or Wei, with Yi exposed, becomes very wealthy; sitting on Hai, with Bing exposed, brings distinction. Winter birth is favored.[^25]

**Ren Water is autumn dew.** Spring also has dew: why compare Ren with autumn alone? Spring dew is dew amid rain's moistening; autumn dew is dew after frost's descent. Dew is one thing, but spring presides over generation and autumn over cutting down. Their functions differ in this way. I take Ren as autumn dew because dew belongs to Water and Ren Water is born at Shen. Water by nature generates Wood; yet if Water is born here, why does Wood reach Extinction here? We therefore know that Ren's dew is autumn dew. A Ren day born in autumn gains greatest prominence on meeting Ding Fire. Ding is the starry river; Ren is autumn dew. Together they wash away summer's steaming heat, and the constellations shine clear.

**Gui Water is spring rain.** Gui Water is born in the Mao month and is called spring rain because yin Wood receives rain and springs into growth. Yet at Shen it dies: the seventh and eighth months often bring drought. One position ahead of Mao is Chen, the Dragon Palace. Mao is close to that palace and Water is born; when the dragon stirs, it transforms into rain. Mao is Thunder's Gate: once thunder shakes, the dragon must rise. From this we see Gui Water as spring rain. If a Guimao day reveals the stem [Ji], it has the image of clouds moving and rain being bestowed; the person must possess the ability to order the world and aid its people. Spring and summer are auspicious; autumn and winter are not. The poem says:[^26]

> A Gui day born in the region of Jisi:  
> The Killing star needs Wood to bring it low.  
> Though fame and profit climb to lofty eminence,  
> Alas, the span of life is not prolonged.
## 5. Assigning the Twelve Branches to Terrestrial Images
### 十二支分配地理

<!-- source: L98-L120 -->
**Zi is an ink pool.** Zi lies due north, belongs to Water, and has a color resembling ink; hence the image of an ink pool. A person born in a Zi year likes Guihai 癸亥 in the hour. This is called **Water Returning to the Great Sea**, and also **Two Fish Swimming in Ink**. Such a person must be a man of letters.[^27]

**Wu is a beacon tower.** Wu occupies the southern cardinal position and belongs to Fire and Earth; its colors are red and yellow. Hence it is named a beacon tower. Wu is also the horse, and beacon towers are places of warhorses, arms, and military fires. For a person born under Wu, Chen in the hour is beneficial: when the true dragon emerges, ordinary horses count for nothing. This is called **The Horse Becoming a Dragon Steed**.

**Mao is a jade grove.** Mao belongs to Yi Wood and stands due east. Its season is mid-spring, when the myriad things are born; its color resembles the green of fine jade. Hence “jade grove.” A Mao year meeting a Jiwei hour gives the image **The Hare Entering the Moon Palace**, indicating great distinction.

**You is a temple bell.** You belongs to Metal and lies near Xu and Hai. Xu and Hai are Heaven's Gate. A bell belongs to Metal; when a temple bell is struck, its sound reaches Heaven's Gate. Moreover, You is due west, and the temple belongs to the western realm of the Buddha. You meeting Yin is auspicious, called **The Bell Sounds and the Valley Answers**.

**Yin is a broad valley.** Yin lies in Gen's direction. Gen is mountain, and Wu Earth's Birth is here: the meaning of broad valley is thereby apparent. Yet Yin's palace contains the tiger. A person born under Yin, with Wuchen in the hour, has **The Tiger Roars and the Valley Wind Arises**; its might shakes ten thousand li.

**Shen is a renowned capital.** Kun is Earth, boundless in substance; nothing less than a renowned capital can illustrate it. Shen is Kun, and a capital is the dwelling of emperors and kings. Ren Water is born in Shen's palace, which also stands opposite Gen Mountain: Water encircles and mountains surround it. A chart favors a Shen year and Hai hour; this is **Earth and Heaven Joining in Peace**.

**Si is a great posting station.** A great posting station is a place where people gather and roads open in every direction. Bing Fire and Wu Earth within Si give this image. Moreover, ahead of Si stands Wu, the horse; hence “great posting station.” A person born under Si likes to obtain the Chen hour: the snake transforms into the Azure Dragon, a configuration called **The Thousand-Li Dragon Steed**.

**Hai is a suspended river.** The Celestial River's Water rushes onward without turning back; therefore it is called a suspended river. Hai is Heaven's Gate and also belongs to Water: is this not the image of a river hanging in the sky? For one born in a Hai year, Yin and Chen appearing in the day and hour make **Water Arching toward Thunder's Gate**.

**Chen is a grassy marsh.** The *Zuo Tradition* says: “In deep mountains and great marshes, dragons and snakes are born.” A marsh is where Water gathers. Chen stands next to the eastern position and is Water's Storehouse; therefore it is grass and marsh. Chen meeting Renxu or Guihai forms **The Dragon Returning to the Great Sea**.

**Xu is a burned plain.** The Xu month is the ninth, in autumn. Plants have withered, and farming households burn the ground before plowing. Xu also belongs to Earth; hence the name burned plain. Thus Xu and Chen are both places where the Noble Person does not alight. Someone born under Xu who meets Mao has **Spring Entering the Scars of Burning**.

**Chou is a willow bank.** Within Chou are Water, Earth, and Metal. The bank is Earth, serving to hold back Water; hence “willow bank.” The poem says, “Willows tender with a golden hue.” A Chou person meeting Jiwei in the hour has **The Moon Shining on Willow Tips**, a configuration of the highest order.

**Wei is a flower garden.** Why assign the flower garden to Wei rather than Mao? Mao is flourishing Wood and naturally becomes a wooded slope. Wei is Wood's Storehouse, like walls built to protect a multitude of flowers. Speaking of the hundred flowers points to the mixed qi within Wei. A Wei year entering the **Twin Flight** configuration is especially subtle: Xinwei meeting Wuxu, with the two stems unmixed, is an example.[^27]
<!-- source: L122 -->
Someone asks: “The images taken for the ten stems differ. Some are drawn from combinations, some from generation and control, some from direction, some from seasonal command, and some from beginning and ending. Why?” The reply is: “I have spoken of one aspect of each. A good student extends the analogies, and will then find in every stem the workings of waxing and waning, fullness and emptiness, and the subtlety of sounds answering and qi seeking its kind. This Way is the natural Way of creative transformation. How could it be my private invention?”

Another statement says: “Among the twelve branches, Chen, Xu, Chou, and Wei occupy the four corners. They are the substance of paired opposition: the branches belong to Earth, and Earth is still rather than moving. Wu and Ji dwell in the center. They are the functioning of circulation: the stems belong to Heaven, and Heaven moves rather than remaining still. The four Earth branches are not an addition, for each has its own governing qi. The two Earth stems are not a subtraction, for they lack fixed positions. Without this difference in the numbers of stems and branches, how could transformation be completed and the spirits set in motion?”[^28]

Heaven's origin has ten numbers; I have assigned them to celestial phenomena. Earth's origin has twelve; I have assigned them to terrestrial images. Is this, then, a forced analogy? Surely not. When the sages made the *Changes*, they took the images of the Eight Trigrams from distant things and from the body close at hand, repeatedly illuminating them without abandoning their inquiry. The principles of stems and branches are one with those of the *Changes*. Many practitioners speak of numbers while departing from principle; that is why I have brought this meaning into the open. The rhapsody says: “Speak of the useful influence; speak of the day ruler: each has what is fitting. Take Earth's veins; take Heaven's origin: these may be one road.” Only by embracing this subtlety can one reach the utmost. Otherwise, one has merely the fool's single insight.

The celestial assignments follow their sequence; the earthly branches interweave their four Birth positions, four Defeat positions, and four Storehouses. This, too, reflects considered understanding. Students who read these together with the preceding explanations will already have grasped more than half the matter.

## 6. On the Animal Correspondences of the Earthly Branches
### 論地支屬相

<!-- source: L126 -->
Someone asks: “Why do the earthly branches have animal correspondences while the heavenly stems do not?” The reply is: “The stems move and have no fixed image; the branches are still and have images. What is light and clear is Heaven; what is heavy and turbid is Earth. Within the heavy and turbid there are material creatures. Thus Zi belongs to the rat, Chou to the [ox], Yin to the tiger, Mao to the hare, Chen to the dragon, Si to the snake, Wu to the horse, Wei to the sheep, Shen to the monkey, You to the chicken, Xu to the dog, and Hai to the pig.”[^30]

“These twelve correspondences also distinguish odd and even and employ flourishing and decline. The odd group is rat, tiger, dragon, horse, monkey, and dog. One belongs to yang, and the feet of these six beasts have odd numbers [of digits]. The even group is ox, hare, snake, sheep, chicken, and pig. Two belongs to yin, and the feet of these six have even numbers [of digits]. But the snake has no feet: what meaning is taken from it? Si, as a month, is a month of pure yang, and as an hour a time of pure yang. Its number is even but its time yang; the snake therefore supplies its image. The snake is a yin creature. Without employing its feet, its image is already apparent. Perhaps this also implies an unwillingness to speak explicitly of yin. Moreover, there are double-headed snakes, which can serve as evidence. These twelve correspondences take the foremost of each group among the thirty-six creatures. They naturally distinguish yin from yang, odd from even. This is creative transformation's subtlety.”

Someone asks: “Why is each of the twelve animals deficient in something?” The reply is: “Heaven tilts northwest, and Earth is incomplete in the southeast. If Heaven and Earth themselves lack something, how could the bodies of beasts be complete? Thus the rat has little light in its eyes and goes out at night; the ox lacks teeth and [uses] lips and muzzle; the tiger lacks a neck and must turn its whole body. The hare has a cleft lip and no male; the dragon lacks ears and hears through its horns; the snake lacks feet yet moves swiftly. The horse is deficient in gall, always standing rather than sleeping; the sheep lacks pupils and does not close its eyes at death; the monkey lacks a spleen and likes fruit. The chicken lacks kidneys and is lustful without restraint; the dog lacks a stomach, eats filth, and is good at calling; the pig lacks sinews, often sleeping and seldom standing. These express the incompleteness of yin and yang and correspond to creative transformation. Humanity alone brings their fullness together; this is why humans are esteemed.”[^30]

Wang Chong's *Balanced Discourses* says: “The qi of the Five Phases injure one another, and blood-bearing creatures overcome and subdue one another. Yin is Wood and its creature the tiger; Xu is Earth and its creature the dog. Chou and Wei are also Earth: Chou's creature is the ox, Wei's the sheep. Wood overcomes Earth; therefore dogs, oxen, and sheep are subdued by tigers. Hai is Water and its creature the pig; Si is Fire and its creature the snake. Zi is also Water and its creature the rat; Wu is also Fire and its creature the horse. Water overcomes Fire; therefore pigs eat snakes. Fire is injured by Water; therefore a horse eating rat droppings develops a swollen belly.”[^31]

“Yet there are also cases in which they do not overcome one another. Wu is horse, Zi rat, You chicken, Mao hare. Water overcomes Fire: why does the rat not pursue the horse? Metal overcomes Wood: why does the chicken not peck the hare? Hai is pig, Wei sheep, Chou ox. Earth overcomes Water: why do oxen and sheep not kill pigs? Si is snake, Shen monkey. Fire overcomes Metal: why does the snake not eat the macaque? Macaques fear rats, and dogs bite macaques. The rat is Water and the macaque Metal. Water does not overcome Metal: why does the macaque fear the rat? Xu is Earth and Shen monkey. Earth does not overcome Metal: why does the monkey fear the dog? The creatures of the twelve positions agree still less with the idea of mutual control by qi and nature. In general, blood-bearing creatures subdue one another, even to devouring one another, because their teeth are blunt or sharp and their sinews and strength inferior or superior. It is through these that they overcome and subdue one another.”

## 7. On the Human Origin Holding Office

When the single qi was undifferentiated and form and substance not yet separated, what could be called yin or yang? Once the Great Inception began, the One divided into three, and in an instant distinctions arose. Heaven received the light and clear as yang; Earth received the heavy and turbid as yin. Humanity, positioned between Heaven and Earth, received the harmoniously blended qi of yin and yang. Thus the light and clear become the ten stems, governing Lu and called the **Heavenly Origin**; the heavy and turbid become the twelve branches, governing the body and called the **Earthly Origin**. Heaven and Earth each take their proper places, while what becomes a fully formed power between them is humanity. Therefore what is concealed within the branches governs fate and is called the **Human Origin**, also named the influence holding office. In the terminology of fate calculation, it is the useful influence of monthly command. The classic says: “The useful influence must not be injured; the day ruler most needs strength and vigor.”[^32]

After Beginning of Spring, yang Wood is employed for thirty-six days, with Gen Earth's field of allocation and the Birth of Bing and Wu. Six days after Awakening of Insects, yin Wood is employed for thirty-six days, with Gui Water's lodged Birth. Twelve days after Clear and Bright, Wu Earth is employed for eighteen days, with yang Water returning to Storehouse and yin Water returning to spirit. Summer, autumn, and winter follow the same pattern.”[^35]

“Yet the *Yuanyuan* and *Yuanhai* assign so many days after Beginning of Spring to Ji Earth's residual qi, so many to Gen Earth's field of allocation, and so many respectively to the successive Births of Bing and Wu. They assign so many days in the Mao month to Gui Water's lodged Birth, and so many in the Chen month to yang Water returning to Storehouse and yin Water returning to spirit. They never consider that Chou month's allotted use has already been fulfilled: what could remain over after spring begins?”

“‘Field of allocation’ means the gathering of a direction's flourishing qi. ‘Birth’ means returning to the mother to form an embryo. ‘Successive’ means that Yin must exist before Bing can be born, and Bing before Wu can be born. ‘Lodged Birth’ has only a provisional name, not an actual position of its own. ‘Returning to Storehouse’ cuts off the living qi and gathers it into concealment. ‘Returning to spirit’ continues the qi from its death and transforms it. These are the mysterious workings of Birth and Death, advance and retreat in the Five Phases. How could they be confined to so many days?”

“Moreover, Wood's use in spring and Metal's use in autumn are fixed principles. If one mixes in influences merely lodging there and gives each several days of exclusive use, the number belonging to the palace's ruling qi must be diminished. How, then, can one uphold the continuous circulation of one qi as spring Wood and summer Fire, each flourishing for seventy-two days? Matching the four seasons to the Five Phases' functioning means that the host has room to receive guests; it does not mean that guests can overcome the host. Within the ruling qi's authority, there are naturally the relative depths of beginning, middle, and end. Those employing it must weigh their relative strengths. How could this be confined to periods of three, five, or seven days?”

This account is sufficient to expose the error of the *Yuanyuan*. Furthermore, what is concealed within the branches is discussed only in relation to the month, not the year, day, or hour. There is profound meaning in the importance that fate reading gives to the month's governing framework.[^35]

## 8. On the Four Seasons and Their Solar Terms
### 論四時節氣

<!-- source: L156 -->
Among the seasonal beginnings and central qi, spring and autumn have a “division” but are not called “arrivals”; summer and winter have an “arrival” but are not called “divisions.” From Rain Water and Awakening of Insects onward, the twenty-four qi are assigned their several names. There must be reasons for those names. What are they? The four “Beginnings” are the seasonal boundary-qi of the four seasons. The end of Chou and beginning of Yin form a boundary; the middle of the month forms its center. The two “Divisions,” or equinoxes, mean that yin and yang are equal halves. The two “Arrivals,” or solstices, have a double meaning. From Zi to Si are the six yang stages, and from Wu to Hai the six yin stages. The arrivals stand between Si and Wu, Hai and Zi. At Winter Solstice, Hai's yin has reached its extreme; hence Zi, whose name is explained as “stopping.” Yang is born here, and so it is also called an arrival. At Summer Solstice, Si's yang has reached its extreme; hence Wu, explained as “opposing.” Yin is born here, and so this too is called an arrival.[^36]

From Autumn Equinox, Water begins to dry up; at [Beginning of Winter], Water begins to freeze; at Winter Solstice, the springs begin to stir; at Greater Cold, marsh-water is solid in its depths. What is now called Rain Water was formerly dew, frost, and snow—all Water qi condensed as cold reached its extreme. In spring, warming qi proceeds in its course and heat makes its beginning. Moreover, Heaven's one generates Water, and human beings and other creatures all begin in Water. Spring belongs to Wood, and Wood is generated by Water; it is fitting, therefore, that Rain Water follows Beginning of Spring. In the hexagram qi, the first month is Peace: Heaven's qi descends, fitting Rain Water. The second month is Great Power: thunder stands above Heaven, fitting Awakening of Insects. Rain Water before Awakening of Insects is therefore also appropriate.[^37]

Awakening of Insects means that all things emerge through Zhen, and Zhen is thunder. Clear and Bright means that all things are brought into order through Xun, and Xun is wind. Xun cleanses and orders: hence “Clear and Bright,” whose meaning is precisely cleanliness and order. Grain Rain is the third month's central qi. Since Rain Water, the rich substance and veins of the soil have stirred. When rain comes again at this point, those veins generate things and nourish the seeds of the five grains.

Lesser Fullness is the fourth month's central qi. Earlier scholars say that after Lesser Snow, yang grows by one part each day. Accumulated through thirty days, its thirty parts make one line at Winter Solstice. Yin's growth after Lesser Fullness is the same. The fourth month is the end of Qian; why call it fullness? The first six of Encounter says, “The lean sow is certain to stamp and range”; the first six of Kun says, “Treading on frost: hard ice arrives.” The lean sow illustrates smallness; its stamping illustrates fullness. Treading on frost illustrates smallness; hard ice illustrates fullness. The *Changes* speaks after the first yin has already been born; the calendar speaks at the first stirring of that yin. Its concern is deep and its precautions early. Lesser Snow is followed by Greater Snow, but here there is only Lesser Fullness and no Greater Fullness. The intention may be understood.[^38]

As for Grain Rain, the third month's central qi, and Grain in Ear, the fifth month's central qi, these two terms alone speak specifically of grain and wheat. With grain one must trace the beginning of its life: it is sown in spring and receives Wood's qi, then is cut down in autumn, when Metal controls Wood. With wheat one must attend to the end of its completion: it is sown in autumn and receives Metal's qi, then ripens in summer, when Fire controls Metal.[^38]

Lesser Heat is the sixth month's boundary term and Greater Heat its central qi. Since heat is already strong after Summer Solstice, ought it still to be called lesser? This overlooks the *Changes*: “Cold departs and heat comes; heat departs and cold comes. Cold and heat push one another onward, and the year is completed.” The entire first half of the year may be spoken of as warmth, and the entire second half as cold. The first month begins warmth; the twelfth ends cold. Greater Heat and Lesser Heat are merely designations within that warming half of the year. At the middle of the sixth month, heat reaches its extreme and is therefore called greater; before it reaches that degree it is still lesser.

End of Heat is the seventh month's central qi. The seventh month ends heat and begins cold. The Great Fire star flows westward, and heat then retires. Here “ending” means concealing or lying hidden. White Dew is the eighth month's boundary term; Cold Dew the ninth month's. Autumn originally belongs to Metal: Metal's color is white and its qi cold. White is the dew's color; cold is the dew's qi. Whiteness comes first and the qi then begins to chill: there is a gradual progression. Frost's Descent is the ninth month's central qi, when chilled dew begins to congeal into frost.

After Beginning of Winter come Lesser Snow and Greater Snow. Cold qi begins with dew, reaches its middle in frost, and ends in snow. Before frost there is dew, which passes from whiteness into coldness; after frost there is snow, which passes from lesser to greater. All proceed by degrees. As for Lesser Cold and Greater Cold, the *Airs of Bin* says, “In the first month, the wind bites; in the second, the cold is keen.” The biting is the wind's cold; thus what follows the eleventh month is Lesser Cold. The keenness is the qi's cold; thus the twelfth month's end is Greater Cold.[^39]

Taken together, the first half of the year governs birth and growth: rain, thunder, and wind are all qi of generation. The second half governs bringing life to completion: dew, frost, and snow are all qi of completion. In the second half, the terms speak of Heaven's seasons rather than agricultural seasons, for agricultural affairs are most urgent in spring and summer. Earlier scholars say: “Change is transformation's gradual advance; transformation is change's completion.” After Beginning of Spring and Rain Water, cold qi gradually changes; by Beginning of Summer, cold has entirely transformed into [heat]. Yet Lesser Heat and Greater Heat show that transformation still advances by degrees. After Beginning of Autumn and End of Heat, hot qi gradually changes; by Beginning of Winter it has entirely transformed into cold. Yet Lesser Cold and Greater Cold show that this transformation, too, advances by degrees.[^37]
It is also said: “The sun and moon travel and the four seasons are completed, because their courses have constancy; therefore the sages established methods for calculating them. Yin and yang interweave and the myriad things are born, because their changes are inexhaustible; therefore the sages pointed to things as signs by which to observe them.” They penetrated the beginnings and endings, earliness and lateness of the six qi, and the greatness and smallness, fullness and emptiness of the five movements. Tracing these to ultimate principle and examining them through exact number, they left instruction for all ages without error.

The classic says: “Five days are called a *hou*; three hou a qi; six qi a season; four seasons a year.” It also says: “The sun is yang and the moon yin. Their movements have divisions and measures; their circuits have ordered courses. The sun travels one du and the moon thirteen du with a remainder.” Thus long and short months form a year of three hundred and sixty-five days, and the accumulation of the qi's remainder fills an intercalation. The classic says that the sun regularly travels one du of Heaven in a day and night, making one day; after three hundred and sixty-five days and a quarter, it completes Heaven's circuit and forms a year. A hou of five days regularly responds to this. Three hou therefore make a qi, or fifteen days.[^40]

Three qi make a division. The divisions are Beginning of Spring, Spring Equinox, Beginning of Summer, Summer Solstice, Beginning of Autumn, Autumn Equinox, Beginning of Winter, and Winter Solstice—these “four divisions,” as the text calls them. Three times eight gives twenty-four qi, distributed over the four seasons, completing a year.[^41]

Spring and autumn are called divisions because, in terms of the six qi, at the middle of the second month the first qi ends and passes into the second; at the middle of the eighth month the fourth qi ends and passes into the fifth. In terms of the four seasons, these are the moments when yin and yang, cold and warmth, can be divided. Day and night divide into fifty ke each: this, too, is yin and yang's equal division. Thus the classic says, “At the divisions, the qi differ.” This is its meaning.

Winter and summer are called arrivals because, in terms of the six qi, at the middle of the fifth month the qi governing Heaven reaches its station, and at the middle of the eleventh month the qi at the spring reaches its station. In terms of seasonal command, yin and yang have then reached their utmost. At Summer Solstice, the long day does not exceed sixty ke: yang reaches its extreme here. At Winter Solstice, the short day does not exceed forty ke: yin reaches its extreme here. In both cases Heaven's signs have not yet changed; hence the classic says, “At the arrivals, the qi are the same.” This is its meaning.[^40]

Heaven turns from west to east, while the sun, moon, and five planets follow Heaven from east to west. Thus the *Comprehensive Discussions in the White Tiger Hall* says: “Heaven revolves leftward; the sun, moon, and five planets travel rightward.” Within Heaven, the sun, moon, and five planets are yin; therefore they move rightward, as ministers facing their ruler. The sun travels one du of Heaven in a day and night; the moon travels thirteen du with a remainder. The remainder means dividing one du into nineteen parts and taking seven. In broad terms, the moon moves swiftly, completing one circuit of Heaven in twenty-seven days. Summing thirteen du and seven nineteenth-parts over twenty-nine days gives more than three hundred and eighty-seven du.[^42]

Compare the moon's swift movement with the sun's slow movement: in twenty-nine days the sun has traveled only twenty-nine du, while the moon has already completed one circuit of three hundred and sixty-five du and gone another twenty-two. It is therefore still seven du short of the sun. Yin–yang specialists explain that the sun and moon's movements vary in advance and delay, slowness and speed, and have no single invariable measure; therefore the endings of long and short months differ.

The basis of a year is three hundred and sixty-five days and a quarter—a quarter being twenty-five ke. Remove the year's excess and there are three hundred and sixty days. Remove the six days lacking in the short months, and only three hundred and fifty-four days remain to form the year. The total deficiency is eleven days and twenty-five ke, which accumulate into an intercalation. Under the system of twelve months, each has a qi establishing its beginning; that qi is the arrival of three hou. At the month's middle, observe the direction in which the Dipper establishes its position: it is one of the twelve branch directions. An intercalary month has no establishing qi or direction of its own; its qi belong elsewhere. Follow the calendar's eight divisions, calculate the accumulated remainder, and the intercalation is formed, completing Heaven's measure.[^43]

Hence the classic says: “Establish the starting point at the beginning, mark the proper alignment in the middle, and calculate the remainder at the end.” Such is its meaning. In Heaven's dark immensity, where are there actual degree marks? By taking the twenty-eight mansions as evidence of the sun and moon's travel within a day, their movement is recorded in du. Thus the classic says, “The stars and mansions are that by which the courses of sun and moon are measured.” To measure here is to mark their du. Heaven likewise has no inherent hou markings. Wind, rain, frost, dew, grasses, and trees respond at their appointed times and can be checked; measuring these responses is called observing the hou. The days of a hou also correspond to the qi of the five movements generating one another and taking their turn—namely five days. Like a ring without a beginning, the circuit ends and begins again. The *Documents* says: “A full cycle is three hundred and sixty-six days; by means of the intercalary month, determine the four seasons and complete the year.” This expresses the same meaning.

## 9. On the Day's Ke Divisions
### 論日刻

<!-- source: L160 -->
A day and night contain twelve hours, which must be evenly distributed through the day. Thus the wisest devised a bronze vessel holding water, which drips to the vessel's indicator. The indicator is divided into a hundred ke to measure time. Whatever the sun and moon's brightness or obscurity, they cannot escape this reckoning: within one day there are a hundred ke to observe. The six qi together govern a year, so each governs sixty days and eighty-seven and a half ke. From this one knows the earliness or lateness of the qi transitions. Winter and summer differ in day-length: daylight and darkness displace one another, and sunrise and sunset occur at different ke. Yet their total remains a hundred ke. The ke of the qi transition itself cannot be moved.[^44]

It does not recognize that their qi begin at the same ke—Heaven's own natural subtlety.[^45]

## 10. On Hours and Their Divisions
### 論時刻

<!-- source: L164 -->
The twelve hours of day and night divide a hundred ke equally. Each hour contains eight large ke and two small ke. The large ke total ninety-six, the small ke twenty-four; six small ke equal one large ke, making a hundred ke in all. The first half of an hour has four large ke. Its first is called **chu-chu**, followed by **chu-1**, **chu-2**, and **chu-3**; its final small ke is **chu-4**. The second half likewise has four large ke: **zheng-chu**, **zheng-1**, **zheng-2**, and **zheng-3**; its final small ke is **zheng-4**.[^46]

In the Zi hour, the first half falls before midnight and belongs to the previous day; the second half falls after midnight and belongs to the present day. This resembles Winter Solstice, the eleventh month's central qi: one yang returns, beginning Heaven's course. In ancient calendars each hour began with its two small ke, each followed by four large ke. That arrangement is less convenient for reckoning than the present calendar's. The popular statement that Zi, Wu, Mao, and You each contain nine ke while all other hours contain eight is incorrect.

The discussion says: In the method of reading fate, the hour determines rise and fall. An hour has eight [large] ke, and the qi of its initial and central halves differ. The initial half has its beginning-qi; the central half its middle-qi. Therefore, in using the hour, one ordinarily employs its central half. For example, Gui corresponds to the lower four ke of Zi, and Gen to the lower four ke of Chou, because these receive the heavenly stem's proper qi. The initial half still carries the previous hour's qi and has not yet occupied the following hour's qi. Moreover, failure to distinguish the day at midnight makes the day itself immediately wrong; between Zi and Hai, the hour is difficult to determine. Apart from chu-chu and zheng-4, within the intervening six ke, cloud and sunshine may suddenly change, and cold and warmth differ greatly. Can a person's recorded birth-hour really be assumed to be exact? For the present I distinguish them according to the *Season-Granting Calendar*. The essential task is for a discerning person to observe closely and inquire in detail, so that error may be avoided.[^50]

## 11. On the Sun's Stations, the Moon's Stem Assignments, and Their Rising, Setting, and Conjunctions
### 論太陽躔次太隂納甲及出入㑹合

<!-- source: L216-L218 -->
Chen [Tuan] says: “The sun is yang within yang, the image of the sovereign. Its virtue is supremely firm and its substance supremely vigorous. Its travel through Heaven divides day from night and distinguishes cold from heat. In one day it makes a circuit of Heaven, yet falls short of Heaven by one du. Accumulated over a year, this brings it exactly into conjunction with Heaven. The sun thus has three paths. The middle path is the Yellow Path. [Northward] it reaches Eastern Well, nearer the pole; southward it reaches Ox-Leader, farther from the pole; eastward at Horn and westward at Bond, its distance from the pole is intermediate. Taken together, the middle, southern, and northern paths make three.”[^51]

“At the extreme south, in Ox-Leader, it is Winter Solstice: daylight forty ke, night sixty. At the extreme north, in Eastern Well, it is Summer Solstice: daylight sixty ke, night forty. Midway between the northern and southern extremes come the spring and autumn equinoxes, with day and night fifty ke each. Its divisions in traveling through Heaven are these:”[^52]

“The moon is yin within yang, the image of the empress and consorts. Its virtue is supremely soft and its substance supremely compliant. Its travel through Heaven assists the ordering work of the sun; its appearance at night is examined to determine waxing and waning. The moon originally has no light: attached to the sun, it has brightness. Considered in its unilluminated substance, it is pure yin and images Kun; this is the time of the dark moon and new moon.”

“After [three] days, it falls short of the sun by somewhat more than thirty-seven du, and the brightness first comes into being. Beginning to borrow the sun's brightness, it gains light and is called the first crescent: the first birth of yang. At dusk it appears in Geng, the image of Zhen ☳. By the eighth day it falls short of the sun by somewhat more than ninety-eight du, receiving half the sun's brightness. It is called the bowstring phase: yang is approaching half. At dusk it appears in Ding, the image of Dui ☱. By the fifteenth day it falls short of the sun by somewhat more than one hundred and eighty-two and a half du, standing opposite it. Receiving the full illumination of the sun, it becomes wholly round and is called the full moon: all three yang lines are complete. At dusk it appears in Jia, the image of Qian ☰.”[^53]
“Three days later it falls short of the sun by somewhat more than two hundred and nineteen du, and the dark body first comes into being. Its opposition to the sun is now oblique, and the yin body begins to appear; hence it is called the dark-body phase, meaning that yin has begun to stir again. At dawn it appears in Xin, the image of Xun ☴. Five days later it falls short of the sun by somewhat more than two hundred and eighty-one du. Half is dark and half borrows the sun's brightness; hence the name lower bowstring, meaning that half the moon's light remains. At dawn it appears in Bing, the image of Gen ☶.”

“Another six days, together with ‘somewhat more than four hundred and ninety parts of the day’ [the numerical wording is defective], exhaust the shortfall of three hundred and sixty-five and a quarter du and bring it into conjunction with the sun. It can no longer borrow any of the sun's brightness and becomes dark again; therefore it is called the dark moon. It sinks entirely in Yi, the image of Kun ☷.”[^54]

“The measure of its daily travel through Heaven is three hundred du and seven nineteenths of a du; relative to Heaven, it falls short by twenty du and seven nineteenths, and relative to the sun by thirteen du and seven nineteenths. After twenty-nine days and four hundred and ninety-nine nine-hundred-fortieths of a day, it meets the sun in a branch station. Thus in one year there are twelve conjunctions, giving three hundred and fifty-four days and three hundred and forty nine-hundred-fortieths of a day, when it meets Heaven and forms a year.”[^54]

“Therefore the moon has nine courses. These are two Black Paths, emerging north of the Yellow Path at Beginning of Winter and Winter Solstice; two Red Paths, emerging south of it at Beginning of Summer and Summer Solstice; two White Paths, emerging west of it at Beginning of Autumn and Autumn Equinox; and two Blue-Green Paths, emerging east of it at Beginning of Spring and Spring Equinox. Together with the Yellow Path, these make nine. Thus at Beginning of Spring and Spring Equinox it follows the Blue-Green Path, its division lying in Jia's sector; at Beginning of Autumn and Autumn Equinox it follows the White Path, in Geng's sector; at Beginning of Winter and Winter Solstice it follows the Black Path, in Ren's sector; at Beginning of Summer and Summer Solstice it follows the Red Path, in Bing's sector.”[^55]

“The branch at which sun and moon meet, and the direction illuminated by its triple combination, determine the Heavenly Virtue and Monthly Virtue stars. Thus the third month establishes Chen, whose triple combination is Shen–Zi–Chen. Sun and moon meet at You, emerge at Geng, and enter the enclosure at Ren; therefore Heavenly Virtue and Monthly Virtue are at Ren. The sixth month establishes Wei, whose triple combination is Hai–Mao–Wei. Sun and moon meet at Wu, emerge at Bing, and enter the enclosure at Jia; therefore both virtues are at Jia. The ninth month establishes Xu, whose triple combination is Yin–Wu–Xu. Sun and moon meet at Mao, emerge at Jia, and enter the enclosure at Bing; therefore both virtues are at Bing. The twelfth month establishes Chou, whose triple combination is Si–You–Chou. Sun and moon meet at Zi, emerge at Ren, and enter the enclosure at Geng; therefore both virtues are at Geng.”[^56]

“Zi and Wu are the endings and beginnings of sun and moon; Mao and You are the gates of their emergence and disappearance. Their divisions are therefore manifold, including Greater Yin and Greater Yang. Someone born in the sun's proper station, during the four greatly auspicious hours and under the two virtues of Heaven and Moon, is said to gather the excellent virtue of a whole month. Such people are often eminent, distinguished, humane, and compassionate. If the stem-assignment and trigram qi also accord, their wealth and distinction extend without end, sharing substance with sun and moon.”

Yet I have further doubts about the theory of assigning stems to the trigrams. The *Changes* does not discuss it; the *Seal of the Unity of the Three* does. Practitioners of the numerical arts rely upon it as a subtle principle, without considering that the moon's substance is never actually diminished or deficient. Its names arise because it receives illumination at different times; its roundness has never ceased to exist. If stems are assigned to trigrams, the Eight Trigrams have definite yin and yang, broken and unbroken lines, fixed and unchanging. How can these be matched with the moon's appearing in Geng at dusk as Zhen's image, or in Ding as Dui's image? Kan and Li, one full in the middle and the other empty in the middle, do not resemble the moon when tested against it. To assign Wu and Ji to them cannot escape being forced.[^57]

Examine the *Azure Satchel Classic*: “Jia, Yi, Ren, and Gui belong to yang, and the four branches within yang are attached to them. Geng, Bing, Ding, and Xin belong to yin, and the four branches within yin are paired with them.” Chen Tuan's commentary says: “This explains the principle of assigning stems and pairing branches—the way the sages brought them into use.”

“Qian is yang substance and occupies the ninth palace: the trigram is yang and its number odd. Jia receives Heaven's three as yang and is the first yang heavenly stem. Qian is called father; therefore Jia is assigned to it. Kun is yin substance and occupies the first palace: the trigram is yin and its number even. Yi receives Earth's eight as yin and is the first yin heavenly stem. Kun is called mother; therefore Yi is assigned to it. Qian and Kun, father and mother, thus receive stems in the east. The east is where things are born, and Qian and Kun hold authority over it.”[^58]

“Gen is young yang and occupies the sixth palace: the trigram is yang and its number odd. Bing receives Heaven's seven as yang; Gen is the youngest son, and therefore Bing is assigned to it. This is the first transformation of Kun. Dui is young yin and occupies the fourth palace: the trigram is yin and its number even. Ding receives Earth's two as yin; Dui is the youngest daughter, and therefore Ding is assigned to it. This is the first transformation of Qian. The youngest son and daughter thus receive stems in the south. The south is where things transform, and Gen and Dui govern that transformation.”

“Zhen is eldest yang and occupies the eighth palace: the trigram is yang and its number even. Geng receives Heaven's nine as yang; Zhen is the eldest son, and therefore Geng is assigned to it. This is Qian's second transformation. Xun is eldest yin and occupies the second palace: the trigram is yin and its number even. Xin receives Earth's four as yin; Xun is the eldest daughter, and therefore Xin is assigned to it. This is Kun's second transformation. The eldest son and daughter thus receive stems in the west. The west is where things are completed, and Zhen and Xun exercise its command.”
“Li is yin in function and occupies the third palace: the trigram is yin and its number odd. Ren receives Heaven's one as yang; Li is the middle daughter, and therefore Ren is assigned to it. This is Qian's fourth transformation. Kan is yang in function and occupies the seventh palace: the trigram is yang and its number odd. Gui receives Earth's six as yin; Kan is the middle son, and therefore Gui is assigned to it. This is Kun's fourth transformation. The middle son and daughter thus receive stems in the north. The north is where things are stored, and Kan and Li administer that office.”

“Wu and Ji receive the completion numbers Heaven's five and Earth's ten, becoming the central qi of Heaven and Earth. They therefore dwell in the center, honored at the August Ultimate, with no corresponding assignment. They complete transformations at the endings of the four seasonal qi and bring nourishment to Kun. Kun's substance is Earth, the meeting place of Fire and Metal, and therefore they attach to it. Their yin and yang follow one another according to number; their isolation and flourishing are determined by the trigrams.”[^58]

“As for assigning the twelve earthly branches to trigrams, the four phases are distributed and attached to the four cardinal palaces. Kan has Water as its substance, and Shen, Zi, and Chen are Water's enclosure; therefore they are assigned to Kan and follow Water. Li has Fire as its substance, and Yin, Wu, and Xu are Fire's enclosure; therefore they are assigned to Li and follow Fire. Dui has Metal as its substance, and Si, You, and Chou are Metal's enclosure; therefore they are assigned to Dui and follow Metal. Zhen has Wood as its substance, and Hai, Mao, and Wei are Wood's enclosure; therefore they are assigned to Zhen and follow Wood. Thus yang branches follow yang stems and yin branches follow yin stems.”

“The five qi are thereby generated and completed, and the Eight Trigrams close and unite. The sages, following ultimate principle, traced their waxing and waning and made subtle use of them. Heaven's yang and Earth's yin open and close, create and transform, generating and completing without fixed limit. How fine are these incipient workings!”

The stem-assignment accounts of alchemical classics and calendrical arts go no further than Laozi's chapter beginning, “Carrying the vital soul and embracing the One, can you keep them from separating?” Interpreted through sun and moon, this means that the sun places its light upon the moon's dark body and illuminates it, as a person boards a carriage and is borne upon it. Before the full moon, the light rides upon the body's western side; after the full moon, it finally remains upon the eastern side. Does the moon not turn toward the sun?[^59]

As the moon begins to grow, the sun's light covers the western side of its dark body and gradually fills the eastern side, until at the full it becomes round. After the full, the sun's light remains upon the eastern side while the western side gradually wanes, until at the dark moon it is exhausted. The moon receives its brightness by facing the sun. Before the full, the sun is on its right; after the full, on its left. It receives illumination from whichever side the sun occupies. Assigning these changes to trigrams does not seem a sound account of the actual process.

## 12. On Seasonal Strength and the Twelve Palaces of Lodged Life
### 論五行旺相休囚死並寄生十二宫

<!-- source: L220-L222 -->
Abundant virtue riding its proper season is called **flourishing**. Thus Wood flourishes in spring. Flourishing Wood generates Fire; Fire is Wood's child, inheriting the father's undertaking, and is therefore **supported**. Wood uses Water for its generation: what generates me is my parent. Now that the offspring has obtained its time and ascended to a bright and prominent position, its parent ought to know when to withdraw. Water therefore **rests**. Rest here has the sense of consummate goodness, at ease without affairs. Fire controls Metal, which is Wood's ghost. Controlled by Fire, Metal cannot exercise its function and is therefore **confined**. Fire generates Earth, and Earth is Wood's Wealth. Wealth is something hidden away. As plants emerge, Earth disperses and its qi becomes dust; thus spring Wood controls Earth and Earth is in **death**.[^60]

In summer Fire flourishes. Fire generates Earth, so Earth is supported; Wood generates Fire, so Wood rests; Water controls Fire, so Water is confined; Fire controls Metal, so Metal is in death. In the sixth month Earth flourishes. Earth generates Metal, so Metal is supported; Fire generates Earth, so Fire rests; Wood controls Earth, so Wood is confined; Earth controls Water, so Water is in death. In autumn Metal flourishes. Metal generates Water, so Water is supported; Earth generates Metal, so Earth rests; Fire controls Metal, so Fire is confined; Metal controls Wood, so Wood is in death. In winter Water flourishes. Water generates Wood, so Wood is supported; Metal generates Water, so Metal rests; Earth controls Water, so Earth is confined; Water controls Fire, so Fire is in death.

Observe a great summer drought: metal and stone flow, Water and Earth are scorched. In the sixth month hot qi increases and cold qi disappears. In autumn Metal prevails and plants yellow and fall. In the depth of winter, cold is extreme, Water congeals into ice, and Fire's qi abruptly diminishes. Their flourishing and death can broadly be seen in these examples. In the succession of the four seasons, a term withdraws as soon as it reaches fullness; in the nature of the Five Phases, completed work must turn over. Yang reaches its extreme and descends; yin reaches its extreme and rises. The sun declines after noon; the moon wanes after fullness. This is Heaven's constant Way.

Human beings live between Heaven and Earth. Accumulated power must diminish; gathered wealth must disperse. Youth turns to decline, and joy at its extreme turns to sorrow. This is humanity's constant condition. Thus flourishing and decline, gain and loss, glory and withering, advance and retreat cannot escape this principle. The classic says: “Though humanity is more numinous than the myriad things, its fate cannot escape the Five Phases.” These words say it all.

The Five Phases lodge their life in twelve palaces: Birth, Bathing, Cap and Belt, Arrival at Office, Imperial Prosperity, Decline, Sickness, Death, Tomb, Extinction, Gestation, and Nurturing. They circulate without beginning, completing a round and beginning again. Creative transformation broadly resembles human life; its circuit through the twelve palaces resembles the world's recurring cycle of lives. The *Essentials of the Three Fates* explains them as follows, beginning at the reception of qi.[^61]
**First: Receiving Qi**, also called **Extinction** or **the Womb**. The myriad things lie within the earth without yet possessing an image, like an empty maternal womb in which nothing has yet formed.

**Second: Receiving the Embryo.** Heaven and Earth's qi join, their mingling bringing things into being. Within the earth, things begin to sprout and first possess their qi, as a human being receives the qi of father and mother.

**Third: Taking Form.** Things form within the earth, as a person takes form within the mother's womb.

**Fourth: Birth.** Things emerge and turn toward flourishing, as a person is newly born and begins to grow.

**Fifth: Bathing**, also called **Defeat**. Newly born things are tender and fragile, easily damaged, like a person bathed on the third day after birth and thereby brought near exhaustion and extinction.

**Sixth: Cap and Belt.** Things gradually flourish and become beautiful, like a person putting on cap and clothing.

**Seventh: Arrival at Office.** Things have flowered and borne fruit, like a person taking office.

**Eighth: Imperial Prosperity.** Things mature, like a person in full flourishing.

**Ninth: Decline.** The forms of things decline, like the weakening of a person's qi.

**Tenth: Sickness.** Things become sick, like a person's illness.

**Eleventh: Death.** Things die, like a person's death.

**Twelfth: Tomb**, also called **Storehouse**. Things complete their work and are concealed in storehouses, like a person reaching the end and returning to the grave. After returning to Tomb, they again receive qi, enter womb and gestation, and are born.

Whenever one examines creative transformation, the presence of Birth and flourishing need not immediately be judged auspicious, nor the presence of rest, confinement, Death, or Extinction immediately judged ominous. If Birth and flourishing are excessive, restraint and subjugation are appropriate; if Death and Extinction indicate insufficiency, generation and support are appropriate. The subtlety lies in recognizing how to adapt to change. The ancients called Gestation, Birth, flourishing, and Storehouse the four honored states; Death, Extinction, Sickness, and Defeat the four avoided states; and the remaining four neutral. This, too, is only a general statement.

## 13. On Deriving the Month and Hour
### 論遁月時

<!-- source: L226-L238 -->
In a fate, the year is the root and the father; the month represents brothers, colleagues, and friends; the day is the ruler, the wife, and one's own person; the hour represents descendants, the imperial seat, and the foremost governor of a lifetime's honor and disgrace. It is also said: “The year is the root, the month the seedling, the day the blossom, and the hour the fruit.” A seedling cannot grow without a root, and fruit cannot set without a blossom. Thus the month is derived from the year and the hour from the day. To derive the month: in a Jia or Ji year, the first month begins with Bingyin, the second is Dingmao, and the twelve months proceed in order.[^62]

The old song says:

> In Jia and Ji years, Bing takes the first place;  
> In Yi and Geng years, Wu stands at the head.  
> For Bing and Xin years, seek the start at Geng;  
> For Ding and Ren, flow onward from Ren's place.  
> Where, then, do Wu and Gui begin?  
> Upon Jiayin, pursue the answer well.

To derive the hour: for someone born on a Jiazi day in the Zi hour, “Jia and Ji return to Jia.” One therefore knows the Zi hour is Jiazi, the Chou hour Yichou, and the twelve hours proceed in order.

The old song says:

> Jia and Ji return to Jia;  
> Yi and Geng begin with Bing.  
> Bing and Xin arise from Wu;  
> Ding and Ren dwell at Gengzi.  
> From where do Wu and Gui set forth?  
> Renzi is the direct road.

The foregoing methods for establishing month and hour take the combining numbers of the heavenly stems, the pairing of yin and yang. Once the combining number is taken, the transforming number naturally arises. For the month, take generation; for the hour, take control. For example, Jia and Ji transform into Earth. Fire generates Earth, so the month begins at Bingyin. [Wood] controls Earth, so the hour begins at Jiazi. Beginning the month sequence at Yin expresses humanity's birth at Yin, the time when eastern cultivation first arises. Beginning the hour sequence at Zi expresses Heaven's opening at Zi, the moment when one yang is first born.[^63]

Investigated fully, both turn through mutual generation and circulate without beginning. The calendrical origin of high antiquity had Jiazi for year, month, day, and hour. The rule that Jia and Ji begin at Jiazi derives from this. Once Jiazi is established, Yichou and Bingyin follow in sequence through the twelve palaces. Yang generates yang and yin generates yin, with one position intervening; corresponding kinds become husband and wife. Thus the method for establishing months is already contained within the method for establishing hours.

## 14. On Year, Month, Day, and Hour
### 論年月日時

<!-- source: L242 -->
In discussing a person's fate, year, month, day, and hour are arranged as the Four Pillars. Since the month is derived from the year, the year is taken as the **root**; since the hour is derived from the day, the day is taken as the **ruler**. The ancient method reads from the year, while Ziping reads from the day: this is their basis. If a person's root is Wood and obtains the Mao month to ride upon, or the ruler is Metal and obtains the You hour, this is called **Root and Ruler Riding Flourishing Qi**.[^64]

If a Water root obtains a Jiashen, Bingzi, Renxu, or Guihai month; a Fire ruler a Bingyin, Wuwu, Jiachen, or Yisi hour; a Wood root a Jihai, Xinmao, Jiayin, or Gengyin month; or a Metal ruler a Xinsi, Guiyou, Gengshen, or Renshen hour, this is called **Root and Ruler Returning Home**. If a Wood root obtains a Guiwei month, a Metal ruler an Yichou hour, a Water root a Renchen month, or a Fire ruler a Jiaxu hour, this is called **Root and Ruler Holding the Seal**. With the four positions arranged in this manner, auspicious influences coming and going, and baleful influences keeping away, it is called **Root and Ruler Gaining Their Places**.

When the root is stronger than the ruler, one generally receives ancestral protection. When the ruler is stronger than the root, one must establish oneself independently. When both are strong, wealth and distinction are complete. Even then, the Four Pillars must be moderated upward and downward toward the center, without excess or deficiency, before the fate can be judged good. If one position is deficient, obstruction and delay must follow.

Yet the various schools of fate calculation all say: “A good year is not as good as a good month; a good month not as good as a good day; a good day not as good as a good hour.” Broadly, the year covers an entire year, the month thirty days, while day and hour are more individually specific. But they fail to recognize that an auspicious day and hour become useless if the month does not respond. Moreover, the useful influence is commonly taken from the month. Can the month, then, be treated lightly?

Li Xuzhong of the Tang singled out the day stem as ruler, yet examined year, month, and hour together for generation, control, restraint, transformation, flourishing, support, rest, and confinement, establishing configurations from them. Compare this to a balance: the year is its hook, fastening the object; the month its suspension cord, lifting the object; the day its beam, whose star-marks and liang measurements must not err; the weight distinguishes heaviness from lightness by the smallest additions and subtractions. This disclosed what earlier worthies had not disclosed; therefore present-day practitioners follow him.[^65]

Nevertheless, the ancients distinguished three rulers and established three life periods. Year and month govern the first ruler; month and day the middle ruler; day and hour the final ruler. Their method is the same as the astrologers' division into three rulers. If the first ruling star gains power in the birth year, the first period is good; without power, it brings obstruction. The middle and final rulers are treated in the same way. As for the three periods: the birth month is the first, governing twenty-five years; the birth day the middle, governing twenty-five years; the birth hour the final, governing fifty years.[^66]

If the first period meets Lu, Horse, and noble influences, without encountering Void, Severed Road, or the Crossing, Retreating, and Hidden Spirits, advancement occurs in the first period. If the middle period has these conditions, establishment comes in middle age. If the final period has them, success comes in old age. Dividing the Four Pillars into three rulers and three periods thus shows that they should be weighted together.

It is also said: “The year is Tai Sui, governing the blessings and disasters of a person's whole life. “The month is the origin of the luck course, which begins from the monthly establishment. Excessive Earth that blocks and stops needs a place where it is loosened and opened; excessive Water that floods needs a place of return; excessive Fire that exposes everything needs a place of dimming and repose; overstrong Metal and Wood need places of quiet concealment.”

“The luck-course origin may gather blessings from the birth hour, or bring blessings into effect through the birth hour. The day is the meeting of three yangs, the image of an emperor; the hour is the close attendant minister, standing near the imperial seat. Day and hour may form **Sovereign and Minister Celebrating Together** or **Heaven and Earth Joining in Virtue**. This is called **The Imperial Seat Sending Blessings to the Four Positions**.”[^67]
“When blessings gather at the imperial seat, the heart is sincere, substantial, loyal, and trustworthy. When blessings issue to the four positions, one advances through intelligence, rectitude, and uprightness. For a close attendant minister, excessive Earth, Fire, and Metal qi are especially to be avoided, since one cannot hold such office for long. Those with clear and exceptional Water and Wood are often members of the Hanlin Academy. Where year and month bring blessings, the birth hour must not destroy them; where they bring damage, the birth hour must release and resolve it. From this perspective, year, month, day, and hour are all important, yet the hour is especially important. Status, longevity, poverty or success, gain or loss depend upon distinguishing the birth hour. Its eight ke differ in initial, central, and final qi; one must examine them closely.”

Birth under the Trailing-Sash Noble Person indicates long-lasting blessings and Lu, because much qi is occupied. Birth under the Noble Person Without Continuation indicates wealth and distinction that do not last, because little qi is obtained.”[^68]

## 15. On the Conception Origin
### 論胎元

<!-- source: L248 -->
The embryo is the beginning of receiving form. Thus the *Changes* says, “Qian knows the Great Beginning,” speaking in terms of form. The month is the time when qi is completed. Thus the *Tradition* says, “Accumulated days make a month,” speaking in terms of qi. Some who discuss fate today do not attach importance to the conception month. They fail to consider that it is the root and seedling of the Four Pillars. Day and hour are indeed crucial, but if they do not violate or break the conception month, or if it rides flourishing qi and places of Lu and Horse, blessings are especially plentiful. If an auspicious day and hour are assailed by the conception month, their auspiciousness becomes useless. The conception month is therefore a chief pivot. There was good reason for Yuhu to devote particular attention to conception numbers.[^70]

Nowadays people commonly derive the Conception Origin by a formula, without examining whether it is exact. For example, for a Wuzi year and Jiayin birth month, they often simply take [Yisi] as conception, saying that Yisi is ten [months] before the birth month. They do not establish whether an intercalary month intervenes. Some select a day whose stem and branch combine with those of the birthday as the time of conception. Sometimes even when both combinations are not present, they still choose it: this has no basis.[^71]

There is one method that takes the three hundred days before birth as the qi of ten months, regarding this as the proper conception date. Thus for a Jiazi birthday, Jiazi is also taken as the conception day, since five [sixty-day circuits] total three hundred days. Examine the month in which the birth falls: any intercalation is then included. For example, someone born in a Wuzi year, Jiayin month, on an Yichou day must search for the corresponding Yichou day ten or eleven months earlier, within the half-month adjustment [the wording here is uncertain]. This is the exact three-hundred-day conception date.[^71]

The classic says, “Conception generates the original fate.” Earlier people explained this as a Zi birth obtaining Zi conception, a Chou birth obtaining Chou conception. This account is also unsatisfactory. Suppose someone born in Xinwei obtains a Renchen month and takes Guiwei as conception. Xinwei Earth is controlled by Guiwei Wood, which becomes the body's ghost. How can this be called conception generating the original fate? If the Five Phases control one another and conception also occupies a place of the Six Harms, then even with a beneficial day and hour, does this not indicate someone obliged to stand alone through personal strength?

The *Subtle Selections of the Orchid Terrace* has the configuration **The Child Returning to the Mother's Womb**. It says that conception in a Metal year followed by birth in an Earth year is auspicious, but birth in a Fire year ominous. Mutual generation among month, day, and hour is auspicious; mutual control is ominous.
It is also said: “A person's conception number being long, the lifespan must be long; being short, the lifespan must be short. Ordinarily, the intervening remainder in the conception count is taken as the limit of life. A virtuous influence adds to that remainder; a baleful influence subtracts from it. Conception that is not deep cannot endure for long: once its support fails, life readily ceases. One must also examine the nayin. If conception and hour mutually generate one another in stems, branches, and nayin, without punishment or conflict, they indicate longevity.”[^72]

Xiyin says: “A noble influence in the conception month must bring blessing and protection; punishment, clash, breaking, and harm assuredly bring hardship.” Guiguzi says:

> If Lu is present in conception,  
> Birth is in a wealthy, honored house.  
> Should it instead encounter Void,  
> Poverty brings grievance and lament.

An old poem says:

> The hour is the final ruler; conception governs life,  
> Both reaching into the last years, after fifty.  
> The imperial seat turns toward conception; living qi conjoins:  
> Look then for a lifespan rich in venerable age.

This, broadly, is its intended meaning. According to the *Classic of Gestational Divisions*, people are born after two hundred and seventy days in the womb. Physicians' reckoning of delivery after ten months includes a month concerned with the blood organs' dryness and moisture. Yet some people are born after more months and some after fewer: what can be taken as the fixed standard? Births after more months are not merely recorded in ancient and recent accounts. Of the two or three people I personally know, all came from poor households. As for fewer months, Censor Du Zheng had only seven: my colleagues and I heard him say this ourselves. Master Wu Lai, known as Yuanying, and Lord Song Lian, posthumously titled Wenxian, were also both born after seven months. How, then, can one rely upon the claim that an eminent person's conception must fall exactly three hundred days before birth?[^72]

## 16. On Establishing the Life Palace
### 論坐命宫

<!-- source: L252 -->
A spirit without a temple has nowhere to return; a person without a house has nowhere to dwell; a fate without a palace has nowhere to be governed. Hence the theory of the **Life Palace**. Without it, upon what would judgments of the yearly stars' good and ill fortune depend? In this method, examine the person's birth month and birth hour; only then determine the palace in which the fate sits.[^73]

First lay out the birth months, beginning the first month upon Zi, the second upon Hai, the third upon Xu, the fourth upon You, the fifth upon Shen, the sixth upon Wei, the seventh upon Wu, the eighth upon Si, the ninth upon Chen, the tenth upon Mao, the eleventh upon Yin, and the twelfth upon Chou, moving backward through the twelve positions. Next place the birth hour upon the position of the birth month and count forward through the twelve positions. Where the count reaches Mao, establish the Life Palace. The classic says: “Heaven's wheel turns out upon Earth's wheel; at Mao, the Life Palace is plain.”

For example, take someone born in the third month of a Jiazi year, in the Xu hour. Place the first month at Zi, the second at Hai, and the third at Xu, where one stops. Then place the Xu hour upon Xu: Hai falls upon Hai, Zi upon Zi, Chou upon Chou, Yin upon Yin, and Mao upon Mao. On reaching Mao, the palace is established; the fate therefore sits in Mao. Assign its stem according to the Jiazi year, using the same method as deriving month stems: “In Jia and Ji years, Bing takes the first place.” The palace is therefore Dingmao. Next examine the three directions together with the natal fate and the circulating stems, judging good and ill according to the stars encountered.

## 17. On the Major Luck Cycles
### 論大運

<!-- source: L256 -->
The luck course is the series of lodging houses through which human life passes. In investigating fate, first use the Three Origins, Four Pillars, and the Five Phases' Birth and Death, bringing configurations into fitting relationship, to establish the foundation. Then examine the qi of the luck course in accord with that foundation and determine a lifetime's good and ill fortune. The foundation is like Wood; the luck course like spring. Without Wood, spring has nothing through which to reveal itself; without spring, Wood does not flourish. A thin and shallow foundation resembles insignificant wormwood and weeds: the spring wind quietly stirs them, and they too can grow luxuriant—but how long can this last? A deep and strong foundation resembles the substance of pine and cypress, unchanged by the year's cold. This is what is meant by discussing the foundation first and the luck course afterward.

Why did the ancients make one branch of a major luck cycle govern ten years, converting three days into one year? A month completes its circuit from dark moon to new moon in thirty days; a day completes its circuit of daylight and darkness in twelve double-hours. For the qi of a ten-year cycle, three days contain thirty-six double-hours, representing the three hundred and sixty days of a year. A month contains three hundred and sixty double-hours; converting the interval to the seasonal term gives three thousand six hundred days, or ten years for one branch. Human life is reckoned as a circuit of one hundred and twenty years.[^74]

The conversion must use the actual days and hours elapsed between birth and the seasonal term. For a **yang male or yin female**, count forward from birth to the coming term, using its day and hour, and proceed forward. For a **yin male or yang female**, count backward from birth to the preceding term, using its day and hour, and proceed backward.[^75]

For example, a male in a yang Jiazi year is born on the twenty-fourth day of the twelfth month, in the Si hour. Beginning of Spring falls on the twenty-ninth, in the Shen hour. A yang male counts toward the coming date. From Si on the twenty-fourth to Si on the twenty-fifth is one full day. By Shen on the twenty-ninth, the interval to the term is exactly five days and three double-hours. The elapsed count is given as “sixty-three days” [that is, sixty-three double-hours]; converting those sixty-three double-hours yields six hundred and thirty days, or one year and nine months. The major luck course is reckoned from Dingchou. Only after twenty-one [months] have actually elapsed from the twelfth-month birthday does it change palace. This falls within the ninth month of the third [counted] year: for this twelfth-month Jiazi birth, it is the start of the major luck cycle at one year and nine months.[^76]

Nowadays people often use an approximate reckoning for the luck course. They speak of a start at one year and eight months, then simply call it two years, failing to understand the numbers of conversion and actual elapsed time.

It is also said: “The major luck course is the outward and inward unfolding of the Eight Characters. In selecting what to use, measure its depth; in completing the year-count, compare surplus and deficiency. Three days make one year. What exceeds this is called a remainder; what falls short is called borrowing. People know the words remainder and borrowing, but not why the remainder remains or why borrowing is required.”

Suppose a yang fate is born on the first day of the first month at Chou zheng-1, and Beginning of Spring falls on the fourth day at Chou zheng-1: this makes one complete year. If Spring begins in the Yin hour, there is one extra double-hour, a remainder of ten days. If one double-hour is lacking, ten days must be borrowed.

Consider also the subsequent reckoning of the luck course. Suppose someone is born on the first day of the first month of a Jiazi year at Zi zheng-1. Counting to the first day of the first month of Yichou at Zi zheng-1 gives what is called one year. But if six intervening months are short, advance six days: only on the seventh day at Zi zheng-1 is a full year reckoned. The intention is to count twelve complete [thirty-day] months. Yet suppose the current year has an intercalary fourth month: an extra month has been added. One must then move the change of luck back to the seventh day of the current year's twelfth month at Zi zheng-1. From there, count ten full years before changing to another cycle. If the student knows only the birth hour, not its precise ke, calculating from that hour against the time of the “six terms” will not be far wrong.[^77]

<!-- source: L258 -->
When the luck course is considered through its stem, also employ the influences in its branch. When it is considered through its branch, set aside what belongs to its stem. The major luck course emphasizes the earthly branches; hence the distinction among going [east], south, west, and north. What injures the useful influence should be controlled by the luck course; what benefits it should be generated. A weak body wants the course to lead it into flourishing territory. Officer wants generation, not injury; Killings wants restraint, not assistance; Wealth wants support, not robbery; Seal wants flourishing, not decline; Food God wants generation, not extinction by the Owl. Further examine the Four Pillars' strength and weakness: whether the influence was originally present or absent, slight or heavy.[^78]

Thus, for a Wood person using Metal as Officer, a yang male's course leaves Wei and enters Shen; a yin male's leaves Hai and enters Xu. For a Metal person using Wood as Wealth, a yang male leaves Chou and enters Yin; a yin male leaves Si and enters Chen. All approach Lu and arrive at Horse. If Officer is originally present, an Officer cycle develops office; if Wealth is originally present, a Wealth cycle develops wealth; if disaster is originally present, a disaster cycle develops disaster. Examine further the depth of qi received in the birth year and hour. If the Four Pillars receive qi deeply, results emerge even as the cycle approaches. If qi is shallow, results begin only after the transition. If middle qi is received, results emerge at the cycle's middle.[^79]
Luoluzi says: “As for qi, what is coming advances; what has completed its work withdraws.” Monk Ying says: “Meet it through Arrival at Office and Imperial Prosperity: what is coming advances. Turn from it through rest, disuse, Death, and Extinction: what has completed its work withdraws.” It is also said: “Born in a place of rest and Defeat, one is lonely and poor early in life; meeting vigorous flourishing in old age, one encounters obstruction in later years.” Monk Ying says: “The body must follow the luck course, borrowing its support; power must reach its proper time, borrowing time to complete its force.”

It is further said: “Born in flourishing years, the luck course must lie in flourishing territory; meeting declining years late in life, the course appropriately lies in a place of constraint.” Huzhongzi says: “The old and young should take care not to sit in overstrength; those in robust maturity should indeed approach flourishing.” Birth and flourishing, though called auspicious, are not necessarily auspicious; decline and extinction, though called ominous, are not necessarily ominous. Only one who understands this can discuss the luck course.[^80]

From birth to old age, a person must pass from smallness into youth and strength. At ten, one is still young and should pass only through Gestation, Nurturing, Birth, Bathing, and Cap and Belt. At thirty and forty, yang is strong and the body vigorous: flourishing places may be traversed. At fifty and sixty, the Heavenly Gui is drying up; only Decline, Defeat, Death, and Extinction are suitable. The opposite is called a lifetime with the luck course turned against it, driven to and fro through the three periods. Even if flourishing territory is reached late, it is no longer a fitting correspondence.[^80]

Another account says: “If the Five Phases in the natal fate are weak, the luck course should be strong; if they are strong, the course should be weak. Weak phases again entering a weak course are called insufficient; insufficiency brings obstruction and stagnation. Strong phases again entering a strong course are called excessive; excess brings collisions, upheaval, success, and failure. The essential thing is simply to return to the center.”

Luoluzi says: “Though the year has reached Cap and Belt, residual calamity remains; though the luck course has first entered Decline, a little blessing still clothes it.” Master Wang comments: “When year or luck course has just left Bathing's region of violent Defeat and proceeds to Cap and Belt, one must not immediately call it blessed: residual calamity from decline and defeat remains. Huzhongzi says: “About to pass through, yet not through: can there not be the calamity of prolonged obstruction? Wishing to change, yet not changed: the danger of imminent ruin remains.” This means that when the course is in Decline or Extinction and is about to enter auspicious territory, there may be renewed severe disturbance at the very point of departure. When it is in auspicious territory and about to enter Decline or Extinction, there may still be substantial blessings upon first entering. It is also said: “An auspicious course brings blessings before it arrives; an ominous course brings calamity only after it has passed.” This is the principle of smoke appearing before Fire flames and dampness remaining after Water has gone. Examine it still more closely.

Yang males and yin females receive qi in a forward course; although the entry and departure years need not be the time of response, they must still avoid the adverse meeting with Yuanchen. Another account says: “The ancients named stems and branches Jiazi, [Yichou], and so on, using the word ‘flower’ for the sixty stem–branch pairs. All these meanings are illustrated through Wood. When heavenly stems and earthly branches obtain their season, they naturally flower, bear fruit, and flourish. Monthly command is Heaven's origin; the luck course starts from the month, like a tree's seedling. By seeing its seedling one recognizes the tree's kind; from the month's useful influence one recognizes the configuration. Hence the saying, ‘Changing luck is like changing trees.’ A fate has root, seedling, blossom, and fruit: this is precisely the meaning.”[^88]

“Leaving Gui and entering Jia is like someone whose sweat turns back, generally inauspicious. The old saying runs: ‘In Cold Damage, a change of yang; in the luck course, a change to Jia. Pass through the change and remain human; fail to pass and become a ghost.’ For example, Jiaxu joining Guihai is Fire and Earth meeting Water. Chou changing to Yin, Chen to Si, Wei to Shen, and Xu to Hai turn the corners of the eastern, western, southern, and northern regions. This is called **Moving the Root and Grafting the Tree**. If a change to Jia also occurs, an ominous configuration often brings death; even a good one brings trouble. The old should especially fear it; for the young its effect is milder.”[^88]

“But Yin–Mao–Chen, Si–Wu–Wei, Shen–You–Xu, and Hai–Zi–Chou each continue one qi without interruption. These are not instances of grafting the tree. Even if the course crosses to Jia, no great disaster follows. For example, for Jia and Yi, a course through Yin and Mao is called Rob Wealth and Defeated Wealth, indicating harm to parents, injury to wealth, financial loss, and contention. Bing and Ding, Si and Wu make a Hurting Officer course, indicating harm to children, lawsuits, and imprisonment. Geng and Xin, Shen and You form the region of Seven Killings and Officer, indicating reputation and advancement; excess brings disasters, illness, and severe disease. Ren and Gui, Hai and Zi form a course of living qi and Seal-Ribbon, indicating blessings and increased property. Chen, Xu, Chou, Wei, Wu, and Ji form a Wealth course, indicating success in both fame and profit.”

“This is a rigid [formula]: judgment must follow the configuration's preferences and aversions. A strong stem suits a declining course. As the saying has it, ‘A weak stem seeks support where qi is strong; one with excess seeks the management of what is insufficient.’ Adaptation to change is essential. Combine this with examination of the annual circulating influences and the various spirits and sha, and the response is wondrously exact.”[^89]

## 18. On the Minor Luck Progression
### 論小運

<!-- source: L278 -->
The major luck cycle governs ten years of good and ill; the minor progression governs a single year's calamities and auspicious events. The minor progression receives its name because it supplements what the major course leaves incomplete. Why did the ancients begin a man's progression at Bingyin and a woman's at [Renshen]? The primordial qi's gestation begins at Zi and establishes itself at Si. Zi is the beginning of qi; Si the beginning of enclosure. Counting from Zi, the male goes leftward and at thirty is established in Si; the female goes rightward and at twenty joins Si. Si is proper yang, which yin indeed follows. The sages therefore established ritual according to this, taking Heaven three times and Earth twice: the natural numbers form their pair.[^90]

“From Si, conception proceeds: the male's ten months bring birth at Yin, the female's ten months at Shen. Shen is three yin, Yin three yang; therefore the annual progression begins there. The sun is born at Jia and the moon at Geng. Sun and moon, east and west, form the image of husband and wife. Jia governs Yin and Geng governs Shen. Yin and yang's union is thereby correctly ordered, following the principles of nature and life.” Thus the minor progression begins for a man at Bingyin and runs forward, for a woman at Renshen and runs backward: this is fixed and cannot be changed.

Commentators explain: “The male's primordial qi is yang Fire. It begins at Wuzi; thirty [years] bring Dingsi, and ten months lead to Bingyin: Wood generates Fire. The female's primordial qi is yin Water. It begins at Gengzi; twenty [years] bring Xinsi, and ten months lead to Renshen: Metal generates Water.” These are its numbers. The *White Tiger Discussions* says: “At thirty a man's sinews and bones are firm and strong, and he can become a father; at twenty a woman's flesh and skin are full and abundant, and she can become a mother. Together they make fifty, responding to the Great Expansion's number, by which the myriad things are generated. Yang is odd and expansive, hence three completions; yin even and contracted, hence two completions.” This is the principle of taking Heaven three times and Earth twice.[^90]
Some instead distinguish the ten-day rounds. For a Jiazi round, they begin as above. For a Jiashen round, men begin at Bingxu and women at Renchen; for a Jiawu round, men at Bingshen and women at Renyin; for a Jiachen round, men at Bingwu and women at Renzi; for a Jiayin round, men at Bingchen and women at Renxu. This is not the meaning of the sages' original establishment of the annual progression.[^91]

Those who discuss fate today use only the major course, overlooking the minor progression's crucial importance. Though the major course is auspicious, if the minor progression is blocked, one cannot immediately pronounce good fortune. Though the major course is ominous, if the minor progression is auspicious, one cannot immediately judge disaster. The minor progression is also called **the progressing year** and must be investigated.

Zuixingzi holds that the minor progression for men and women alike begins from the birth hour, with forward or backward direction determined by the year. For example, a male in a yang year, born in a Jiazi hour, begins Yichou immediately upon birth; in the second year he proceeds to Bingyin. Each position governs one year, and the circuit repeats. A female in a yang year proceeds backward in the same manner. I have tried this repeatedly and found it borne out. Its good and ill must also be weighed against the major course, the useful influence in the pillars, and the day ruler. During childhood, before the major course begins, use this method especially. A progression into Death or Extinction, or a palace where sha flourish, must bring danger. First examine the Eight Characters' strength, weakness, preferences, and aversions; then compare this progression with them, and scarcely a judgment will fail.[^91]

## 19. On Tai Sui, the Grand Duke of the Year
### 論太嵗

<!-- source: L282 -->
Tai Sui is the ruler of one year and leader of its influences. The term has two applications: the birth year in the Four Pillars is called **natal Tai Sui**; the year that rotates from one year to the next is called **traveling Tai Sui**. Natal Tai Sui governs the whole life, as explained earlier. The traveling Tai Sui moves through the twelve palaces, determining one year's blessings and calamities and the good and ill of its four seasons. The classic says: “Tai Sui is lord of the many sha. Its entry into a fate is not necessarily ominous, but if it encounters a region of conflict, it must bring punishment to the natal fate.” Tai Sui is like the ruler, the major luck course like the minister. If ruler and minister are harmonious, the year is auspicious; if they meet in punishment and conflict, the year is ominous.[^92]

The classic also says: “If the year injures the day stem, the harm must be light; if the day offends the year's ruler, calamity must be grave.” Distinguish these carefully. The year's ruler injuring the day is, for example, a Geng year controlling a Jia day, as Indirect Officer. It resembles a ruler governing his minister or a father governing his son. Though trouble and obscurity arise, they do not become great harm. Why? Those above governing those below accords with the order, and the bond of feeling is not entirely severed.

The day offending the year's ruler is, for example, a Jia day controlling a Wu year, as Indirect Wealth. It resembles a minister murdering his ruler or a son murdering his father, deeply unfavorable. Why? Those below assailing those above reverses the order, and its misfortune cannot be escaped. But the Five Phases may supply rescue, and the Four Pillars may possess a sympathetic relation. Suppose a Jia day controls a Wu year, but Geng or Shen Metal was originally present in the Four Pillars, or the major course contains what restrains Jia Wood, keeping it from controlling Wu Earth. This is called rescue. The classic says: “Wu and Ji fear meeting Jia and Yi; Geng and Xin are needed among the stems.”[^93]

If the major course or Four Pillars contain a Gui that combines with Wu, this is called sympathetic relation. The classic says: “Ren gives its younger sister Gui to Wu; an ill omen becomes a good one.” If both rescue and combination are present, the year's ill fortune turns auspicious. With one, the harm is halved; with neither, it cannot be resolved. The classic says: “If the Five Phases supply rescue, that year instead becomes Wealth; if the Four Pillars lack sympathetic relation, the judgment is called Controlling the Year.” This is its meaning.

There are also the accounts of **True Tai Sui** and **Campaigning against Tai Sui**. The classic says: “The birth time encounters True Tai Sui.” For example, a Jiazi-born person meeting another Jiazi year encounters True Tai Sui, also called the **Turning-Heel Sha**. The major luck course and day ruler must accord harmoniously with Tai Sui, and the year is then auspicious. If punishment, clash, breaking, or harm bring mutual struggle and control, it is ominous.[^94]

A Guisi day meeting a Dinghai circulating year, with the day's stem and branch clashing with and controlling Tai Sui, is called campaigning. The luck course's stem and branch injuring or clashing with Tai Sui is also called campaigning. Tai Sui's stem and branch clashing with the day's stem and branch is likewise called campaigning. That year is ominous and disaster cannot be entirely avoided.

If a Jiazi circulating year coincides with a Jiazi luck cycle, this is called **Year and Luck Arriving Together**. Only when these are Yang Blade or Seven Killings is the configuration ominous; Wealth, Officer, and Seal-Ribbon can instead be auspicious. The classic's statement, “Year and luck arrive together: calamity comes at once,” refers specifically to Yang Blade.[^95]

A Jiazi day meeting Jiazi Tai Sui is called **Day and Year Joining Together**. For a gentleman it is **Sovereign and Minister Celebrating Together**: the year favors memorials and responses to the throne, with the joy of an imperial audience. For a scholar awaiting examination or selection, it indicates recommendation and entry into office. It must also accord with the year's ruler and imperial seat before it becomes exceptional. For an ordinary person of low station, however, it is most unfavorable. If the birth hour is harmonious, the disaster is somewhat lighter. Thus the classic says:

> Tai Sui stands above the head:  
> The other spirits dare not withstand it.  
> If official troubles do not disturb you,  
> Heavy mourning must be seen.

Is this not its meaning? If Hook and [Strangling], Yuanchen and Loss, Salty Pool and the solitary harms, dwelling and tomb, Sickness and Death, Official Talisman, Mourning and Condolence, White Tiger, Yang Blade, Sudden Defeat, and Heavenly Distress also arrive together, troubles arise a hundredfold; in severe cases death follows.[^96]
For example, suppose a Jia day meets a Wu year as Tai Sui, and Jia is born amid Yin, Mao, Hai, or Wei in year, month, day, or hour, with repeated Jia and Yi combining to control the Wu year. If the pillars lack Geng or Xin, or a Si–You–Chou Metal configuration to control Wood, and lack Bing or Ding Fire configurations to burn Wood, this is greatly ominous.

One chart was **Wuchen / Wuwu / Wuxu / Jiayin 戊辰／戊午／戊戌／甲寅**: **The Yang Blade Turns Its Spear**. In the fourth month of a Renshen year, a malignant sore arose on the back of the neck; the head seemed about to fall, and the person died. Another chart was **[Yichou] / Yihai / Renshen / Yisi 乙丑／乙亥／壬申／乙巳**, traveling through the Xinwei major cycle in a Bingyin year. The day's Ren controlled the year's Bing; Geng within the day branch Shen controlled Jia within the year branch Yin. Yin also punished Si, Si punished Shen, and Shen punished Yin. The Xinwei course joined Tai Sui's Wood configuration, Hurting Officer: none of these was auspicious. In that year's Jiawu month, Fire flourished in conflict and control, and the person died an unnatural death.[^97]

In general, the day offending the year's ruler is more severe with the five yang stems and lighter with the five yin stems. If the day stem is Heavenly or Monthly Virtue and Tai Sui is the useful influence, there is no harm; instead there is gain. If a heavenly clash and earthly collision were already present in the pillars and recur in the circulating year, there is likewise no great harm. If Tai Sui controls the birth hour, or the hour controls Tai Sui, there is also disaster, but it is judged through the children's position.[^98]

## 20. A General Discussion of Year and Luck Course
### 總論嵗運

<!-- source: L286 -->
Tai Sui is the year's Son of Heaven, supreme among its spirits and sha. It governs and orders directions, circulates the six qi, and turns the four seasons to accomplish the year's work. None stands above it. If a person's chart encounters control, clash, pressure, or subjection from it, these are all signs of misfortune. The luck course harmonizes the twenty-four qi, carries a lifetime's good and ill, supports the Four Pillars, and assists the Three Origins. The luck course and circulating year are outward and inward aspects of one another; upon them depend the blessings, disasters, life, and death of the natal fate. For the year, employ the heavenly origin; for the luck course, employ the earthly branch.[^99]

If the course is favorable but the day stem injures the circulating year's heavenly origin, the harm is light. If the course is unfavorable, or is leaving a Wealth or Officer cycle, and the day stem injures the year stem, the harm is grave. For a fate whose success has already manifested, trouble arrives immediately. Yet one must not call every unfavorable course decline and extinction at once. The essential thing is to know whether success has already appeared, and whether its allotted qi and number have passed.

The luck course takes the birth month as its origin and especially fears clash and control between the course and Tai Sui. If year and course clash with the month, disaster must result. If they stand opposite the day, this is called **Fanyin**, the returning echo; if they lie upon the day, **Fuyin**, the repeated echo. Both are unfavorable to close kin and may bring untoward loss of wealth; neither is a good sign.[^100]

In judging year and luck, if the natal heavenly origin or branch originally lacks an Officer star, but the course's heavenly origin supplies Direct Officer, or if Indirect Officer was originally present but restrained too heavily and an Officer star is met in the course's heavenly origin, blessings can arise. A course whose branch is not Wealth can still develop wealth if its stem is Wealth; a course whose branch is not Killings can still bring disaster if its stem is Killings.

<!-- source: L288 -->
Another account says: “**Obscuring Qi** is the image of lost brightness, the way of darkness and confusion. Jia–Ji and Yi–Geng are examples: combination obscures. The day stem and hour stem should not combine with Tai Sui's heavenly origin; such combination is called Obscuring Qi. One must distinguish the day stem combining with Tai Sui, as a Jia day with a Ji year, from Tai Sui combining with the day stem, as a Ji day with a Jia year. Jia combining with Ji brings heavier calamity; Ji combining with Jia, lighter. Nearness of the year's position makes the harm heavier; distance makes it lighter. If the year lies five positions ahead of the day and combines, this is called **Tai Sui Entering the Dwelling**. Obscuring Qi arriving at the door indicates adversity.”[^101]

The *Shenbai Classic* says: “In discussing Obscuring Qi, the day is [light], the hour heavy. Examine the Human Origin as well: if flourishing, it indicates trouble among household and family; Death and Extinction combined with clash indicate trouble to one's own person.” If the branches unite through the Six Combinations, this is called **Mandarin-Duck Combination**. When useful, it indicates approaching good events. If both stem and branch combine, it indicates additions to the household. With an auspicious influence sharing the position, a scholar benefits from seeing officials, submitting recommendations, and favorable documents. Mutual hostility instead brings the pain of separation. If punishment is present and the position is also in rest and [confinement], personal disaster follows.[^102]

“At a position of the Six Harms, young members of the household may become ill, or slaves and servants run away. In the dwelling and tomb positions of day and hour, the household is unsettled, with disturbance through women. If someone is pregnant, there will be unrest after childbirth. It favors the birth of a daughter, not a son; if a son is born, either mother or child is lost. The year's ruler combining with the major luck course is judged in the same way.”[^102]

<!-- source: L290 -->
Another account says: “The major luck course should not control or clash with Tai Sui. It is especially objectionable for the course to control the year, equivalent to the day's offense: it indicates loss, depletion, and mourning. Noble Person, Lu, and Horse can resolve it and make it somewhat auspicious; if the Eight Characters contain rescue, there is no cause for fear.” The classic says: “The year clashing with or controlling the course is auspicious; the course clashing with or controlling the year is ominous. With an inauspicious configuration, death follows. Mutual generation between year and course is auspicious, as are the combination and exchange of Lu, Horse, and Noble Person. Examine closely and infer in detail: nothing will fail to be borne out.”[^103]
## 21. On the Advancing, Crossing, Retreating, and Hidden Spirits
### 論進交退伏

<!-- source: L294 -->
Yan Dongsou says: “Using the ten stems, establish four periods of fifteen days each. Twelve days belong to the Advancing Spirit; the three remaining days belong to the Crossing, Retreating, and Hidden Spirits.” Thus Jiazi is the first Advancing Spirit, and Bingzi, Dingchou, and Wuyin are its Crossing, Retreating, and Hidden Spirits. Jimao is the second Advancing Spirit, followed by Xinmao, Renchen, and Guisi in those three roles. Jiawu is the third, followed by Bingwu, Dingwei, and Wushen. Jiyou is the fourth, followed by Xinyou, Renxu, and Guihai.[^104]

“Meeting the Advancing Spirit brings swift advancement and success. Meeting the Crossing Spirit makes affairs discordant. Meeting the Retreating Spirit brings demotion and loss of official rank. Meeting the Hidden Spirit delays and obstructs what one undertakes.” The classic says: “The Advancing Spirit's four seats are also exceptional; noble and baleful influences assisting them become a force of blessing.”

Huzhongzi says: “Moving forward with Tai Sui is called the Advancing Spirit; moving backward against Tai Sui is called the Retreating Spirit. Meeting advancement gives sharp and accomplished writing; meeting retreat makes understanding dark and confused.” The *Guangxin Collection* instead takes Jia–Yi–Bing–Ding and Zi–Chou–Yin–Mao as advancement, Ding–Bing–Yi–Jia and Mao–Yin–Chou–Zi as retreat. If stems and branches both retreat, something unsatisfactory follows amid satisfaction. Thus Gengxu obtaining Jiayin advances; obtaining Yisi retreats. What lies outside the same ten-day round acts more slowly. But this supplies only advancement and retreat, without Crossing and Hidden Spirits. I fear it is not correct.[^105]

## 22. On the Combinations of the Ten Stems
### 論十干合

<!-- source: L298 -->
Combination means harmony. When yang encounters yang, the two yangs contend and bring control; when yin encounters yin, the two yins are insufficient and bring control. Only yin meeting yang, or yang meeting yin, makes combination, like man and woman joining to establish the way of husband and wife. The *Changes* says: “One yin and one yang are called the Way; one-sided yin or one-sided yang is called disorder.” This is the meaning.[^106]

<!-- source: L300-L308 -->
The eastern Jia and Yi Wood fear control by the western Geng and Xin Metal. Jia is yang and the elder brother; Yi is yin and the younger sister. Elder brother Jia therefore marries younger sister Yi into the Metal family as Geng's wife. Thus Yi combines with Geng. Though Yi becomes Geng's wife, when spring comes Wood flourishes and Metal is confined. No longer fearing Metal's control, Yi returns to her original home and joins Jia. Yet she conceived in the Metal house and gives birth in the Wood house. Wood is blue-green and Metal white: hence, in spring, garden trees bear green leaves and open white flowers.

The southern Bing and Ding Fire fear control by the northern Ren and Gui Water. Bing is yang and the elder brother; Ding is yin and the younger sister. Bing therefore marries Ding into the Water family as Ren's wife. Thus Ding combines with Ren. In summer Fire flourishes and Water is confined. No longer fearing Water's control, Ding returns to the Fire house and joins Bing. Yet she conceived in the Water house and gives birth in the Fire house. Water is black and Fire red: after Lesser Fullness, mulberries ripen and there must be red among them.[^107]

The central Wu and Ji Earth fear control by the eastern Jia and Yi Wood. Wu is yang and the elder brother; Ji is yin and the younger sister. Wu therefore marries Ji into the Wood family as Jia's wife. Thus Jia combines with Ji. In the sixth month [Earth] flourishes and Wood is confined. No longer fearing Wood's control, Ji returns to the Earth house and joins Wu. Yet she conceived in Jia's house and gives birth in Wu's. Earth is yellow and Wood blue-green: hence the sweet melons of the sixth month, though ripe, have yellow flesh and green skin.[^107]

The western Geng and Xin Metal fear control by the southern Bing and Ding Fire. Geng is yang and the elder brother; Xin is yin and the younger sister. Geng therefore marries Xin into the Fire family as Bing's wife. Thus Bing combines with Xin. In autumn Metal flourishes and Fire is confined. No longer fearing Fire's control, Xin returns to the Metal house and joins Geng. Yet she conceived in the Fire house and gives birth in the Metal house. Fire is red and Metal white: autumn's ripe jujubes are half red and half white; maple leaves are crimson.

The northern Ren and Gui Water fear control by the central Wu and Ji Earth. Ren is yang and the elder brother; Gui is yin and the younger sister. Ren therefore marries Gui into the Earth family as Wu's wife. Thus Wu combines with Gui. In winter Water flourishes and Earth is confined. No longer fearing Earth's control, Gui returns to the Water house and joins Ren. Yet she conceived in Wu's house and gives birth in Ren's. Water is black and Earth yellow: in deep winter's frost and snow, plants die and their yellowness emerges.

<!-- source: L310-L312 -->
Why is **Jia–Ji** called the **Combination of Centrality and Uprightness**? Jia is yang Wood, humane in nature and standing first among the ten stems. Ji is yin Earth, tranquil, sincere, and substantial, possessing the virtue of generating things. Hence this name. The combination indicates someone respected and weighty, generous, even, and upright. If sha accompany it and the Five Phases lack qi, however, the person is often resentful and angry, obstinate and unwilling to yield.

Why is **Yi–Geng** called the **Combination of Humanity and Righteousness**? Yi is yin Wood, humane but overly soft; Geng is yang Metal, firm and unbending. Hardness and softness therefore assist each other, drawing upon both humanity and righteousness. It indicates someone decisive and constant, not deceived by soft flattery, humane in dealings and righteous in advance and retreat. With the Five Phases in Birth and flourishing, the bones are refined and the form clear. With Death, Extinction, and sha, the person is headstrong and aggressive, unimpressive in appearance, approving himself and condemning others. Jia–Ji and Yi–Geng combinations are not objectionable for women.[^108]
<!-- source: L314-L318 -->
Why is **Bing–Xin** called the **Combination of Authority and Restraint**? Bing is yang Fire, brilliant and flourishing in itself; Xin is yin Metal, cutting with its edge and delighting in killing. Hence this name. It indicates an imposing and stern appearance that inspires fear, harshness and cruelty, a liking for bribes, and fondness for illicit pleasure. If sha accompany it or the Five Phases are in Death and Extinction, the person is deficient in kindness and righteousness, without feeling. A woman who has it together with Heavenly Void, Great Consumption, and Salty Pool is beautiful in appearance and low in voice; with a triple combination she is alluring and lustful.[^109]

Why is **Ding–Ren** called the **Combination of Lust and Deceit**? Ren is pure yin Water, unilluminated by the three lights; Ding is Fire concealing yin, obscure and unilluminated in itself. Hence this name. It indicates bright eyes and a captivating spirit, abundant feeling and easy susceptibility, no pursuit of lofty purity, low habits and little aspiration, absorption in pleasure and attachment to beauty—miserly with one's own possessions and greedy for another's. If the Five Phases are in Death or Extinction, or sha accompany Salty Pool, Great Consumption, Heavenly Void, and self-Defeat, there is the disgrace of sexual misconduct staining the household. Such people draw close to the petty, insult the gentleman, act greedily and recklessly, and stop only when they have prevailed. Women are described as lustful, treacherous, easily provoked and seduced, frequently drawing shame upon themselves. They may marry a younger husband when old, or an old husband when young; pass from low status into respectable standing, or from respectable standing into degradation.[^109]

Why is **Wu–Gui** called the **Combination Without Affection**? Wu is yang Earth, an old and ugly husband; Gui is yin Water, a graceful and swaying woman. Old yang and young yin combine, but without affection. It indicates either beauty or ugliness. If Wu obtains Gui, there is charm and spirit, beauty fittingly expressed: a man marries a young woman, a woman a handsome husband. If Gui obtains Wu, the appearance is plain and antiquated, aged and commonplace: a man marries an old wife, a woman an old husband. The classic takes Wu obtaining Gui in combination as “youth and age without affection.”

<!-- source: L320 -->
Someone asks: “Why do the ten stems always combine across six counted positions?” I reply: “The numbers of Heaven and Earth each number no more than five. The upper five positions are generating numbers, the lower five completing numbers. Only when a generating and completing number meet is combination formed. Heaven's one generates Ren; Earth's two generates Ding; Heaven's three generates Jia; Earth's four generates Xin; Heaven's five generates Wu. Earth's six completes Gui; Heaven's seven completes Bing; Earth's eight completes Yi; Heaven's nine completes Geng; Earth's ten completes Ji. Heaven's first number meets Earth's second number and then combines: hence the interval of six.” The *Changes* says: “Heaven has five numbers and Earth five numbers. The five positions obtain one another, and each has its combination.” This is the meaning.[^110]

The *Essential Discourse on the Five Phases* says: “Heaven's one generates Water, which in things is essence: essence is generated by one. Earth's two generates Fire, which in things is spirit: spirit is generated by two. Heaven's three generates Wood, which in things is the ethereal soul: the ethereal soul follows spirit. Earth's four generates Metal, which in things is the corporeal soul: the corporeal soul follows essence. Heaven's five generates Earth, which in things is the body: the body exists after essence, spirit, ethereal soul, and corporeal soul are complete. From Heaven's one through Heaven's five are the generating numbers of the Five Phases; from Earth's six through Earth's ten are their completing numbers.”

“What begins odd becomes even in completion; what begins even becomes odd. Each is completed by five. Five is the center of Heaven's numbers, by which things are completed. The Way is established at one, completed at three, and changes at five; then the numbers of Heaven and Earth are complete. Ten merely doubles them.” The classic says: “One and six share a lineage; two and seven share a Way; three and eight are companions; four and nine are friends; five and ten travel together: opening and closing, odd and even.”

<!-- source: L322 -->
In combination, what matters is attaining the center without one-sidedness. One Jia obtaining one Ji, each riding Birth and flourishing, attains the center without bias. If Jia is excessively strong and Ji excessively soft, they do not match: this is excess and deficiency. One Ji combining with two Jia, or two Jia combining with one Ji, is called yin and yang's one-sided withering. Like too many wives and too few husbands, or too many husbands and too few wives, it brings contention and jealousy—the way of disorder. Hence, “One-sidedness brings disorder.” Huzhongzi says: “One qi withered on one side: in old age, merely a commonplace creature.”[^111]

The *Book of Transformations of the Heavenly Origin* says: “When heavenly stems combine, yang obtaining yin brings blessings slowly; yin obtaining yang brings blessings swiftly.” Thus Jia obtaining Ji in combination is Wealth, while Ji obtaining Jia is Officer. A yang stem meeting its yin partner receives only the blessing of stem combination. A yin stem meeting its yang partner also receives the assisting combination of Direct Officer—a [double] blessing. This is why their speed differs. Apply the same rule to the other stems.[^111]

It is also said: “If stem combination is joined by branch combination within the same ten-day round, as Jiaxu meeting Jimao or Jiachen meeting Jiyou, it is called **Sovereign and Minister Celebrating Together**. If it occurs across two rounds, as Jiazi meeting Jichou or Jiawu meeting Jiwei, it is called **Husband and Wife Gathering Together**. In worldly affairs there is the ruler of one's own country, but not a minister from a different country: therefore only a meeting within one round receives the former name. One must also distinguish yang as ruler and yin as minister. The ruler's position belongs above and the minister's below; the reverse violates the order.”[^112]

“In worldly affairs, a husband may belong to one's own district while a wife comes from another. Hence mutual meeting across rounds is called Husband and Wife Gathering Together, also **Heaven and Earth Obtaining Combination**. Even then, harmonious qi and noble influences must assist before the combination is useful. Internal clash, breaking, or injury, and punishment or sha within the combination, are all inauspicious.” The *Imperially Composed Conversations* says: “Lu within combination assuredly makes dukes and marquises; mutual injury at the place of combination leaves it without benefit.” This is the meaning.

## 23. On the Ten Stems Transforming Qi
### 論十干化氣

<!-- source: L326-L336 -->
Fuyangzi says: “The ten stems combining and transforming express yin and yang's pairing, the way of husband and wife. Upon reaching six, they combine; upon deriving three, they transform. Starting with the five Zi pairs, count onward to Si to obtain combination. Once combined, derive the Tiger and govern the Dragon. The Dragon presides over yang virtue, governing Heaven and accomplishing transformation. Zi is Kan's position: Heaven's one generates Water, the image of essences joining. The embryo is conceived within yang. Thus the male proceeds leftward from Zi, reaching Si at thirty: this is yang, and therefore he marries at thirty. The female proceeds rightward from Zi, reaching Si at twenty: this is yin, and therefore she marries at twenty. ## 25. On the Six Combinations of the Branch Origin

Combination is harmony. Yin and yang harmonize, and their qi combine of themselves. Although creative transformation joins yin and yang, within qi and number the position of yang qi is honored. Zi is one yang and Chou two yang: one and two make three. Yin is three yang and Hai six yin: [three] and six make nine. Mao is four yang and Xu five yin: four and five make nine. Chen is five yang and You four yin: five and four make nine. Si is six yang and Shen [three] yin: six and three make nine. Wu is one yin and Wei is given as “two yang”: one and two make three. ## 26. On the Triple Combinations of the Branch Origin
### 論支元三合

<!-- source: L468 -->
Examining the calendrical specialists, we find that the first qi of Shen, Zi, and Chen all begin at the water-clock's first ke; those of Si, You, and Chou at the twenty-sixth; those of Yin, Wu, and [Xu] at the fifty-first; those of Hai, Mao, and Wei at the seventy-sixth. Their qi begin at the same ke. This is Heaven and Earth's natural principle; therefore they are called **triple combinations**.[^128]

Another explanation compares the triple combination to the functioning of a human body. Essence is the origin of [qi], and qi the root of spirit. Essence is therefore qi's mother and spirit its child. Mother and child generate one another, and essence, qi, and spirit, complete and undispersed, form a combination. Since the branches belong to the Human Origin, they are discussed in this way. Thus in Shen–Zi–Chen, Shen is Zi's mother and Chen Zi's child; Shen is Water's Birth, Zi its flourishing, and Chen its [Storehouse]. Birth means bringing forth, flourishing means completion, and Storehouse means gathering. With birth, completion, and gathering, all things obtain their beginning and end: this is natural principle. Shen–Zi–[Chen] therefore forms a Water configuration. If one of the three characters is missing, transformation does not complete the configuration, and one cannot judge it as a triple combination transforming a configuration.[^128]

In the principle of Heaven and Earth, two transform—this means one yin and one yang. Three transform—this means three generating the myriad things. Si–You–Chou, Yin–Wu–Xu, and Hai–Mao–Wei are all the same. Earth is not separately named among the Five Phases here because the other four all depend upon Earth to complete their configurations, and all things return to concealment in Earth. If Chen, Xu, Chou, and Wei are all present, they form an Earth configuration in their own right.

Whenever a fate has combination, obtaining a configuration is best. For Bing and Ding people, Hai–Mao–Wei as Seal and Si–You–Chou as Wealth obtain configurations. Yin–Wu–Xu Fire is their own configuration; Shen–Zi–Chen Water their Officer configuration; Chen–Xu–Chou–Wei Earth their Hurting configuration. A Bing person meeting Si–You–Chou, or a Ding person meeting Yin–Wu–Xu, has the **Three-Position Lu Configuration**: Bing takes Si as Lu, Ding takes Wu as Lu; You and Chou combine with Si, Yin and Xu with Wu. Luoluzi says, “Lu has three meetings,” and also, “Obtain one, divide into three: former worthies did not record it.” Huzhongzi says, “Obtain one, divide into three, and pluck the immortal cassia in the moon.” This is the meaning. Apply it to the other cases by analogy.[^129]

When the Six Combinations or triple combinations enter a fate, they indicate handsome form and bearing, settled spirit and qi, a love of life and aversion to death, an even and upright heart, ease in dealings, and penetrating intelligence. If the combination involves mutual generation, undertakings often succeed. Auspicious influences coming and going deepen the blessings: a life of ease, many arts and talents, gentle speech and pleasant appearance, little wrangling over right and wrong, support through good and bad fortune, and affection from others.

If the combination involves mutual control, the person is difficult to serve but easy to please, often set upon a plan; action often invites loss. With additional ominous sha, entanglement in unforeseen affairs and violent alarms follows, though not deep calamity. Combining in Death and Extinction indicates undertakings that never satisfy, little weight or authority, a common and crude spirit that draws contempt, low aspirations and narrow capacity, liking for the petty and dislike of the gentleman, base habits and self-degradation, and little fulfillment throughout life.[^130]

Combination with Established Lu brings windfalls and unexpected blessings of reputation. Combination with Direct Seal and Noble Person brings imperial favor and the help of eminent people. Combination with Food God brings abundant clothing, emolument, and plentiful food. Combination with Yuanchen and Great Consumption indicates discourtesy, pure words but impure deeds, intimacy with the lowly and contempt for the gentleman. Joined with Salty Pool, it brings illicit intercourse and acts of greed and corruption. Joined with Official Talisman, it frequently brings punishments, imprisonment, lawsuits, collateral entanglements, and hidden disputes. Joined with Heavenly Void, actions lack real substance and lowly people deceive one.

Women particularly avoid sha within combination. Salty Pool stains conduct and brings a reputation for sexual misconduct; Great Consumption leads to illicit flight. The *Jade Well* says: “Combination is not confined to triple combinations and the Six Combinations. ## 27. On the General Star and Canopy

The **General Star** resembles a general encamped in the central army; hence the middle position of a triple combination is called the General Star. **Canopy** is likened to a jeweled canopy. The General Star always wants auspicious stars to assist it. Only when noble influences descend upon it does it bring favorable results. The *Liyu Song* says:

This means that auspicious assistance makes it noble. With Canopy and Direct Seal but without the flanked Storehouse, it is a configuration of the two central councils. Canopy and Seal accompanied by a properly established Traveling Horse are called the **Seal of Command Insignia**, indicating the distinction of standards and insignia. If the year stem's Storehouse is also the same Storehouse, this is a double blessing and indicates great distinction.[^133]

It is also said: “Those whose fates obtain Canopy are often alone or widowed. Even distinction does not remove solitude. Judge it in terms of Buddhist and Daoist religious life, arts, and specialized skills.” Huzhongzi says: “Canopy is the star of arts and techniques.” The *Liyu Song* says:[^134]

> Canopy, though auspicious, also brings impediment:  
> An offspring born outside the principal line, or a lonely widow;  
> A replacement wife, a husband entering his wife's household—often a missing mouth;  
> Furnace and tongs, a broad hat, robes of black or yellow.

It also says:

> Beneath Canopy's star, brothers are few:  
> A lofty, solitary mansion in Heaven.  
> If at birth it lies in hour or conception,  
> One is adopted into another branch, or born to a secondary wife.

Lin Kai says:

> Seal and Tomb together with Canopy make a clear and noble character;  
> Repeated descent upon Seal makes dukes and ministers.  
> But if Void and breaking come into that place,  
> One is a secluded, unoccupied practitioner of arts.

He also says:

> Repeated Canopies are welcome:  
> Let no breaking or clash arrive.  
> Though one's nature is intelligent,  
> One travels east and west by one's art.  
> If they occupy flourishing and support,  
> One assuredly becomes a Three-Dukes minister.  
> The gentleman meeting them should gain blessing;  
> The petty person at Birth fears Hanging Needle.

Another account says: “Canopy seated in the hour indicates interruption and decline throughout life. Ren and Gui people especially avoid it: it indicates losing children in old age. Offending the day, it harms the wife; in a woman's hour, it indicates a lifetime without childbirth.” The *Three Fates* says, “Canopy fears Metal and Wood.” Yet Huzhongzi has the configuration **Repeated Metal, Repeated Canopy**, such as Gengchen meeting Gengchen or Xinchou meeting Xinchou. When two Metals and repeated Canopies meet, with Lu, Horse, and refined qi assisting, one should receive enfeoffment and rank.[^135]

The *Classic Illuminating the Spirits* says: “Canopy is a clear spirit of shelter and protection. It indicates an open and penetrating disposition, a clear spirit, an intelligent nature, quietness and few desires. Material wealth is unfavorable throughout life. Only when joined with Flanking the Noble Person does it become a blessing of pure distinction and exceptional attainment.”

## 28. On Salty Pool
### 論咸池

<!-- source: L476-L480 -->
〈Note: This sha requires the heavenly-stem and nayin relation to be of the same kind as the earthly position. Merely saying that Yin–Wu–Xu places it at Mao is not sufficient if the heavenly-stem and nayin affiliation is not Fire. This sha is likewise taken from the triple combinations and is therefore appended here.〉[^136]

The *Huainanzi* says: “The sun rises at Fusang and enters Salty Pool.” Thus the Five Phases' Bathing positions are named **Salty Pool**, taking the meaning of sunset, when the myriad things become obscure. For Yin–Wu–Xu it is Mao; for Si–You–Chou, Wu; for Shen–Zi–Chen, You; for Hai–Mao–Wei, Zi. These are the Bathing palaces, the second positions from Birth. It is also called **Defeat Spirit**, and also **Peach Blossom Sha**.[^137]
This influence governs treachery, sexual misconduct, and baseness. In Birth and flourishing, it gives a beautiful appearance, indulgence in drink and pleasure, lavish spending and fondness for enjoyment, dissipation of family property, and absorption in lust. In Death and Extinction, it gives destitution and lack of restraint, deceitful speech and conduct, roaming and gambling, ingratitude and broken faith, illicit intercourse and promiscuity—nothing is left undone. When joined with Yuanchen and occupying Birth and flourishing, it often brings an unworthy wife. When joined with Noble Person and Established Lu, it often provides a livelihood through trade in oil, salt, wine, and goods, or establishes a household through a woman's concealed wealth.[^138]

Throughout life it brings danger from Water, wasting illness, repeated losses, and hidden misfortune. When this [influence] enters a fate, there is breaking without completion; it is no auspicious sign. Women especially avoid it. [Shen Zhi] says, “Salty Pool is avoided in day and hour,” and also, “Salty Pool fears Water.” Huzhongzi says, “Bathing coming to the year: Boyi had his hunger on Mount Shouyang.” He also says, “Meeting Bathing at birth is called **Naked Form**; meeting Bathing in the progressing year is called **Impoverishing Sha**.” Again: “Among the Five Phases, Bathing is especially to be avoided.”[^138]

The *Purple Void Scheme* says:

> Amorous, alluring excess is called Salty Pool;  
> When its influences gather, calamity answers in its time.  
> Drink and desire bring punishment in two or three positions;  
> Add further spirits and sha, and bloodshed follows.

Examination of these books shows that Salty Pool is not an auspicious influence. Encountered in day or hour, or by a Water fate, it is especially ominous.

## 29. On the Six Harms
### 論六害

<!-- source: L484-L492 -->
The qi of daylight and darkness, yin and yang, respond to one another and form the Six Combinations. From the Six Combinations arise the Six Harms; because of the Six Harms, the yin–yang qi of day and night also become objects of avoidance. The **Six Harms** are positions among the twelve branches where encroachment and conflict occur.[^139]

**Zi–Wei harm.** Wei is flourishing Earth, [harming] Zi's flourishing Water. This is called **Mutual Harm Between Powerful Houses**. Thus Zi meeting Wei constitutes harm.

**Chou–Wu harm.** Wu uses flourishing Fire to encroach upon Chou's dead Metal. This is called **Mutual Harm of Officer and Ghost**. Thus Chou meeting Wu constitutes harm; if Wu additionally carries the true ghost of Chou's stem, the harm is especially severe.

**Yin–Si harm.** Each relies upon Arrival at Office, monopolizes ability, and presses forward, thereby harming the other. If the stems meeting between them contain a ghost, the harm is especially severe. Moreover, punishment is involved: one must therefore increase or decrease the judgment of calamity and blessing accordingly.

**Mao–Chen harm.** Mao uses flourishing Wood to encroach upon Chen's dead Earth. This is the younger harming the elder. Chen meeting Mao constitutes harm; if Mao additionally carries the true ghost of Chen's stem, the harm is especially severe.

**Shen–Hai harm.** Each relies upon Arrival at Office, envies the other's ability, and competes for advancement, thereby harming the other. Shen meeting Hai and Hai meeting Shen are equally harmful. Mutual control in the nayin makes it heavier.

**You–Xu harm.** Xu uses dead Fire to harm You's flourishing Metal. This is mutual harm through jealousy. A You person meeting Xu suffers misfortune; a Xu person meeting You has no calamity. If an Yiyou person obtains Wuxu, Yi is true Metal and Wu true Fire, so the harm is especially severe.[^140]
<!-- source: L494-L498 -->
It is also said: “‘Six’ means the six relations; ‘harm’ means injury. Encountering it indicates damage among the six relations, hence the name Six Harms. Zi and Wei pierce straight through the heart. Through clash and combination, enmity is born before affection can be established: this is harm. A Zi-born person fears Wu's clash, but Wei goes and combines with Wu. Chou fears Wei's clash, but Wu combines with Wei. Yin fears Shen's clash, but Si combines with Shen. Mao fears You's clash, but Chen combines with You. Shen fears Yin's clash, but Hai combines with Yin. You fears Mao's clash, but Xu combines with Mao. This is why all are harms. A person carrying these, and additionally meeting Yang Blade, Robbery Sha, or Official Talisman, suffers especially grave calamity.”[^141]

Another account says: “Yin–Si and Hai–Shen in Birth and flourishing indicate a clean spirit and handsome appearance, fondness for contention and competition, and eagerness for forceful action. In Death and Extinction, there are many schemes but few achievements, strained imitation of others' work, and relentless pressing forward. Within a noble configuration there is integrity and skill in strategy; within a low configuration, deceit, greed, baseness, and miserliness.” It is also said: “Repeated Shen–Hai: guard against injury to the Five Peaks. Yin–Si at the two barriers: the four limbs must fear disablement.”[^142]

“Mao–Chen and Wu–Chou in Birth and flourishing indicate competitiveness, anger, stern resolve, and lack of forbearance. In Death and Extinction they indicate poisoning, grievous injury, and overthrow. In a noble configuration they bring great authority over punishment and prisons; in a low configuration, livelihood obtained through unrighteous means. Zi–Wei, whether in Birth and flourishing or Death and Extinction, is unfavorable to close relations. In a noble configuration it brings burdens through wives and concubines; in a low one, loneliness without support.”

“Xu–You in Birth and flourishing indicates intolerance and much obstinacy. In Death and Extinction it indicates cruelty, hatred of goodness, and jealousy of ability. In a noble configuration, it entraps the innocent and conspires to draw them into lawsuits, with much treachery and flattery. In a low configuration, it indicates concealed cunning, harm to others, and an ingratiating but unsound nature.”

In general, the Six Harms entering a fate indicate injury and solitude, close kin separated like Shen and Shang, and scant wealth. Women especially avoid them. Also establish the Life Palace and examine into which palace division they fall, judging each in detail. Luoluzi says: “Those of the Six Harms have the affairs of seven injuries in their fate.” The *Instructions of the Golden Book* says:[^142]

> With Six Harms, day and hour are feared:  
> Disabled in old age, where can suffering find support?  
> If Yang Blade also comes, the influences devour one another:  
> If no keen blade strikes, the tiger still assails.

Thus a fate meeting the Six Harms especially avoids mutual injury between stems and branches. Day and hour matter most; the Body and Life Palaces come next. Even a noble configuration does not erase it: distinction remains distinction, harm remains harm; neither conceals the other.

## 30. On the Three Punishments
### 論三刑

<!-- source: L502 -->
The *Scripture of the Hidden Talisman* says: “Kindness arises from harm, and harm arises from kindness.” The Three Punishments arise from the [triple] combinations, just as the Six Harms arise from the Six Combinations. Place Shen–Zi–Chen above the three positions Yin–Mao–Chen: Shen punishes Yin, Zi punishes Mao, and Chen meeting Chen punishes itself. Place Yin–Wu–Xu above Si–Wu–Wei: Yin punishes Si, Wu meeting Wu punishes itself, and Xu punishes Wei. Place Si–You–Chou above Shen–You–Xu: Si punishes Shen, You meeting You punishes itself, and Chou punishes Xu. Place Hai–Mao–Wei above Hai–Zi–Chou: Hai meeting Hai punishes itself, Mao [punishes] Zi, and Wei punishes Chou.[^143]

Punishment arising within combination resembles husband and wife joining yet instead bringing injury upon one another. Creative transformation and human affairs share one principle. The classic says: “Metal is hard and Fire strong: they punish their own direction. Wood falls and returns to its root; Water flows toward the east.” Thus the Metal positions Si–You–Chou all inflict their punishments in the west, and the Fire positions Yin–Wu–Xu in the south: this is hard Metal and strong Fire punishing their own direction. The Wood positions Hai–Mao–Wei all inflict their punishments in the north. Hai is Wood's root; “Wood falls and returns to its root” means plants shedding in winter and returning to their roots. The Water positions Shen–Zi–Chen all inflict their punishments in the east. Chen is Water's repository; “Water flows toward the east” means that its current goes eastward and does not return. Zi–Mao is one punishment; Yin–Si–Shen the second; Chou–Wei–Xu the third.
<!-- source: L504 -->
Another account says: “The method of the Three Punishments begins from number. In the *August Ultimate's Central Heaven*, ten is the number of sha. When accumulated number reaches ten, it overturns and empties itself. Heaven's Way abhors fullness; what is full overturns. Count forward from Mao to Zi, or backward from Zi to Mao: at the limit of ten, this becomes **Punishment Without Propriety**. Count backward from Yin to Si, or from Si to Shen: at the limit of ten, this becomes **Punishment Without Gratitude**. Count forward from Chou to Xu, or from Xu to Wei: at the limit of ten, this becomes **Punishment Through Reliance on Power**. Seven gives clash, ten punishment, six combination: these share one natural principle.”[^144]

Why is **Yin–Si–Shen** called **Without Gratitude**? Jia Wood within Yin punishes Wu Earth within Si. Wu combines with Gui Water as wife, and Gui Water is Jia Wood's mother. Since Wu Earth is Gui Water's husband, Wu is Jia's father. To punish one's father is to forget his kindness. Bing within Si punishing Geng within Shen, and Geng within Shen punishing Jia within Yin, follow the same meaning.

It is also said: “Yin contains Fire at Birth, which punishes Metal born at Si. Earth lodging its Birth at Si punishes Water born at Shen. Water born in Shen punishes Fire born in Yin. Without regard for what is generated, they control and restrain one another at a distance; hence Without Gratitude.” In Birth and flourishing, it indicates reserve, few words, few desires, and little feeling, frequently inviting the disturbance of ingratitude and loss of righteousness. In Death and Extinction, it praises to one's face and slanders behind one's back, forgetting kindness and abandoning righteousness. In a noble configuration, it is cruel, fond of killing, and eager to establish achievements; in a low configuration, speech and conduct run awry, with insatiable greed and miserliness. For women it often brings bleeding in childbirth and damage to the embryo, is unfavorable to close kin throughout life, yet gives a notably chaste and upright disposition.[^145]

<!-- source: L506 -->
Why is **Chou–Xu–Wei** called **Reliance on Power**? Chou contains flourishing Water and is Earth within Water. Xu contains entombed Fire. Chou relies upon its flourishing Water to punish Xu's entombed Fire. Xu holds the honor of the six Jia; Wei the low place of the six Gui. Xu relies upon the six Jia's honor to punish the six Gui's low place in Wei. Wei contains flourishing Earth and in turn relies upon its strength to punish Chou's flourishing Water.

Another account says: “Wei relies upon Ding Fire's power to punish Metal within Chou; Chou relies upon flourishing Water to punish Fire within Xu; Xu relies upon Xin Metal to punish Wood within Wei. Hence Reliance on Power.” In Birth and flourishing, it indicates heroic spirit and force of will, thick eyebrows and a broad face, straightforward dealings with others. In Death and Extinction, the form is exposed, thin, and small; the spirit is perverse and cunning, embroiled in disputes and base flattery, delighting in calamity and rejoicing at others' misfortune. In a noble configuration, it is public-spirited, incorruptible, fair, and upright, inspiring fear. In a low configuration, it frequently encounters penal documents and hidden disasters. For women it brings injury and solitude.[^146]

<!-- source: L508 -->
Why is **Zi–Mao** called **Without Propriety**? Zi belongs to Water and Mao to Wood. Water generates Wood, so Zi Water is the mother and Mao Wood the child; mother and child punish one another. Moreover, Mao is the sun's gate and Zi the birthplace of yang. The sun rises at Mao, but Zi and Mao confront one another without the way of reverence and humility. Disregarding their bond of generation, they inflict punishment and harm in turn; hence Without Propriety.

Another account says: “Zi employs only Gui Water. Gui employs Wu Earth as its husband star, and Wu is in Defeat at Mao: therefore Zi punishes Mao. Mao employs only Yi Wood. Yi employs Geng Metal as its husband star, and Geng dies at Zi: therefore Mao punishes Zi. These two houses imprison the husband and meet punishment. A woman's fate encountering them is particularly unfavorable; hence Without Propriety.”[^147]

In Birth and flourishing, it indicates an imposing and stern manner, a face without geniality, forceful qi and a violent nature, excessive scrutiny and intolerance. In Death and Extinction, it indicates contempt and carelessness, narrowness and harsh exploitation, little filial or brotherly regard, harm to wife and children, and close relations made enemies like Wu and Yue. In a noble configuration, it often brings military power, but does not favor close attendance upon the ruler or long tenure. In a low configuration, it brings rebellion and violence, frequently inviting punishment.

<!-- source: L510 -->
Why are **Chen, Wu, You, and Hai** called **Self-Punishment**? Among Yin, Shen, Si, and Hai, Yin, Si, and Shen punish one another, leaving Hai without punishment. Among Chen, Xu, Chou, and Wei, Xu, Chou, and Wei punish one another, leaving Chen without punishment. Among Zi, Wu, Mao, and You, Zi and Mao punish one another, leaving Wu and You without punishment. These four remaining positions are therefore called self-punishing. No other thing is imposed upon them, hence “self.”

Another account says: “Chen is Water's Tomb; surging, it overfills. Wu is Fire's flourishing; violent, it burns. You is Metal's position; rigid, it chips. Hai is Water's Birth; flourishing, it rots. Each receives excessive qi, already overabundant, and brings disaster upon itself: hence Self-Punishment.”[^148]

In Birth and flourishing, it indicates quietness with inward poison and a weak, inferior appearance. In Death and Extinction, there is deep malice and heedlessness, scrutinizing so keenly as to see fish in the depths, and frequent injury to limbs, joints, hands, and feet. In a noble configuration, it gives adaptability and strategic calculation; in a low configuration, many worries, stubborn stupidity, lack of feeling, and self-inflicted harm. With other ominous sha, the end is not good. For women it indicates sexual abandon and disastrous premature death.

<!-- source: L512 -->
Although there are four named kinds, they are called only the Three Punishments because the four clash positions, four extremes, and four Storehouses each lack one member and thus tilt away from balance. The three groups push against one another, acting with unequal intent; hence Three Punishments. The *Comprehensive View of the Three Vehicles* calls Yin–Si–Shen **Reliance on Power**, since each of its three palaces contains the power of Birth or Arrival at Office, and calls Chou–Xu–Wei **Without Gratitude**, since all three belong to Earth and are brothers through equality and harmony. Its explanation, too, is intelligible.[^149]

<!-- source: L514 -->
On seeing punishment, one must not immediately judge misfortune. Examine whether the Five Phases contain auspicious positions, flourishing and support, Officer stars, Seal-Ribbon, noble influences, virtue, blessing, and the like. With these auspicious influences supporting and assisting, punishment does no harm and instead becomes useful. Without them, if Loss, Robbery, Heavenly Void, Yang Blade, and other sha also appear, evil assists evil and the calamity is beyond words. It is also said: “The Three Punishments fear Metal.” The *Writings Bequeathed by Guigu* says:[^150]

> Without punishment, the gentleman does not rise;  
> Upon the official road, it often brings swift ascent.  
> For the petty person, arriving here brings disaster—  
> If nothing else, the magistrate's lash.

Huzhongzi says: “The Eight Characters have no configuration to support them; the nine fates have punishment to govern their mixture. One may become a person of the lanes and alleys, or a fellow of the marketplace.” Examining this discussion shows how the gentleman finds it auspicious and the petty person ominous.[^150]

If Chen, Wu, You, and Hai are all present and auspicious influences suppress them, they should bring distinction and authority. What is most objectionable is Chen meeting Chen, Wu meeting Wu, You meeting You, or Hai meeting Hai. With additional harmful sha, this is especially unfavorable. Shen Zhi says:

> Self-Punishment with sha is no good sign;  
> Year and month punish the flesh: injury must be seen.  
> If not a wasted death within a prison,  
> Then death upon the edge of sword or blade.

He also says: “Chen, Wu, You, and Hai all present: loss of sight in middle age,” and, “Self-Punishment fears Fire.” But if restraint exists within the punishment, one cannot immediately judge in this way. The *Classic of Penetrating Mystery* says: “You–You is disliked for excessive hardness; if Fire subdues its punishment, what is there to fear? Wu–Wu is disliked for excessive violence; if Water reduces its power, there is no fault. Wood grows together and its force diminishes 〈meaning Chen meeting Chen〉; Water flows cold and overflows 〈meaning Hai meeting Hai〉. Wood falling comes from loss; Water flowing is not flourishing.”[^151]

If a fate possesses Officer and Seal-Ribbon, it is auspicious when Officer and Seal come to punish it. If the fate instead punishes Officer and Seal, it is ominous. The *Rhapsody Pointing Through Delusion* says: “Officer punishing the fate is welcome; do not let the fate turn back and punish Officer. If Officer [and Seal] receive punishment, even distinction means being a soldier or minor functionary.” Chan Master Yixing says:[^152]

> Jiazi with Jimao has this explanation:  
> Direct Seal—the secret of the Phoenix Pool.  
> Bingyin with Xinsi is the same:  
> Promotion to the emolument and rank of the Three Dukes.

Jiazi meeting Jimao and Bingyin meeting Xinsi are instances of Officer and Seal punishing the fate auspiciously.
<!-- source: L516 -->
Another account says: “When all Three Punishments are present, distinguish whether the punishment **penetrates** or **does not penetrate**. Take the year as host and month, day, and hour as guests. If the host punishes a guest, penetration brings distinction and failure to penetrate brings low status. If a guest punishes the host, only failure to penetrate makes a noble configuration; penetration brings low status.”

Suppose Chou punishes Xu: examine precisely which Chou and which Xu. Yichou punishing Gengxu is punishment between the same kind and is inauspicious. Punishing Bingxu or Renxu combines generation with punishment and is not judged as punishment. Wuxu and Jiaxu combine control with punishment: Wuxu can be penetrated, Jiaxu cannot. Examine further the position where blessing or calamity arises. If Wuxu is a place where blessing gathers, Yichou coming to punish it is greatly auspicious. Failure to penetrate a place where blessing gathers does not make a noble fate. Judge the rest by analogy.[^153]

<!-- source: L518 -->
The classic says: “To determine whether a fate is without punishment, first consider Tai Sui.” This means that a person's fate dislikes the Three Punishments, but if month, day, and hour carry sha without Tai Sui being involved, they are not judged here. Hence “first consider Tai Sui.” It is also said: “Using sha to stop sha often brings responsibility for arms and punishment.” Tai Sui receives punishment, but another punishment restrains what punishes Tai Sui. For example, a Guisi-born person obtains a Wuyin day and a Gengshen hour. Guisi is controlled by Wuyin, but Gengshen Wood then controls Wuyin Earth. What originally had punishment thereby becomes free of punishment. Hence “using sha to stop sha.”[^154]

<!-- source: L520 -->
An old song says:

> The Three Punishments' positions carry the Three Wonders;  
> Heaven's One is also obtained in day and hour.  
> If punishment is evenly matched and the stem meets Virtue,  
> The highest official rank is assured, without deficiency.

Thus the Three Punishments need equality of force, with the Three Wonders, Noble Person, and Heavenly Virtue to make them auspicious.

<!-- source: L522-L524 -->
The *Jade Well* says: “In the method of mutual punishment, when essence and splendor gather tightly, punishment is indispensable. When the useful influence is closed away, punishment is indispensable. When Wealth and Officer's qi are solid, punishment is indispensable. When the qi of generation and combination is cut off, punishment is indispensable. When going or returning is appropriate, punishment is indispensable. Whether provisional or genuine, [punishment is indispensable]. When Blade and Killings lie hidden, punishment is indispensable. When meanness and stagnation hold things back, punishment is indispensable.”[^155]

It also says: “If the instrument of punishment and harm is excessive, another branch influence may in turn punish that instrument. What is heavy is commonplace rather than refined; what is refined is not necessarily extraordinary.”

〈Another account says: “The Three Punishments are punishment upward, punishment downward, and self-punishment. For example, when Zi punishes Mao, Mao is the downward punishment and Zi the upward. When Chou punishes Xu, Xu is downward and Wei upward. When Yin punishes Si, Si is downward and Shen upward. This explanation is better.”〉[^156]

## 31. On Clashes
### 論衝擊

<!-- source: L526-L528 -->
〈Qi clashing directly from opposite positions is what is called the **One–Seven Sha**.〉

The earthly branches take the seventh counted position as a clash, just as the heavenly stems take the seventh as Killings. Zi and Wu oppose one another: counted from Zi, Wu is seventh. Jia meeting Geng is Killings: counted from Jia, Geng is seventh. In number, six combines; seven exceeds the measure. Hence they clash and become sha. Consider the *Changes*: Kun's originating power uses six; its number has six and not seven. Seven is the exhausted number of Heaven and Earth, the extreme qi of yin and yang. In writing the character *zao* 皂, “black,” it incorporates “seven,” [following this principle], because blackness is color's extreme and can change no further. The *Changes* says, “In seven days comes return,” and, “Do not pursue: in seven days it is obtained.” This is the meaning.[^157]
Clashes are influences of battle and collision among the twelve branches. Generally they are ominous, yet some bring very great blessing: these are cases where generation occurs at the clash, as Xinsi Metal meeting Guihai Water. They indicate widespread reputation, exceptional brilliance, and conspicuous success in the examinations. If control occurs at the clash, as Renshen Metal meeting Gengyin [Wood], the person has a clear spirit and handsome appearance, an uncommon bearing, openness and distinction, walking with head raised and eyes looking upward. In Birth and flourishing, the spirit is strong and the appearance stern, with courage, boldness, and daring action, and many successes and failures. In Death and Extinction, there is impoverishment and baseness, an ungainly appearance, movement that invites disaster and humiliation, and frequent early death.[^158]

The four Storehouses, [Chen, Xu, Chou, and Wei], conceal the ten stems' Wealth, Officer, Seal-Ribbon, and other influences; these particularly welcome the stirring of clashes. If Yin, Shen, Si, and Hai are all present, or Zi, Wu, Mao, and You are all present, they instead form a great configuration and are not judged merely as clashes. Clashes between the same kind, such as Jiazi meeting Jiawu or Jimao meeting Jiyou, often indicate destruction of ancestral property, a mind never at rest throughout life, firmness, clarity, and decisiveness. Even with high emolument and a weighty name, some loss ultimately occurs.[^158]

<!-- source: L530-L534 -->
Another account says: “Whenever the One–Seven Sha is encountered, an auspicious fate is stirred into development by the clash; an ominous fate suffers calamity. If another position clashes with a place where calamity gathers, this is called **Breaking Calamity to Make Blessing**. If another position clashes with a place where blessing gathers, it breaks blessing and makes calamity. If a position in Void is met by a clash from another position, this too breaks calamity to make blessing. Encountered in year, month, day, or hour, it must produce someone who receives official emolument.”

“If the month clashes with day or hour, or the hour clashes with the year, this is called **Enmity Sha**. It indicates little kindness between oneself and others, much hatred and dislike, chronic illness or sudden death. With Robbery Sha and Loss Spirit accompanying the clash, it indicates punishment by law. In Death and Extinction, it indicates disability and frequent illness. With noble influences entering a configuration and refined qi bringing examination success, one often enters the Censorate, yet ultimately dies of a severe illness. With Yuanchen and Void in the clash, one is either of low status or poor. Withered Five Phases indicate low status; refined qi brings an empty reputation.”[^159]

The *Jade Well* says: “In the method of clashes, an auspicious image should come and clash with me; with an ominous image, I want to clash with it. In a Zi–Wu clash, take the useful influence as ‘me’ and the idle influence as ‘the other.’” Then examine: does the useful influence have qi? Does it have support? Does the idle influence have qi? Does it have support?[^160]

Shen Zhi says:

> Breaking Seal, breaking Wealth, and breaking Lu;  
> Breaking Horse has seldom brought me blessing.  
> Add broken combination and a clash in day or hour:  
> The illness strikes hands or feet, head or eyes.

**Breaking Seal** is, for example, a Wood person carrying Guiwei with Yichou Metal within the chart. **Breaking Wealth** is a Metal person taking Yin and Mao as Wealth, then meeting Shen or You. **Breaking Lu** is Jia, whose Lu is Yin, meeting Shen. **Breaking Horse** is Horse at Si meeting Hai. **Breaking Combination** means a stem combination being broken through its branches: a Jiawu person meeting Jihai, for example, then meeting [Si], which breaks Jihai; or Jihai meeting Zi, which breaks Jiawu's combination.[^161]
A poem says:

> Though clashing, they still generate one another;  
> Gathered at the imperial seat, the position has no punishment.  
> With Canopy and the influence of authority besides,  
> One holds pure, distinguished office and commands a mighty army.

Another says:

> In the coming and going of clashes, Birth is needed;  
> In robust flourishing, Lu prospers further.  
> If Tan 〈Greedy Wolf〉 and Wu 〈Martial Song〉 also reach the hour's influence,  
> One holds pure, honorable office and commands a mighty army.

Another says:

> Birth and flourishing continue, and auspicious influences appear;  
> The [One–Seven] is also there as neighbor.  
> Soon one enters straight into the court,  
> Holding the military tally to assist the sagely ruler.

Another says:

> Born where the four clashes gather, one is poor from the first;  
> With ominous spirits besides, there is nothing to commend.  
> A crooked heart delights in becoming a thief;  
> The father is always angered, the son always deceives.

Examining these poems shows that clashes and breaking can be auspicious or ominous. They cannot be judged by a single rule.[^162]

[^01]: **Text and comparison witnesses.** **S** means the repository’s [Chinese juan two](../source/juan-02.md), exported from [Wikisource revision 761691](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=761691); its SHA-256 is recorded above. It controls both content and order. **G596**, **G597**, and **G598** mean the parallel *Sanming tonghui* material in the *Gujin Tushu Jicheng*, Arts volumes [596](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/欽定古今圖書集成/博物彙編/藝術典/第596卷), [597](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/欽定古今圖書集成/博物彙編/藝術典/第597卷), and [598](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/欽定古今圖書集成/博物彙編/藝術典/第598卷), consulted as online transcriptions. **I** means the scanned [1909 *Gailiang Sanming Tonghui*, juan 2, Shanghai: Jiangzuo Shulin](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:SSID-13035019_改良三命通會_卷2.pdf). Its page references below are one-based PDF pages, not printed leaf numbers. Selected images were examined directly; this is not a complete facsimile collation. **U** means the shorter [unlabelled Wikisource juan two](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/三命通會/卷二), also consulted but not used to replace S. Witnesses were consulted on 25 September 2026. Agreement between online texts is evidence for a reading, not proof of independent descent. Added punctuation in a comparison transcription is not treated as evidence for the original punctuation or quotation boundaries. The original Chinese files have not been emended. The English section numbers and subordinate headings are editorial; the separate Siku preface is not translated here.

[^02]: **How to read the terminology.** A capitalized phase is an explanatory category, not necessarily the physical substance; a named stage such as Death or Sickness is a position in a cycle, not itself a diagnosis. *Qi* is retained rather than consistently reduced to “energy.” Lu can mean a designated stem position, emolument, or the prosperity associated with that position. “Useful influence” renders 用神 according to the local argument; it does not invariably mean a modern chart’s single corrective element. The text’s predictions about disease, longevity, reproduction, character, gender, or status are historical claims, not established facts or advice about a living person. Keeping them visible preserves the source without endorsing them.

[^03]: **The opening trigram substitutions.** In Yi’s exchange, S gives the middle line, but substituting that line alone does not produce Li. G596 gives the upper and lower lines, which do; the bracketed repair follows that witness. Other sentences in the ensuing general account of exchanges remain difficult to reconcile with their stated diagrams, especially those involving Li, Qian, and Kun. They are not silently rebuilt into a new system. The opening quotation assigned to the *Appended Statements* includes material conventionally placed in the *Explanation of the Trigrams*; the source’s attribution is retained as its attribution.

[^04]: **Hongfan is not ordinary phase assignment.** This opening scheme distributes transformed affiliations over the twenty-four compass positions, including stems and trigrams. It must not be substituted indiscriminately for ordinary stem phases, nayin, or the five paired-stem transformations discussed later. The mnemonic 震艮四位, “Zhen and Gen—the four positions,” names only two of the four positions it claims. Its preceding prose also assigns Si to Wood. The missing specification is not supplied in the translation. The author’s defense of the scheme is an argument from correspondence and conjunction, not a demonstrated physical derivation.

[^05]: **Jia’s timber image.** S’s 離上之木 is read as 離土之木, “Wood separated from the soil,” following G596; its severed roots and branches support this reading. S’s 杪椿 is likewise rendered from G596’s 杉椿, “fir and chun.” The second tree name is retained without an exact botanical identification. “Dead Wood” here means felled timber in a material analogy. It is not interchangeable with the technical Death stage, nor does the analogy necessarily describe every Jia chart.

[^06]: **Yi’s living Water and seasonal imagery.** S’s 沽水 is read as 活水, “living Water,” supported by G596 and the immediate contrast with dead Water. Clear substitutions among 己, 已, and 巳 are normalized only where the intended stem, ordinary verb, or branch is unambiguous. The claim about rice flowers opening in the Wu hour belongs to the source’s seasonal reasoning. Its reference to Yi within Wu is retained; it is not replaced by the hidden-stem list familiar from many modern presentations.

[^07]: **Bing’s different frames.** The passage moves between solar Fire and a furnace that requires human ignition, then distinguishes naturally arising yang from yin dependent on human affairs. The resulting tensions are not resolved by deleting one image. In the final sentence 木炁 belongs to a planetary-astrological comparison. “Wood and Qi” leaves the second term visible: it may concern the astrological influence commonly styled Purple Qi, but this wording alone does not justify a full identification or importing a planetary chart method into the Four Pillars argument.

[^08]: **Ding’s oil and wick world.** Vegetable oils are used as the rich substance of Yi Wood, feeding the soft Fire of lamps. This is a material analogy alongside the celestial image of stars. The source’s Yi within Wu is retained even though a different hidden-stem scheme ordinarily lists Ding and Ji there. “Fire becomes bright and is extinguished” concerns the passage’s argument about daylight obscuring lamplight and stars; it should not be silently turned into a universal rule that every increase of Fire is harmful.

[^09]: **Ji’s 磁石.** Both S and G596 have 磁石 at the point where firing destroys Ji Earth’s balanced quality. Literally this can suggest lodestone, while the firing context invites a ceramic or hardened-stone interpretation. “A stone-hard substance [磁石]” keeps the difficulty visible rather than asserting an unambiguous mineral or silently replacing the graph with 瓷. G596’s language of firing supports the general process, not an exact mineralogical identification. The description is part of the author’s elemental analogy.

[^10]: **Xin and the moon.** S reads 在天為日月乃太陰之精, which awkwardly introduces “sun and moon.” G596 reads 在天為月月乃太陰之精: “in Heaven it is the moon; the moon is the essence of Greater Yin.” The bracketed “moon” adopts that reading. The toad’s radiance is lunar imagery. Later, the distinct celestial-assignment section calls Geng the moon and Xin frost. These are different explanatory series in a compilation, not grounds for rewriting either passage into uniformity.

[^11]: **Unseen opposition and following configurations.** The Shen–Zi–Chen cluster’s invocation of Yin–Wu–Xu belongs to methods that infer an absent configuration through opposition or arching. It is not the same as six visibly present branches clashing in an ordinary chart. The paragraph alternates between this inferred configuration and openly present Wealth and Officer. Its favorable judgments are conditional upon the stated supports and the absence of weakness; no single quoted combination is offered here as a complete reading of a real chart.

[^12]: **Earth’s disputed Birth.** The small-character commentary is translated in full. Minor defects are resolved by their immediate context and G596: for example, the Si position contains Wu Earth, and Ji “follows” Ding to Lu at Wu. The quoted wording 巳懐 in the song attributed to a later Earth scheme remains defective; it is shown rather than replaced with an invented rhyme. The following empty square in S represents a missing character and is acknowledged in the translation. The criticism concerns rival assignments of Earth’s Birth and Lu, not a single undisputed table.

[^13]: **Substance and function.** The discussion offers Birth at Si, Yin, and Shen under different explanations. G596’s 父母之祿 supports “the parents’ Lu” against S’s 父之之祿. The opposition between 體, substance, and 用, functioning, explains why one author can describe more than one Birth for Earth. The statement about physicians records an attributed historical school of correspondence; it is not a claim about contemporary medicine or a clinical use of these cycles.

[^14]: **The critic Zhou Shi.** The name is transmitted as 周視 and has not been expanded into an uncertain biography. His objection treats Jia and Yi as the dynamic and settled aspects of one Wood rather than two separately living substances. S’s list Kan, Li, Xun, and Dui is retained even though the accompanying cardinal positions suggest Zhen rather than Xun at Mao. The passage’s argumentative force does not require silently correcting every diagrammatic inconsistency, and the reader should distinguish a quoted criticism from the compiler’s final preference.

[^15]: **Why the compiler returns to distinction.** After granting force to the shared-substance argument, the source favors the earlier account because it preserves differentiated technical relations: Officer versus Killings, Food God versus Hurting Officer, Seal-Ribbon versus Owl Spirit, and Rob Wealth versus Companions. These are not freely interchangeable translations of one influence. The compiler’s conclusion is retained despite the preceding praise of the competing view. “Earlier account” is therefore left relational rather than supplied with an overconfident modern school label.

[^16]: **Branch function depends on position.** The source explicitly distinguishes the branch in year, month, day, and hour, while giving monthly command particular weight. Zi as a flourishing place for Wu Earth is retained; G596 agrees. It reflects a different Earth cycle from the Fire-following account, not a warrant to replace Wu with Gui. Likewise, the text mentions both Greater Snow and the return of one yang. These should not be collapsed into one exact astronomical instant when the wording itself distinguishes the seasonal approach from the turn.

[^17]: **Yin–Si–Shen can be read more than one way.** The phrase “honored guests” is conditional upon strength and the relation of punishment to combination. Later sections discuss the same branches as an ominous punishment group, but also explain how punishment can become useful. Both treatments are retained. Similarly, opening a Storehouse through clash is not presented as an unrestricted guarantee of benefit: the ensuing Chen paragraph warns against repeated smashing of the Storehouse door.

[^18]: **Si’s Wu mother.** S’s 困頓戊母 does not fit smoothly into the sentence explaining Geng Metal’s Birth. G596 has 因賴戊母, “depending upon the Wu mother.” The translation adopts this with brackets. The mother is Wu Earth as the generator of Metal, not an additional personal relative in a modern kinship chart. The following sentence separately derives Wu Earth’s Lu from its Fire mother.

[^19]: **A retained variant in You.** Where S says that Water flourishes in day and hour, G596 reads Wood. The latter might alter the rationale for avoiding a southern course, but the comparison alone does not settle which reading is prior. Water is therefore retained. The paragraph’s challenge to treating all yin Metal as gentle pearls is intentional: a Si–You–Chou combination can give it a harder collective character.

[^20]: **“No single fixed reality.”** 無真實 and 借名 are read in the immediate warning against rigid interpretation: the names do not supply one invariant verdict irrespective of circumstances. This is not translated as a wholesale denial of the art the author proceeds to teach. A hard stem can lose its effective hardness and a soft one gain effective strength. 中和 is contextual balance and harmonious function, not an instruction to count equal numbers of the five phases.

[^21]: **Celestial assignments are a separate series.** Thunder, wind, sun, stars, rosy glow, cloud, moon, frost, autumn dew, and spring rain form this section’s analogical set. They need not match the earlier living/dead images. The configurations named under Jia—類象, 趨乾, 遙巳, 拱貴—are preserved as names, not expanded into unsupported instructions. Geng’s lunar analogy, including the moon’s initial appearance in the Geng direction, belongs to the text’s stem-assignment cosmology rather than a modern assertion that Metal physically causes lunar light.

[^22]: **Yi at Si and in autumn.** The argument for Defeat at Si proceeds through wind breaking the Wood that produced it. It should not be silently harmonized with every version of the twelve-stage table. The subsequent reference to transformation and following invokes specific configuration behavior, not an unconditional statement that autumn benefits every Yi person. The text itself qualifies the image through hardness, sharpness, and the process of shaping useful material.

[^23]: **Solar images and family judgments.** “The Sun Rising Above the Earth” at a Chou hour is a named symbolic configuration, not a literal sunrise timetable. The compiler’s repeated 有旨哉 is retained. Wan Qi’s rhapsody and the remarks about a Dingsi day remain attributed material. The claims about damage to father, brothers, wife, or children are not independently established outcomes of a birth date; their historical presence is recorded without turning them into a personal warning to the reader.

[^24]: **Clouds meeting Jia, not Seal.** S reads 貴見印, “distinction on meeting Seal.” G596 reads 貴見甲, “distinction on meeting Jia.” The latter is directly visible in I, PDF p. 11, and fits the preceding Jia–Ji combination. The translation therefore supplies “[Jia].” This is an attested repair, not merely an inference from the modern Ten Gods. The repeated 至妙 is preserved, as is the separate warning against Yi when sitting on Hai.

[^25]: **Punctuating Xin’s favorable conditions.** 坐卯未透乙 can be read “sitting on Mao or Wei, with Yi exposed,” or, with different division, “sitting on Mao, with Yi not exposed.” The translation chooses the former: Mao and Wei both supply a plausible relation to Yi in the local discussion. I, PDF p. 11, is unpunctuated and cannot decide the matter merely by appearance. S’s later 坐亥, “sitting on Hai,” is retained against G596’s 生亥, “born at Hai.”

[^26]: **Ji in Gui’s rain image.** The stem written 巳 in 透巳字 is normalized to 己, Ji, consistent with the cloud image and the following Jisi poem. The poem’s adverse longevity judgment is the source’s, not a medical inference. 經濟 in this context means ordering the world and aiding people, not “economics” in its modern disciplinary sense. The contrast between a capable public life and a shortened span is left intact rather than softened into a generic caution.

[^27]: **The terrestrial sequence and its frames.** The twelve images are grouped as four cardinal positions, four Birth positions, and four Storehouses, not simply listed from Zi through Hai. Several examples explicitly begin from the birth year and end at the hour; these have not been converted into day-stem judgments. “Twin Flight” is a named configuration for which the text provides an example, not a complete algorithm. Its observation that Noble Person does not alight in Chen and Xu likewise belongs to the particular inherited rule being invoked.

[^28]: **Two Earth stems and four Earth branches.** The discussion contrasts circulating celestial function with stationary terrestrial opposition. Its purpose is to explain the different numbers, not to declare that two branches have been lost. “Heavenly Origin” and “Earth’s veins” can function as technical terms in this literature; they are not straightforward equivalents of modern astronomy and geology. The compiler presents the analogy with the *Changes* as a defense of his method.

[^29]: **Zuixingzi’s admonition.** The full parallel passage is retained, including its warning against careless speech. The traditional length units *cun* and *zhang* are not replaced by “inch” and “fathom”: a zhang contains one hundred cun within this system. S’s 乙未秋生, Yiwei born in autumn, is retained although G596 offers 乙木秋生, Yi Wood born in autumn. The timber and furnace contrasts are material analogies, not necessarily names from the thirty nayin images. Preserving their parallelism should not disguise their shifts between substance, season, and configuration.

[^30]: **Animal correspondences and purported deficiencies.** S’s 丑屬午 is repaired to Chou belonging to the ox, supported by G596 and the list itself. “Digits” clarifies what the numerical claim about feet requires. The subsequent statements about anatomy and breeding—including the hare having no male, the horse lacking gall, and various animals lacking organs—are transmitted beliefs, not accepted zoological facts. They have not been modernized into accurate anatomy, since that would replace the author’s argument. His ranking of humanity above animals is also his argument, not an independently demonstrated conclusion.

[^31]: **Wang Chong’s counterargument.** The passage attributed to the *Lunheng* first states the phase-control explanation and then tests it against contrary cases, finally appealing to teeth, sinews, and strength. Both stages of the argument are translated. Its claim about a horse eating rat droppings belongs to the inherited example and is not veterinary advice. The compiler’s inclusion of a skeptical voice matters: it would misrepresent the juan to extract only the initial correlations and omit their criticism.

[^32]: **The Human Origin here.** This section assigns Lu to the stems, body to the branches, and fate to what is concealed in the branches. Elsewhere in the work a different threefold allocation uses stem, branch, and nayin. The schemes have not been forced into one terminology. Here 用神 is explicitly linked with the monthly influence holding office. It should first be read in that local sense, rather than automatically imported into a later balancing-element doctrine.

[^33]: **The first allocation table.** Each month totals thirty days. The text uses Gen or Kun Earth in certain positions and names yang stems in several Storehouse entries; these have not been replaced by a standardized modern list of hidden stems. The table records the order in S, not a newly calculated solar-calendar schedule. Its precision is subsequently challenged by the compiler’s inclusion of Zuixingzi’s argument, so the table and critique need to be read together.

[^34]: **Numerical defects in the *Jade Well* scheme.** Eight stems at thirty-five days each plus Wu and Ji at fifty each total **380**, not the stated 360. The following monthly entries total **358**: the eighth month has 32 days, the eleventh 26, and the others 30. Those figures are retained rather than redistributed to force a result. Their inconsistency may involve copying, a damaged tabular arrangement, or more than one scheme; the available comparison does not establish a single certain repair. The seasonal-term labels in small type remain attached to their months.

[^35]: **The criticism of fractional command.** Zuixingzi’s four phases at seventy-two days each plus Earth’s four periods of eighteen total 360. He accepts broad beginnings, middles, endings, and relative depths, but challenges giving each subsidiary influence an independent three-, five-, or seven-day sovereignty. His conclusion that concealed influences are discussed through the month, not year, day, or hour, is retained even though later practice often treats hidden stems in every pillar. “Guest” and “host” are his explanatory distinction.

[^36]: **Jie and central qi.** 節 can denote the boundary term, 中氣 the intervening central term, while 氣 can also name a solar term more generally. “Division” translates 分 in the equinox names, “arrival” 至 in the solstices. The explanations linking Zi with stopping and Wu with opposition are the source’s sound-and-meaning associations, not asserted historical etymologies. The half-year growth of yin and yang must be distinguished from the alternating yin/yang labels of individual branches.

[^37]: **Two supported seasonal repairs.** The first statement that Water begins to freeze reads 冬至, Winter Solstice, in S, followed almost immediately by a different Winter Solstice event. G597 gives 立冬, Beginning of Winter; the translation follows it. Near the end, S says cold has transformed into 水, Water, by Beginning of Summer. G597 gives 暑, heat, fitting the paired discussion of cold and heat; this is also adopted in brackets. These are textual repairs, not updates to the historical weather scheme.

[^38]: **Lesser Fullness and Grain in Ear.** The argument connects 小滿 with the first onset of yin, using the *Changes’* images of a lean sow and frost. It is an exegetical analogy, not a literal description of crop fullness alone. S calls Grain in Ear the fifth month’s central qi, although its own nearby term list pairs it as the boundary term before Summer Solstice. This inconsistency is retained. The grain–wheat contrast illustrates the compiler’s phase argument; it does not establish one universal sowing schedule.

[^39]: **Great Fire and the winter poem.** “Great Fire” 大火 is a celestial name in the sentence about westward movement, not simply a large flame. The quotation from the *Airs of Bin* uses an older calendrical idiom; “first” and “second” months there should not be read as modern January and February. The source employs it to distinguish the wind’s chill from the deeper cold of the qi. No present-day weather forecast is implied by the sequence.

[^40]: **The units du and ke.** The quoted year and celestial circle use 365¼ *du*, not 360 modern degrees. A day is divided into one hundred large *ke*; as a fraction of a modern twenty-four-hour day, one such ke would be 14.4 minutes. That arithmetic does not make the historical sunrise table universally applicable. The forty/sixty-ke extremes express the source’s model of seasonal daylight, not a latitude-independent astronomical fact. “Three hou to a qi” uses five-day hou, unlike the fifteen-day divisions in section 21.

[^41]: **Eight stations called four divisions.** The passage lists the four seasonal beginnings, two equinoxes, and two solstices—eight stations—yet then calls them 四分, “four divisions.” The translation preserves that verbal discrepancy. Its next arithmetic, three times eight equals twenty-four, works with the eight named stations. Correcting the word “four” silently would hide a real problem in the controlling transcription without adding much practical clarity.

[^42]: **Relative celestial motion.** Leftward and rightward motion, Heaven’s rotation, and the sun and moon’s progress are expressed in the source’s own reference scheme. They should not be treated as modern coordinate statements without conversion and interpretation. The lunar value thirteen du and seven nineteenths appears here as an average; the text itself subsequently recognizes unequal motion. The approximate twenty-seven-day circuit and the later conjunction interval are not the same quantity.

[^43]: **The intercalation arithmetic.** The model subtracts a 354-day lunar year from 365¼ days, leaving 11¼ days to accumulate. It distinguishes the schematic 360-day basis from both totals. Its account of an intercalary month lacking an establishing qi or position is not silently restated as a modern calendar rule. Readers wishing to compute historical dates would need a specified calendar, locality, and epoch; this passage alone does not provide a reliable modern conversion algorithm.

[^44]: **The water clock and six qi.** Sixty days plus eighty-seven and a half hundredths of a day, multiplied by six, gives 365¼ days. This explains the four-year cycling of quarter-day remainders discussed next. The physical bronze vessel supplies an image for measuring time independently of visible sunlight. Its asserted constancy concerns the schematic transition count, not a claim that a surviving ancient instrument kept perfectly uniform time.

[^45]: **The six transition rows.** The sequence retains S’s ke and fen wording and its hour labels, including discontinuities such as the fourth period ending at fifty-one ke. Straight quarter-day arithmetic would suggest a different boundary at that point. The source also switches between fractional ke and separate fen. Rather than regularize the whole table, the translation preserves its numbers and identifies it as transmitted. The subsequent explanation of triple combinations through equal starting quarters is explicitly offered as a rival to the Birth–flourishing–Storehouse explanation.

[^46]: **Dividing the double-hour.** Twelve hours each contain eight large and two small ke. Ninety-six large ke plus twenty-four small ke, at six small to one large, total one hundred. Chu and zheng name the two halves of a traditional double-hour, not modern half-hours. Each half has four large divisions and a final small one. The source assigns the first half of Zi to the previous day and the second to the current day. This is its chosen boundary convention, not a statement that every later school or software package uses the same one.

[^47]: **How to use the solar tables.** Every “now” belongs to the historical text’s comparison of positions, not to 2026. Mansion names are accompanied by Chinese characters because Wei, for example, can represent 危, 尾, or 胃. Positions are not converted into current longitudes. The source supplies no independently verified modern epoch, latitude, or equation-of-time correction for these entries. They are retained as textual data; using them directly as a contemporary sunrise table would be a category error.

[^48]: **Repairs to the twenty-four entries.** G597 supports the following readings adopted in brackets or clarified syntax: Greater Cold’s night lengths are 57 and then 56 ke, not S’s seventeen and defective following numeral; Awakening of Insects has 室, Shi, rather than 空; Beginning of Summer’s 卯 time is sunrise; Lesser Heat’s 酉 time is sunset; and the solar station is 析木, Ximu, not 折木. The Greater Cold correction to 57 is also clearly visible in I, PDF p. 19. These selected repairs do not certify every remaining numeral in either witness.

[^49]: **Unresolved entries are still unresolved.** The Rain Water entry’s 尾 at the initial du, several “now” longitudes, the unspecified initial/central half at Grain Rain, the words “no [further] division” at Grain in Ear, and Frost’s Descent’s 角初十度 remain awkward. Greater Snow’s early-Mao sunrise also sits uneasily with the surrounding winter entries. S is retained where comparison does not securely settle the matter. Other differences, such as Kui’s degree after Spring Equinox, are not silently substituted from G597. The resulting table is readable but deliberately not presented as an astronomically corrected ephemeris.

[^50]: **Why the author worries about recorded time.** The eight-ke shorthand in this paragraph concerns the large ke; the two small divisions were explained immediately before. Weather changes, uncertain birth reports, and the Zi–Hai boundary are explicitly cited as obstacles to precision. The author refers to the *Shoushi* or Season-Granting Calendar, but the translation does not infer a modern clock conversion from that name alone. His recommendation is careful observation and inquiry, not confidence unsupported by the record.

[^51]: **Solar paths and an attribution.** The transmitted 陳 name is identified in brackets as Chen Tuan, following G597’s 陳摶. S’s 此至東井 is read as 北至東井, “northward to Eastern Well,” supported by G597 and the opposed southern clause. The three-path description is an inherited astronomical model. “Yellow Path” translates 黃道 without erasing the text’s own distinction between the middle route and northern and southern limits.

[^52]: **Two different station timetables.** The twelve-row table is not a reconciliation of the earlier twenty-four entries. Its delays after the central qi sometimes differ from those already given: Rain Water, for instance, is followed by a different number of days before the named station. Both schedules belong to the source and are preserved separately. The associated compass positions are likewise kept in their transmitted order rather than rearranged into a standardized modern diagram.

[^53]: **Three days: a conjectural repair.** S, G597, and I (PDF p. 21) read 越三十日, “after thirty days,” before the first crescent. This conflicts with the ensuing eighth/fifteenth-day sequence and with the stated relative separation of roughly thirty-seven du. The bracketed “three” is therefore an emendation inferred from the argument and its arithmetic, not an attested variant in these witnesses. The moon’s images are shown as the actual trigram signs ☳, ☱, ☰, ☴, ☶, ☷ rather than reproducing ambiguous typographic placeholders. These diagrams represent the argument’s successive illumination patterns.

[^54]: **Damaged lunar arithmetic.** Several numerical statements are not mutually consistent: the expression involving six days and roughly 490 parts lacks a secure denominator, and the daily totals and relative shortfalls do not form a clean modern calculation. They remain visible rather than being replaced wholesale. One check is decisive: twelve times 29 + 499/940 days equals **354 + 348/940**, not S’s **354 + 340/940**. The translation retains the latter as the transmitted number. A critical reconstruction would require more than making isolated figures agree arithmetically.

[^55]: **The nine lunar courses.** The source counts two each of four colored paths, plus the Yellow Path. The colors and seasonal directions are part of that nine-course account; they do not describe nine physical colored tracks in the sky. Nor are the named compass sectors silently converted into modern ecliptic or equatorial coordinates. Their relevance here is to the ensuing correspondence argument about the virtues and the Sun–Moon meeting.

[^56]: **Heavenly and Monthly Virtue.** The passage assigns both virtues to the same named stem in four illustrated months, deriving the result through the solar station and the branch triad. This does not authorize replacing all other Heavenly Virtue rules with the four-row derivation, or treating it as a complete modern lookup table. The translation preserves the author’s stated conjunction of the two virtues and his subsequent favorable judgment as parts of this particular explanation.

[^57]: **The compiler’s objection to lunar stem assignment.** The criticism depends on distinguishing the moon’s enduring round body from its changing visible illumination. It is important that this objection follows a long exposition of the very method it questions. The account is therefore not an unqualified endorsement of every Najia diagram. Kan and Li’s middle lines are discussed as diagrammatic forms; no missing lunar phase is invented to complete their analogy.

[^58]: **The alternative *Azure Satchel* scheme.** The quoted assignments and palace numbers are retained, including places where the stated odd/even quality does not match the literal palace numeral. The text’s Ren-to-Li and Gui-to-Kan assignments, and Wu–Ji’s central nonassignment, must not be silently replaced by another familiar Najia scheme. “Substance,” “function,” generation numbers, and completion numbers are all active categories here. The passage offers an attributed correspondence system, not an independently verified geometrical proof.

[^59]: **Laozi and the carriage analogy.** 載營魄抱一 supplies the wording on which the source builds its interpretation of solar light riding upon the moon’s dark body. The translation gives that local reading rather than presenting it as the only possible translation of Laozi. Before and after fullness, “east” and “west” are also the sides within this historical explanatory account. The final objection is the compiler’s judgment about the fit between fixed trigram forms and changing illumination.

[^60]: **Seasonal condition versus the twelve stages.** The five seasonal conditions—flourishing, supported, resting, confined, dead—form one scheme; the twelve lodged-life palaces form another. The body preserves the source’s seasonal relationships, normalizing the obvious 因 for 囚 in the summer Water clause. “Death” in a seasonal diagram is not biological death. The following paragraphs explicitly warn that favorable-sounding states can be excessive and unfavorable-sounding ones may need support rather than condemnation.

[^61]: **The twelve stages in a different starting order.** The *Essentials of the Three Fates* begins at Receiving Qi/Extinction, then gives Embryo and Taking Form before Birth. Taking Form occupies the place otherwise called Nurturing; it is not a thirteenth stage. The source’s image of a newborn bathed on the third day is a historical analogy, not infant-care advice. Its final classification into four honored, four avoided, and four neutral states is explicitly qualified as a broad statement.

[^62]: **Deriving month and hour stems.** The two songs give starting stems, after which the stems advance regularly through the branches. The first month is placed at Yin and the first hour at Zi. The first formula uses the year stem; the second uses the day stem. These are different inputs and must not be interchanged. The section’s root/seedling/blossom/fruit imagery also explains why the compiler connects month to year and hour to day, without making the metaphor itself a complete chart-reading procedure.

[^63]: **Wood controls Earth.** In explaining the Jia–Ji derivation, S has Water controlling Earth, while G597 gives Wood controlling Earth. The latter agrees with both the ordinary control cycle and the stated Jiazi hour origin; “[Wood]” therefore follows the comparison. The month formula’s use of generation and the hour formula’s use of control are part of this local derivation. “Tiger” and “Dragon” in related discussions refer to the Yin and Chen positions, not additional animal indicators.

[^64]: **Root and ruler are not synonyms.** 本 here is the year-root; 主 is the day-ruler. The examples mix natal affiliations, branch places, and nayin relationships. They have not all been rewritten as modern day-stem examples. One conspicuous retained entry is Jiayin among the Wood-root examples, although Jiayin’s nayin is Water; G597 also transmits it. That tension is recorded rather than concealed by substituting a different stem–branch pair.

[^65]: **The historical attribution to Li Xuzhong.** The assertion that Li Xuzhong singled out the day stem is the compiler’s account of the art’s history. It is not independently established here as a precise reconstruction of Li’s original method. The scale analogy moves from hook to suspension cord to marked beam and then to a movable weight. The translation does not supply a missing explicit equation of weight with hour, though the structure invites that inference.

[^66]: **The three periods.** The source gives twenty-five years to the month, twenty-five to the day, and fifty to the hour, totaling one hundred. This is not the same as the twelve ten-year courses totaling one hundred and twenty in the later major-luck discussion. The two schemes are preserved as separate accounts. Nor are the three overlapping “rulers”—year/month, month/day, day/hour—identical to these three periods.

[^67]: **The imperial-seat metaphor.** The day is compared with an emperor, the hour with the close attendant or imperial seat through which blessings gather and issue. The wording is not forced into one literal office chart. References to Hanlin learning, military authority, and court attendance express the social world of the text. “Blessings gathering at the seat” and “blessings issuing from the seat” are directional relationships within that account, not two interchangeable labels for generic good fortune.

[^68]: **Trailing Sash and Without Continuation.** 垂帶 and 無後 describe the source’s argument about how much of a seasonal transition is received. “Without Continuation” is not automatically translated as “without descendants,” since the stated consequence is that wealth and distinction do not endure. The text combines lunar-month language with solar-term boundaries. Its examples are retained without pretending that they supply a complete modern birth-date conversion system.

[^69]: **The *Discourse Establishing the True*.** The quoted method explicitly changes reference points: year as ancestors or natal ruler, month as parents or brothers, day as self or wife, hour as offspring. Male and female offspring rules differ in the quotation. They are not collapsed into a single contemporary kinship table. Phrases such as “stem and branch are the same” and the allusion to prohibited days remain compressed; the translation avoids inventing a missing worked example to settle their full scope.

[^70]: **Conception Origin is a technical construction.** 胎元, conception month, and an actual biological conception date are related in the author’s discussion but should not be treated as identical modern measurements. The text gives the conception position considerable interpretive weight, then criticizes easy derivations. Yuhu 玉湖 is retained as an attributed authority without an uncertain biographical expansion. Nothing in this section establishes a way to determine an actual pregnancy date from an adult’s chart.

[^71]: **The Yisi and three-hundred-day calculations.** The printed comparison I, PDF p. 26, and G597 support Yisi as the intended conception month and “ten months” where S’s wording is defective. The proposed three-hundred-day interval is five sixty-day cycles, not five Jiazi days taken literally. The phrase concerning a half-month and the tenth or eleventh preceding month remains uncertain in the witnesses consulted. It is marked as such rather than turned into a falsely precise algorithm. The compiler’s objections to formulaic certainty are part of the translated argument.

[^72]: **Gestation and lifespan.** The claimed relation between a longer gestation and a longer lifespan is historical speculation, not medical evidence. The later two-hundred-seventy-day and ten-month explanations are retained as competing claims, including the obscure physiological wording about the blood organs. Most importantly, the compiler supplies contrary cases and asks how the fixed three-hundred-day rule can be trusted. The reported seven-month births of named people are translated as his reports, not independently authenticated clinical records.

[^73]: **The Life Palace algorithm.** First map the birth month backward from Zi; then place the birth hour there and count hours forward until Mao is reached. The worked third-month/Xu-hour example consequently ends at Mao, whose stem is derived as Ding in a Jia year. “Count to Mao” does not mean that every chart’s palace is always Mao: the example happens to align the starting labels that way. This Life Palace method is kept distinct from other astrological palace systems.

[^74]: **The major-cycle conversion.** Three elapsed days correspond to one schematic year; one traditional double-hour therefore corresponds to ten days. Thirty elapsed days correspond to ten years, using 360-day schematic years. The text then discusses corrections based on actual short and intercalary months. These operations must not be conflated with dividing an ordinary Gregorian date interval by three and treating the decimal as a modern civil age without further convention.

[^75]: **Forward and backward direction.** In this passage, polarity is taken from the birth year, with men of yang years and women of yin years moving forward, the converse backward. The historical male/female rule is translated as stated. It is not silently replaced by a day-stem polarity convention or generalized beyond the source’s binary framework. The calculation depends on which boundary term is being counted toward or back from, including the recorded time within the day.

[^76]: **The worked start age.** Five days and three double-hours equal sixty-three double-hours. At ten schematic days per double-hour, that gives 630 days, or twenty-one thirty-day months: one year and nine months. S’s “sixty-three days” is therefore identified as a unit error, not followed literally. Its defective twenty-one-day/month wording is read as months. “Third [counted] year” distinguishes inclusive year counting from elapsed age. Dingchou is the originating monthly position in the example; the wording should not be mistaken for a fully printed list of successive ten-year pillars.

[^77]: **Actual and schematic reckoning are not identical.** The discussion advances the anniversary by six days for six short months, then moves it back for an intercalary month. That procedure belongs to its effort to reconcile schematic 360-day periods with actual lunar months; it is not a statement about Gregorian leap years. The phrase 六節, translated “six terms,” remains obscure and has not been silently changed to 交節, the term transition. Exact historical implementation requires conventions beyond those supplied here.

[^78]: **Stem and branch emphasis.** S repeats the southern direction where G598 supplies the expected east/south/west/north sequence; “[east]” follows G598. The advice to include branch influences when considering the stem, but set aside stem influences when considering the branch, is preserved. It is not expanded into an unstated mechanical rule of five stem-years followed by five branch-years. The important question in this paragraph is whether the course supports or injures the particular useful influence, with the natal chart’s strength kept in view.

[^79]: **Approaching a favorable region.** The phrases “Wood person” and “Metal person” are retained without imposing one universal reference point on all the examples. The text’s distinction between deep, shallow, and middle reception of qi governs whether results begin before the transition, after it, or in the middle. These are interpretive timing claims, not independently measured response intervals. Lu and Horse here also participate in the broader language of office and wealth.

[^80]: **Age, strength, and opposing prescriptions.** One account matches youthful, mature, and old bodies with analogous stages; another asks a weak chart to receive a strong course and a strong chart a weak one. Both are present and are not collapsed into a single rule. 天癸 is a traditional physiological term; the age statements attached to it are not modern medical thresholds. The compiler’s explicit warning that flourishing need not be good and decline need not be bad should control the reading of the surrounding categorical language.

[^81]: **Residual fortune at a transition.** In the commentary on first entering decline, S has “cannot immediately call it blessing,” which conflicts with the statement that a little blessing remains. G598 reads 禍, misfortune, and the bracketed correction follows it. The preceding and following five years are invoked to explain carry-over across a transition. This is not, by itself, a complete specification of how every stem and branch is to be divided into separate five-year administrations.

[^82]: **Yuanchen and the Chen–Cai allusion.** 元辰 is retained as the technical name Yuanchen rather than assigned a misleading literal English title. The source gives special attention to it in one directional class of luck progression. Confucius’s hunger between Chen and Cai is an illustrative traditional allusion, not evidence that the corresponding birth and luck calculations have been historically verified. Its presence in the argument is preserved without claiming such verification.

[^83]: **“Parts” are heuristic, not probabilities.** The ten-part, five-part, and three- or four-part comparisons describe relative strength of natal protection and adverse luck. They are not statistical percentages of risk. The later statement that ten parts produce five parts of response and five parts produce ten is retained despite its unclear relation to the earlier scale. The phrase 令疾 in the death-through-illness clause is also uncertain; the translation avoids specifying a clinical diagnosis. None of the fatality assertions is offered as a forecast for a living reader.

[^84]: **Half-year polarity.** Here Zi through Si form the yang half and Wu through Hai the yin half. That grouping differs from the alternating yin/yang branch labels used elsewhere. It also differs from the male/female rule for the direction of the luck course. Keeping those three uses distinct prevents the claim about development through opposite qi from becoming an incoherent universal rule.

[^85]: **Wealth becoming a ghost, and a ghost becoming support.** These are relational phase arguments: what first appears beneficial may generate an influence that controls the natal reference, while an apparent adversary may generate its support. S’s incomplete 固是為 is supplied as “[blessing]” from G598’s 固是為福. “Damage to qi” is a separate named judgment in the following example; it is not a claim that Wood is always harmful or that Water is always unfavorable to a Water fate.

[^86]: **Named transitions and a compressed warning.** 退人 is read as Retreating Spirit, 退神, supported by G598. Dingchou/Dingwei is treated through its specific nayin transformation and then explicitly excepted when both were already present at birth. The phrase concerning Yi and Geng people with “You in year, month, day, and hour” remains compressed in the witnesses consulted; the translation leaves its wording visible rather than inventing a complete condition. Individual examples should not be extended beyond the stated natal context.

[^87]: **Qi and image.** The table preserves both ordinary stem affiliations and combined-stem transformations. G598 supports 甲己 where S’s wording appears to repeat hour labels, 純 where S’s graph is defective in the five pure images, and 戌 in the Metal–Fire row where S has 戊. The list includes Metal–Earth twice under different presentations and does not provide every possible phase pair. Those features are retained; it has not been silently recast as an exhaustive symmetrical matrix.

[^88]: **Changing or grafting the tree.** The initial 丑丑 is normalized to Yichou, supported by G598’s 乙丑. The metaphor compares the month-rooted luck sequence to a tree and its seedlings. The phrase about Cold Damage and “changing yang” belongs to historical medical imagery, not treatment advice. Jiaxu followed by Guihai is retained as the text’s example even though it is not simply the standard adjacent Jiazi transition. The four directional corners and a change into Jia are related but distinct conditions in the passage.

[^89]: **A formula declared rigid.** S’s 死發 is read through G598’s 死法, hence “a rigid [formula].” This qualification is essential: the preceding list assigns luck-course results for Jia and Yi, but the compiler immediately requires modification according to configuration, strength, and preference. The text’s strong predictions about parents, children, litigation, and disease are not being turned into universal outcomes of encountering the named stem or branch.

[^90]: **Minor progression, marriage, and gestation.** S first gives 丙申 for the woman’s starting position; G598 and S’s own subsequent derivation give 壬申, Renshen, which is adopted. The thirty/twenty and ten-month calculations explain the author’s numerological model of pairing and birth. They do not establish appropriate present-day marriage ages or a medical gestation rule. The symbolic fifty is connected to the Great Expansion’s number in the quoted *White Tiger* explanation.

[^91]: **Two minor-progression methods.** The first fixes male and female starting pillars and criticizes a ten-day-round variation; its printed list does not supply every round, and no missing one is invented. Zuixingzi then offers a different method starting from the birth hour, with direction determined by the year’s polarity and the person’s sex. One position governs one year. The compiler’s claim of repeated successful trials is retained as his report, not independent validation. These two methods should not be merged into one calculation without stating a choice.

[^92]: **Two meanings of Tai Sui.** Natal Tai Sui is the birth-year pillar; traveling Tai Sui is the current annual pillar. The comparison with ruler and minister is part of the interpretive hierarchy. “Grand Duke of the Year” is a conventional English designation, not a literal translation of each character. This section does not calculate Jupiter’s actual astronomical position, so its annual pillar must not be identified uncritically with a present-day planetary longitude.

[^93]: **Direction matters in the ruler–subject analogy.** Jia controlling a Wu year is not treated like a Geng year controlling Jia, even though both involve phase control. The source uses the stronger image of a minister or son killing his superior; it has not been softened into ordinary disagreement. “Rescue” means an influence restraining the offending stem, while “sympathetic relation” means an appropriate combination. Neither term means that a named protective sign guarantees safety in real life.

[^94]: **True Tai Sui and campaigning.** The example of Jiazi meeting Jiazi is a return of the complete stem–branch pair, not merely the animal branch. “Campaigning” 征 is then applied in more than one direction—to day or luck attacking the year, and to the year attacking the day. This broader later usage is preserved rather than forced to match only the first example. Its effect remains conditional upon the larger relation of the chart and the luck course.

[^95]: **Year and luck arriving together is not automatically fatal.** 歲運並臨 here means the annual pillar and major-luck pillar coincide. The text explicitly distinguishes Yang Blade and Seven Killings from Wealth, Officer, and Seal-Ribbon, which can be favorable. Its qualification of the ominous maxim is translated in full. Extracting only “calamity comes at once” would reverse the force of the surrounding explanation.

[^96]: **The accompanying sha list.** S’s 勾紋 is read as 勾絞, Hook and Strangling, following G598. Other compressed pairs retain their traditional names rather than receiving invented modern psychological descriptions. Noble status, official petitions, mourning, and punishment belong to the source’s historical judgments. The ominous verse is a quoted maxim whose application the surrounding paragraphs qualify; it is not a diagnosis or an instruction to fear a particular year.

[^97]: **Two reported fatal cases, one damaged chart.** The second case’s duplicated 乙 in S is removed to give the eight characters 乙丑／乙亥／壬申／乙巳, supported by G598 and visible in I, PDF p. 32. A further difficulty remains: Yichou year and Yihai month do not agree with the month-stem derivation taught earlier. The printed comparison shares that mismatch. The year is not silently changed to Jichou merely because that would fit. The reported illnesses and deaths remain the compiler’s examples, not independently verified medical causation.

[^98]: **The hour and the children’s position.** 子位 at the end is read as the offspring position because the sentence concerns injury involving the birth hour. It is not automatically the branch Zi. The comparative severity assigned to yang and yin stems, and the exceptions involving virtue, prior opposition, or useful influence, are retained as the source’s judgments. They should not be converted into absolute risk categories for contemporary people.

[^99]: **Annual stem, luck-course branch.** The source privileges the heavenly origin in the annual pillar and the earthly branch in the major course, yet immediately allows the course’s stem to bring Wealth or Killings. This is emphasis, not permission to discard the other component altogether. It also asks whether success has already manifested and whether its allotted qi has passed. Those conditions belong to its timing argument and cannot be inferred merely from a single favorable-looking pillar.

[^100]: **Fanyin and Fuyin.** 反吟 and 伏吟 are technical names, rendered here as returning and repeated echo. The former concerns the opposed relation specified in the passage; the latter repetition or overlay. Their use elsewhere can depend on the system and reference pillar. The translation does not broaden this sentence into a universal rule that every repeated branch, repeated stem, or repeated chart feature must harm relatives.

[^101]: **Obscuring Qi is an asymmetric account.** The passage distinguishes the day combining toward the year from the year combining toward the day, with different alleged severity. It also adds distance and a five-position relation. Those details are not flattened into the usual unordered pair “Jia–Ji combines.” This account of combination as obscuring sits alongside earlier favorable accounts; the compilation has not been made artificially unanimous.

[^102]: **Day light, hour heavy.** S’s 日清 is read as 日輕, “the day is light,” supported by G598 and the contrast with a heavy hour. 休困 is normalized to rest and confinement. The childbirth, sex-of-child, slave-flight, and household judgments are preserved as historical claims. They are not clinically reliable pregnancy guidance, and the translation does not infer actual harm to any reader’s family from them.

[^103]: **Conflicting generalizations about year and course.** This concluding quotation says that the year controlling or clashing with the course is favorable, while the reverse is unfavorable. Earlier passages warn more broadly against their conflict. The difference is left visible rather than hidden behind one supposedly universal rule. It belongs to a collection of attributed methods whose application depends on what is being used and protected.

[^104]: **Four fifteen-day divisions.** Here 候 denotes four divisions of the sixty-day cycle, not the five-day meteorological hou defined earlier. Each consists of twelve Advancing days followed by Crossing, Retreating, and Hidden days. Jiazi, Jimao, Jiawu, and Jiyou mark their beginnings. The twelve named exceptional days fit the remainder of those four fifteen-day blocks. The text’s practical judgments are attached to that arrangement, not to every occurrence of the ordinary word “advance.”

[^105]: **A different advancing/retreating scheme.** The *Guangxin Collection* reads forward and backward movement in stems and branches, illustrating it with Gengxu meeting Jiayin or Yisi. The compiler objects that it accounts only for advance and retreat, not Crossing and Hidden Spirits. The two methods remain separate. Where G598’s online wording of the forward branch series is defective, S’s clear Zi–Chou–Yin–Mao sequence governs the translation.

[^106]: **Combination and the quoted Changes.** 合 is combination in the technical sense, with harmony as its explanatory image; it does not mean that every encounter between unlike polarities actually combines. The five specified stem pairs govern the discussion. S and G598 both attribute the whole sentence beginning 一陰一陽 to the *Changes*. The first clause is found in the [*Appended Statements*, first part](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/易傳/繫辭上); the appended judgment about one-sidedness is not part of that sentence there. The two should not be treated as one verbatim canonical quotation merely because this compilation presents them together. The translation preserves its attribution.

[^107]: **Seasonal marriage allegories.** The colors of flowers, fruit, and vegetation illustrate the relationships rather than establish botanical laws. S's 六月木旺木囚, “in the sixth month Wood flourishes and Wood is confined,” is corrected to 土旺木囚, “Earth flourishes and Wood is confined,” supported by G598 and the surrounding Earth-family argument. Both S and G598 have 當有赤 in the mulberry passage. Its incomplete-looking color contrast is retained, not expanded conjecturally into a description of red and black fruit.

[^108]: **Ethical names and conditional judgments.** 中正 and 仁義 invoke centrality and uprightness, humanity and righteousness. Their accompanying character descriptions are the text's moralized readings of combinations, not personality findings independently established for people born with them. The qualifying conditions—strength, Death and Extinction, and accompanying sha—remain part of each judgment. 不揚 means unimpressive or undistinguished in appearance here; the image-only glyph in S is not an omitted word.

[^109]: **Severe judgments and incompatible levels of polarity.** These passages retain the source's language concerning sexuality, status, and women rather than turning it into contemporary relationship advice. Ren is called 純陰之水, “pure yin Water,” in both S and G598, although Ren has already been classified as a yang stem. This may invoke Water's yin nature at another level, but the text does not explicitly reconcile the accounts. S names Ding–Ren 淫慝, rendered “Lust and Deceit”; G598 has 淫暱. 三合夭冶 is construed as a triple combination accompanying seductive beauty, not as an unqualified prediction of early death: 夭冶 here describes alluring appearance. 天中 is rendered Heavenly Void, with its technical rather than geographical meaning.

[^110]: **Six counted positions and two numerical arrangements.** From Jia through Ji, counting both endpoints, there are six positions; the same is true of every combining stem pair around the ten-stem cycle. Do not confuse these combinations with the generating/completing number pairs listed at the paragraph's end. Those pairs associate one with six, two with seven, and so on within the same phase; they are not themselves the five stem marriages. The intervening statement that Heaven's first number meets Earth's second appears in both S and G598 and is retained. No substitute numerical proof has been silently inserted. 精, 神, 魂, and 魄 are distinguished as essence, spirit, ethereal soul, and corporeal soul.

[^111]: **Repeated example and a supported repair.** S gives “one Ji combining with two Jia” and “two Jia combining with one Ji”: the same arrangement viewed from opposite ends, although the ensuing analogy contrasts too many wives with too many husbands. The translation does not change one example to two Ji. Later, S's defective 為而里福 is read as 為兩重福, “a double blessing,” supported by G598. The passage's asymmetry—Jia receiving Ji as Wealth, Ji receiving Jia as Officer—belongs to this particular argument; it does not establish that all yin-stem combinations are universally superior.

[^112]: **One ten-day round or two.** 旬 means one of the six ten-position divisions of the sexagenary cycle, not a month or a ten-year luck cycle. Jiaxu and Jimao occupy the same round, as do Jiachen and Jiyou; Jiazi and Jichou occupy different rounds, as do Jiawu and Jiwei. The text requires both stem and branch combination. Its ruler–minister and husband–wife comparisons explain the names; they do not remove the explicitly stated requirements of harmonious qi and freedom from damaging interactions.

[^113]: **Deriving the Tiger and governing the Dragon.** The “three” are the first three month positions, Yin–Mao–Chen: for a Jia or Ji year, Bingyin, Dingmao, Wuchen. Chen, the Dragon, supplies the stem identifying the transformation. The preceding Zi-to-Si series demonstrates stem combination by counting six positions inclusively. The thirty/twenty marriage analogy is an inherited normative explanation, not a biological proposition or a recommendation concerning marriage age. Keep the two counting operations separate.

[^114]: **Heavenly colors and control relationships.** S's 升天 in the Fire series is read as 丹天, Cinnabar Heaven, supported by G598 and the parallel color terms. Its 仍究然氣 is read with G598's 仍究煞氣, concerning the sha qi to be examined and controlled. “Mother,” “child,” “ghost,” and “wife” denote, respectively, the generating, generated, controlling, and controlled phase relative to the point of reference. “Governing the Dragon” is the monthly stem derivation illustrated immediately afterward, not a missing astronomical observation.

[^115]: **Jealous transformation.** The metaphor expresses an additional stem's preventing exclusive adherence to the proposed transformation. S's 妬花 is read as 妬化 and 成刑 as 成形, both supported by G598; 戌癸 in the list is the stem pair 戊癸, Wu–Gui, not Xu–Gui. The obstructing stems listed here do not agree in every instance with the later list of “jealous combinations.” Both lists are retained. The example of Ding–Ren with Gui and Zi is specifically said to fail genuine Wood transformation; it is not silently replaced by a modern rule under which abundant Water necessarily confirms it.

[^116]: **An explicit change of reference.** This account expressly takes the day stem as ruler of the transformation. Earlier year-centered methods in this juan should not therefore be reread as though they had always referred to a modern Day Master. Here the hour has a decisive role: month support without hour support is rejected, while hour support without month support can suffice. The stronger month requirements in the ensuing account remain visible as another formulation, not a silently harmonized universal rule.

[^117]: **Repairs in the five transformation rules.** In the Earth paragraph, G598 supports 己亥, Jihai, for S's 巳亥, and 甲乙寅卯, Jia–Yi–Yin–Mao, for S's 亥卯亥未 in the Wood-Officer list. In the Fire paragraph, G598 supports 寅午戌, Yin–Wu–Xu, for S's 丙午戍. The English marks these interventions. “Receiving Qi at Arrival at Office” is retained as the source's technical expression. Its Earth example uses Hai, consistent with the Earth-follows-Water sequence discussed earlier; the other examples also warn against forcing all five rules into one later standardized table. The “fourth position” counts Birth itself as first. “Encountered doubly” is not expanded into a more precise condition than the transmitted wording supplies.

[^118]: **Transformation is not merely the presence of a combining pair.** These paragraphs add particular hours, months, support, and prohibitions. The Wuchen, Gengchen, Renchen, Jiachen, and Bingchen hours are the Dragon hours appropriate to the corresponding day-stem groups. The Earth account's objection to Fire, despite the preceding account's use of Wu–Gui Fire as blessing, and the Metal account's fear of Zi and Yin are transmitted distinctions. They have not been rewritten into one smoother doctrine. “Other months welcome Bing” is also retained rather than generalized beyond its immediate Earth context.

[^119]: **The sixteen pairs of proper transformation.** The table preserves all sixteen examples and their order. S's impossible 丑丑 in the third row is corrected to 己丑, Jichou, supported by U and by the Jia–Ji combination with adjoining branches. U has its own defects elsewhere in this same list; it is a supporting witness, not a replacement base text. The English labels “first pair” and “second pair” are presentation aids, not an additional classification in the source.

[^120]: **Corner transformations and transformation beneath the seat.** “Advancing” and “retreating” describe the order of the branch positions within these examples, not the direction of the person's entire major luck sequence. 座下／坐下 refers to the branch beneath a stem: the combining partner is sought in the relevant Lu position within that branch, as the Renwu and Dinghai explanations show. This is a separate account from the preceding requirement that both combining stems be visibly present. The six named examples and their differing judgments are all retained.

[^121]: **How to read the monthly tables.** The four three-month tables reproduce S's arrangement, giving ten rows across twelve months: 120 positions in all, including the unfilled position. The introductory reading instructions are editorial. The month numbers refer to the successive seasonal terms in this calendrical framework, not fixed Gregorian months. 化, 形, 象, 秀, and 器 have not all been translated as “transformation”: the differences between transformation, form, image, refinement, and a completed useful vessel may matter. The table genuinely assigns such results as Ding–Ren transforming into Fire and Yi–Geng into Wood. Correcting these merely to match the conventional five stem transformations would erase what this particular table says.

[^122]: **The table's textual problems.** The ninth-month Jia–Ji entry in S is defective, 甲甲巳化; G598 has 甲己化土, supporting “[Transforms into Earth].” S leaves the eighth-month Chen–Xu–Chou–Wei result blank; the dash marks that blank, not “no effect.” G598 supplies 正位 there, but this has not been imported. Other differences are also left visible: S gives Wood transformation for Hai–Mao–Wei in month three where G598 has no transformation; S gives 純形 for Si–You–Chou in month two where G598 has 成形; S gives 辛苦 for that group in month five where G598 has 化金. S's fifth-month 化容 and third-month 成無信 are translated conservatively, not converted into a reconstructed technical rule. Obvious 戍／戌 and 己／巳 confusions in the fixed branch groups are normalized.

[^123]: **Branch polarity and seasonal line counts are different.** Chou is a yin branch in the alternating stem–branch classification but belongs to the season's two-yang-line stage; there is no necessary contradiction between those statements. S's 二六成九 is repaired to 三六成九, and 申為二陰 to 申為三陰, both supported by G598 and the stated totals. The description of Wei as “two yang,” however, occurs in both witnesses and remains in the translation; in the waxing-yin sequence following Wu, two yin would be expected. These distinctions prevent an arithmetic repair from becoming an unacknowledged rewriting of the system.

[^124]: **An obscure explanation of celestial meetings.** S's phrases 謂之會劣謂之集, 隔液, and 此生萬轉圖而放 are problematic. G598's online text preserves essentially the same difficulties rather than resolving them. The English gives a provisional close rendering and marks uncertainty; “a lesser meeting” is not identified with a specific modern astronomical kind of conjunction. G598 initially has 壬癸 where S has 壬亥, but its ensuing monthly examples repeatedly have 壬亥 too. S is therefore retained. The twelve month–station correspondences are complete; the obscure explanation has not been used to manufacture an astronomical diagram absent from this source.

[^125]: **Ritual music and a faulty citation.** Compare the [*Rites of Zhou*, Spring Officials, “Duties of the Grand Director of Music”](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/周禮/春官宗伯#大司樂之職). Its office is 大司樂, whereas S has 太師樂. Its dance for the four-quarter sacrifices is 大韶, *Da Shao*, whereas S has 大罄, preserved here as *Daqing*. The *Rites* also uses 函鐘 and 小呂 where this compilation uses 林鍾 and 仲呂. The table translates this work's citation, not a silently substituted version of the *Rites*. The paired pitches are not Western equal-tempered notes; their branch assignments supply the argument for the Six Combinations.

[^126]: **Combinations through Lu and through absence.** The *Extensive Record* first pairs the Lu seats of combining stems: Yin–Wu for Jia–Ji, Zi–Si for Gui–Wu, Si–You for Bing–Xin, Mao–Shen for Yi–Geng, and Hai–Wu for Ren–Ding. Those branch pairs are not simply another list of the ordinary Six Combinations. The following absent-Lu, absent-Horse, and absent-Noble-Person examples describe drawing in an unseen position by combination. S's 甲戍庚 is repaired to 甲戊庚, Jia–Wu–Geng, supported by G598 and the fact that this is a list of stems. The text's praise of the unseen does not make the missing position an actually present pillar.

[^127]: **The order of a combination matters in this account.** These twelve judgments explicitly distinguish, for example, Zi obtaining Chou from Chou obtaining Zi. An unordered pair chart would lose that distinction. 身, 命, and 祿 in the Jiawu–Xinwei example also retain the older threefold vocabulary of body, fate, and Lu; “body” must not automatically be read as day-stem strength. The final gender-specific prohibitions are reported as this authority's rules, alongside—not in replacement of—the qualifying judgments elsewhere in the juan.

[^128]: **Repairs in the triple-combination argument.** The four initial ke positions divide the hundred-ke cycle into quarters; they are not four modern clock hours. S's 寅午戊 is Yin–Wu–Xu, 寅午戌. In the body analogy, G598 supports 精乃氣之元氣乃神之本, “essence is the origin of qi, and qi the root of spirit,” against S's defective 精乃天之元氣乃神之本. It also supports 辰乃水庫 for S's 辰乃水氣 and 申子辰 for S's repeated 申子申. The explanation combines a parent–child analogy with the Birth–Flourishing–Storehouse sequence. It should not be forced into a literal assertion that ordinary Earth generates ordinary Water.

[^129]: **A complete configuration and its relation to the ruler.** This passage explicitly requires all three branch characters for a transformed triple configuration. It does not thereby supply a universal rule for every partial or hidden combination mentioned elsewhere. For Fire people, the Wood configuration is Seal, Metal Wealth, Water Officer, and Earth the generated or “Hurting” configuration. These are broad relational groupings here, not a full differentiation of every yin and yang Ten-God relationship. The Three-Position Lu examples center on Bing's Si and Ding's Wu. “Plucking cassia in the moon” is the passage's literary image for eminent attainment, especially examination success.

[^130]: **Combination does not override the rest of the chart.** The favorable opening description is immediately restricted by generation, control, strength, accompanying influences, and configuration. The text even says that alarm and entanglement in one controlling combination do not amount to deep calamity. That qualification is preserved. 官符, rendered Official Talisman, is a named influence, not another spelling of Direct Officer; 元辰, Great Consumption, and Heavenly Void likewise remain distinct names. The severe judgments about women's conduct belong to the historical text, not to a translator's assessment of actual people.

[^131]: **Hidden stems expand the meaning of combination.** You and Yin are not an ordinary Six-Combination pair. The cited passage instead directs attention to Xin inside You and Bing inside Yin—the Bing–Xin stem combination—and to the condition of Si. Its exact phrasing about Si being damaged is terse, so the translation does not invent a complete modern eligibility test. The six concluding expressions are parallel descriptions of these connections: they are not six newly calculated stars. 行藏 refers to entering service or withdrawing from it, and is rendered accordingly.

[^132]: **General Star and Canopy are derived positions here.** For Shen–Zi–Chen, the middle position Zi is General Star and the Storehouse Chen is Canopy; for Si–You–Chou they are You and Chou; for Yin–Wu–Xu, Wu and Xu; for Hai–Mao–Wei, Mao and Wei. This is the derivation stated in the text. Its comparison with the canopy over the Great Emperor's celestial seat does not make the calculated branch indicator a measured planet. The source itself explains why these two discussions follow triple combinations rather than the larger collection of shensha in juan three.

[^133]: **Rank, Seal, and the Storehouse.** 出將入相 means going out to command armies and entering court as a chief minister. The following ranks descend through the “two councils,” departmental officers, and ordinary appointments. Their precise institutional equivalence varies with period; they have not been converted into modern civil-service titles. 正印, Canopy, Storehouse/Tomb, and 夾貴 are not treated as interchangeable, even where the compressed wording leaves their overlap difficult to resolve. The surprising praise of Loss Spirit with General Star is retained. 節印 evokes the insignia and seal of delegated command, not merely good fortune in an abstract sense.

[^134]: **The social and religious imagery of Canopy.** 孽子 and 庶出 concern descent outside the principal marital line; 過房 concerns transfer into another family branch; 填房 is a successor wife; 入贅 is a husband joining his wife's household. These are not all synonyms for orphanhood. 闕口, literally “a missing mouth,” remains obscure in this compressed verse and is not assigned a definite medical diagnosis. Furnace and tongs, the broad hat, and black or yellow robes evoke specialized work and religious life. The translation preserves the images rather than replacing the whole passage with “spiritual talent.”

[^135]: **The Canopy authorities disagree.** A general warning against Metal and Wood stands beside the explicitly auspicious Repeated Metal, Repeated Canopy configuration. The closing account also makes material wealth unfavorable while allowing distinction through Flanking the Noble Person. These are preserved as attributed teachings with different qualifications; no single modern “Canopy personality” has replaced them. The claims about losing children, harming a wife, or not bearing children are the source's historical prognostications, not conclusions about any living reader.

[^136]: **The opening restriction on Salty Pool matters.** The small-character note expressly objects to deriving this sha from the triple-combination branches alone. It asks for the relevant heavenly-stem/nayin affiliation to agree with the kind under consideration, illustrated by Fire with Yin–Wu–Xu. The wording 天干納音於地同類 is compressed; it is translated without inventing a more elaborate algorithm. Whatever its precise application, omitting it would make the passage appear to teach a broader and simpler rule than it actually does.

[^137]: **Salty Pool and the solar myth.** The quotation attributed here to the *Huainanzi* does not match the sequence in its [“Patterns of Heaven”](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/淮南子/天文訓): there the sun bathes in Salty Pool before ascending Fusang, and sets at the Yu Abyss. The present work's sunset explanation is nevertheless retained as its own explanation, not silently corrected by replacing the quotation. 咸池 is translated Salty Pool, while 桃花煞 is Peach Blossom Sha. The four positions listed here are the Bathing stages of the four phase sequences; they are not four measured celestial pools or stars.

[^138]: **Gambling, the authority's name, and the influence entering a fate.** G598 supports 賭博, gambling, against the rare-glyph form following 賭 in S; 沈芝, Shen Zhi, against S's 沈足; and 此神入命, “this influence enters a fate,” against S's 此人入命. The substantive repairs are indicated in the English or explained here. The severe sexual and social judgments are retained, including the unexpected favorable livelihood possibilities. 癆瘵 is rendered “wasting illness,” not assigned an unqualified modern diagnosis. The reference to Boyi's hunger at Shouyang is an exemplum in the cited teaching, not independently verified evidence for Boyi's natal chart.

[^139]: **How harms are being explained.** This section offers more than one account: conflict between the phases, and hostility arising through a partner's combination with the opposing branch. S's 未旺土亥子旺水 is read with G598's 未旺土害子旺水: flourishing Wei Earth harms Zi Water. 亥 is therefore not an additional Hai branch in the Zi–Wei rule. “Dead Metal” at Chou and “dead Earth” at Chen belong to the transmitted contrast between active and inactive qi; the translation does not replace them with standardized twelve-stage labels merely because a different table would use another name.

[^140]: **“True” Metal and Fire are not these pairs' nayin.** In the Yiyou–Wuxu example, Yi is called true Metal and Wu true Fire in connection with the stem-transformation affiliations Yi–Geng and Wu–Gui. Ordinary Yi is Wood and ordinary Wu Earth; Yiyou's nayin is Water and Wuxu's Wood. Collapsing these levels would make the passage unintelligible. Its You-to-Xu harm is also expressly asymmetrical: the converse is said to bring no calamity. The additional qualifications concerning a stem's “true ghost” and controlling nayin are retained rather than reducing every harm to a branch-only prohibition.

[^141]: **Combining with the branch that clashes against me.** Zi fears Wu, and Wei combines with Wu; hence Wei is said to harm Zi. The other five examples repeat that construction. 六親, “six relations,” denotes the familial relationships under consideration, not necessarily six individually identifiable relatives. The line “pierce straight through the heart” is the source's violent image for the Zi–Wei interaction; it has not been turned into a medical diagnosis or an assertion that the branch pair predicts a particular injury.

[^142]: **Body imagery, separation, and the final qualification.** “Five Peaks” and “two barriers” appear here as bodily images beside the explicit four limbs; the terse verse does not establish a precise clinical interpretation. “Separated like Shen and Shang” preserves 參商, the conventional image of stars that do not meet, rather than treating the words as additional natal branches. 身命宮 names Body and Life Palaces, not automatically the day stem. The conclusion is emphatic: a distinguished configuration does not, in this authority's view, cancel a harmful feature. Its final ranking—day and hour first, Body and Life Palaces next—is retained.

[^143]: **Deriving punishment from combination.** G598 supports 三合, triple combinations, against S's 二合, and 卯刑子, “Mao punishes Zi,” against S's 卯寅子. The four ordered overlays are all retained, including the four self-punishments they produce. They align the phase's triple-combination group with a three-branch seasonal direction; they do not say that each group is located entirely in that direction. The opening quotation about kindness and harm ends before the compiler's derivation of the Three Punishments; G598's modern punctuation is not independent evidence for extending the quotation.

[^144]: **Ten is counted inclusively and in a specified direction.** Mao forward to Zi and Zi backward to Mao each give ten positions when both endpoints are counted. Yin backward to Si, Si backward to Shen, Chou forward to Xu, and Xu forward to Wei do likewise. These are the directions stated by this explanation, not a rule that every tenth branch in either direction has the same effect. 皇極中天 is retained as the cited expression, without assigning it an author or identifying an independently established book. The numerical explanation complements, rather than replaces, the preceding overlay of triple combinations.

[^145]: **Without Gratitude: two explanations, not one pedigree.** The first explanation uses stem relations and a marriage metaphor: Jia's mother is Gui, whose husband Wu becomes the father harmed by Jia. The second uses the phase's Birth places and control relations. Earth lodging Birth at Si is one of the Earth sequences already discussed in the juan. Neither explanation should be silently substituted for the other. The account's unfavorable childbirth prognostications stand beside its favorable description of chastity and uprightness; that tension remains in the translation. These are historical judgments, not medical advice or conclusions about an individual pregnancy.

[^146]: **Reliance on Power and the six Jia/six Gui.** The paragraph shifts from flourishing Water against entombed Fire to the honor of 六甲 and the low place of 六癸, then offers another explanation through concealed Fire, Water, Metal, and Wood. The compressed reference to the six Jia and six Gui is retained without inventing a correspondence table that the passage does not supply. “The form is exposed, thin, and small” closely follows S's 刑露痩小, taking 刑 as the graphic confusion for 形; it is not an additional anatomical judgment beyond the source. Noble and low configurations receive different moral and practical descriptions.

[^147]: **Without Propriety and the husband-star argument.** In the second explanation, Gui seeks Wu as husband and Yi seeks Geng. The husband, rather than the branch's own dominant phase alone, is then examined at Mao or Zi. This is why the two clauses refer to Wu's Defeat and Geng's Death. The first explanation instead treats Water as mother and Wood as child. The two accounts are kept separate. “Like Wu and Yue” renders the image of close relatives becoming estranged as hostile polities; it does not name an additional pair of natal indicators.

[^148]: **Self-Punishment and excess.** The four self-punishing branches first appear as positions left outside the other punishment relationships; a second account explains them through excess of their own qi. In particular, Hai's “flourishing, it rots” is retained rather than changed into a more familiar description of Water. The passage's self-inflicted harm is its account of an adverse configuration, not an attribution of intention to a real person. The image of seeing fish deep in the water expresses excessively searching scrutiny here, not a newly introduced divinatory technique.

[^149]: **Why three when four kinds are named?** The source itself raises the discrepancy. Its explanation that each four-member group lacks one is not fully consistent with its immediately preceding observation that both Wu and You remain in the Zi–Wu–Mao–You group. The translation leaves that difficulty visible. More importantly, the *Comprehensive View of the Three Vehicles* reverses the earlier labels: Yin–Si–Shen becomes Reliance on Power, and Chou–Xu–Wei Without Gratitude. The compiler accepts that explanation as intelligible. Standardizing the names throughout would suppress this recorded disagreement.

[^150]: **Punishment can be useful, but the quotation is partly obscure.** The section expressly forbids judging punishment as automatically ominous: the supporting configuration and influences are decisive. Its gentleman/petty-person contrast belongs to the source's moral and status vocabulary, not a translator's classification of readers. Huzhongzi's 九命, rendered “nine fates,” is difficult and also occurs in G598. S has 而馭雜, rendered closely as “to govern their mixture”; G598 instead has 而駁雜, “and [they] are mixed or impure.” Neither witness resolves the entire sentence, so no complete reconstructed maxim is substituted for it.

[^151]: **Self-Punishment's warnings and their limits.** The source differentiates all four self-punishing branches appearing together under auspicious control from a branch being repeated; these are not collapsed into one rule. The verse's 年月刑膚, “year and month punish the flesh,” is retained: G598 and U also have this wording. “Self-Punishment fears Fire” is immediately qualified by the possibility that Fire beneficially controls over-rigid You Metal. The Chen–Chen and Hai–Hai lines are terse; their source glosses remain attached. S's 木落由喪 is rendered “Wood falling comes from loss,” while G598 has 木落由衰, “from decline.” The warning of middle-age loss of sight is translated as a historical claim, not as a reliable medical prediction.

[^152]: **Who punishes whom?** This account distinguishes Officer and Seal acting upon the fate from the fate acting upon Officer and Seal. G598 supports 官印受刑, “Officer and Seal receive punishment,” for S's repeated 官刑受刑; the supplied Seal is marked in the English. Jiazi–Jimao and Bingyin–Xinsi combine stem relationships with the relevant branch punishments, and must not be reduced to a prohibition against all Zi–Mao or Yin–Si encounters. The Phoenix Pool is an image of distinguished court service; it has not been substituted with a precise modern office.

[^153]: **Penetration is assessed through the whole stem–branch pair.** Here the year is explicitly host and the other three pillars guests. The examples use nayin: Yichou Metal and Gengxu Metal are the same kind; Bingxu Earth generates Metal, and Renxu Water is generated by Metal; Yichou Metal can control Wuxu Wood but cannot control Jiaxu Fire. “Penetrates” therefore translates 刑得入, not an additional physical injury. The subsequent qualification concerning a place where blessing gathers remains essential: a branch-only table, or a comparison of ordinary stems alone, would not reproduce this argument.

[^154]: **Tai Sui here means the natal year's authority.** The example begins with a person born in Guisi, not an unspecified currently transiting year. Guisi's nayin Water is controlled by Wuyin Earth; Gengshen's nayin Wood then controls that Earth. Reading Gengshen only as ordinary Metal would reverse the explanation. This is the stated sense of “using sha to stop sha.” The following song adds equality of force, the Three Wonders, Heaven's One Noble Person, and Heavenly Virtue; these supporting conditions are not omitted in favor of the mere presence of three punishments.

[^155]: **The repeated indispensable punishment.** S's 或假或真真刑不可 is repaired with G598's 或假或真非刑不可, preserving the eightfold parallel refrain “punishment is indispensable.” This is an attested repair, not a free completion of the rhythm. 假 and 真 remain provisional and genuine; the terse statement does not identify exactly which configuration is provisional. The next sentence allows another branch to punish an excessive instrument of harm. Its distinction between heaviness, refinement, and extraordinariness is preserved without converting it into numerical weights or a scoring system.

[^156]: **A third interpretation of “Three Punishments.”** The small-character note interprets the three as upward punishment, downward punishment, and self-punishment. In the Chou–Xu example it names Wei as the upward position, and in the Yin–Si example Shen. Both unexpected names also occur in G598; neither has been changed merely to make them resemble the preceding Zi–Mao example. The note's assertion that this explanation is better belongs to the source commentary, not to the translator's endorsement of one system over another.

[^157]: **The seventh position and the character 皂.** Counting both endpoints gives seven from Zi to Wu and from Jia to Geng. The former is a branch clash, the latter a controlling stem relation; their shared count is the source's explanation, not identity of the two operations. G598 supports 從七，本此, “incorporates seven, following this,” for S's 從七木此. The ensuing derivation of 皂, black, is translated as this work's character-based analogy, not certified as historical etymology. The two phrases attributed to the *Changes* invoke return after seven days; they are not converted into a seven-day prediction for every natal clash.

[^158]: **Clashes with generation or control, and the four Storehouses.** G598 and U support 庚寅木, Gengyin Wood, for S's 庚寅才. G598 supports 辰戌丑未 for S's defective 戌丑未未 in the Storehouse list. The examples Xinsi Metal–Guihai Water and Renshen Metal–Gengyin Wood concern nayin relationships alongside opposing branches. The same-kind examples Jiazi–Jiawu and Jimao–Jiyou share nayin as well as a stem. The source's praise of complete four-branch groups and of stirring the Storehouses is conditional within its argument; it has not become a universal instruction to welcome every clash.

[^159]: **Breaking calamity is not the same as breaking blessing.** The outcome depends on what is struck, with the text distinguishing a place where calamity gathers, one where blessing gathers, and a position in Void. S's 不位 in the Void sentence is read as 他位, “another position,” supported by U and the preceding parallel clauses; G598 has 下位 there. The specified month/day/hour/year relations in Enmity Sha are retained, not extended to every pair of natal pillars. The source also permits official distinction to coexist with an unfavorable health judgment; the latter is translated, not asserted as medically reliable.

[^160]: **“Me” is expressly the useful influence.** In this quotation, 用神 is the influence being put to use and 閒神 the idle or uninvolved influence. “Me” is not automatically the birth-year stem, the day stem, or the person considered in isolation. The text asks four questions: whether each side has qi and whether each has support. Preserving those questions prevents the maxim from being reduced to “clash the bad branch, protect the good branch” without examining their circumstances.

[^161]: **The five kinds of breaking.** The Seal example retains the older nayin terminology: Guiwei Wood is called Direct Seal in juan one. It is therefore not silently changed into Water merely because Water is the mother of ordinary Wood in a later day-stem reading. Yichou Metal controls Guiwei Wood while Chou clashes with Wei. The Wealth, Lu, and Horse examples each state their own reference. In Breaking Combination, S's 己 is read as 巳, Si, supported by G598 and U: Si clashes with Jihai's Hai, while Zi clashes with Jiawu's Wu. This restores the stated branch-breaking mechanism without changing Jia–Ji's stem combination.

[^162]: **The closing poems and the final warning against a single rule.** S's 一切 in the third poem is repaired to 一七, “One–Seven,” supported by G598 and the section's repeated name for a clash. The annotations identifying Tan and Wu as Greedy Wolf and Martial Song are part of the source and remain in angled brackets. The military and court images are translated as poetry, not converted into modern career advice. All four poems are retained, including the unfavorable final one. The juan closes by expressly rejecting a single blanket judgment of clashes and breaking.
