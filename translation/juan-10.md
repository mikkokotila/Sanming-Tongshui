# Sanming Tonghui · Juan Ten
## A Comprehensive Gathering of the Three Fates

<!-- source: L14-L20 -->
**Attributed to Wan Minying of the Ming dynasty.** From the imperially commissioned *Siku Quanshu*.[^01]

*Reading conventions.* Capitalized terms such as Wealth, Officer, Seal, Food God, Killings, Lu, Birth, and Extinction retain their technical meanings from the preceding juan. “Body” commonly means the day stem and its capacity to sustain the chart. “Root” and “master” distinguish the year and day where the text makes that distinction. **〈 〉** marks the source’s small-character commentary; square brackets mark supplied readings or explicit repairs. The three treatises, their quoted authorities, and their different analytical frameworks are not silently reconciled. Predictions concerning health, death, character, and family relationships are historical claims, not established findings about living people. Section numbers and paragraph divisions are editorial.[^02]

## 1. Formulas for Reading a Fate
### 看命口訣

<!-- source: L22-L22 -->
In reading a fate, first examine whether the monthly branch contains Wealth or Officer; only then consider the rest. The monthly command constitutes the fate. For the month, take the branch spirit; for the year, the heavenly stem; for the day, the heavenly stem; for the passing year, the heavenly stem; for the major luck course, the branch spirit. The year is the root, the day the master. If the month contains Direct Officer or Indirect Officer while the hour enters another configuration, take only what is in the month: the other configuration is not employed. Only when the monthly command offers nothing usable should another configuration be examined.[^03]

〈An old song says:〉

> Three palaces bearing configurations are hard to distinguish in their mixture;  
> Who knows which is the source of distinction?  
> Let all three palaces bear configurations as they may:  
> One must still take the governing thread.

The monthly command is taken from the earthly branch. With Officer Star, for example, the finest condition is its manifestation above and below, in stem and branch. If it emerges in the stem but not in the branch, this indicates intelligence and refinement. The year and hour must not clash with the monthly branch; a clash from the daily branch itself does not obstruct. When the major course or Lord of the Year comes to clash with the monthly branch, however, calamity follows.

One Direct Officer denotes a gentleman of standing: substantial, pure, upright, incorruptible, and clear-sighted. Seal in the year or hour makes it finer still. A multiplicity instead indicates alternating accomplishment and defeat; four positions of pure Officer give only an empty name in official life. One Seven Killings gives intelligence and quick understanding; two or three give clarity first and turbidity afterward. Four pillars of pure Killings give distinction with restraint, poverty without it. One Wealth must above all obtain its season: wealth, distinction, and the founding of a household follow, with a restless, urgent temperament. With two, this temper is reduced by half. Three or four drain qi and weaken the body. If the body is exceedingly strong, establishment remains possible; if weak, there is toil and hardship. As for Seal, one, two, or four positions are all good; the configuration should not contain Wealth that damages Seal.

During a good luck course, a day stem injuring the passing year’s stem brings lighter calamity; during a bad course the same injury brings heavier calamity. If development has already run its course, death follows. Chen, Xu, Chou, and Wei each retain three parts of residual qi. Thus, on passing from Wu luck into Wei, three parts of Fire remain; on passing from Zi into Chou, three parts of Water remain. They cannot all be treated simply as Earth. In a Yang-Blade configuration, clashes and combinations in year and luck are especially feared. The Tai Sui stem combining with the day or hour stem is **Obscuring-Qi Sha**. The day or hour stem and branch recurring in the passing year is **Turning-the-Heel Sha**—for example, a Gengshen day meeting a Gengshen or Gengyin Tai Sui. In its lighter form it means distant removal; in its heavier form, the destruction of the house and loss of wealth.[^04]

Auspicious spirits in year, month, and day should be led by the hour into Birth and flourishing; inauspicious spirits should be led into the region of restraint. If the hour itself carries an auspicious or inauspicious spirit, the year, month, and day should likewise generate the auspicious and restrain the inauspicious. A useful spirit in the month brings strength from the ancestors; one in the hour brings strength from descendants. Reverse these conditions, and the contrary follows.

<!-- source: L24-L24 -->
In reading a fate, use the day stem as Heavenly Origin: thus the stem is Lu. Use the daily and monthly branches as Earthly Origin: thus the branch is Fate. A Ren or Gui day in Jiwei month, with Wealth and Officer manifest in stems and branches, is an example. With Wealth and Officer, distinguish what is originally present from what is originally absent. If they are originally in the earthly branches but do not emerge in the heavenly stems, these are “not considered.” If the earthly branches lack them and they appear only in the heavenly stems, even a good course cannot accomplish the matter.[^05]

For the passing year’s Lord, use Heavenly Origin alone. For the luck course, although the earthly branch receives greater weight, its Heavenly Origin must also be examined. If Officer or Indirect Officer in the natal pillars has been restrained excessively, Officer or Killings appearing in the course’s stem can bring development. Even when its branch has no Wealth, a Wealth stem can bring blessing; even when its branch has no Killings, a Killings stem can bring calamity.

The birth month is the **Origin of Luck**; most feared is a clash from both major course and Lord of the Year. The natal Officer Star is the **Origin of Lu**; most feared is its destruction by clash. A Ding-day person, for example, takes Ren as Officer. Born in Hai month, the person has Ren within Hai as Ding’s Lu; Si in year or hour clashes and destroys this Lu-Origin. The natal Wealth Star is the **Origin of Horse**; most feared is its robbery. A Geng-day person takes Jia and Yi Wood as Wealth. In Yin or Mao month, Jia in Yin is Indirect Wealth and Yi in Mao Direct Wealth. Xin in year or hour creates contention and seizure. Years and luck courses are judged in the same way.

Officer for the day appearing in the year stem gives the deepest blessing; Seven Killings for the day appearing there cannot be removed throughout life. Officer Star is Lu and Wealth Star is Horse. Officer luck develops office; Wealth luck develops wealth. Neither is dispensable, and each has its own use. Wealth and Officer in year and month indicate birth into a rich and distinguished family, with a foundation laid by father and grandfather. If Officer-and-Lu luck comes while young, appointment in childhood and early achievement are common. If year and month lack Wealth and Officer but day and hour possess them, the person establishes himself.

Wealth and Officer are the foundation of a fate; either one in the pillars can bring blessing. Even with no original Officer and no other configuration, much Wealth in the stems and branches of year, month, day, and hour, followed by flourishing-Wealth luck, can bring achievement and reputation. Flourishing Wealth can itself generate Officer—but only a strong body permits this judgment. No Wealth or Officer in year and month, together with bad luck in childhood, commonly means humble origins, the breaking of ancestral property, injury to the father, and no ready-made blessing to enjoy. Mixed Officer and Killings, with repeated Hurting Officer and combining spirits, lead a man into absorption in wine and sex; a woman encountering them marries without a matchmaker.

<!-- source: L26-L26 -->
In reading a fate, take the day stem alone as master and take as the fate what the governing thread employs. If the monthly command employs Metal, Wood, Water, Fire, or Earth, select the one relevant thing. Examine closely its position before or after the solar term, its weight, the depth of its qi, the formation of its configuration, and its breaking or clash. Officer, Seal, Wealth, Killings, Food God, and Hurting Officer: by these six methods weigh increase and decline. Encountering Officer, examine Wealth; encountering Killings, examine Seal; encountering Seal, examine Officer. Take the four without partiality or imbalance. Generation, control, restraint, and transformation make the upper judgment; breaking, harm, Rest, and Imprisonment make the lower. In luck, generation and removal can bring blessing; assistance and stripping can bring calamity.[^06]

Some configurations are formed by combinations among the branches of year, day, and hour, yet even these employ the monthly command. If the month employs Metal, use Metal alone; if Fire, Fire alone. Of the eighteen configurations, these six carry the greatest weight. Use mutual generation to establish the pattern and complete the configuration; then examine year, day, and hour for weight and depth. If Officer is encountered and Seal employed, Killings need not be feared: Killings gathers to Seal, and Seal gathers to the body, so it remains an upper configuration. Encountering Seal, examine Killings. With Officer or Killings in the fate, a course through their country can also be judged distinguished. The monthly command communicating with Officer and Wealth appearing in the pillars give flourishing Wealth Generating Officer: wealth and distinction. Wealth in the pillars calls for flourishing-Wealth luck to develop blessing. But once Killings appears, give precedence to Killings and do not employ Wealth. Flourishing-Wealth luck would then generate and join the Killings faction, and the judgment becomes poverty and low station. In every configuration, Killings takes precedence.

<!-- source: L28-L28 -->
First examine whether the stem spirits are controlled or restrained, whether the branch spirits are punished or clashed, and whether the stems, branches, and nayin fight or submit. Jia takes Yin as Lu: what stem stands above Yin? Jia takes Xin as Officer: what branch does Xin obtain? When stems do not encroach upon branches, Heaven keeps its dignity. When branches do not offend stems, Earth keeps its lowly place. When the Five Phases do not injure one another, the human order is harmonious. When the four seasonal beginnings do not harm one another, Horse can gallop. If stems encroach and branches offend, and the Five Phases injure one another, distinguish whether master and root have qi, have use, have rescue, and form a configuration. Within the interweaving stems and branches and the transformations of the Five Phases lies the work of creation.[^07]

〈Li Chunfeng says:〉 When the Five Phases are at Birth and flourishing, examine the coming and going of blessing qi; when they are at Death and Extinction, look to rescue by auspicious spirits. If the Five Phases obtain their ground and the nayin generate one another, honor comes even without the aid of auspicious spirits. If the Five Phases lack qi and the nayin obstruct one another, auspicious spirits are of no use even when present.

<!-- source: L30-L30 -->
Heavenly Origin welcomes Lu in Earthly Origin. Thus Jia–Ji welcome the four seasonal endings; Yi–Geng, Shen and You; Bing–Xin, Hai and Zi; Ding–Ren, Yin and Mao; Wu–Gui, Si and Wu. Earthly Origin welcomes combination with Heavenly Origin: Zi and Chou welcome Wu; Yin, Ji; Mao and Chen, Geng; Si, Xin and Gui; Wu and Wei, Jia and Ren; Shen, Yi; You and Xu, Bing; Hai, Ding. When both Heavenly and Earthly Origins possess these, blessing is lofty throughout life; when neither does, name and profit come to nothing. Damage to Heavenly Origin makes achievement difficult before thirty-nine; damage to Earthly Origin makes blessing from forty onward inferior to what preceded it. Refined Heavenly Origin seated on Lu—Gui obtaining Zi or Jia obtaining Yin, for example—gives wealth if not distinction. Earthly Origin dislikes control from Heavenly Origin: Zi and Chou fear Ji; Yin, Geng; Mao and Chen, Xin; Si, Jia and Ren; Wu and Wei, Yi and Gui; Shen, Bing; You and Xu, Ding; Hai, Wu. Yet examine the particular likes and dislikes; do not cling rigidly to this.[^08]

<!-- source: L32-L32 -->
In a fate, take stem, branch, and nayin of the same kind. Renzi and Renwu are True Wood; Jiyou and Jimao, True Earth; Bingzi and Bingwu, True Water; Wuzi and Wuwu, True Fire; Yichou, Yiwei, Gengchen, and Gengxu, True Metal. Yiyou day with Gengchen hour is Refined Metal; Dingsi day with Bingwu hour, Refined Fire; Guihai day with Renzi hour, Refined Water; Jichou day with Wuchen hour, Refined Earth; Jiayin day with Dingmao hour, Refined Wood. All these indicate wealth and distinction. A Fire person with a Bing day and Xin hour, or Xin day and Bing hour; a Wood person with Jia day and Ji hour, or Ji day and Jia hour; an Earth person with Wu day and Gui hour, or Gui day and Wu hour; a Water person with Ren day and Ding hour, or Ding day and Ren hour; a Metal person with Geng day and Yi hour, or Yi day and Geng hour—although these constitute True Nobility of the Five Phases, repeated occurrences diminish blessing.[^09]

<!-- source: L34-L34 -->
Take also the reciprocal interchange of the Five Phases’ true qi. A Xinhai Metal person obtaining Dingsi Earth has Ding–Ren combining as True Wood and coming and going between them, and Bing–Xin combining as True Water and coming and going between them. A Dingsi Earth person obtaining Guihai Water has Wu–Gui combining as True Fire and Ding–Ren combining as True Wood in reciprocal passage. Thus **Wuxu / Guihai / Dingsi / Xinhai**, with the interchange of true qi complete, is a chancellor’s fate. Wuwu Fire obtaining Renzi Wood contains Ding–Ren True Wood and Wu–Gui True Fire. Bingshen Fire obtaining Yiyou Water contains Bing–Xin True Water and Yi–Geng True Metal. Gengyin obtaining Jimao Earth contains Jia–Ji True Earth and Yi–Geng True Metal. Thus **Gengyin / Jimao / Gengyin / Jimao**, with reciprocal interchange complete, is a fate for the Two Councils.[^10]

<!-- source: L36-L36 -->
First consider transformed qi. The *Discourse on the Five Circuits* takes Jia, Bing, Wu, Geng, and Ren combining with the five yin stems as excess; Yi, Ding, Ji, Xin, and Gui combining with the five yang stems as insufficiency. Between excess and insufficiency lies the power of adjustment. The *Book of the Transformations of Heavenly Origin* further distinguishes day and night. The six Jia are Wood when born by day and transform into Earth by night. Thus a person of the six Wu obtaining Jia takes it as ghost in a daytime birth and employs it as Officer in a nighttime birth. The six Yi employ Metal by day and Wood by night; thus a person of the six Ji encountering Yi takes it as Officer by day and ghost by night. Only the six Ji and six Geng do not change.[^11]

Accordingly, the five yang stems born by day are considered in their original substance, by night in transformation; the five yin stems born by night are considered in their original substance, by day in transformation. Among the “six kinds” of yang fate, a man encountering Lu-ghost or Inverted Food should be taken as born at night to turn the inauspicious into the auspicious: call ghost Officer and Inverted Food the Joy Spirit. Yet daytime birth is called the forward order. Among the “six kinds” of yin fate, a man encountering Lu-ghost or Inverted Food should be taken as born [by day] to reverse the inauspicious into the auspicious; the remaining rules are as before, but nighttime birth is called the forward order. For women, seek the reverse in every case. These day-and-night manifestations concern the pairing of yin and yang, and the substance and function of firmness and yielding.

<!-- source: L38-L38 -->
When the Five Phases generate from below upward, it is called **Assisting Qi**: the person enjoys his own blessing throughout life. Generation from above downward is **Stealing Qi**: throughout life he supplies the blessing enjoyed by others. Control from above downward is the forward order: authority and power to govern others. Control from below upward is the reverse order: repeated stagnation and difficulty in rising. At Death and Extinction the effect is more urgent; at Birth and flourishing it is somewhat slower. Many ghosts among the four pillars’ nayin, with master and root in season, are called **Officer Star Riding Flourishing**. Much nayin Wealth, with master and root lacking qi, is called **Excess Wealth Injuring the Body**.

<!-- source: L40-L40 -->
The Five Phases prize equality between yin and yang. Two Metals meeting two Woods, or two Fires, two Earths, or two Waters, must each form their own image to be auspicious. Excess and insufficiency—three Waters and one Wood, one Water and three Woods, and the like—are not blessings. Suppose a Metal person has three Metals and one Wood. Metal controls Wood as Wealth; three Metals contend for one Wood, dividing the blessing, and wealth and possessions commonly fail to satisfy. With one Metal and three Fires, the many Fires overcook the little Metal, and the person is never at rest throughout life. A Jia person encountering three Ren and three Ji is called **Three Swallowings and Three Pairings**, an inauspicious condition. Two Ji and two Geng are **Repeated Pairing and Repeated Injury**, more ominous even than the three: if not poverty, then early death. Infer the rest by analogy.[^12]

<!-- source: L42-L42 -->
In reading a fate: Hurting Officer Meeting Officer brings early death; Seven Killings meeting Wealth brings an early end. Wealth wholly exhausted by robbery means death; repeated Wealth breaking Seal is ominous. Water flourishing and Wood drifting ends in becoming a ghost away from home. Food God meeting Owl brings death in prison. Heavy Rob Wealth encountering Wealth means death; flourishing Killings with a hanging root brings an end. Loss Spirit and Seven Killings with clash and punishment bring penal exile or death in bonds. Repeated Hurting Officer and Yang Blade bring death through bloodshed even if the body remains whole. Wealth Star meeting Blade scatters wealth and ends life.[^13]

Birth and flourishing die in Storehouse and Tomb; Storehouse and Tomb are extinguished in Birth and flourishing. There may be auspicious luck in later life, yet death occurs before the inauspicious course is entered—how is the death to be distinguished? An inauspicious course may be coming, yet blessing develops before the auspicious course arrives—how is this to be explained? Investigate advancing and receding qi, and examine the meaning of development already accomplished or not yet accomplished. What is coming advances swiftly; what has completed its work returns before its time. A lifetime of inauspicious years and courses gives death while young. If the Fate Star obtains its ground in the final period, longevity rises higher in old age. The old fear Birth and flourishing; the young dislike Death and Extinction.[^14]

Yang Blade meeting Birth often brings a violent death; rooted and flourishing Killings certainly brings an ominous end. In spring, flourishing with much Fire favors the northwest; the Storehouse is the time of return. Summer brilliance with flourishing Metal favors the southeast; ghost-country is the ground of longevity. Four heavy Blade Stars bring death beneath Direct Wealth; one Officer with shallow noble qi ends amid Yang Blade. When all four pillars are injured, the person dies. Metal Spirit entering Water brings drowning. Yang Blade Reversing the Spear makes a headless ghost; Killings Star piled upon Blade leaves half a body. Restraint is balanced, yet Killings reaches its full extreme and qi dies; generating assistance is excessive, Seal flourishes further, and the body ends. Hurting Officer entering the Tomb brings death: the final configuration especially must be examined. Yang is born while yin dies; yin dies while yang is born. Killings meeting Triple Combination must overturn when excessive. Within the Five Phases, examine all this minutely.

<!-- source: L44-L44 -->
Excess or insufficiency in the Five Phases is certainly no blessing, yet there are subtle differences. Water and Earth do not dislike Death and Extinction, for all between Heaven and Earth is filled with Water and Earth, without distinction of the four seasons: how could they truly die or be extinguished? Distinguish only their weight. A drop of Water in a mass of Earth dries up; a pinch of Earth damming a mass of Water disperses. Consider quantity and weigh the greater against the lesser. Metal is not born without Earth; Wood does not grow without Water. Metal and Wood therefore desire Birth and flourishing and fear Death and Extinction. Dead Metal sinks; dead Wood becomes ash. They differ from Water and Earth. Fire is hidden in Wood and lodges in Earth. It does not desire excessive flourishing, for then it burns things up; nor does it desire Death, for then it goes out. Only balance is good. All the Five Phases depend upon Water and Earth, which are especially essential in Wood, Metal, and Fire fates.[^15]

<!-- source: L46-L46 -->
When taking the images of the Five Phases, take an original image as original: Jia, Yi, Bing, and Ding, for example, form a Wood-and-Fire image. Take a transformed image as transformed: Wu–Gui and Ding–Ren likewise form a Wood-and-Fire image. A Metal-and-Water image must not meet Earth. Earth mixes with Water and clouds it, so Metal is no longer clear; Earth encountered in years or luck also causes obstruction. Pure, unmixed Metal and Water born in an autumn month are most distinguished. The Ming Shenzong emperor’s **Guihai / Xinyou / Guihai / Xinyou** has stems and branches entirely of Metal and Water without mixture. Water is born in a Metal month; Metal assists it and the Water is clear. Two Waters and two Metals form the image called **Metal White, Water Clear**, with no intrusion. It also agrees with **Two Stems Unmixed**; hence the dignity of Son of Heaven. But in **Guiyou / Guihai / Gengzi / Xinsi**, Metal is born in a Water month and instead drains its qi, sinking into Hai and Zi; danger from Water therefore cannot be avoided.[^16]

A Metal-and-Earth image must not meet Wood: Wood controls Earth, preventing Earth from generating Metal, so the image fails. Accumulated Earth forms Metal. Much Earth and little Metal give substantial blessing; heavy Metal and light Earth bring blessing through hardship. A Metal-and-Fire image must not meet Water: Fire goes out and Metal sinks, so no useful vessel can be formed. Heavy Metal and light Fire give late development and increased longevity; light Metal and heavy Fire give early development, swift decline, or a shortened life. A Metal-and-Wood image should not meet Fire. Living Wood fears Metal and becomes refined when Fire appears; dead Wood must obtain Metal before it can become useful. Heavy Metal and light Wood bring bone pain; heavy Wood and light Metal indicate loss of money or an attack of lung disease. Only their proper proportion is auspicious.

A Water-and-Wood image is refined, clear, and lofty; it must not meet Mao or Si, for Water dies or is extinguished there. A Wood-and-Fire image is refined and abundant; it must not meet Metal, which controls Wood. Encountering it in a passing year also brings calamity. A Water-and-Fire image is finest as **Completion**; **Incompletion** is also possible. It must not meet Earth. Much Fire gives an impatient temperament; much Water, eye disease. Fire fears Death, Water fears Bath. At You, Fire dies and Water bathes: hardship and death are indicated. Years and luck follow the same rule, and this image is especially avoided in day and hour. A Water-and-Earth image must not meet Fire. Heavy Earth and light Water give refinement without substance; heavy Water and light Earth can instead give examination distinction.

A Fire-and-Earth image must not meet Water. Fire hollow and Earth piled up cannot form a useful thing; carried along by Water, they sink into oblivion. Wuzi, Wuwu, Jichou, Jiwei, Dingsi, Dinghai, Bingchen, and Bingxu place Bing/Ding and Wu/Ji on either side of one another: Fire hollow, Earth accumulated. Li Jiuwan considers **Wuzi / Jichou / Wuwu / Jiwei** and **Bingchen / Dingsi / Bingxu / Dinghai** images of intermingled Fire and Earth. They must not be called noble on the ground of a String of Pearls. Ren or Gui in the hour makes Earth stagnate and Fire go out: lifelong obstruction and scant blessing. 〈Another account says:〉 Fire upon Fire meeting Earth grows dim; Earth upon Earth meeting Fire becomes hollow. Light Earth and heavy Fire are parched, as in Jimao day with Bingyin hour. Light Fire and heavy Earth lack brightness, as in Dingyou day with Wushen hour. Yet Academician Han’s **Wuxu / Dingsi / Wuxu / Dingsi** forms a Fire-and-Earth image and also the **Phoenix Stem-and-Branch Configuration**, hence distinction.[^17]

〈The classic says:〉 Metal and Water tend toward clarity, Metal and Earth toward substance, because they generate one another. Metal and Fire tend toward firmness, Metal and Wood toward rectitude, because they control one another. Fire and Earth tend toward poison, Fire and Wood toward intelligence, Fire and Water toward obscurity, Fire and Metal toward vehemence. Wood and Fire give literary brilliance; Wood and Water, clear excellence; Wood and Metal, uprightness; Wood and Earth, poisonous harm. Water and Fire give wisdom; Water and Wood, wisdom and benevolence; Water and Metal, refined beauty; Water and Earth, heaviness and turbidity. Infer each from the nature of its phases.

<!-- source: L48-L48 -->
A fate fears mutual breaking among things of the same kind: Jiwei meeting Jiachen, Jiachen meeting Jichou, Jichou meeting Jiaxu, and Jiaxu meeting Jiwei. At the four clashing positions, take nayin of the same kind and count backward by pairs. Use the same method for Yin–Shen–Si–Hai and Zi–Wu–Mao–You. It indicates lifelong insufficiency and commonly a failure to become useful. 〈The *Classic of the Way* says:〉 Mutual breaking at the Well-Railing has no medicine that can cure it; meeting Void offers some hope. Years and luck also fear it.[^18]

<!-- source: L50-L50 -->
Master and root must not meet their Death ground in years and luck. Bingyin Fire fears Yimao Water; Xinsi Metal fears Dingyou Fire; Jiashen Water fears Jimao Earth; Wushen Earth fears Renwu Wood; Jihai Wood fears Jiazi Metal. The meaning is the same as a living person’s fear of death. If master and root share the same path in life and death, however, it is not feared.[^19]

<!-- source: L52-L52 -->
A fate especially fears control by ghost, and **Ghost in the Nest** is the most poisonous: Bingzi Water meeting Gengzi Earth, Dingchou Water meeting Xinchou Earth, and the like. Control takes place in the very seat of the nest, hence its severity. There is **Ghost in the Tomb**, as when Renchen Water meets Bingchen Earth, or Bingchen Earth meets Wuchen Wood. There is **Ghost beyond the Wall**, as when Gengzi Earth meets Guichou Wood; and **Ghost in Void**, as when Jiaxu meets Jiashen or Yiyou. All indicate harm. Tomb-ghost is lighter than nest-ghost; wall-ghost lighter than tomb-ghost; void-ghost lighter than wall-ghost.[^20]

If a Wood-fate person has a Fire month and Metal in day and hour, Fire controls Metal so that Metal cannot injure Wood. This is **Warding off Ghost**: ghost does no harm. If a Water-fate person has Fire and Earth in the four pillars, Earth controls Water and Fire further generates Earth. This is **Assisting Ghost**, making ghost especially ominous. The rule applies to both stems and branches, but nayin is most important. Warding off ghost means establishing oneself amid hardship; assisting ghost brings much breaking and conflict among blood kin.

Ghost within ghost is called **Ghost Howling**. For example, an Earth-fate person has a Wood month and Metal in day and hour: Wood controls Earth and Metal controls Wood. A weak foundation makes this ominous; a strong master and root do not fear it. In **Jiwei / Yihai / Bingyin / Xinmao**, Triple Combination generates, while Yin and Mao further lead Ji into Officer’s country. Bing combining with Xin gives great distinction. Yet Ji yin Earth meets Yi Wood as ghost, and Xin in turn becomes Yi Wood’s ghost. Yin and Mao’s Officer is changed into a ghost for Ji Earth. Throughout the Hai–Mao–Wei Triple Combination, ghost controls: this is Ghost Howling, hence a violent death.[^21]

〈The classic says:〉

> Within the Five Phases, above all avoid the lower injuring the upper:  
> Lifelong insufficiency, and affairs entangling one another.

〈It also says:〉

> Ghost Howling clearly makes an evil configuration;  
> Add punishment and sha, and calamity cannot miss.  
> Though wealth and distinction came in earlier years,  
> Know that afterward one will weary of life’s passing seasons.

<!-- source: L54-L54 -->
In reading a fate, take Embryo, Birth, flourishing, and Storehouse as the **Four Noble Positions**; Death, Extinction, Illness, and Defeat as the **Four Avoided Positions**; the remainder as the **Four Level Positions**. Take the Tai Sui stem as master, assign its phase, and distinguish high from low by these positions. Many noble encounters give distinction; many lowly encounters give low station. Among the Four Noble Positions, [Birth], flourishing, and Storehouse are superior; Embryo comes next.[^22]

If conception, month, day, and hour contain three noble positions, with assistance in all their stems, or with Direct Lu, Direct Officer, and Direct Seal, it is a fate for the Three Excellencies. Direct Tianyi means, for example, a Chou- or Wei-born person obtaining Jia, Wu, and Geng in month, day, and hour. Lu of one’s own house means a Yin-born person carrying Jia in those positions. This is called **Blessings Assembling**. Twofold combination with Tianyi Noble likewise gives a fate for the Three Excellencies. Three noble positions carrying upper-and-lower combination, or one Officer and one Seal together with one Direct Tianyi, or one Lu of one’s own house and two or three Noble Persons combining, give the fate of a chief minister. Two noble positions in day and hour carrying the same conditions are judged likewise.

If one position contains Disaster Sha, Earth Sha, Loss, Robbery, Yang Blade, or similar spirits, it also indicates military authority: the dignity of the Minister of War and command with tally and axe. If conception month, birth month, day, and hour contain a noble Embryo or Storehouse position, together with Direct Tianyi and upper-and-lower combination, or Tianyi Noble combining with one’s own Lu, Direct Officer, and Direct Seal; or if one’s own Lu merely possesses qi, or a Noble Person also carries the aforesaid Lu stem—these too are fates for chief ministers and the Nine Ministers. A month in an avoided position with a noble day, or a day in an avoided position with a noble hour, need not prevent a pure and illustrious post in close attendance. But noble month and day followed by an avoided hour indicate only an official selected through the ordinary appointment system.

The Four Avoided Positions indicate poverty and low station, with differing severity: Death, Defeat, and Extinction are heavier; Illness is lighter. Each phase has three positions, as Fire has Yin, Wu, and Xu. Bing and Ding people encountering these obtain nobility. They correspond to three of the Four Noble Positions, lacking only Embryo, yet the blessing of distinction is the same. Yang Lu is noble at Coming to Office; yin Lu at Imperial Prosperity. Yang Lu at Prosperity or yin Lu at Coming to Office, although still in its own region, has only half the blessing. Flourishing without Lu—as Bingyin obtaining Wuwu in day and hour—further controlling the natal fate and carrying punishment and sha, indicates sexual dissipation, stupidity, and low station. In reading a fate, distinguish the Five Phases’ Birth, flourishing, Death, and Extinction. Jiashen, Bingyin, Jihai, Xinsi, and Wushen are the phases’ **Self-Birth**. Without regard to the four seasons, they possess in themselves the principle of being born. A person receiving this is quick, penetrating, and clear-minded. Guiwei, Renchen, Bingchen, Jiaxu, and Yichou are **Self-Tomb**, the time of returning to the root and restoring the allotted life. Wherever a Storehouse stands, things must gather to fill it. Renchen Water, for example, needs many Waters to converge before it flourishes; Metal coming and going in mutual generation then gives weighty authority. But if Water controls Fire and Fire controls Metal, with Heavenly Center arriving as well, this is **Bearing Seal but Unable to Rise**, indicating poverty and low station.

Yimao, Dingyou, Renwu, Jiazi, and Jimao are **Self-Death**. Birth is exertion; death is rest: such is the natural principle. Without a place of death, a thing would have nowhere to return. Self-Death obtains this true principle of return. Those encountering it are outstanding, clear-minded, rich in insight but poor in blessing. Stillness and silence are their substance; purposeful action is unfavorable. Modesty of means is their occupation; launching new undertakings is unfavorable. Only study of the Way and the search for immortals suit them, passing beyond the gate of life and death.

Guisi, Yihai, Gengshen, Renyin, and Dingsi are **Self-Extinction**. Nothing in Heaven’s Way can finally be extinguished. Stems and branches meet their appointed conjunction, and after extinction comes renewed birth. Those encountering this have sorrow and joy not yet settled. Guisi’s extinguished Water obtaining the support of Guiyou’s flourishing Metal, for example, is **Extinguished Water Meeting Birth**, especially auspicious.

A fate carrying these Deaths, Extinctions, Births, flourishings, Storehouses, and Tombs cannot be judged merely by what it carries. Examine the monthly command to distinguish clear from turbid. Clarity means that there is restraint. Diseased Water meeting Earth becomes turbid, yet without an earthen embankment it cannot be brought to rest; once at rest, it gradually clears. Turbidity means lack of restraint: much Water without Earth floods without a destination. At its extreme Water generates Wood; extremity brings change, and change brings passage. The Five Phases prize transformation rather than constancy, concealment rather than display. Death and Extinction with rescue are **The Soul Returning**, often a noble judgment. Birth and flourishing with control are **Qi Dispersing**, instead indicating shallow blessing.

In mutual generation between the other and oneself, the forward order strengthens the root, the reverse steals qi. In mutual control, the forward order makes force strong, the reverse causes injury. 〈The classic says:〉 To use the small to insult the great is to bring harm upon oneself; to use the weak to overcome the strong is to gather one’s own calamity. One Water controlling three Fires is the weak overcoming the strong. Yin overcoming yang brings a calamity that does not become conspicuous; yang overcoming yin causes no deep harm. Two yang forces blocking one another bring disaster swiftly; two yin forces opposing one another cannot rest in place. Yisi Fire controlling Renshen Metal is yin overcoming yang; Renshen Metal controlling Jisi Wood is yang overcoming yin. Yin and yang have affinity, hence no great harm. Dingmao meeting Guiyou, however, is two yin forces in opposition; Wuwu meeting Jiazi is two yang forces resisting. Yang is firm and yin yielding, but neither ceases until it has prevailed: hence calamity.

〈Taiyi says:〉 The mechanism of Heaven and Earth’s yin–yang transformation always works through yin summoning yang and yang summoning yin. But yang following yang or yin following yin makes yin and yang emerge lopsidedly and disorders movement and stillness: therefore blessing and calamity take different paths. In general, lopsided yin and yang prevent creation from being accomplished. Much Fire and little Metal cannot gather and disperse into proper form; little Fire and much Metal cannot melt and refine it and instead risk the extinction of flame and smoke. [^01]: **Controlling text and comparison witnesses.** **S** is the repository’s [Chinese juan ten](../source/juan-10.md), exported from [Wikisource revision 761703](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=761703); the header records its SHA-256 checksum. **G620**, **G621**, and **G622** are the parallel extracts in the *Gujin Tushu Jicheng*, Arts juan [620, revision 1942583](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=1942583), [621, revision 1942584](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=1942584), and [622, revision 1942585](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=1942585). They were consulted selectively, not collated leaf by leaf. Their volume pages transclude separately editable Page texts: a parent revision does not freeze every underlying transcription. The parallel collection is not another Siku edition, and agreement does not prove that witnesses are independent. S controls coverage and sequence; additions or repairs from another witness are identified rather than silently absorbed. Machine-supplied punctuation on the comparison pages is not treated as independent textual evidence. Selected final passages were also checked directly against the image of *Gujin Tushu Jicheng*, volume 472, leaf 26 recto ([image 52 and its page transcription, revision 1898878](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=1898878)); the relevant notes identify those checks. Primary historical and literary parallels used for particular notes are linked there, rather than represented as additional witnesses to the whole juan.

[^02]: **Several methods coexist.** The text moves between day-stem analysis, year-based Lu/Fate/Body analysis, stem-combination transformations, nayin, and spirits and sha. “Useful spirit” translates 用神 as the influence actually employed in a particular analysis; it should not automatically be restricted to a modern single “favorable element.” “Image” translates 象, an intelligible configuration or manifestation of qi, not necessarily a pictorial figure. 本, 主, 體, and 用 retain the distinctions root, master, substance, and function or use. These categories vary with the passage. Historical moral, medical, and prognostic assertions are translated as assertions of the text, not verified diagnoses or descriptions of living people. Unambiguous graphic confusions among 己, 已, and 巳 are resolved by context; doubtful or substantial changes are documented individually.

[^03]: **Year-root, month-command.** S says 年為本, “the year is the root”; G620 has 月為本, “the month is the root.” S is retained. This does not erase the same passage’s priority for the monthly command. 提綱, literally the cord that lifts and governs a net, is rendered “governing thread” when the image matters; here it denotes the monthly command. The three palaces are the three non-day positions being considered for configurations, not three additional calculated palaces.

[^04]: **A repeated pillar and its opposite.** The definition of Turning-the-Heel Sha mentions identical day/hour and annual stems and branches, yet the example includes both Gengshen and Gengyin. G620 preserves the same wording. The contradiction is not resolved by deleting the opposite branch or inventing a second name. 三分, “three parts,” of residual qi is retained without converting it into a precise modern percentage. The source gives no denominator in this sentence.

[^05]: **An ambiguous restriction.** 地支原有財官天干不露出者不問 occurs in both S and G620. 不問 can mean that such an influence is not taken into consideration, or that absence of exposure need not be inquired into. The translation keeps the terse “not considered” and leaves that ambiguity visible. The following sentence clearly denies the efficacy of Wealth/Officer appearing only above without a branch basis, while later passages explicitly allow unexposed influences to operate. These are not silently rewritten into one universally applicable exposure rule.

[^06]: **Six categories, then four.** The source lists Officer, Seal, Wealth, Killings, Food God, and Hurting Officer, then speaks of “the four” while discussing the first four. Both numbers are retained. “Generation and removal” versus “assistance and stripping” cannot simply be translated as generation good, assistance bad: the object of each action and whether it is useful or harmful must be supplied from the configuration. The text itself leaves these objects compressed.

[^07]: **Heaven, Earth, and the human order.** The hierarchy of stems, branches, and the human Five-Phase relations is part of this cosmology, not a social recommendation by the translator. Four seasonal beginnings means Yin, Shen, Si, and Hai. Calling their harmonious relations a condition for Horse to run belongs to this older framework and is not a claim that every such branch is independently a Traveling-Horse star.

[^08]: **Transformed phases in the first series.** Jia–Ji with the Earth endings, Yi–Geng with Metal, and the other pairs follow transformed-phase relationships. The later series of preferred and feared stems belongs to the passage’s own Earthly-Origin analysis. The two series are preserved instead of being reduced to ordinary day-stem generation and control. 三十九 and 四十 divide the stated periods at thirty-nine and forty; no modern age-count conversion is implied.

[^09]: **True qi is not ordinary stem phase.** Renzi and Renwu, for example, have Wood nayin although Ren itself is Water in ordinary stem analysis. The “Refined” pairs and the following Fire-person/Bing–Xin examples introduce further correspondences. The translation preserves the stated elemental identities rather than changing them merely to make all three lists obey one rule. 精 is rendered “Refined,” 真 “True,” keeping the terms distinct.

[^10]: **Hidden partners participate.** These exchanges involve the hidden stems of the named branches as well as visible stems. S gives the complete repeated Gengyin/Jimao chart; G620 abbreviates that example. 兩府 refers to the paired high councils of civil and military government, here “the Two Councils,” rather than two provincial prefectures. The precise personal identity of either example is not supplied.

[^11]: **An expressly variable older system.** Both witnesses state that Ji and Geng do not change, after giving a broader rule for the five yang and five yin stems. Both also retain 六般, “six kinds,” without a satisfactory enumeration. These are not silently corrected to five. S has 日主 in the yin-male sentence; G620’s 日生 supports “[by day].” The rule concerns transformed qi and the birth’s day/night condition, not a replacement for the calendar’s day-stem calculation.

[^12]: **Repeated signs, not new arithmetic.** Three Ren are Jia’s repeated Inverted Food; Ji are its combining partners, and Geng its controlling influences. S’s ambiguous 巳 forms are read Ji in this stem-based series, as G620’s 己 confirms. The comparative judgment that the twofold combination is worse than the threefold one is the transmitted claim, not an error repaired by changing either number.

[^13]: **Compressed prognostic formulas.** These clauses preserve the historical text’s categorical claims. 煞旺掛根休, here “flourishing Killings with a hanging root brings an end,” is difficult and is also transmitted by G620. The later 制伏中和煞極全而氣死 is likewise syntactically compressed. Neither has been expanded into a newly invented technical rule. A chart’s “root,” the technical stages Death/Extinction, and an asserted literal death must not be treated as interchangeable concepts merely because the text sometimes moves rapidly among them.

[^14]: **Anticipatory effects, not a missing date supplied.** S and G620 both have the difficult sequence about death before entry into a bad course and blessing before a good course arrives. Its stated explanation is advancing/receding qi and work already completed or not completed. The translation retains the questions’ uneven wording rather than silently rewriting them into a symmetrical theory. 歸, “return,” and 終, “end,” may carry both cyclical and mortal force in this passage.

[^15]: **The phases are not given one uniform life-cycle rule.** This paragraph exempts Water and Earth from a simple fear of Death/Extinction, distinguishes Metal and Wood, and requires Fire to avoid both excessive flourishing and extinction. A later paragraph says Water and Fire do not fear Death/Extinction. Both formulations stand in S and G620; the translation does not replace either pair to create uniformity.

[^16]: **Original and transformed images are expressly separate.** Wu–Gui forms Fire, Ding–Ren Wood; the order of the pair names need not match the phrase “Wood and Fire.” The named Shenzong chart is a transmitted example, not an independently verified imperial birth record. 既濟 and 未濟 are rendered Completion and Incompletion, preserving their resonance with the Changes without substituting a separate hexagram calculation.

[^17]: **Lists and exceptions preserved.** Both the first eight-pair list and Li Jiuwan’s differently ordered two four-pillar series are retained. A favorable Phoenix case follows the warning about Fire-and-Earth mixture; it is not omitted for doctrinal uniformity. The subsequent ordered pairs sometimes assign different qualities to the same two phases in reversed order. These are the source’s distinctions, not an English deduplication error.

[^18]: **Nayin, not simply identical day stems.** The four examples combine stem relations with same-category nayin and the four Earth branches. The text explicitly extends its operation to the other two groups of four branches. “Well-Railing” here is the name attached to mutual breaking; it should not automatically be identified with every later configuration bearing a similar name.

[^19]: **Master and root may be judged together.** The examples concern the relevant phase at its Death position and an opposing nayin. The final exception, 主本生死同途則不忌, is retained without an added worked example, since none is supplied. It prevents treating the preceding pairs as unconditional predictions independent of the rest of the chart.

[^20]: **Ghost is a technical controlling relation.** 鬼 does not always name a separately calculated spirit or sha. Here nest, tomb, neighboring position, and Void differentiate how a controlling influence is situated. The comparative order of severity is the source’s own: nest, tomb, wall, Void.

[^21]: **A change of reference point within the example.** The Jiwei/Yihai/Bingyin/Xinmao example turns explicitly to Ji yin Earth, the year stem, although the day is Bing. That older root-based analysis is preserved. Control of a controller is not automatically beneficial here: the paragraph distinguishes weak foundations from strong master/root and calls the unstable chain Ghost Howling. The English does not silently recast the entire example around a modern Bing Day Master.

[^22]: **One internal repair.** S and G620 have 又分四旺庫為上, literally an unexplained “four” before flourishing and Storehouse. “[Birth]” is an internal emendation of 四 to 生, supported by the preceding four-item list and the following statement that Embryo is secondary; it is not an attested variant in G620. The later 三位/四貴 comparison and the distinction between yang and yin Lu are retained. 胎 is the conception position; it is not an additional birth hour.

[^23]: **Self-Birth and related labels are nayin classifications.** The five examples in each group represent five phases, not five ordinary stem-stage assignments. They are therefore not corrected from a Day Master’s standard twelve-stage table. The author immediately qualifies them by monthly command, restraint, and rescue. Heavenly Center is the older Void designation in this context.

[^24]: **Substance and configuration precede minor indicators.** 體面 is rendered “substantial form,” 局勢 “the disposition of the configuration.” 土厚, water banks, timber, smelting, and the directions develop the text’s physical analogies; their appearance is not modern engineering, medical, or relocation advice. The distinction between actual birthplace and directional luck is left to each sentence’s grammar.

[^25]: **The transmitted exception remains visible.** S and G620 here say 水火不嫌死絕, Water and Fire do not dislike Death/Extinction. This differs from the earlier Water-and-Earth statement and the explicit warning that Fire at Death goes out. Quiet autumn Fire and excessive summer Fire explain part of the context, but no conjectural change of 水, 火, or 土 is imposed.

[^26]: **The text reasons through culmination and renewal.** 成功, “completed its work,” is not necessarily worldly success; it can mean that a phase has passed its climatic culmination. 將來, “what is coming,” is not a specified future year. The examples at Birth/Death are evaluated through nayin support and obstruction, so a nominally favorable stage can fail and an unfavorable one can recover.

[^27]: **Two supported repairs, two retained variants.** S’s Earth-Extinction gloss gives 丁巳土, Dingsi Earth, although the main clause requires Metal; G620 gives 辛巳金, supporting “[Xinsi Metal].” S’s Metal-Extinction gloss gives 壬寅金, Renyin Metal, although the clause requires Water; G620 gives 甲寅水, supporting “[Jiayin Water].” S’s label 歸魂, Returning Soul, differs from G’s 壽, Longevity, and is retained; S’s 繼體 likewise remains rather than importing G’s fuller 精復繼體. The Snake/Horse and gallbladder argument belongs to the passage’s system of correspondences, not an anatomical verification by the translator.

[^28]: **Two meaningful variants retained.** S says 庫多則閉, many Storehouses bring closure, and 五行不可太相安, the phases must not be too mutually at ease. G620 instead has 虛, emptiness, and 傷, injury. S’s intelligible readings are retained, not normalized to G. The image-only graph in 無〔glyph〕揚 is read with G’s 無激揚 as no rousing or uplifting impulse, hence the brackets in English. “The noble” and “Noble Person” are not invariably the same: the former can be a social person, the latter a technical indicator.

[^29]: **A transmitted phase label, not corrected to Fire.** S and G620 both call the Renzi/Bingwu/Bingwu/Renzi example 水木精神, the essential spirit of Water and Wood, although Bingwu is Fire in ordinary stem analysis and Water in nayin. The phrase remains Water and Wood. Similarly, 三停, three stations, is retained in the following account of Yin and Shen rather than silently replaced by a familiar seasonal label.

[^30]: **Different hour preferences remain.** The first statement requires Yin/Mao for Yi–Bing–Ding; the quoted *Transformations of Heavenly Origin* instead particularly welcomes You/Hai. S and G620 retain both. A regional or phase explanation is not supplied where the text supplies none. “Death with Birth still present” and “Wonders with only an empty reputation” are the author’s cautions against identifying a label with its promised result.

[^31]: **The omitted year is the issue.** 掉太歲 is rendered Shaking off Tai Sui: combinations can gather among the other positions without the natal year participating. “Following the mother,” household transfer, and resident-son-in-law marriage are the social outcomes alleged by the text, not interchangeable modern adoption categories. The claimed extension to descendants is preserved without endorsement.

[^32]: **A natal derivation, not a property appraisal.** The dwelling is counted five positions forward from the natal pair/branch, as the Jiazi→Jisi and Gengwu→Hai examples show. This is not the twelve-palace Dwelling Palace automatically imported from another system. The requirement that the subject be the senior member is explicit in Shen Zhi’s qualification.

[^33]: **An awkward compound retained.** Both S and G620 have 宅生剋身, the dwelling “generates and controls the body.” The text also says the dwelling’s control of the body can yield a good dwelling. The English does not change every instance of control to generation. In the subsequent clause G reads 宅鬼 where S has 宅剋; the translation follows the sense of the controlling influence without importing a new calculated sha.

[^34]: **The backward count is separate.** The fields-and-gardens position is five branches behind: Zi gives Wei. Guiwei in the Wuzi example belongs to the named Jiashen ten-day group’s Void. The method is retained as a distinct derivation from the preceding forward-counted dwelling.

[^35]: **An alternative reading of harmony.** S has 支干太和而塞, stems and branches overly harmonious becoming blocked; G620 has 失和, losing harmony. S is retained, consistent with its earlier warning against excessive mutual ease. The very compressed 將德是衝 is translated without inventing a missing diagram or object of clash. “Husband few, wife doubled” belongs to the text’s role/phase language; it is not advice about a reader’s household.

[^36]: **Dance rather than a silent change to Five.** S and G620 both write 舞數 and later 舞全. Although 五數, “five numbers,” might be conjectured, the translation retains the repeated dance graph as a patterned cosmological motion. The numerical reductions of one-third and one-half are transmitted proportions, not empirical probabilities.

[^37]: **The question and answer differ.** The question describes decline at beginning and end with success in between; the answer explains success at beginning and end with decline between. S and G620 have the same mismatch. Both are translated in their respective order rather than reversing one without notice. The final appeal to Confucius is the compiler’s attribution, not a newly established exact quotation.

[^38]: **Scope of the fatalistic assertions.** The repeated “must die” belongs to this passage’s language; elsewhere in the same juan rescue, balance, timing, and changes of reference point qualify such judgments. It has not been weakened into a statistical claim or turned into individualized advice. Penal exile, dismissal, and execution are distinct stated outcomes and remain distinct in English.

[^39]: **Certainty is asserted by the source, not established by the translation.** The instruction to decide life and death without doubt stands beside the instruction not to be rigid. The text’s examples are preserved as historical claims. Its physical and character diagnoses must not be used as evidence about an actual person merely because they share the signs listed here.

[^40]: **Uncertain personal designations and a color mismatch.** S and G620 both give 張易金, 馬睦閣, 勇九淮王, and 壅七公相; the romanizations retain those strings rather than identifying them conjecturally with better-known historical persons. 雲孫 is rendered a distant descendant without assigning a precise genealogical degree here. The dream’s “gold dog” is not silently changed to a yellow dog to match Wuxu’s ordinary Earth stem. The pig, dog, level-ground Wood, and house/Earth images mix animal signs and nayin images.

[^41]: **The damaged genealogy is not reconstructed.** The strings 文式五師, 式敬籌六老, 姜沉閨懷, and 淑正求二者 are difficult to segment. The main text offers provisional names, marking Elder Chouliu, while retaining the rabbits, chamber, births, dates, and all-Xinmao assertion. It does not establish that these were independently documented twins. The Jiachen date beside the four Xinmao pillars is unresolved in both witnesses. The poem’s lofty mountain is an image of reverence, not a new geographical identification.

[^42]: **Chronology is demonstrably damaged.** S and G620 both give 成化三十一年, Chenghua thirty-first year. The *Ming History*, [juan 14, revision 2594628](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=2594628), records the emperor’s death in Chenghua twenty-third year; there was no literal thirty-first regnal year. Both witnesses also give 正德四年乙巳, whereas the chronological table in [*Ming History*, juan 109, revision 2594725](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=2594725), gives Zhengde fourth year as 己巳, Jisi. A local 乙→己 repair is plausible, but would not repair the narrative as a whole, so its dates remain as transmitted. S’s 宜義郎 is G620’s 宣義郎, supporting “[Xuanyi] Lang.” 孝介經三叟 and 達逵 are not securely identifiable; their provisional segmentation is marked rather than converted into an invented biography. 衛侯 remains Wei Hou because the exact intended title is not established by this passage.

[^43]: **The numerological line remains difficult.** 丙隆一氣應多齊 is shared by S and G620 and is rendered with Bing retained. It is not replaced by a conjectured generational total. The four named sons’ Jiachen, Bingwu, Wuwu, and Xinyou birth years and the grandson/great-grandson signs are all retained. The concluding claim that every omen came true is the source’s assertion, not independent historical verification.

[^44]: **The title and quoted authority.** Wuxian is retained as the traditional name in 巫咸撮要, not identified here with a securely dated individual author. The opening explicitly quotes 天元神趣經; the English title renders 神趣 as the spirit’s subtle working rather than suggesting a modern recreational “interest.” The following repeated 又曰 introduces additional pronouncements. These are not silently presented as one newly systematized argument.

[^45]: **Trigram directions and a numerical variant.** Gen, Qian, Kun, and Xun indicate the northeast, northwest, southwest, and southeast sectors in the directional scheme. The compressed 北、西、南、東 qualifiers are retained as movement toward those sectors. S says barely one or two in a hundred attain distinction when transformation is damaged; G620 has two or three. S’s number remains, and neither number is an observed success rate. “Husband” and “wife” in transformation may designate paired stems, not literal spouses in every clause.

[^46]: **Not every apparent weakness is treated identically.** 伏身潛匿 and 伏象 contrast concealed efficacy with an image suppressed by the month’s injured qi. The text then shifts among ordinary day-stem, transformed-image, and following-image analysis. The translation does not erase these shifts by assigning one fixed modern definition to every instance of 身 or 鬼.

[^47]: **A distinction with limits.** The statement separating Wealth/Officer analysis from configuration analysis concerns the exceptional-pattern approach in this context; later passages again call Wealth and Officer configurations. The numerical contrast of one/two and three/four patterns is retained. 九流 means the miscellaneous learned, technical, and professional paths here, not a precise list of nine modern occupations.

[^48]: **奇儀 is left broader than Three Wonders alone.** The expression joins the language of wonders and ritual or cosmological instruments. It is not silently restricted to a single Jia–Wu–Geng or Yi–Bing–Ding formula in passages that do not specify the signs. “Meanness and evil” renders the difficult 寡惡, shared by S and G620, without inventing a separate sha named Evil Widowhood.

[^49]: **Literary examples, not verified natal records.** Boniu evokes illness and Bu Shang bereavement; Meng Haoran, Shi Jilun, Jia Yi, and Li Si provide images of talent without office, riches, frustration, and confinement. These compact clauses do not establish their birth charts. S and G620 read 李斯專於上蔡; “confined to Shangcai” is a contextual rendering of the difficult 專, not an attested emendation to another graph. 背駝驛馬 is rendered a burden-bearing Traveling Horse without diagnosing a literal spinal condition.

[^50]: **An unusual word retained.** S and G620 have 官在長生須富學, literally abundant learning, not the more predictable 富貴, wealth and distinction. The translation retains learning and does not repair 學 merely to match the adjacent Wealth clause.

[^51]: **Rank and literary imagery.** Dark hair signifies a chancellor still young; the pale-faced scholar contrasts learning without advancement. Jade belts, golden fish, golden insignia, and brocade saddles are images of official distinction, not additional chart stars to calculate. Penglai’s islands supply an immortal alternative to courtly success.

[^52]: **The later recurrence is not automatically beneficial.** The text first welcomes Wealth and Officer in the month, then calls their repetition in day/hour “encountering yet not encountering.” It does not supply a general numerical threshold. The translation preserves the reversal and its relation to the earlier emphasis on useful qi rather than merely repeated names.

[^53]: **Earth within the fourfold scheme.** 四象 here leads immediately to hidden Earth and the Five Phases. It is not expanded into a separate procedure using four trigrams or four named animals. S’s 干秀, refined stems, is retained against the damaged 千秀 in G620. “Reforming,” “Moistening Downward,” and “Sowing and Reaping” retain the phase-pattern vocabulary of previous juan.

[^54]: **The distribution of early and late fortune is as transmitted.** In particular 月在旺鄉晚年不足, the month flourishing but later life deficient, is not silently made into an hour statement. The same paragraph explicitly contrasts month weak/hour strong and hour declining/day refined. 三遷, three moves, remains the stated count rather than being generalized to frequent travel.

[^55]: **An explicit alternate derivation of children.** Wife is what the day controls, and child what the wife generates. This differs from deriving children directly from what the day produces; the later Jade Well commentary explicitly recognizes both. The translation keeps the relation rather than replacing it with a single contemporary convention.

[^56]: **The counterintuitive placements are retained.** 東金西木 and 離壬坎丙 occur in S and G620. Metal in the east, Wood in the west, Ren at southern Li, and Bing at northern Kan are not reversed to their ordinary phase directions. The passage concerns the conditions under which transformation and placement obtain efficacy. S’s 衰樂, decline and pleasure, differs from G’s 哀樂, sorrow and pleasure; S’s graph is not silently altered.

[^57]: **A difficult compound literary image.** 五湖雲擾 is shared by S and G620. The Five Lakes and Shouyang combine the imagery of withdrawal and deprivation; no missing natal calculation is supplied. 重拜爺娘 means acknowledging parents again, compatible with the text’s fostering and household-transfer language, not a second funeral ritual invented from the following 墓時.

[^58]: **Named patterns are conditional.** The qualifications to Six Yin Facing the Yang and Ren Approaching Gen are retained even where another part of the anthology gives a favorable Hai-month rule. “A rat,” “the dragon,” and the moon palace are the text’s own images for Zi, Chen, and examination distinction; no animal omen is being prescribed.

[^59]: **An awkward pair of analogies.** S and G620 compare ground without timely qi to fields receiving rain and timely qi without ground to a damaged yoke-pin in mud. The English preserves those images without reversing the categories to improve the analogy. The following born/extinguished/born/released sequence also remains; it should be read beside the earlier discussion of Birth that fails and Death with rescue.

[^60]: **Operational success rather than a bare label.** 化、聚、合、秀 may be present nominally yet fail in effect, or absent nominally yet arise through the larger configuration. The source’s four contrasts are all retained. The favorable two-Ren/one-Ding example is not changed to a warning about jealous combination merely because other passages fear competing partners.

[^61]: **Names versus literal objects.** Hooked Array and Dark Warrior name the configurations already treated earlier in the work. 右職 is military office, not an unspecified “right-hand job.” The Three Terraces are an image of high ministerial standing. These phrases do not add a new star-chart procedure to this passage.

[^62]: **The transmitted enumeration is not completed by invention.** Seven/eight Officers, four combinations/punishments, and seven/eight clashes/attacks are retained, without constructing missing lists. S’s 夾邱 and G620’s 夾丘 both read “enclosing Mound”; the intended technical referent is uncertain. A conjectured 夾祿, enclosing Lu, would be plausible in context but is not supported by the consulted parallel, so it is not silently substituted.

[^63]: **Compressed text remains compressed.** 暗中藏煞須明月下用神見處無財必受空中禍害 is difficult to punctuate. The English links hidden Killings to identifying the monthly useful spirit and leaves the ensuing absence-of-Wealth warning intact. It does not add a missing condition or a new name for an invisible sha. Three Indirects and Three Directs remain the source’s unexpanded expression.

[^64]: **A counterintuitive rule retained.** Both S and G620 say a weak body meeting Seven Killings falls when luck reaches restraint. No 不, “without,” is inserted. The object and degree of restraint are not specified, so an explanation through over-restraint remains only a possibility. G620 also has several additional contrasts after 始終 and a further concluding sentence after 終困; neither addition belongs to S and neither has been silently imported. Five Gates is left as the text’s unexpanded term.

[^65]: **Outward and inward effects.** 刑出 and 刑入 distinguish sending an influence out from bringing it in, not a blanket claim that every punishment is harmful. S reads 衝衰, clashing decline; G620 has 干衰, a declining stem. S remains. The following one-yang/one-yin language concerns the seasonal turning of qi, not an independently specified calendar conversion.

[^66]: **The contradiction is in the witnesses.** Both S and G620 place “Established Lu” immediately before the designation “Lu and Horse Both Extinguished.” The precise intended reference point is not supplied. The subsequent exceptions—development at Lu’s Extinction and depletion through Companions—are also retained, along with the concluding prohibition on rigid judgment.

[^67]: **Authorship and textual layers.** S attributes the Jade Well to Du Qian of Andong. The concluding note repeats that attribution and is signed Yuwu. The maxims, small-character explanations, quoted earlier commentaries, and later objections are visibly distinguished. Attribution to Du Qian does not establish that every explanatory sentence was written by him. The anthology’s compiler also speaks in the first person in places.

[^68]: **Earth restored from the parallel.** S’s 化象是上, “its transformed image is above,” is G621’s 化象是土. “[Earth]” follows that attested repair. Jia’s original Wood and transformed Earth are expressly distinguished from the outset; neither is substituted for the other throughout the treatise.

[^69]: **Five-day periods, not five-day luck cycles.** 候 denotes the climatic subdivisions used in the traditional calendar. The question concerns which hidden stem’s qi holds seasonal authority on the birth day. The alternative reference to Virtue/Refinement remains an alternative, not an additional mandatory calculation silently attached to the first reading.

[^70]: **A genuine methodological alternative.** The commentary first announces a day-master approach, then argues that examination of the four branch foundations may be clearer than applying day-based labels immediately. Its five searches and the four branch operations are different enumerations and remain distinct. Establishing something “within emptiness” refers to influences inferred through arching, clash, punishment, or combination, not to adding fictitious printed pillars.

[^71]: **有情 is relational.** Affinity here extends beyond stem combination to generation, control, and even punishment when they convey useful noble qi. It is not simply “favorable emotion” or a claim about the subject’s disposition. The sixth-month Major Heat example retains Earth flourishing and Metal assisted as the source’s seasonal assignment.

[^72]: **The criticism of Xu Ziping is explicit.** The commentary says his day-centered Wealth/Officer method misled later readers. That objection is translated in full, although the treatise itself begins by taking the day as master. The scale metaphor assigns different functions to all four pillars rather than resolving the disagreement by deleting either passage. 星兩 are the scale’s graduated marks and weights, not astrological stars in this sentence.

[^73]: **Two distinct combinations.** 隨合, following combination, infers a paired stem-and-branch companion, as Bingwu→Xinwei. 遙合, remote combination, concerns hidden stems in separately located branches. The text supplies Shen–Mao, Zi–Si, and Hai–Wu; these are not silently replaced by the ordinary Six Combinations list.

[^74]: **Breaking can release a useful or harmful influence.** The Mao/Wu discussion invokes Yi and hidden Ji, then You’s possible restraint. Thus “breaking” cannot be uniformly translated as destruction of good fortune. The striking tiger-in-arms image is the source’s warning about a concealed harmful influence, not an introduced metaphor.

[^75]: **No forced correspondence between the numbers.** The commentary refers backward to Eight Methods, then enumerates more than eight paired conditions of qi. It does not explicitly identify those pairs with eight previously defined operations. All the contrasts and examples are retained without trimming the list to eight or inventing a missing enumeration. 三竊一生 and related counts elsewhere likewise remain the stated formulas.

[^76]: **Past qi need not be useless.** The commentary develops six additional temporal conditions after its paired definitions: full but unstable, deficient and weak, declining yet lasting, flourishing but short-lived, tender and fragile, and past its time yet enduring. They are all retained. Renewed support can make residual qi function through another image; this qualifies, rather than cancels, the earlier rule that completed qi should retire.

[^77]: **Bing–Xin and children.** Bing–Xin is Water by transformation, hence Wood can be its child in this passage. The alternative derives children from what the wife generates. Both definitions are explicitly allowed by the commentary. “Vessel” translates 器 as realized capacity or a completed configuration; it is not necessarily a physical object or a single named pattern.

[^78]: **A compressed final judgment.** The commentary’s last clauses are awkward in S and G621. The English distinguishes natural transformation from pursuing an unrealized transformation while the original image actually receives support. This is an interpretation of its contrast between root and extremity, not a repaired Chinese text. “Taking another embryo and changing the bones” preserves 奪胎換骨 as transformation imagery, not a literal reproductive operation.

[^79]: **Three procedures and conflicting elemental preferences.** The two stem-based Void calculations and Road-Cutting Void are all preserved, including the compressed Jiachen/Bingwu→Geng–Xin example. The source does not explain each step fully, so no reconstructed table is added. The statement that Water fears Void and the alternative that Water welcomes it remain separate. Later in the treatise a different division of a void ten-day group is given and differs substantially from G621.

[^80]: **Not a universal favorable-stem rule.** Gui preceding Jia is favored here through combination, generation, and transformed-image support; Yi following Jia is feared through robbery, combination, transformation, and Blade. Later Void commentary instead calls the immediately preceding stem excessively heavy. Both judgments belong to the text and are not silently reconciled into a single ten-stem table.

[^81]: **The source’s incomplete backward example.** It gives Hai, Xu, Shen, and Wei behind Zi, omitting You. The list is translated as an example, not silently completed. Later instructions judge some immediately advancing positions adversely; their purpose differs from this general description of forward-looking branch qi.

[^82]: **An explicit rejection of a common equivalence.** The commentary says wife is what combines with me, Wealth what I control, and criticizes treating them as identical. That statement is preserved although other treatises in the same juan expressly take what is controlled as wife. The author immediately requires transformed-image analysis as well.

[^83]: **One conjecture and several retained variants.** S and G621 read 不如格, an awkward phrase. “[Not entering] a configuration” conjectures 不入格; it is not claimed as a recovered G reading. S’s 金人夜生, Metal people born at night, differs from G’s 金火夜生, Metal and Fire born at night; S’s shorter wording remains. Likewise S’s 福寡 in the following majority/minority discussion differs from G’s 惡寡; the English retains slight blessing rather than changing it to few evils.

[^84]: **The object of conflict matters.** The commentary does not say every battle is harmful. It distinguishes injury to master/useful qi from encounters through which strong, properly placed forces gain authority. Its examples include both two-party and multiple-phase conflicts. The prose remains differentiated rather than being reduced to a rule that “balance is always good.”

[^85]: **The same branch is named by its function.** 庫 stores useful and available qi; 墓 entombs qi that is harmful, exhausted, or inaccessible. This passage defines the distinction relationally, not by assigning one permanent English term to Chen, Xu, Chou, or Wei in every chart.

[^86]: **Three forms of downward generation.** The commentary separately treats stem→branch→nayin, year→month→day→hour, and generation of an idle child-spirit. Its three-or-four-Waters example explains a mother’s depletion. These are different ways to describe drainage, not three extra pillars.

[^87]: **Idle does not mean permanently useless.** 閒神 denotes an influence not initially selected as master or use. It may become central when its time and relations make it effective. The passage’s refusal to discard apparently useless material is part of its argument, not a modern editorial generalization.

[^88]: **True Officer belongs to the transformed frame here.** S and G621 explicitly call the enclosed Jiashen 真官 for a Yi person. This should not be corrected solely because Jia is not Yi Wood’s ordinary Officer. The surrounding discussion concerns true/transformed qi and remotely enclosed positions. The prohibitions on Fire, Geng, and filling the position remain as transmitted.

[^89]: **Different accounts remain distinct.** The commentary repeatedly introduces 一云, another account. True combination with sha, general avoidance of combination by sha, and Lu/Horse welcoming Sixfold Combination are not merged into one rule. S’s “timely rain” in the maxim differs from G621’s “hourly use”; the rain image is retained, with the commentary’s recovery of living intent.

[^90]: **Different reference pillars, overlapping indicators.** Some indicators appear under both day and year because different traditions derive them differently. The parenthetic attribution of Hook/Entanglement, Loss, and Original to the year is retained inside the day-based list. No item is removed as a duplicate. “Clashing Spirit” and “Death Spirit” remain separately named entries.

[^91]: **Five observations with an explicit limitation.** The commentary itself hesitates to make the hour the sole determinant, even while stressing its importance. All five observations, the one/two/three harmonious-pillar comparison, and the distinction between day urgency and slower year/month effects are retained. The first-person hesitation is the commentator’s voice.

[^92]: **Historical timekeeping, not a modern birth-time correction algorithm.** 刻 refers to the subdivisions assumed by the text. The water-clock/sun discrepancy illustrates observational uncertainty near an hour boundary. No conversion to current time zones, daylight saving, or a fixed number of modern minutes is supplied by the source, so none is invented.

[^93]: **An incomplete threefold exposition.** The maxim promises “three arts,” but the following commentary gives one main procedure and an alternative without a fully explicit numbered three-part list. The Five Tigers method and year/day distinction are translated in full. The enumeration is not completed with procedures borrowed from other sections.

[^94]: **The useful spirit has its own relations.** The text explicitly distinguishes Wealth/Officer relative to the useful spirit from those relative to the Day Master’s own house. This additional reference point is essential to the following discussions of Hurting Officer’s Tomb and Rob Wealth’s Storehouse.

[^95]: **The relation has a Tomb of its own.** S and G621 both call Earth Hurting Officer for Bing here and give Chen as its Tomb; the following example gives Xu for Rob Wealth. The passage is not silently narrowed to Ji Earth in the first example, nor is Chen changed to Bing Fire’s Xu Tomb. Its governing distinction is between the Day Master and the birth, flourishing, and burial positions of the influences acting upon it.

[^96]: **Arrival and generation differ between witnesses.** S has 月神來上, the month-spirit arriving above; G621 has 月神來生, the month-spirit coming to generate. The English follows S. The preceding condition that Void join Killings before the most severe judgment is made is also retained; it is not an unqualified prediction from a single adverse branch.

[^97]: **Different objects of injury.** S speaks of the Day Master’s Birth/flourishing and the palace ruler of its useful spirit’s Birth/flourishing. G621 instead gives 日主生旺宮主月神生旺宮主, the palace rulers associated with the day and month. The translation does not silently substitute the month for the useful spirit. Both witnesses transmit the unusual series Mao Wood, You Metal, Chen Earth, and Si Fire for a Water person; it is not converted into a new universal table of Water’s life stages.

[^98]: **One strong, four weak.** S and G621 both have 一強四弱. The fivefold count need not mean five natal pillars: the surrounding discussion concerns concentrated Five-Phase qi. Nevertheless, the text does not explicitly enumerate the four weak members, so the translation keeps the expression without supplying them. Its next sentence expressly returns to the Four Pillars.

[^99]: **Polarity and consecutive halves are different rules.** S divides the Jiazi ten-day group into its five yang-stem days, assigned Xu Void, and five yin-stem days, assigned Hai Void. G621 instead assigns Xu to Jiazi through Wuchen and Hai to Jisi through Guiyou: consecutive halves, not alternating polarities. S’s rule governs the main text. S also expressly equates the preceding-shoulder condition with Ten Evils and Great Defeat, a phrase absent at this point in G621. These are substantive differences, not merely punctuation choices.

[^100]: **A compressed condition remains uncertain.** 官隱無印更隱, rendered Officer concealed, without Seal and more deeply hidden, occurs in both S and G621. The syntax does not securely establish a more elaborate rule about Seal’s exposure or concealment. Both configurations also retain adverse inner-character judgments, despite their different outward appearances; the translation does not manufacture a neatly symmetrical moral contrast.

[^101]: **Concentration matters more than an auspicious label.** The text contrasts an effective adverse influence with nominal but ineffectual favorable qi; merely naming an influence useful does not give it a foothold. S specifies 贊合六神, the Six Spirits assisting in combination, where G621 has 贊合之神, spirits assisting in combination. S’s sixfold designation is preserved without inventing an enumeration. Heavenly Center here denotes a Void condition, not an additional physical location.

[^102]: **An explicit defense of nayin.** The attribution to Da Nao and the interval-of-eight procedure are the commentator’s statements. This maxim directly rejects discarding nayin and permits its qi to remedy a deficiency. It therefore cannot be translated as if this treatise used only the ordinary phase of the day stem. “Slower” describes the stated effect of Rest or Imprisonment, not a specified number of years.

[^103]: **Retreat is not automatically misfortune.** The author deliberately favors Tomb and Extinction for qi that has passed its proper season, contrasting it with qi still advancing. The examples of Jia in the third and twelfth months and Yi in the first are not changed to a single rule that every instance of Birth/flourishing is good and every instance of Tomb/Extinction bad. These month numbers belong to the text’s seasonal calendar.

[^104]: **Control can establish a meaningful relation.** Both generation and control are explicitly welcomed when noble nayin would otherwise have no connection with the self. That is the passage’s relational argument, not a claim that every controlling influence is auspicious. The distinction between a favorable name and an effective affinity continues the preceding analysis of true and false use.

[^105]: **A ten-day group, not a ten-year course.** 旬 is one of the six groups of ten in the sixty-pair cycle. 本旬 here means the same group as the natal day; it is not the major luck course. The Dingwei example says that a seasonally vigorous influence can bring out weak Xin and Gui from Chou: their small effect is preserved, rather than assuming that every clash or punishment of a Storehouse releases equally powerful qi.

[^106]: **Ding and Ji as yin stems.** S and G622 write 丁巳 before Wei. The grammatical subject is 陰干, yin stems, so 巳 is read 己: Ding and Ji, not the complete Dingsi pillar. Xin’s Blade at Xu is retained as this tradition’s rule. The chain Wei combines Wu, Wu clashes Zi, and Zi offends Wei concerns successive relations; it does not turn Wei and Zi into a standard opposing pair.

[^107]: **Five explanations and six explanations, without forced symmetry.** Both lists are translated completely. Several entries describe a failure of balance rather than a prescription for attaining it. S’s third matching example has 一興一衰, one rising and one declining; G622 has 一興二衰, one rising and two declining. S’s number remains. Its final matching clause begins 或, “or,” rather than a further 一; the English divisions make the announced six intelligible but do not claim six uniformly numbered sentences in the Chinese.

[^108]: **A continuous transformed-phase sequence.** Ji Earth, Yi Metal, Xin Water, and Ding Wood belong to the transformed-stem framework. The ordinary stem phases would not yield this generation chain. Both S and G622 give it expressly, with the missing combining partners Jia, Bing, Ren, and Geng completing the qi. The translation therefore preserves the transformed identities rather than correcting Yi to Wood or Xin to Metal.

[^109]: **The dissent is in the source.** The final sentence rejects the immediately preceding explanation that a Metal configuration destroys useful Yi Wood. It is not the translator’s criticism. In G622 it is set off as a smaller, differently colored annotation; S includes it inside the commentary delimiters. A paragraph break makes that change of voice visible without deleting either side or identifying an annotator the text does not name. The first example’s Wu Fire and Yi Metal likewise belong to true/transformed qi, not ordinary stem phases.

[^110]: **A method qualified by uncertainty.** The text objects to a uniform ten-month estimate and allows nine or eleven months in its cyclical procedure. This is transmitted conception-divination, not a medical method of dating a pregnancy. The Four Seals song contains six complete seven-character lines for Earth, Wood, Fire, and Metal; the ensuing 壬癸未詳 says Ren and Gui are not explained. No Water couplet has been invented to complete the pattern.

[^111]: **One branch can signify different stages for different phases.** The Si example combines newborn Metal, extinguished Water, and Fire at Coming to Office. S’s 及官 is repaired to 臨官 with G622’s reading. At Mao, the commentary separately considers dying Water, flourishing Wood, and defeated Fire. A position’s life stage for one phase must therefore not be substituted for its stage relative to another.

[^112]: **Successive Noble relations.** The example cycles from Ding’s Lu to Bing at Wu, then to Xin at You or Hai, and finally back to Xin’s Noble at Wu. Each step has its own reference stem. It is not a single list of Noble Persons calculated directly from Ding. The Xin Water and Ding Wood statements again use transformed phases. G622 adds another Ding/Wu sentence after the Jia examples that is absent from S; it is not silently inserted.

[^113]: **A prohibition followed by an exception.** One account judges an added day-Horse adverse; another allows the combined day- and year-Horses when useful and harmonious, then rejects a further repetition. Both positions are preserved. The opening discussion also distinguishes an ordinarily favorable Lu position from one that obstructs a particular transformation; its technical role is not fixed solely by its name.

[^114]: **A cycled stem, not an additional pillar.** Lu’s Guest and Horse’s General are the stems obtained at the respective branch positions. S’s 禄官 is read as Lu’s palace with G622’s 禄宮. The Dingsi-year example explicitly gives a Bing day: Xin is then the combining spouse in that frame. Its travel-marriage interpretation is the source’s, not an identification of a particular historical person.

[^115]: **A backward reference, not a new enumeration.** The text directs the reader to the earlier Loss and Robbery discussions and retains the number sixteen. It supplies no sixteen-item list here, and the translation does not fabricate one. This sentence also keeps the distinction between deriving Wealth/Officer from the day and deriving these sha from the year, despite their eventual interaction in the same chart.

[^116]: **Two fivefold schemes.** The five grades of distinction and five levels of wealth are retained separately; they are not condensed into a single scale. S’s third wealth description has 財宫, Wealth’s palace, whereas G622 has 財官, Wealth and Officer. The English follows S. The first wealth description expressly says that great wealth need not be measured only by the weight of Wealth Spirit.

[^117]: **Prosperity and longevity are not equated.** The commentator allows socially unproductive qi to coexist with an unharmed, balanced body, and conversely allows conspicuous noble qi to overwhelm an unstable or insufficient body. The alternative explanations and their qualifications remain distinct. The presence of technical Death or Extinction is not by itself a literal death prediction: the commentary expressly discusses receiving qi there and remaining beyond the course’s power to harm.

[^118]: **A rare word retained.** S and G622 read 闉實, with 闉 suggesting enclosure or a surrounding wall. “Enclosed solidity” preserves that image rather than silently normalizing it to a different familiar adjective. The commentary contrasts stable, accumulated well-being with achievement attempted upon empty air; it does not claim that every Fire–Earth chart produces the same temperament or career.

[^119]: **Mildness and effective government are not equated.** The source attributes both destructive violence and decisive action to the force of sha, depending on their relation to use. It then criticizes an entirely yielding image as incapable of ordering affairs. The translation preserves this unsettling contrast rather than changing 無綱常倫序之能 into general praise of gentleness. 屠儈 names slaughtering and dealing as livelihoods; the moral hierarchy is the historical commentator’s.

[^120]: **Adoption and mixed households.** Three surnames under one roof, two surnames making a living together, and grafting plum upon peach are images of altered or composite family ties. The last expression should not be read as literal horticultural advice. Nor does the passage establish one precise legal form of adoption or marriage. The Blade exception expressly requires a concentrated, capable self and Wealth with affinity; it is not a blanket recommendation to strengthen Blade.

[^121]: **Graphic names and separate traditions.** Level Head and Suspended Needle refer to the shapes and traditional classifications of written signs. The list Jia, Bing, and Ding is introduced by 等, “and the like,” and is not represented as an exhaustive table. The several 一云 clauses are alternative accounts; their different conditions concerning marriage, illness, and separation have not been combined into one diagnosis. 巳人 is read as a Ji person in the stem-based example preceding Jihai, Jimao, and Jiwei.

[^122]: **The judgment has stated conditions.** The travel description requires overflowing Water and an agitated Horse; the Net description requires a useful spirit deprived of ground and qi unable to form an image. The presence of Hai/Zi or Chen/Si/Xu/Hai alone is not the full rule given here. “Dining on wind and lodging in rain” preserves the literary image of an exposed, itinerant livelihood.

[^123]: **Two derivative positions, not two more natal pillars.** The fifth positions before and after the year branch are defined here as Fate Dwelling and Lu Dwelling. Their assignments to residence versus fields and tombs are retained. The text does not specify a new inclusive-counting convention. It also explicitly distinguishes urgent Salty Pool from slower Bath; the related names are not made wholly interchangeable in English.

[^124]: **Not solely religious heterodoxy.** 異端 is explained here by keeping creatures, hunting, dressing up, and 社火, communal festivities and performances. “Unconventional pursuits” follows that local explanation rather than making the whole list a charge of doctrinal heresy. The positive integrity and martial courage in the first account are retained alongside the later condemnation of excessive drinking.

[^125]: **Reversing the reference point.** Mao is Peach Blossom for the Yin–Wu–Xu group, and You for Shen–Zi–Chen; here the named person’s position stands first and the corresponding group arrives around it. The source calls this Reversed Insertion. It also gives an explicit day-relation condition before applying the personal judgment. Fragmenting before the Face is a separate label, not another name for Peach Blossom.

[^126]: **A local identification.** This commentary expressly identifies Official Talisman with Loss Spirit. The equation is preserved here but does not require every occurrence of 官符 elsewhere in the compendium to have an identical calculation. Both contrasting judgments require the specified influence in Void to control or drain the day; those qualifications remain in the translation.

[^127]: **Tomb is not determined from the branch alone.** Gengchen carries Metal nayin, while Chen is not Metal’s Tomb; this helps explain why the example cannot simply be called self-entombed because Chen belongs to the four storage branches. The commentary nevertheless combines Self-Tomb with its own Birth/flourishing condition, rather than replacing the phrase with a single ordinary-stem stage. In the preceding marriage account, 鶻突 means muddled or confused, not a separately specified illness, and 服訟 brings together mourning and litigation.

[^128]: **Three grouped influences.** The commentary counts Canopy, Tomb-spirit, and Heavenly/Monthly Virtue Combinations as three items, grouping the last two names together. “Springs and rocks” evokes a withdrawn life of cultivation. The opposing image of a scholar in the dust is retained as the text’s contrast between realized practice and merely possessing a favorable name; it is not a ranking of present-day religious communities.

[^129]: **An unspecified canonical reference.** 道藏經 invokes a Daoist canonical source without naming a particular work or chapter. The note therefore cannot responsibly supply a single ten-day list as the uniquely intended rule. 黃冠 and 緇衣, yellow cap and black robe, are conventional images for Daoist and Buddhist religious life. Their identification here follows the commentary, not an inference about any living person’s religion.

[^130]: **The three are explicitly identified.** Here 三貴 means Birth, Noble Person, and Lu. It does not mean three separate Tianyi Noble Persons or the Three Wonders configuration. The same passage defines Chen as Heavenly Gang and Xu as Heavenly Kui; the broader compound Kuigang is retained without substituting the narrower four-day list familiar from other parts of the book.

[^131]: **Si and Hai, not the Ji stem and Hai branch.** S writes 己亥 in the maxim and its explanation. G622 gives 巳亥 in both, supporting “[Si] and Hai” as two branch positions. The requirement for repeated signs among both stems and branches is retained; neither one repeated branch nor a single Jihai pillar is silently made sufficient. 重拜父母 means acknowledging parents anew, potentially through adoption or a changed family relationship; the source does not specify a single legal arrangement.

[^132]: **The Qin Tower is a literary image.** 秦樓 here evokes a place of amorous pleasure, not a required geographic location or a natal palace named Qin. The commentary expressly explains reciprocal combinations among Ren, Wu, and the hidden Ding and Gui of Wu and Zi. Its clear/turbid distinction is retained without erasing the lifelong erotic attachment or supplying a new star-calculation procedure.

[^133]: **Relational and moral language remain distinct.** The wife-stem may be the combining spouse rather than simply the phase called Wealth; an earlier maxim expressly distinguishes these. Here the source joins that technical account to categorical judgments about fidelity and social standing. Those judgments are translated as its assertions, not modern biographical conclusions. “Seizure” describes the Companion’s proposed technical relation and is not evidence that a real spouse has been unfaithful.

[^134]: **Six stems in twelve days.** The list contains two occurrences each of Bing, Ding, Wu, Xin, Ren, and Gui; “six stems” does not mean only six listed days. All twelve and the hundred-day condition are retained. 外家 can refer to maternal or marital kin outside the paternal line; the compressed list does not determine that the same precise relationship is meant at every occurrence. 殘房入舍 suggests entering a household or marital place left vacant, without securely specifying widowhood, adoption, or a particular legal form.

[^135]: **Five methods, with alternatives inside them.** The first uses the controlling relation produced by the wife; the following methods use the couple’s transformed phase, what that phase generates, what the original phase generates, and properly formed Officer qi. They must not be merged into one child-star formula. In 男以偏官為子女以正官為子, the English takes male/female as the subjects whose charts are being read, prompted by the following alternative 女以我生為子. A reading distinguishing sons and daughters is also grammatically possible. The final Water-one through Earth-five count is an additional account, not a sixth fully expounded method.

[^136]: **An adopted heir is not a biological prediction.** 義子 and 螟蛉之子 are rendered adopted son or heir. The source explicitly introduces this alternative after first predicting no child, so the apparent tension should not be removed by deleting either clause. S’s 妻秀更能掌財 says the wife is refined and can manage wealth; G622 instead says she brings wealth, 更主得妻財. The English follows S’s management wording.

[^137]: **Target Sha and the void group.** 的煞 is a transmitted name, not a missing character. Compare the [Hidden-Gold Target Sha discussion in juan three](../source/juan-03.md), where the names and forms of this sha are explained. Here the Jiayin ten-day group leaves Zi and Chou unfilled, so Gui’s Lu at Zi is void. “Not a tile” preserves the metaphor for a household without property. The medical and violent-death list belongs to the historical Five-Image correspondences.

[^138]: **Not the conception pillar calculated earlier.** The commentator explicitly identifies this 胎神 with White Tiger Sha and gives Water–Wu and Metal–Mao correspondences. It should not automatically be substituted for the ten-month conception calculation discussed elsewhere. 橫亡 signifies a violent or unnatural end, not specifically a judicial finding of wrongful death. The second half concerns departure from the ancestral establishment through adverse influences behind Lu.

[^139]: **Names rather than invented geography.** Bingzi, Guiwei, and Guichou are transmitted whole pairs; Eastern Well and Three Rivers are the names assigned to the latter two. The source gives no coordinates or method of choosing an actual river or well, so none is supplied. S has 合兼, combination also involving, where G622 has 合嫌, combination disliking; the main text retains S’s wording. 自縊 denotes suicide by hanging, not every kind of execution by hanging.

[^140]: **The objection to onward generation is explicit.** S states that without Xin combining with Bing one must not say Jia goes to generate Bing. That restriction is translated rather than replaced with a generic diagram of the generation cycle. G622 has a different intervening instruction to ask first about the day and then the year; it does not contain S’s full objection. Both accounts then connect an impaired root with harm to the day’s qi and year stem.

[^141]: **Directional images and two kinds of activation.** Heaven and Earth here refer to Hai and Shen, while Wind and Thunder refer to Si and Mao. The commentary distinguishes manifest positions from positions obtained by empty arching or enclosure. It both welcomes activation by clash and warns that excessive pressure can drive noble qi away. These are conditional alternatives, not a universal assertion that every clash is either beneficial or harmful, and not predictions of literal weather.

[^142]: **Three positions and four stems.** Wondrous Treasure is expressly explained as the Triple Combination associated with the hour’s seat. The four stems are those of year, month, day, and hour: the text expands attention beyond the more usual year/day Noble calculations. It does not supply four additional natal pillars or require four separate physical persons. The institutional and hereditary language of honor remains distinct from the technical Noble Person influence.

[^143]: **Hooked Array is not replaced by White Tiger.** S and G622 name Azure Dragon, Dark Warrior, Vermilion Bird, and Hooked Array as the Four Sides. The last must not be silently changed to White Tiger to match a more familiar four-symbol scheme. The commentary gives Wood and Fire examples and says to infer the rest; no additional Metal or Water examples are invented. 元武 is the transmitted form rendered Dark Warrior, conventionally also written 玄武.

[^144]: **Hall and lodge have different reference points.** 學堂 is tied here to the Day Master’s Birth and flourishing, 學館 to Officer Star’s; Coming to Office is also allowed. These are technical chart positions, not necessarily actual schools. The book-basket and traveling hat preserve the image of an itinerant scholar. A Horse in the learning positions is not automatically favorable: without sufficient noble qi, the text predicts learning without accomplishment and repeated travel.

[^145]: **Direct and outward arching.** The commentary itself defines direct arching by the flanking branches, such as Hai and Chou around Zi, and outward arching by the other two members of a Triple Combination, such as Shen and Chen around Zi. The implied position is not a newly inserted natal character. 薇垣 evokes an elevated governmental enclosure through astral court imagery; it is not an instruction to substitute a different system’s Purple-Tenuity chart. Hanlin retains its institutional name.

[^146]: **Rapid ascent has its corresponding danger.** Concentrated one-sided qi is explicitly credited with sudden high office, then qualified by rapid retreat or an unnatural end. That claim stands alongside the treatise’s preference for balance; neither is deleted to make its doctrine uniform. The contrast between swift, forceful Indirect Officer and steadier Direct Officer is likewise the commentator’s, including his distinction concerning authority over life and death.

[^147]: **Priority does not mean exclusivity.** The course’s branch and the year’s stem receive chief weight, but the commentary explicitly requires the course’s stem, the annual branch, and their connections with the whole natal arrangement. The unfavorable day/year relations also have stated transformation and affinity exceptions. The maxim cannot therefore be reduced to reading an annual stem alone or judging every combination with the year as inauspicious.

[^148]: **Three connected metaphors.** Useful influences are traveling supplies, the course is a place of lodging, and the year is a commander whose arrival precipitates events. They express resources, conditions, and activation without being three extra calculations. The final possibility that noble qi itself becomes harmful is retained: an auspicious label is not an unconditional guarantee. The military language emphasizes urgency and force, not a prediction that the reader must enter an actual battle.

[^149]: **Punctuation affects the ages; a difficult negative remains.** The [facsimile at volume 472, image 52, leaf 26 recto](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=1898878) was examined directly. It confirms the unpunctuated sequence also transmitted by S: the youthful stages followed by twenty, Coming to Office/Imperial Prosperity followed by thirty or forty, and declining stages followed by fifty or sixty. The English attaches each age to the stages preceding it rather than following the comparison transcription’s misleading sentence breaks. The initial 一 is retained as a list marker, “First”; it has not been moved to manufacture “ten or twenty.” 天癸, heavenly gui, belongs to the traditional account of maturity and reproductive vitality, as in the [Suwen’s opening chapter](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=2083230), not simply a Gui stem in the chart. The closing 仍不要與日主用神表裡生意 contains the negative 不要 in S, G622, and the facsimile. Its subject is compressed: it may refer back to the inauspicious spirits rather than require the removal of every living affinity. The translation preserves the negative and leaves the subject implicit instead of claiming a secure repair.

[^150]: **Palace restored; two meanings retained.** S’s 逐官 is read as 逐宮, following each palace, with G622’s support. The English marks “[palace].” S then has 二者, the two, while G622 has 一者, one; S’s reference to both substantial and transformed images remains. “Family transmission” is the commentator’s attribution to Li Xuzhong, not independent proof of a manuscript genealogy. This passage does not replace the natal chart with an hour-stem formula alone.

[^151]: **Literary images of advancement.** Feihuang is the soaring mount evoked by the phrase also found in Han Yu’s [“Fu Reading South of the City”](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=1569791): 飛黃騰踏去. The leopard’s transformation recalls the upper line of the [Changes’ Ge hexagram](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=2674393), where the cultivated person’s transformation is associated with the brilliance of its markings. These images convey conspicuous attainment and renewed form, not additional named chart spirits. The explanation distinguishes a resource present in the birth chart from its realization through a course or year.

[^152]: **Strength is relative to the task.** Weakening an overstrong body can release support for its useful influence; strengthening the body excessively can instead damage the balance of substance and use. The two clauses retain that contrast. The compressed discussion of a weak natal substance and excessive use is not expanded into a universally prescribed favorable phase. Its stated measure is capacity, proportion, and a coherent direction of use.

[^153]: **Attribution, metaphor, and a parallel-only quatrain.** The attribution to Du Qian of Andong and signature Yuwu are preserved as the source’s statements. 數見而用藏 is read as numbers or configurations being manifest while their use remains concealed; 數 could alternatively carry an adverbial sense, “repeatedly,” so that division is interpretive rather than an emendation. “Obtaining one and distinguishing three” and “changing the embryo and bones” preserve compressed transformation imagery without inventing a calculation.
