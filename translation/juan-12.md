# Sanming Tonghui · Juan Twelve
## A Comprehensive Gathering of the Three Fates

<!-- source: L14-L18 -->
**Attributed to Wan Minying of the Ming dynasty.** From the imperially commissioned *Siku Quanshu*.[^01]

*Reading conventions.* Capitalized names—including Wealth, Officer, Seal, Food God, Killings, Lu, Birth, and Extinction—retain the technical meanings used in the preceding translations. “Body” commonly denotes the Day Master and its capacity to sustain the configuration. **〈 〉** marks the source’s small-character commentary; square brackets mark supplied readings and explicit repairs. The authors’ maxims, their commentators’ explanations, and the translator’s endnotes remain distinct. Competing methods and contradictory judgments are not silently reconciled. Claims concerning health, death, conduct, women, and kinship belong to the historical text; they are not established assessments of living people. Section numbers and paragraph divisions are editorial.[^02]

## 1. Rhapsody on the Original Principle
### 元理賦

〈Written by Xu Dasheng; explained by Wan Yuwu.〉[^03]

The primal, single qi generates the Five Phases, governs the Three Powers, and pervades the myriad things. It brings forth Qian and Kun’s subtle workings and lays open the pivotal mechanism of yin and yang. By tracing the four directions, distinguishing high and low station, and obtaining the middle way, the Eight Characters determine flourishing and decline. Therefore strive to understand their generation, control, restraint, and transformation; their clarity and turbidity, distinction and lowliness, long and short life, worthiness and folly.

〈This traces creation and transformation to their beginning.〉

<!-- source: L26-L30 -->
Metal depends on Earth for birth; too much Earth buries Metal. Earth depends on Fire for birth; too much Fire scorches Earth. Fire depends on Wood for birth; too much Wood makes Fire blaze. Wood depends on Water for birth; too much Water sets Wood adrift. Water depends on Metal for birth; too much Metal makes Water turbid.

Metal can generate Water; too much Water sinks Metal. Water can generate Wood; abundant Wood diminishes Water. Wood can generate Fire; too much Fire burns Wood. Fire can generate Earth; too much Earth obscures Fire. Earth can generate Metal; too much Metal alters Earth.

Metal can control Wood; hard Wood chips Metal. Wood can control Earth; heavy Earth breaks Wood. Earth can control Water; abundant Water washes Earth away. Water can control Fire; blazing Fire heats Water. Fire can control Metal; too much Metal extinguishes Fire.

Declining Metal encountering Fire must melt. Weak Fire encountering Water must go out. Weak Water encountering Earth must be silted up. Declining Earth encountering Wood must collapse. Weak Wood encountering Metal must be cut and broken.

〈These passages show that excess and insufficiency each have their own harm. Thus the Five Phases in the Four Pillars cannot dispense with balanced harmony.〉

Strong Metal obtaining Water has its cutting edge tempered; strong Water obtaining Wood has its force released; strong Wood obtaining Fire has its stubbornness transformed; strong Fire obtaining Earth has its flame checked; strong Earth obtaining Metal has its harmful force restrained.

〈Here the point is that control and restraint among the Five Phases must attain balanced harmony; excess and insufficiency alike miss it. This illuminates the preceding passage.〉[^04]

<!-- source: L32-L34 -->
To grasp principle throughout and bring it into living understanding is to fathom the hidden and examine the manifest. In substance, penetrate change and pursue the primal subtleties; in application, weigh strength and weakness, what was originally present and what was originally absent. Heaven’s principle endows good and ill, movement and stillness; human life receives its allotted obstruction and peace, fullness and depletion.

〈This discusses in general the stems and branches, yin and yang, generation, control, restraint, and transformation. Substance is hidden, application manifest; weight and lightness, presence and absence, and hence good and ill, movement and stillness, obstruction and peace, fullness and depletion, all arise here. The subtlety lies in understanding adaptation, pursuing the primal mystery, and proceeding from the manifest to the hidden: thus one obtains the principle. What follows explains it in detail.〉

<!-- source: L36-L38 -->
**Killings without Blade lacks authority; Blade without Killings lacks distinction.**

〈Killings controls me; Blade robs me: they are the most inauspicious influences in a fate. To speak of Killings and Blade first—does this not show an understanding of what matters most? The rhapsody says: “Blade is the weapon; without Killings it can scarcely stand. Killings is the military command; without Blade it lacks authority. Blade and Killings both manifest, their might awes Heaven and Earth.” Xu Dasheng saw that *Preferences and Aversions* and *Continuing Goodness* did not fully encompass human fates; he therefore composed this rhapsody to supply what they had left incomplete.〉[^05]

<!-- source: L40-L42 -->
**Authority and Blade both manifest, evenly matched: rank rises to prince or marquis. Blade and Killings unequal and ungoverned: the person serves as a petty clerk.**

〈Killings and Blade evenly matched give the highest distinction; unmatched, the lowest station. Such is the distance between high and low arising from whether Blade and Killings are evenly poised.〉

**A lifetime of wealth and distinction: heavy Killings, a yielding body. Sudden death or danger midway: a course supporting the stem’s strength.** 〈When they are not evenly matched, following Killings is better. Following becomes possible only when Killings is heavy and the body yielding; otherwise it cannot occur. Once Killings is followed, judge through Killings alone. Do not encounter renewed bodily strength that sets itself against it: resistance instead breeds calamity.〉[^06]

<!-- source: L44-L52 -->
**At the head of monks and Daoists, the Killings employed is instead light; appointed to the censorate and remonstrance offices, Indirect Officer has obtained its ground.**

〈Seven Killings is a star of authority and also a solitary star. Body and Killings both strong, Seven Killings restrained, or a weak body following Killings: all can give distinction, commonly in the censorate and remonstrance offices. But if the body is strong and Killings light, with a further quality of pure refinement, the person must become a leader among monks or Daoists.〉

**Know that the greatly distinguished employ Wealth rather than Officer, and those who wield authority employ Killings rather than Seal. Seal depends on Killings for birth; Officer flourishes through Wealth.**

〈Employing Wealth rather than Officer means that Wealth generates Officer; employing Killings rather than Seal means that Killings generates Seal. Hence “Seal depends on Killings for birth; Officer flourishes through Wealth.” This does not mean that Seal and Officer have no use. Concentrating on Wealth and Killings already includes Officer and Seal within them. The two clauses beginning “Seal depends” closely explain the four preceding clauses.〉[^07]

**Through the Five Phases’ waxing and waning, the original principle can be known; through a clear reading of the Four Pillars, the useful spirit appears. Food comes first and Killings afterward: achievement and reputation are both complete. You breaks Mao, Mao breaks Wu: Wealth and Officer both excel.**

〈The Eight Characters depend entirely upon the useful spirit. Employing Killings, Blade, or Wealth rather than Officer or Seal, as above, involves a profound principle whose application depends on one’s discernment. Killings needs Blade; without Blade it needs restraint. Strong Killings with restraint is always judged distinguished. Killings governs reputation, Food advantage: hence achievement and reputation are both complete. You breaking Mao and Mao breaking Wu likewise expresses Food before and Killings after. You takes Mao as Wealth and Wu as Killings; with Wealth and Killings both present, Wealth and Officer both excel. Their breaking occurs through control: the four cardinal positions must break one another.〉[^08]

<!-- source: L54-L60 -->
**Enjoyment of blessing comes when the Five Phases return to Lu; venerable age, when the Eight Characters are evenly poised.**

〈These are the essentials of a fate: return to Lu and even poise, not Death, Extinction, or one-sided factions. Enjoyment of blessing belongs to returning to Lu, venerable age to even poise. Each expression has its own purpose.〉

**Fire grows dim and lightless amid Sowing and Reaping; Wood robbed of its qi is often distressed by Bing and Ding.**

〈From here the text explains how failure to return to Lu and attain even poise prevents enjoyment of blessing and long life. Earth covers Fire’s light, and Earth depends on Wood to loosen it. Wood originally generates Fire, but excessive Fire instead steals its qi. The want of balanced harmony is apparent.〉

<!-- source: L62-L68 -->
**Fire hollow within has flame.** 〈Fire fears obscurity; hollow within, it has flame and is not obscured.〉 **Metal solid throughout has no voice.** 〈Metal needs Fire: without Fire’s refining it does not become a vessel. Solid throughout, how could it give out a sound?〉[^09]

<!-- source: L70-L76 -->
**The Wood set adrift by flooding Water is living Wood; the Metal buried by heavy Earth is yang Metal. Water in excess brings danger; Fire blazing bright burns out.**

〈Here unequal poise among the Five Phases is further distinguished by yin and yang, showing that none should be excessive. Flooding Water sets Wood adrift: Yi Wood fears this, Jia Wood does not. Heavy Earth buries Metal: Geng Metal fears this, Xin Metal does not. Yi Wood dies at Hai, whereas Jia Wood is born there. Geng is Metal emerging from Earth and therefore is born at Si; Xin is Metal bearing Water and therefore is born at Zi. Excessive Water floods, hence danger; blazing Fire becomes embers, hence extinction.〉[^10]

**Yang Metal refined too far brings change and restless wandering; yin Wood returned to its enclosure but out of season remains weak in body.**

〈Solid Metal has no voice; overrefining transforms and unsettles it. Returning to Lu brings blessing; losing the season weakens the body: harmony is needed. Heavy Earth buries yang Metal, yet Metal without Earth fears overrefining. Yin Wood out of season is weak; even returning to its enclosure then diminishes longevity. This continues the discussion of living Wood and yang Metal: Water and Earth’s generating nourishment cannot be dispensed with either.〉

<!-- source: L78-L86 -->
**Thick Earth covers Fire and extinguishes its light; abundant Water floats Wood without a resting place. The Five Phases must not become excessive; the Eight Characters require balanced harmony.**

〈“Thick Earth” restates Fire obscured and without light; “abundant Water” restates Water flooding and Wood drifting. The two concluding clauses gather the argument together and return it to balanced harmony.〉

**Earth checking flowing Water makes blessing and longevity complete; Water unchecked by Earth must bring injury and disability.**

〈The following passage says that even when harmony is lost, rescue among the Five Phases can still give an auspicious judgment. Only the absence of rescue and assistance makes it ominous. Flowing Water checked by Earth, for example, gives both Lu and longevity. Infer the others by analogy.〉

**Abundant Wood gives much benevolence; thin Earth little trustworthiness. Flourishing Water in its enclosure must possess wisdom; firm Metal governs righteousness and ability. Metal and Water give intelligence and a taste for sensual pleasure; Water and Earth confused together often give stupidity.**

〈This concerns the Five Phases’ nature and qi. Even in excess each has its own predominant quality; their partiality creates harm. The Five Phases apportioned to the four seasons and the Five Constants matched with the phases express a natural principle. Abundance, thinness, flourishing, excess, or mixture bring benevolence, righteousness, wisdom, trustworthiness, intelligence, or dullness according to their respective kinds.〉[^11]

<!-- source: L88-L92 -->
**Advanced age is obtained through balanced harmony; life is cut short through lopsided depletion.** 〈This again declares that a fate must receive harmonious qi, restating the earlier saying that venerable age comes when the Eight Characters are evenly poised. Excess or insufficiency falling into lopsided depletion—how could such a person enjoy advanced years?〉

**Chen and Xu controlling and clashing together must bring penal trouble; Zi and Mao punishing one another leave the household without ritual propriety or virtue.** 〈This takes up the earthly branches’ clashes and punishments. Chen and Xu, Kuigang, clashing together must be ominous; Zi and Mao, mother and child punishing one another, must bring disorder. These are the most serious clashes and punishments; the others are somewhat lighter. Yin, Shen, Si, and Hai constitute the Four Births: even control, restraint, punishment, or clash there does no great harm.〉[^12]

<!-- source: L94-L104 -->
**In abandoning Seal for Wealth, distinguish direct from indirect.** 〈Earlier it was said that the greatly distinguished employ Wealth rather than Seal. Wealth has direct and indirect forms, and so does Seal. Direct Seal encountering Wealth brings calamity; Indirect Wealth encountering Seal does no harm. Direct Wealth dislikes encountering Seal; Indirect Wealth does not. The principle is the same.〉[^13]

**In abandoning the stem to follow Killings, distinguish firm from yielding.** 〈Earlier it was said that those in authority employ Killings rather than Seal. Killings has firmness and yielding. Abandoning the heavenly stem to follow the earthly branches: yang is firm, yin yielding; Metal, Water, and Earth can follow, Wood and Fire cannot. Only after understanding what can and cannot follow does one know whether Seal can be employed.〉[^14]

**Hurting Officer without Wealth to rely upon remains poor despite its cleverness; Food God restraining Killings, on meeting Owl, brings poverty or early death.** 〈Hurting Officer and Food God belong to the same kind. Hurting Officer strips Officer, a great prohibition in fate. With Wealth it can be good, because Injury generates Wealth and Wealth generates Officer; without Wealth, judge poverty. Food God restraining Killings greatly fears Owl. Owl takes Food away; Killings loses restraint and attacks the body, hence early death.〉

<!-- source: L106-L116 -->
**A man with many Yang Blades must marry again; a woman offending Hurting Officer must remarry.** 〈Yang Blade meeting Killings in even balance can indeed give distinction, but too much harms the wife. A man takes Wealth as wife, and Blade controls it, hence repeated marriage. Hurting Officer with Wealth to rely upon can indeed give distinction, but too much harms the husband. A woman takes Officer as husband, and Injury controls it, hence remarriage.〉

**Poverty and low station arise when Officer’s place is injured; solitude and widowhood when Wealth Spirit is robbed.** 〈Officer is Lu: possessing Lu, how could one be poor and lowly? But a strong body with slight Officer, further entering a Hurting-Officer course, turns its back on Lu and loses Officer: how could poverty and low station not follow? Wealth is wife: possessing a wife, how could one be solitary? But slight Wealth with a strong body, further entering a Rob-Wealth course, drives away Horse and loses the wife: solitude and widowhood are fitting. Are not Wealth and Officer the essentials of human fate?〉

**Wealth approaching flourishing ground gives abundant blessing; Officer encountering Birth must make the fate illustrious.** 〈Earlier Food and Injury generating Wealth were called flourishing; here Wealth directly approaches flourishing ground. Jia employing Wu and Ji as Wealth at Si and Wu is an example. Earlier flourishing Wealth generated Officer; here Officer directly encounters Birth. Jia employing Geng and Xin as Officer at Si and Zi is an example. In both cases the body must be strong before blessing and distinction can follow.〉[^15]

<!-- source: L118-L128 -->
**Remove Killings and retain Officer, and blessing may be judged; remove Officer and retain Killings, and the result need not be lowly.** 〈A fate greatly fears mixed Officer and Killings. Employ Officer alone, or Killings alone: only with removal and retention can distinction be discussed. Hurting Officer and Yang Blade commonly prove inauspicious, yet when used to remove Officer and retain Killings, or remove Killings and retain Officer, they too may bring blessing.〉

**Who would know that Direct Officer may give no more than a lifetime’s stipend, whereas Seven Killings brings a name?** 〈Direct Officer and Seven Killings distinguish the gentleman from the petty man. Could the gentleman be inferior? Direct Officer may be pure, yet Seven Killings, once restrained, immediately develops distinction and reputation. But pure Direct Officer brings enduring blessing: how could Seven Killings compare with that? This saying emphasizes one particular aspect.〉

**Hurting Officer may instead bring a husband when Wealth and Fate possess qi; Owl may take away a child when blessing qi has no support.** 〈Women’s fates greatly fear Hurting Officer because it harms the husband, an easily understood principle. Yet Injury can instead bring a husband when Wealth and Fate possess qi: Hurting Officer generates Wealth, and Wealth generates Officer Star as husband. A woman takes Food God as child. When Owl seizes Food, even a child born does not survive. A woman relies on children for blessing; without a child, what blessing remains to speak of? Husband and child are the two principal stars in a woman’s fate, hence the discussion through Hurting Officer and Food God.〉[^16]

**Killings displayed in the heavenly stems without restraint is lowly; Wealth concealed in the branches and generated in secret is exceptional.** 〈Killings carries great weight in a fate. The foregoing discussion requires combination with Blade, restraint by Food, or following Killings. Without combination, restraint, or following, manifest Killings in a heavenly stem has no affinity and gives poverty and low station. Wealth is blessing. The foregoing discussion requires generation by Injury, breaking by You, or flourishing Food. But Wealth dislikes exposure and should be concealed: something hidden in the earthly branches generating it makes the endowment substantial and exceptional.〉

<!-- source: L130-L144 -->
**Three Xu clashing with Chen bring no slight calamity.** 〈Kuigang especially fears mutual clash. If the influences are evenly poised as Wealth and Officer Storehouses, however, there is no prohibition. Three Xu against one Chen on a Jiachen day bring calamity through greed for Wealth. Earth Net clashes with Heaven’s Net; hence the avoidance.〉[^17]

**Two unmixed stems give both profit and reputation.** 〈Two stems are rarely unmixed; hence both profit and reputation. Yet they cannot invariably be called noble. They may be Wealth and Killings, Officer and Seal, Killings and Blade, or a Five-Phase image. Only when they also enter a configuration should distinction be judged.〉

**Bingzi and Xinmao punish one another: wantonness like rolling waves.** 〈Bing and Xin combine, Zi and Mao punish: stems combine while branches punish. Bing–Xin forms a Water image; Zi–Mao lacks propriety, hence extreme sexual license. Women’s fates particularly avoid it.〉

**Zi, Wu, Mao, and You all complete: lost in wine and sensual pleasure.** 〈The preceding passage stressed Zi and Mao; this one adds Wu and You as the Four Defeats, called Peach Blossoms over the Fields. Their completeness often gives distinction, but also absorption in wine and sex. Women’s fates particularly avoid it.〉

<!-- source: L146-L156 -->
**Wealth brings calamity; greedy eating plants illness. A nephew becomes the heir; another family’s adopted daughter becomes the wife.**

〈Above, Wealth concealed was called exceptional. Yet Wealth is what all contend for: Yang Blade robbing it brings calamity through Wealth. Wealth cannot be reserved exclusively to oneself. Above, Food God restraining Killings was called excellent. Yet food is what people eat: Owl seizing it brings illness through food. Eating must not become greed. A man takes Officer and Killings as children. If Yang Blade or Rob Wealth makes Officer and Killings serve the other party, the brother has a son while I have none; thus a nephew becomes my heir. Direct and Indirect Wealth are wife. Without Wealth in its proper place in the pillars, but with it lodging for birth in another palace, one marries a daughter raised by someone other than her birth family.〉[^18]

**Day and hour clashing, with Mao and You at birth, must bring moves and changes; creation encountering Xu and Hai gives lifelong reverence for the gods.** 〈This again singles out Mao and You, the earthly branches that form the gates of sun and moon. Their appearance in day and hour brings unsettled movement. Xu and Hai are Heaven’s Gate: encountered in day and month, they commonly give reverence for the gods, or a monk’s or Daoist’s life.〉

**Yin controlling yin, yang controlling yang: Wealth Spirit becomes useful. Officer with no Officer, ghost with no ghost: excess brings the danger of collapse.** 〈Wealth and Officer are important, hence their mention again. Everyone knows to employ Direct Wealth, but not that yin controlling yin and yang controlling yang make Indirect Wealth surpass Direct Wealth in usefulness. Officer should not be absent, yet too much Officer instead means no Officer and is inauspicious. Killings should not be present, yet with much Killings one may follow its ghost and escape harm. In sum, when the influences are too strong and the body too weak to oppose them, danger and collapse follow.〉[^19]

<!-- source: L158-L164 -->
**Obtaining a configuration but losing the enclosure brings lifelong frustration; returning to the enclosure and obtaining the configuration gives early distinction.**

〈Obtaining the configuration means a Triple Combination; returning to the enclosure means the stem returning to Lu. A fate needs Birth, flourishing, and a formed configuration’s force to make blessing. If it obtains the configuration but loses the enclosure, even similar-image stems and Triple-Combination branches can constitute the day stem’s Rest, Imprisonment, Death, or Extinction: lifelong frustration follows. Obtaining the configuration and returning to the enclosure resemble the five planets ascending their halls and entering their enclosures: proper ground and proper season are both obtained. Early blessing must result. Bending and Straightening, Moistening Downward, and such configurations embody this.〉

**A fate meeting Owl works on behalf of a wealthy house; Dragon concealed in Hai and Mao brings a merchant’s profit in silk and strings of cash. Wealth and Officer both defeated mean death; Food God meeting Owl is ominous.**

〈Owl is indeed objectionable, but a person of humble station can put it to use by working for a wealthy household. Jia takes Bing Fire as Food; Bing can generate Wu Earth as Jia’s Wealth. Ren Water, however, is Jia Wood’s Owl and is subject to Wu’s command; Bing Fire relies upon Jia Wood. Their reciprocal generation creates a relation of host and guest. Others conduct commerce for themselves: this is Dragon concealed in Hai and Mao. Yin is Azure Dragon, Si the Grand Constant. Hai–Mao–Wei forms Wood; the Eight Characters employing what is concealed there as useful spirit, or employing Wealth, indicate profits in silk and cash. Wealth and Officer are Lu and Horse, most essential to a person. If both are at Defeat or Extinction, or luck again reaches those places and damages the useful spirit’s qi, how could death be avoided? Food God is a star of rank, generating Wealth and restraining Killings. When Owl takes Food, Killings has no restraint and Wealth no source: how could the result not be ominous?〉[^20]

<!-- source: L166-L176 -->
**Dingsi’s Solitary Luan gives an intelligent woman of verse; a day offending Naked Form and Bath brings turbid, unrestrained sensuality.**

〈This concerns women’s fates, though men are judged likewise. Solitary Luan comprises days such as Jiayin, Dingsi, Wushen, and Xinhai, seated on the Four Births, hence commonly intelligent. Naked Form and Bath are the Four Defeats—Zi, Wu, Mao, and You. In month or hour their occurrence does no harm; what is feared is the day stem seated on them, as in Jiazi, Gengwu, Dingmao, and Guiyou. The body sits on Peach Blossom Sha and also encounters combination, hence turbid sensual excess.〉[^21]

**Ding seated on Mao and encountering Ji Earth gives a voracious eater; Hai, the drink-spirit, meeting You Metal gives a lover of the wine cup.** 〈Ding seated on Mao Wood has Owl; meeting Ji brings Food, hence greed for food or calamity through eating. Hai is Dengming. You with Water added writes “wine”: a You-day person meeting Hai must love the cup. Punishment and clash added bring destitution or death through drink.〉[^22]

**Returning Lu with Wealth obtains blessing; Returning Lu without Wealth must remain poor.** 〈Returning Lu makes the body strong, so Wealth is employed. Without Wealth, Returning Lu alone has no use. Injury or Food generating Wealth is good in the month; Officer and Killings stealing Wealth’s qi are again feared.〉

**Wealth and Seal confused together bring lasting distress; direct and indirect disordered together must injure the body.** 〈Greed for Wealth Destroys Seal, hence the avoidance of mixture. But Wealth first and Seal afterward can instead accomplish blessing and is not judged by this rule. Mixed Officer and Killings with proper removal and retention can also be auspicious; disorder instead injures the body. A woman’s fate particularly dislikes direct and indirect mingling: those choosing a wife should understand this. Another account takes direct and indirect here to mean Wealth and Seal’s respective forms, but that makes “injuring the body” difficult to explain.〉[^23]

<!-- source: L178-L184 -->
**Tai Sui avoids battle; Yang Blade dislikes punishment and clash.**

〈This discusses blessing and calamity when the day stem and Year Ruler offend one another. The day offending the Year Ruler does no harm if the year serves as useful spirit. The six Ren days taking Bing and Ding as Wealth, with roots already present in the pillars, can turn even an offense against Tai Sui to good. A strong body gives an ominous result, a weak one no harm. Only when both day and luck course offend it does great calamity follow; rescue among the Five Phases diminishes its severity. Heavenly Clash and Earthly Strike are especially feared. Carefully investigate nature and affinity, yin and yang, the origin of things, and their true principle. The six Yi meeting Ji in year or luck have living Wood controlling living Earth, yet living intent remains and Wealth’s source is doubled. The six Jia meeting Wu have dead Wood controlling dead Earth: inauspicious, and in severe cases fatal.[^24]

A Yang-Blade configuration dislikes punishment and clash in year or luck; the petty man cannot endure offending it. Even where the Eight Characters already contain Yang Blade restrained by plentiful Killings, a Yang-Blade year or course is ominous; original Wealth makes it more serious. Injury, clash, punishment, and battle bring unforeseen calamity. The classic says: “Yang Blade clashing or combining with the Year Ruler—calamity comes suddenly.” Tai Sui is singled out for battle and Yang Blade for punishment and clash because these are their gravest dangers.〉

**Geng disturbed by Bing often lacks benevolence; Gui following Wu in combination brings no affinity between younger and elder.** 〈This concerns character and the workings of the heart. Metal and Fire punish one another, giving this fault and a harsh, violent nature. Gui is young yin, Wu old yang. Their combination and transformation are without affinity, hence the reference to younger and elder. A man born on a Wu day who meets Gui should marry a young woman; a woman born on a Gui day who meets Wu must marry an elderly man.〉[^25]

<!-- source: L186-L194 -->
**Neither following nor transforming, one remains delayed in official life; following and transforming achieved, one rises to achievement and reputation.**

〈No communication with the month’s qi, no destination in the hour, and solitary spirits offended: this is neither following nor transforming. Communication with monthly qi and a destination in the hour allow following and transformation to be discussed. A flourishing husband is followed in his transformation; a flourishing wife is followed in hers. Could a person’s public life and withdrawal be fixed by one circumstance, unchanging throughout life? Following and transformation forming a configuration complete wealth and distinction. Discuss following and transformation first, Wealth and Officer afterward. Following means the heavenly stem following the earthly branches: Yi born in the eighth month with repeated Metal branches, for example, is judged through Metal.〉

**Transformation traveling through flourishing Lu lives; transformation returning to extinguished Lu dies.** 〈Following and transformation require flourishing Lu, not Death and Extinction. Once transformation forms, a course through its own configuration’s flourishing Lu brings life: Ding–Ren transforming into Wood with a spring monthly command or eastern and southeastern courses, for example. Entering Metal territory, or meeting Shen or You in the hour, brings death.〉

**Meeting Birth ground can end one’s Lu in the prime of life; an hour returning to Defeat ground leaves old age without a secure end.** 〈If the fate already has Birth and Coming to Office and the course meets them again, consider Geng and Xin: their Coming to Office and Imperial Prosperity are Shen and You; Bing and Ding are their Officer, Jia and Yi their Wealth. Fire reaches Illness and Death in Shen–You courses, leaving them without Officer; Wood reaches Death and Extinction, leaving them without Wealth. Wealth and Officer both defeated and the useful spirit damaged can end one’s Lu even in the prime of life. The hour is the fruition and should not occupy the Five Phases’ Defeat ground: Metal’s is Wu, Wood’s Zi, Water and Earth’s You, Fire’s Mao. The hour governs the end; Defeat there brings an old age of obscurity, ruin, and no secure conclusion.〉[^26]

<!-- source: L196-L204 -->
**Ding seated upon You Metal, encountered by Bing or Xin, cuts off heirs; Wealth approaching Killings’ place gives a father who dies without returning home.**

〈Bing takes Ren as child, and Ren is defeated at You; Xin takes Bing as child, and Bing dies at You. The hour is the child’s palace: with Defeat and Death there, how could heirs remain? If there are heirs, the father must lose his Lu. Wealth is father. Wealth and Killings in one palace make matters difficult for him. Geng and Xin taking Jia as father, with Jiashen in year, month, or hour seated upon Killings’ palace, indicate a father dying abroad when Killings flourishes in year or luck. Another account takes Killings here as Robbery Sha: Wealth sits on Robbery Sha, and one further examines the father’s position. This also makes sense of the rhapsody.〉[^27]

**The Eight Exclusives have stem and branch of one kind; Killings courses and Killings years often prove ominous.** 〈Stem and branch of one kind are Jiayin, Yimao, Bingwu, Dingwei, Gengshen, Xinyou, Renzi, Guichou, Wuwu, Jiwei, Wuxu, and Jichou. A course gathering Killings must end Lu: trusting their strength, they struggle against Killings, which ultimately controls the body.〉[^28]

**Study these closely and ponder them: among ten thousand judgments of high and low station, not one will miss.**

〈I found that the old annotations split the text into fragments and lost the rhapsody’s purpose. I have therefore supplied these brief explanations to make the author’s intention clear.〉

## 2. Rhapsody on the True Treasure
### 真寳賦

〈Written by Wan Qi, Minister of War of the Ming; explained by Wan Yuwu.〉[^29]

**Officer Star bearing Blade, without control or breaking, commands the great powers of war and punishment; Wealth and Seal assisting one another, without punishment or clash, rise to the Yellow Pavilion and the dignity of the Three Excellencies.**

## 4. Rhapsody on the Secrets of the Golden Tripod
### 金鼎神秘賦

〈Written by Yuwu.〉

Human life has its allotted fate, and gain and loss differ sharply. How could wealth and distinction, poverty and lowliness, all be of one form? Red radiance fills the room when all Five Phases gather in noble territory; auspicious qi fills the house when the Four Pillars assemble upon blessed ground. Poverty first and wealth afterward come when the birth hour finds Lu and Horse in one region. Good at first and ill at the end come when day and hour offend Void or breaking. Lifelong hardship joins a thin foundation to a mixture of bad courses; lifelong glory joins a high fate to successive good courses.

Firm Metal encountering Fire becomes a vessel and surely rises above the crowd. Flourishing Fire obtaining Water achieves Completion and must excel. Wood needs Metal, but not too much; Water depends on Earth so that it does not disperse. Wu and Ji meeting Yin and Mao give Hooked Array its position; Ren and Gui seated at Si and Wu give Dark Warrior authority. Noble spirits entering the fate with Wonders and Instruments must make a duke or minister. Canopy approaching the hour with Solitary and Widow must make a monk or Daoist. Appointment as chancellor in the Jade Hall comes from brilliant Fire refined in Li’s palace; attendance before the golden gates comes from abundant Water virtue dwelling in Kan’s position.[^98]

Repeated Water positions indicate a wandering immortal of clouds and streams; repeated pure yang must make a child of the Buddhist gate. Birth gives intelligence and wisdom; Death and Defeat ignorance and obstinacy. Parents are hard to rely upon when year and month both fall in Void; wife and children are easily injured when day and hour both approach Solitary and Widow. Mao and You born into control and battle ruin the household and bring many calamities. Zi and Wu both occupying Death and Tomb drive a person to live as a stranger abroad. Zi and Wu most dislike Si and Hai; Mao and You particularly avoid Yin and Shen. Dwelling and Tomb receiving sha bring many breaches to the household. The hour falling in Heavenly Center gives few children; combinations atop the stems give many wives.[^99]

Without qi in the year, the original foundation is lost while young. Void within the month leaves the household desolate and unestablished. The day approaching Extinction gives much separation even when the wife is unharmed; the hour in the Tomb makes descendants uncompliant even when there are some. Accord with Earth’s refined qi gives distinction; obtaining Heaven’s season gives glory. Five Phases without qi give poverty; Four Pillars injured give low station. Yin or yang entirely alone gives solitude; stem and branch punishment and harm give illness. A useful spirit at Rest or Imprisonment makes wealth and distinction difficult; shallow refined qi commonly leads to an art or craft.

Punishment and control appearing together, with the body strong, make a military convict. Chen and Xu added to one another with injury indicate a prison clerk. Metal and Water idle and sluggish make a destitute, unworldly pauper; Traveling Horse struck by clash makes one who rushes through the dust of the world. Kuigang repeatedly offended gives birth in a butcher’s family; repeated You and Xu put the person among servants. Zi and Wu both enclosed within the pillars give high place in the central government; stems and branches of one qi raise rank to prince or marquis.

An expanse of pure yang controlling the fate alone brings injury even if not death. A full chart of Seal-Ribbon generating the body gives wealth if not distinction. Year and month both injured leave parents, wives, and concubines in difficulty; year and hour both injured endanger parents and heirs. The year clashing with the day, while the parents flourish, makes wives and concubines hard to preserve. The hour clashing with the year, while children flourish, makes parents easily injured. Breaking the fate loses both parents young; breaking the month harms siblings in maturity; breaking the day leaves one standing alone; breaking the hour leaves old age without fruition; breaking conception falls upon the mother alone. These are only the broad outlines, not yet the finest particulars.[^100]

<!-- source: L782-L784 -->

## 5. Rhapsody on the Hidden Mechanism
### 元機賦

The Great Ultimate divides into Heaven and Earth; one qi differentiates into yin and yang. Take the day stem as master and discuss Wealth and Officer in particular. Take the configuration from the monthly branch to distinguish high and low station. A configuration that is incorrect fails; absence of a configuration with something useful succeeds. With Officer, do not seek exceptional configurations; with an exceptional configuration, Officer Star is unwelcome. Officer, Seal, Wealth, and Food unbroken are pure and lofty; Killings, Injury, Owl, and Blade, rightly employed, are most auspicious.[^118]

When good and evil mingle, remove evil and exalt good. When auspicious and inauspicious mingle, avoid injuring the auspicious and turning toward the inauspicious. With Officer and Killings both present, a strong body and restrained Killings are exceptional. With Killings and Seal, flourishing Wealth that assists Killings is feared as calamity. A strong body and slight Killings need not fear a Killings course; heavy Killings and a light body find blessing in restraint’s country. A flourishing body and much Seal welcome Wealth’s ground; much Wealth and a weak body fear Wealth’s country.

A man encountering Companions, Robbers, and Hurting Officer harms wife and child. A woman offending Hurting Officer and Indirect Seal loses children and injures the husband. Losing both parents in childhood comes from excessively heavy Wealth Star; solitary, harmful relations come from a flourishing body without support. The year clashing with the monthly command leaves the ancestors to found a household. The day clashed by the governing thread breaks the marriage string and joins it again. Day and hour clashing injure wife and child. The day communicating with monthly qi obtains ancestral support and settles the person.[^119]

Thus Wood returning to spring’s growth can borrow Geng and Xin as authority; Fire born in summer can obtain deep blessing from Ren and Gui. Earth meeting Chen, Xu, Chou, and Wei wins a name through plentiful Wood. Metal born in Shen, You, Si, or Chou develops blessing in Fire’s country. Water dwelling in Hai and Zi is difficult for Wu and Ji to invade. The body seated in Rest or Imprisonment spends a lifetime without completion. A flourishing body welcomes courses through Lu and Horse; a weak body avoids Wealth and Officer. Obtaining the season, judge flourishing; losing the command, judge decline. Four Pillars without roots can flourish through the season; a day stem without qi becomes strong upon meeting Robbers.

A weak body welcomes Seal; a flourishing master suits Officer. Jia and Yi born in autumn with Metal exposed gain glory in Water, Wood, or Fire courses. Bing and Ding born in winter with abundant Water gain distinction in Fire, Earth, or Wood regions. Wu and Ji born in spring find rescue toward west and south. Geng and Xin growing in summer need not fear Water-and-Earth courses. Ren and Gui encountering flourishing Earth suit Metal and Wood for glory. A weak body with Seal need not fear flourishing Killings. A strong body and slight Killings may employ Wealth. A strong body employing Wealth should possess Killings; a weak body employing Seal should remove Wealth.

Hurting Officer with a weak body and no Seal—upon what can it rely? A strong body without Wealth—through what can blessing develop? Employ Wealth for a flourishing body, Seal for a weak one. Employing Wealth, remove Seal; employing Seal, remove Wealth: thus greater blessing develops. This is what is meant by retaining what is welcomed and removing what is disliked.[^120]

Much Wealth with a weak body finds glory in a body-strengthening course. A strong body with declining Wealth develops blessing in flourishing-Wealth country. Repeated Officer Star needs restraint; repeated Food God must avoid Officer’s country. Stubborn Metal without Fire cannot become a great instrument; strong Wood without Metal struggles to establish an unblemished name. Much Wood obtaining Earth gives substantial property; Fire’s blaze meeting Water gives rank and honor. Officer and Seal unbroken give glory; without Seal or Officer, take distinction from an appropriate configuration.

Yang Blade does not fear Indirect Officer; Metal Spirit most suits restraint. Mixed-Qi Wealth and Officer develop through punishment and clash. Excessive Officer and Wealth must topple upon reaching flourishing. An overly strong body welcomes Wealth and Officer; a master too yielding does not suit Lu and Horse. Flourishing Officer, Seal, and Wealth entering the Tomb encounter calamity. Hurting Officer and Food God with a flourishing body provoke calamity upon meeting the Storehouse. In judging a course’s distinction, take the branch; in weighing the year, seek the stem. Much Seal develops through Wealth; flourishing Wealth need not fear Companions.[^121]

Configuration clear and formation correct bring wealth, distinction, and glory. Seal flourishing and Officer manifest give an exceptional name. Do not necessarily take combining Officer as noble or combining Killings as ominous. Peach Blossom bearing Killings delights in illicit love; repeated Canopy brings much injury. A lifetime without development has Eight Characters at Rest and Imprisonment; a lifetime without authority has a declining body meeting ghosts. A strong body suits draining and expenditure; a declining body welcomes support and assistance. Seek the qi of balanced harmony; do not permit excess or insufficiency. Follow this method in detailed judgment, and blessing and calamity answer like shadow and echo.

## 6. Rhapsody of Interwoven Threads
### 絡繹賦

Investigate creation’s primal mechanism and fathom the Five Phases’ subtle principle; distinguish the gains and losses of a person’s fate and decide a lifetime’s flourishing and decline. Take the rule from the day stem; discuss rise and decline through the monthly branch. Jia and Yi belong to Wood and most welcome spring birth. Ren and Gui belong to Water and particularly suit winter flourishing. Bing and Ding Fire shine in summer; Geng and Xin Metal sharpen in autumn. When the transforming spirit flourishes and the restraining spirit declines, decisive action is lacking. ## 7. Rhapsody of Gold and Jade

Search the Eight Characters, discussing Wealth and Officer above all; next investigate the Five Phases and seek their seasonal qi. Noble spirits attending and arching, Lu and Horse flying through Heaven, remote combination and empty configurations—all must avoid punishment and clash. ## Translator’s Endnotes

[^01]: **Controlling text and comparison witnesses.** **S** is the repository’s [Chinese juan twelve](../source/juan-12.md), exported from [Wikisource revision 761707](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=761707); its checksum appears in the header. **G626**, **G627**, and **G628** denote the parallel *Sanming tonghui* material in the *Gujin Tushu Jicheng*, Arts juan [626](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=1942589), [627](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=1942590), and [628](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=1942591). Comparisons are selective; specific readings and the final verse transition were also checked directly on scan pages 105–106 of the parallel collection’s volume 472, identified in the relevant notes. These are parallel witnesses, not additional Siku editions; agreement does not establish independent descent. Their parent-page revisions do not freeze the separately editable Page transcriptions they include. Machine-assisted punctuation in the comparison texts is not treated as historical evidence. The shorter, unlabelled Wikisource collection explicitly lacks juan ten through twelve and cannot supply a witness for this volume. S governs the material translated, including its repetitions, qualifications, and closing labels.

[^02]: **Terminology and reference points.** 用神 is the “useful spirit,” the influence put to work in a particular analysis, not necessarily one universally favorable modern “element.” 鬼 is “ghost,” often the phase controlling the reference phase; 煞 may be Seven Killings, another named sha, or an adverse influence more generally. 財官, “Wealth and Officer,” is not always restricted to the two direct categories. Ordinary stem phases, transformed phases, nayin, and year-based methods are distinguished where the text distinguishes them. Frequent 己/巳/已 confusions are read according to syntax and the named stems or branches; substantive departures and unresolved cases are noted individually. The commentary’s “I” is its historical author, not the translator. Its claims of unfailing prediction are translated, not endorsed.

[^03]: **Title and attribution.** 元理 is rendered “Original Principle,” retaining the Siku heading rather than silently changing it to 玄理, “mysterious principle.” The source names 徐大升, elsewhere 徐大昇, Xu Dasheng, as author and 萬育吾, Wan Yuwu, as commentator. Yuwu is the name under which the compiler speaks elsewhere in the work. This attribution is the source’s, not an independently established account of authorship.

[^04]: **Four complete sequences, followed by a fifth.** The passage gives five examples each of excessive parental generation, excessive offspring draining their source, resistance or reversal within control, and injury to a weak phase. It then gives five instances of strong qi being released through what it generates. The last five are not an additional cycle of direct conquest: strong Metal’s Water, for example, is its offspring. The commentary nevertheless groups the process under control and restraint. The translation retains that terminology and all twenty-five examples.

[^05]: **A supported small repair and a distinction.** S’s 肯言, literally “willing to speak,” is read 首言, “speak first,” with G626 and the position of the passage. Blade supplies the weapon and Killings the command: “Authority” in the following maxim is therefore a name for Killings, not an extra spirit. 胥吏 denotes the lower clerical establishment, not an official of ministerial rank.

[^06]: **Strength is conditional, not universally favorable.** The second maxim explicitly describes following Killings. Renewed strength of the day stem disrupts that relation by creating resistance. The commentary is not retracting every earlier preference for a strong Day Master; it is distinguishing a different kind of configuration.

[^07]: **Indirect employment.** The commentary explains “not employing” Officer or Seal through the generating links Wealth→Officer and Killings→Seal. Its compressed 非不用印用官 is construed by the explanation on either side: Officer and Seal are included through the influences that generate them. It does not instruct the reader simply to ignore Officer and Seal wherever they appear.

[^08]: **Breaking in a broader sense.** The commentator expressly explains the You→Mao→Wu sequence by phase control and the four cardinal branches. It should not be silently rewritten as a table of the conventional six branch breaks. The relation between “Food first, Killings afterward” and this branch example is asserted by the source without a complete worked chart; none has been supplied by the translator.

[^09]: **Images as well as technical terms.** Hollow Fire and solid Metal evoke a flame’s empty center and the resonant hollow of a finished metal vessel. The commentary makes Fire’s refining essential to Metal’s usefulness. These lines should not be flattened into the modern labels “weak Fire” and “strong Metal”: that would erase the argument’s imagery and change its distinctions.

[^10]: **Different growth cycles retained.** The commentary identifies living Wood with Yi and distinguishes Yi dying at Hai from Jia being born there; it likewise contrasts Geng’s Birth at Si with Xin’s at Zi. Other passages use a single phase-wide cycle instead. Neither is imposed upon the other here. The accompanying material images—Metal emerging from Earth or bearing Water—belong to this explanation.

[^11]: **Ethical correspondences.** The Five Constants are benevolence, righteousness, ritual propriety, trustworthiness, and wisdom. This passage names only the virtues its particular examples require; the absent explicit Fire/propriety clause has not been manufactured. Claims about intelligence, sexuality, or moral worth are the text’s analogical judgments, not modern psychological findings.

[^12]: **Relative severity, not a universal exemption.** The commentary treats the four Birth branches more leniently here, although other passages in this collection assign severe effects to Yin–Si–Shen punishment or the opposed Birth branches. Its qualification remains local to this argument. Zi–Mao’s mother–child image uses Water generating Wood.

[^13]: **The commentary misquotes its own antecedent.** The earlier maxim says the greatly distinguished employ Wealth rather than Officer; this commentary recalls it as Wealth rather than Seal. G626 shares the latter wording. Both are preserved. The several direct/indirect Wealth–Seal judgments also remain as transmitted, rather than being adjusted to a single modern rule.

[^14]: **A restrictive school rule.** “Metal, Water, and Earth can follow; Wood and Fire cannot” is explicitly stated in S and G626. Other discussions in the book allow different accounts of following and transformation. The claim is not silently widened or harmonized. Firmness and yielding here concern the capacity to abandon the original stem’s position.

[^15]: **Officer used collectively.** Jia’s Geng is ordinarily Indirect Officer or Seven Killings, while Xin is Direct Officer. The commentary calls both 官. Its Si and Zi Birth positions follow the differently directed Geng and Xin cycles; replacing Zi with Si to enforce a phase-wide Metal cycle would alter the text.

[^16]: **The author’s gendered social framework.** Husband, wife, son, heir, and official rank are woven into the technical relationships. The assertion that a woman’s blessing depends on children is preserved as a historical judgment, not adopted as an assessment of a woman’s worth. The text’s severe reproductive and mortality predictions are not medical guidance.

[^17]: **A reversed net description.** The commentary says Earth Net clashes with Heaven’s Net, although its three Xu and one Chen would ordinarily suggest the reverse direction under the familiar Xu–Hai/Chen–Si assignment. The main text retains its words. The same passage explicitly exempts a properly balanced Wealth/Officer-Storehouse relation, so clash alone is not its whole argument.

[^18]: **Whose daughter becomes the wife.** 義女 here is explained as a daughter raised by someone other than her birth parents. It is not an instruction to marry one’s own adopted daughter. Likewise the nephew’s succession concerns continuation of a family line, not a general modern category of guardianship. The puns on 財 and 食 move deliberately between technical Wealth/Food and actual property/eating.

[^19]: **An intentionally paradoxical maxim.** 官無官鬼無鬼 is elucidated by the commentary: too much Officer may cancel the benefit of office, while numerous ghosts may admit following and cease to injure. The concluding warning concerns excess that the body cannot bear. The English preserves the paradox instead of replacing it with a uniform “more is worse” formula.

[^20]: **A compressed host–guest chain.** G626 shares the account in which Wu directs Ren and Bing relies on Jia. The grammar and reciprocal agency are compressed; the named links are retained rather than converted into an invented full chart. 青龍 and 太常 are Azure Dragon and Grand Constant, named symbolic influences in this passage, not new heavenly stems. 絲緡 evokes silk and strings of cash, hence commercial wealth.

[^21]: **A difficult word retained.** Both S and G626 read 詩女, literally a woman of poetry, rather than the more predictable 才女, talented woman. “Woman of verse” preserves the transmitted wording. The four example days are the source’s abbreviated illustration of Solitary Luan, not a complete standardized table imported from elsewhere.

[^22]: **A character pun.** 酉 with the Water radical 氵 forms 酒, “wine.” The maxim’s point is lost if You is translated simply as “Rooster.” 登明, Dengming, is Hai’s named spirit. Ding seated on Mao is a day stem on a branch, not the stem 丁 being treated as an earthly branch.

[^23]: **An objection belongs to the source.** The final sentence questions an alternative reading of “direct and indirect” as the two Wealths and two Seals. That reservation is the commentator’s, not a translator’s insertion. His preferred immediate discussion concerns mixed Officer and Killings.

[^24]: **Difficult annual rules preserved.** S and G626 both state that the strong body is in danger and the weak body unharmed in this discussion, alongside the rule that an annual useful spirit may make the encounter auspicious. The statement is not reversed to conform to a more familiar strength-based rule. Living Yi/Ji and dead Jia/Wu are the text’s material analogies; they do not describe actual plants or soil in a birth chart.

[^25]: **An age metaphor within combination.** The commentary calls Gui “young yin” and Wu “old yang,” then extends that metaphor to spouses’ ages. This is not a chronological calculation. Its claim of an unfeeling combination differs from other favorable accounts of Wu–Gui in the same collection and remains unreconciled.

[^26]: **Birth can conflict with what the chart uses.** The commentary explains its adverse judgment by the Day Master’s flourishing coinciding with Wealth and Officer’s decline. “Ending Lu” translates 不祿, an expression that can imply death, not merely loss of salary. The maxims should not be read as declaring every Birth position dangerous.

[^27]: **Kinship assignments differ.** The commentary assigns Ren as Bing’s child and Bing as Xin’s child: the controlling relation is being used, not uniformly what the Day Master generates. Its alternative interpretation of 煞 as Robbery Sha is expressly marked as another account. The reference to the hour explains the child-palace but does not provide a complete natal chart.

[^28]: **Twelve examples under an eightfold name.** S enumerates twelve day pairs under 八專, Eight Exclusives; all twelve are translated. The list includes Dingwei, Guichou, Wuwu, and other cases whose relation is not a simple identity of the stem’s ordinary phase with the branch’s main phase. No entries have been removed or substituted to make a standard modern eight-day list. The source’s own enumeration is the evidence.

[^29]: **A doubtful attributional heading.** S writes 萬騏 and calls him Minister of War. A likely related biographical notice for 萬祺, Wan Qi, in the *Gujin Tushu Jicheng*, [Arts juan 629, revision 1942592](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=1942592), citing the *Jiangxi gazetteer*, describes a fate specialist who rose to Minister of Works (工部尚書). The identity is probable, but this discrepancy does not by itself establish how S’s heading should be repaired. The English retains the office stated in S and records the problem here. The commentator is again Wan Yuwu, not necessarily the same man as the attributed author.

[^30]: **Courtly images and offices.** Yellow Pavilion, Purple Enclosure, the Three Excellencies, Three Terraces, and Eight Seats evoke the highest civil offices; “five horses” evokes a governor’s equipage. The censorate and remonstrance offices exercised supervisory and critical functions at court. These literary titles are not extra astrological indicators to calculate independently. The book uses offices from different periods together rather than describing one dynasty’s administrative chart.

[^31]: **A qualified exception.** This is not the familiar blanket warning against Hurting Officer Meeting Officer. The commentator distinguishes original presence from absence, the need for Food’s assistance, and excessive restraint. 魏相 is the personal name Wei Xiang, not merely “the chancellor of Wei”; compare his biography in the [*Han History*, juan 74, revision 1491986](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=1491986). The Qilin Pavilion supplies an image of commemorated merit, not a second natal case chart.

[^32]: **Similar ingredients, different weighting.** Here merely equal Wealth and Seal are called ordinary, while Seal commanding the month with a little Killings and Wealth is praised. The passage distinguishes strength, placement, and function, not a fixed recipe in which any three favorable labels guarantee distinction.

[^33]: **A configuration name, not virtue in general.** 福德 is illustrated by winter Ren/Gui days and their Wealth-and-Officer requirements. “Without Earth” in the maxim is material to the Officer relationship. It is not silently replaced by “without morality.” The later text also uses 福德 in a broader sense; context governs.

[^34]: **Gen and Qian.** 艮, Gen, is the northeastern Mountain trigram; 乾, Qian, the northwestern Heaven trigram. The examples are the named Renyin-hour and Yihai-hour configurations discussed earlier in the book. Their avoidance of Officer/Killings and filling or clashing the relevant positions belongs to those configurations, not to every Ren or Jia day. The Jia commentary’s own “Direct Officer appears naturally” is retained beside its later avoidance of Officer and Killings.

[^35]: **The four transmitted days.** S and G626 list Bingwu, Dingsi, Xinhai, and Guihai. Xinhai is not silently replaced by Renzi merely to reproduce a more familiar four-day enumeration. The repeated formula introduced as another wording of the rhapsody is translated as an actual quotation within the commentary.

[^36]: **Year Virtue as year-stem Killings.** 歲德 here expressly means Seven Killings in the year stem. It is not automatically identical with Heavenly Virtue or a benevolent deity. The example’s combination with Blade, generation by Wealth, and transformation through Seal are retained separately from direct restraint, which this passage prohibits.

[^37]: **Bing for Xin in Xu month.** The source makes Bing both Heavenly/Monthly Virtue and Direct Officer. “Becoming Virtue” consequently identifies a technical relation with a computed auspicious influence; it need not mean a stem changing its ordinary phase. The following distinctions among Officer, Wealth, Seal, and the day stem describe where the benefit is assigned.

[^38]: **Two interpretations of 德秀.** One account treats Virtue and Excellence as named positions, while the alternative takes the expression more broadly as blessing, virtue, and refined qi. Both are preserved. The Han and Jin figures provide exemplary political outcomes; the text supplies no complete charts for Wang Ling, Xie An, or Wang Shang at these points.

[^39]: **A genuine departure from a common maxim.** S and G626 both praise one Officer amid repeated Hurting Officer while warning against a further Officer course when Officer was already present. The rough-stone/jade image is therefore retained, not “corrected” into an unqualified prohibition of Officer. No extra unstated strength condition has been inserted.

[^40]: **Branches are not sufficient by themselves.** In both four-branch groupings the commentary explicitly asks what the heavenly stems do. Wonders and Instruments translate 奇儀; the text does not furnish an additional calculation here. “Power that makes the sovereign tremble” preserves the potentially alarming force of 威權震主 rather than softening it into ordinary career success.

[^41]: **Trigram language within a phase configuration.** 震 is movement, Thunder, and the eastern Wood region; 離 is brightness, Fire, and the south. The English lets both the imagery and the technical names remain. “West and south” translates 西南 in the context of courses and does not prescribe travel to a particular modern destination.

[^42]: **Palace and local careers distinguished.** 青瑣 and 黃門 evoke close service in the palace; 民牧 and 專城 local administration. 轉運 is rendered transport administration, not simply “changing luck,” because 官居 explicitly makes it an office. The same commentary then returns to luck courses, so the two meanings must not be confused.

[^43]: **Two configurations, not one combined birth-hour rule.** Zi remotely reaching Si and Chou remotely reaching Si are grouped for their common need of Wealth and Seal. “Cold felt mat” is the literary image of a poor scholar’s or minor teaching post. Nothing in the wording requires a chart to contain both Zi and Chou simultaneously.

[^44]: **Whose Lu and Birth.** Xin’s Lu at You and Ji’s Birth at You are the relations singled out for a Jia Day Master. The second uses the yin Earth cycle. The Wu Yue chart and Gengwu death-year are the text’s report, not independently verified natal data; the translation does not turn the case into a modern biographical certification.

[^45]: **An omitted day stem and a name.** Both S and G626 begin 日以庚 without specifying the day stem. Geng as Direct Officer, Ren as Direct Seal, and Wu as Direct Wealth would indicate Yi under ordinary relations, but Yi is not inserted into the main text. These Three Wonders are expressly Officer, Seal, and Wealth, not the usual named stem triads. Both witnesses write 譚論 for Minister Tan Lun; the spelling 譚綸 is supported by the [*Ming History*, juan 212, revision 2594838](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=2594838). The historical spelling does not establish the accuracy of the reported chart. Ding Fire’s Birth at You in Hu Zongxian’s example follows the yin cycle and needs no repair.

[^46]: **A damaged configuration name.** S’s 欄又 is G626’s 欄叉, the abbreviated name of the Well-Railing configuration. The English restores the recognized name, without adding a new rule from an earlier chapter. 補袞, mending the sovereign’s robes, and 阿衡 are images of ministerial assistance, not literal employment as a tailor.

[^47]: **澄 rather than 沉.** S’s 清沉, “clear and sinking,” is read 清澄, “clear and limpid,” as in G626 and S’s own following quotation. The commentary speaks of two complete triple configurations, although these would require more branch positions than the four natal pillars alone; its compression is retained, not filled out with invented year, luck, or conception positions.

[^48]: **Substantially different commentaries.** S emphasizes assistance from Robbers/Blade, Ren/Gui, and Wu/Ji. G626 instead gives an extended example of Yi Wood born in summer, drained by Fire, and relieved by a little Water. S’s full commentary remains controlling. The alternative is not substituted merely because it reads more smoothly.

[^49]: **Two explanations in S, another in G626.** S first uses Azure Dragon and White Tiger with cloud, rain, and remotely summoned Wind, then supplies the explicit Chen–Yin–Ren–Si account. G626 gives a different arrangement involving repeated stems and specified branch positions. The translation preserves S’s two accounts and does not amalgamate them into a synthetic chart formula. Filling an inferred position is different from having that position remain available to remote combination.

[^50]: **Ma’s school and Tao Zhu’s wealth.** The crimson curtain evokes Ma Rong’s teaching. The [*Later Han History*, juan 60A, revision 1567629](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=1567629), describes him teaching students before a crimson gauze curtain. 菁莪 is the traditional imagery of nurturing scholars. Tao Zhu is invoked as the archetype of great wealth; no natal chart for him is given here. S’s detailed Jia-day commentary is retained even though G626’s corresponding passage is much shorter.

[^51]: **An important negative differs.** S says 用神休廢, the useful spirit at Rest or decline; G626 instead says 用神不廢, not disabled. S is retained and the disagreement left visible. Xiao and Cao evoke Xiao He and Cao Shen beginning in clerical service; the passage uses their careers as a literary comparison, not a worked astrological proof.

[^52]: **“My own” is the commentator’s voice.** The four pairs Renwu/Guichou/Gengyin/Bingxu are presented by the historical commentator as his own chart. The Minister He example remains a separate case. No identification of that minister beyond what the text supplies is assumed, and neither chart has been recalculated from an independently established birth record.

[^53]: **A symbolic compound.** The commentary itself assigns Tortoise to Water, Snake to Fire, and the sword to Metal. This particular image should not be replaced by the more usual iconographic description of Dark Warrior as tortoise and snake. It also names two full triple configurations without specifying how every branch position is supplied; the translation preserves the formulation rather than constructing an extra-pillar example.

[^54]: **Not identical stems.** The source explicitly explains 一氣相生 by Jia→Bing→Wu→Geng and rejects the interpretation 天元一氣. “One qi” here describes continuous generation, not four identical day-stem signs or one unmixed phase. The terminal Ren is not added to the abbreviated example.

[^55]: **Heaven and Earth exchanging positions.** The explanation places Kun/Earth above Qian/Heaven, an image of communication rather than a literal cosmography. Month and year are distinguished from day and hour. The placement should not be flattened into a statement that merely having both Shen and Hai is always favorable.

[^56]: **Examination imagery and a compressed list.** Taking the 鰲頭, giant turtle’s head, means occupying the foremost place in examination success. The commentary lists Wu, Xu, Hai, Mao, and Wei without a complete distribution among four pillars. It is an abbreviated selection of relevant branch signs, not a warrant to invent a fifth natal pillar or require all five simultaneously. It also does not explicitly explain the maxim’s Earth condition.

[^57]: **A supported surname repair.** S’s 冠凖 is corrected to 寇準, Kou Zhun, with G626. 子儀 is the conventional short reference to Guo Ziyi. Their names evoke eminent civil and military authority; the passage supplies no personal charts for either and should not be treated as independent evidence for their birth data.

[^58]: **Relations relative to a useful spirit.** Hurting Officer is called Seal’s wife because Seal controls it. The reference phase has shifted from the Day Master to Seal. G626 reads “restraint of Killings” where S specifies “restraint of the year’s Killings”; S’s more specific reference remains. This is not a new category of actual spouse.

[^59]: **Two calendrically intelligible hours.** S’s 巳亥 and 巳丑 are read 己亥 and 己丑—Jihai and Jichou—with G626 and the hour-stem sequence for a Bing day. The imagery differs: Fire upon Heaven in Hai, and sunlight above Earth in Chou. The latter invokes the *Changes*’ language of an honored lord receiving repeated gifts. Yao Lai’s chart is retained as reported.

[^60]: **Three relationships in one hour.** Xinhai supplies Xin in its stem and Jia/Ren in its branch: the commentary identifies them as Indirect Wealth, Direct Seal, and Direct Officer for Ding. The “Three Wonders” therefore differs even from the immediately earlier enumeration of three direct relationships. The English follows the specific account rather than enforcing one definition across the book.

[^61]: **Army, not a geographical injunction.** Kun above Kan is the Earth-over-Water hexagram 師, Army. The source explicitly connects troops with the people as Water is contained in Earth. Its exclusion of Zi in the daily branch, while accepting year or hour, is retained; this is a placement-sensitive image.

[^62]: **Trigram phases are not ordinary branch phases.** Si is read as Xun/Wind/Wood in this image, although Si’s ordinary branch phase is Fire. The commentator therefore calls it Xin Metal’s Wealth. Replacing Wood by Fire would erase the argument. “The yielding advances upward” and the inner-Xun/outer-Li pattern evoke the *Changes*’ 鼎, Tripod, rather than a new standard branch combination.

[^63]: **Specific and general honors differ.** 榜眼 conventionally denotes second place in the highest examination ranking, whereas 鰲頭 is the image of taking the leading place. The commentary equates their technical underlying configuration, not necessarily the literal rank of every person possessing it. “Second place” is not silently changed to first merely to make the expressions identical.

[^64]: **Officer has its own Wealth.** A Day Master’s Blade may serve as the Officer’s wife/Wealth when the relationship is recalculated from Officer. The text is explicit about that change of reference. It separately distinguishes obtaining the season, bearing Blade anywhere in stem or branch, and open exposure in the heavenly stems. These qualifications are retained rather than condensed into “Officer plus Blade is noble.”

[^65]: **A proverb of fruitless waiting.** 守株 evokes waiting beside a tree stump for another accidental windfall rather than adapting to changed conditions. It contrasts the earlier active official career with a now obstructed course. “Purple and vermilion” in the following maxim are the colors of rank, not phase colors to be calculated.

[^66]: **The source openly criticizes a reading.** The maxim says the day stem is strong; the commentary quotes an alternative with strong Killings and explicitly prefers it. Both are translated so that the criticism remains audible. It then questions whether Gong Sheng’s death nevertheless reflects excess. The [*Han History*, juan 72, revision 7906820](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=7906820), relates his refusal to serve a second ruling house and his death after refusing food and drink. This identifies the historical allusion; it does not verify the astrological explanation.

[^67]: **Blade is represented by stems here.** Jia seeing Yi and Yi seeing Jia are explicitly called Blade, and the passage then leads those stems to their own Birth positions. This differs from using only a fixed Yang-Blade branch table. Yuanling in the preceding line is the conventional personal-name reference to Fang Xuanling; Yingzhou supplies the courtly image of an eminent literary circle, not an instruction to seek a geographical island.

[^68]: **Seal in S, Blade in G626.** S reads 因劫帶印, “Robbers bearing Seal”; G626 reads 因劫帶刃, “Robbers bearing Blade,” which agrees more directly with the commentary’s Yang-Blade explanation. S’s wording remains in the main text because the possible variant is substantive and the surrounding doctrine does not make it impossible. The note allows the reader to distinguish the witnesses.

[^69]: **Returning Lu belongs to Officer here.** The commentary expressly identifies Gui’s Lu at Zi for a Bing day. It is not the Day Master returning to its own Lu in the birth hour. 絲綸, silken words, is a metaphor for imperial commands and their composition; it does not add a new spirit called “Silk.”

[^70]: **Two explanatory levels, followed by dissent.** The full chain Blade→Food→Wealth→Officer→Seal→body is preserved. The commentator then states that Wealth and Officer’s Birth positions alone suffice and that the longer chain is unnecessary. This is an internal criticism, not the translator’s abridgment. 蒂 is a stalk or point of attachment, here explicitly glossed as Birth.

[^71]: **Kun’s receptivity.** The example uses repeated Earth and Shen as Kun to develop an image of receptivity and support. Dark Warrior represents Water and Azure Dragon Wood in the preferences that follow. This is not the claim that every strong-Earth chart needs the same useful spirit regardless of season or constitution.

[^72]: **An unresolved numerical phrase.** S and G626 both end the explanation with 四九上下應之, “four and nine respond above and below.” Neither consulted passage supplies a calculation or enough detail to establish a unique hexagram-line reconstruction. The words are retained; no missing six, three, or complete line-pair formula has been invented.

[^73]: **Mountain beneath Earth.** Kun above Gen forms 謙, Modesty. The contrast between a high mountain and low earth explains bending the high beneath the low. It is the stated basis of the image, not a literal instruction to bury a mountain or an assertion that ordinary branch Earth has become a different phase.

[^74]: **Wealth as an auspicious indicator.** The commentary gives Bingshen and Jimao examples and identifies their Wealth stems with Virtue positions. “Becoming Virtue” is thus a shared designation, not necessarily a stem transformation. 胙土分茅 is the formal imagery of receiving land and enfeoffment. The two cases are preserved without supplying missing year or hour pillars.

[^75]: **An identification left open.** 王曾 is Wang Zeng. 葉正 is transmitted as Ye Zheng in both S and G626; the consulted passages do not securely identify the intended examination laureate. The name is not silently replaced by a better-known Ye. The two brief sayings have no separate commentary in S, so none is invented in the translation.

[^76]: **What the compiler says he is doing.** The closing paragraph identifies the rhapsody as an extension of Ziping through Seven Killings, Blade, Hurting Officer, and Food, with Wealth, Officer, and Seal providing support. It attributes the examples to Lord Wan’s hearing and observation. That is the source’s account of its evidence, not a modern verification of the named people’s charts. The paragraph also belongs to this treatise, not to the separate Siku editorial preface.

[^77]: **A title of synthesis.** 金聲玉振 recalls the description of comprehensive accomplishment in [*Mencius*, “Wan Zhang II,” section 1, revision 7912092](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=7912092): the sounding metal begins the ordered performance and the jade concludes it. “Bronze bells and jade chimes” preserves the musical image rather than treating 金 here as the technical Metal phase. The present rhapsody does not explicitly attribute itself to Mencius.

[^78]: **Eight analytical approaches.** 類、屬、從、化、照、返、鬼、伏 are the Eight Methods elsewhere in the collection. This opening compresses the account and contrasts manifest forms with remotely summoned or arched ones. The English does not silently supply every omitted method or make each term a modern standardized configuration name.

[^79]: **A source disagreement within the explanation.** S reads 水不南奔, Water not running south, where G627 has 木不南奔, Wood not running south, the phrase repeated later in S. S’s Water remains in the main text. The quotation linking sweetness with Sowing and Reaping and bitterness with Flaming Upward is found in the [*Documents*, “Great Plan”](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/尚書/洪範). Its purpose is phase imagery, not dietary advice.

[^80]: **Not all “combinations” are the same formal category.** Bing–Xin and Ding–Ren are stem combinations, while Yin–Xu and Chou–Si invoke parts of triple branch formations. The commentary groups them poetically as paired wings. Its second account relaxes the requirement for literal Water imagery; both accounts remain distinct.

[^81]: **Nayin, not only ordinary phases.** Jiawu/Yiwei are Gold in Sand and Renshen/Guiyou Sword-Edge Metal. “Blue-green sand” and “yellow gravel” are the commentator’s descriptions of Wuyin and Jisi. Replacing these with the ordinary Wood, Fire, or Earth of the branch would lose the image. The following selection of sword-edge Metal expressly excludes other kinds of Metal.

[^82]: **A Fire-heavy example retained.** The Incompletion example contains Dingmao/Bingwu/Bingxu/Jiawu, with no manifest Water. The source nevertheless calls it 未濟 and then gives another explanation involving Fire above Water. Neither its chart nor its label is altered to manufacture a more familiar Water–Fire example.

[^83]: **Two reference stems.** Ren and Gui born in Si month receive different direct/indirect names for the same concealed Wu, Bing, and Geng. The comment separately considers support of these useful influences and support of a weak Day Master. “No further support needed” is not made into a universal prohibition of assistance.

[^84]: **Two meanings of Horse.** One explanation uses Traveling Horse and its punishment or clash; the other takes Horse as Wealth at Illness. The commentator explicitly prefers the former through Wang Tingguang’s explanation of Luoluzi. The translation preserves that preference as his judgment, not as a conclusion that all other uses of Horse mean Traveling Horse.

[^85]: **Three named triads.** S here explicitly gives Jia–Wu–Geng, Yi–Bing–Ding, and Xin–Ren–Gui, assigned to Earth, Heaven, and humanity. This differs from the Three Wonders of Wealth, Officer, and Seal just encountered. Chen/Xu’s combination into separate formations is a stated exception to the adverse judgment, not omitted in the English.

[^86]: **Each stem finds its own branch elsewhere in the chart.** Bingyin/Jiawu/Yisi/Jimao places Si, Yin, Mao, and Wu where the four stems’ Lu can be found. It does not mean that each stem sits directly upon its own Lu. S’s 巳卯 is read 己卯, Jimao, as required by its explicit Ji-Lu explanation and supported by G627.

[^87]: **Material imagery and seasonal doctrine overlap.** The three Mao pairs are not interchangeable: the commentary singles out Yimao and Guimao, allowing more hope for Dingmao. “Wet Wood” and “flameless Fire” remain its images. The phrase should not be reduced to a blanket rule that Seal cannot generate Fire in spring.

[^88]: **Historical physiology.** The commentary links Water to essence and Metal to voice and extends its preceding phase images into illness, sexual conduct, and inability to speak. These are historical correspondences, not established causal explanations of bladder disease or speech disability. “Metal solid, without sound” deliberately recalls the earlier vessel image.

[^89]: **Same relationships, different stages of life.** The Jia example takes concealed Xin, Gui, and Ji in Chou as Officer, Seal, and Wealth; the Ding example takes Ren/Jia in Hai and Xin above it. The order in which the commentary names Wealth, Officer, and Seal is collective, not an item-by-item assignment to the preceding stem order. Year and hour are then read as earlier and later life.

[^90]: **Food and Wealth at their own stages.** The commentary assigns the first comparison to Food God at Birth and flourishing and the second to Wealth Star at Lu and Storehouse. It does not give a complete chart or fully specify the stage convention for every sign. Wang and Xie evoke great aristocratic families; Tao Zhu evokes wealth. These are comparisons, not additional birth examples.

[^91]: **An inferred branch is not a fifth natal pillar.** The Jia example explicitly invokes unseen Shen through Zi and Chen. The Bing example uses the Water formation as Officer collectively. This language belongs to the text’s remote and hidden configurations; the English does not claim that an absent sign is physically present in the original four pillars.

[^92]: **The censorate.** 烏臺, Crow Terrace, is a literary designation for censorial office. The following commentary draws the career image from restraining evil influences and removing abuses. 金神, Metal Spirit, remains a named configuration or influence, not merely the presence of ordinary Metal.

[^93]: **An atypical example retained.** S and G627 give Chou and Yin as separated directional positions. Other passages illustrate Separated Angles with different intervals. Their Chou–Yin example is not changed to match another chapter’s table. The text specifically weights day and hour more heavily.

[^94]: **Exemplary strength and a lawsuit.** Wu Huo and Meng Ben are proverbial strongmen. The land dispute involving the Marquis of Xing and Yongzi is related under Duke Zhao’s fourteenth year in the [*Zuo Tradition*](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/春秋左氏傳/昭公). S’s 楚田 is G627’s 鄐田; “fields of Chu” preserves the place-name sound rather than silently identifying it with the state of Chu. Lou’s Metal Dog is a named celestial lodge; the claim that it makes litigants belongs to the commentary.

[^95]: **The chart’s stated Noble assignments.** The four stems are matched individually with branches elsewhere in Renshen/Xinhai/Jisi/Bingyin. In particular, the text uses Ren→Si and Bing→Hai. These are retained as its stated Noble-Person convention, not silently replaced by a different day/night list.

[^96]: **Commemoration after death.** 血食 refers to receiving sacrificial offerings, not consuming blood as an ordinary practice. The commentary develops an analogy from Metal’s firmness to moral constancy and enduring cult. It does not provide evidence that any person’s horoscope causes posthumous worship. Here the Three Wonders again mean named stem triads, not Wealth, Officer, and Seal.

[^97]: **Hexagrams and an alternative chart reading.** 泰, Peace, is Earth above Heaven; 屯, Difficulty at the Beginning, is Cloud/Water above Thunder. The first interpretation concerns the vocation of the exemplary people just described. The second explicitly gives Wushen/Xinhai and Renzi/Yimao as technical configurations. Both are translated without making one erase the other.

[^98]: **The author’s signature and symbolic phases.** S attributes this rhapsody to 育吾, Yuwu. Hooked Array, Dark Warrior, Li, and Kan function through their Earth, Water, Fire, and directional correspondences. Its praise of Water and Earth, and later praise of harmonious Fire and Water, must be read alongside its warnings about excess, not as unconditional recommendations.

[^99]: **Family harm is not a modern diagnosis.** The text draws kinship conclusions from year, month, day, hour, and named adverse influences. Its use of Heavenly Center means Void here. The severe claims about spouses and children remain historical claims; nothing in the translation presents them as a reliable basis for judging a living family.

[^100]: **Five positions, including conception.** The sequence distinguishes year, month, day, hour, and conception. Conception is additional to the four natal pillars, not an accidentally repeated fifth pillar. The source itself calls these broad outlines and postpones precise interpretation; the English preserves that limitation.

[^101]: **A damaged Noble-Person list.** S and G627 both place 己遇坎位乙逢坤方 after the Jia/Wu/Geng and Yi/Bing/Ding groups. The Ji/Kan and repeated Yi/Kun entries are retained rather than silently rewritten into a familiar rhyme. The commentary that follows asks for Officer and Seal’s strength as well: the list alone is not the full judgment.

[^102]: **Phase conquest and concealed support.** The Wood, Water, Earth, Metal, and Fire examples use Wealth as what is controlled, with Lu, Storehouse, and Food furnishing further qualifications. “Heavenly Kitchen” is a named food-and-stipend influence. The contrast between a body capable of using Wealth and one damaged by too much Wealth is stated explicitly, not supplied by the translator.

[^103]: **A lower clerical career, not necessarily permanent exclusion from office.** The catalogue initially places the person among clerks but then explicitly allows advancement if Lu or Noble supplies strength. Governing Net and Suspended Needle are named indicators; their presence is not equivalent to a literal bodily injury. The social hierarchy is the author’s.

[^104]: **A compressed list of stem and branch examples.** The text gives Jia/Mao, Bing/three Ding, Xin/Hai, Ren/two Gui, Yi and Ding/Snake, and Wu/Horse without complete charts. All are retained. “Convict,” “soldier,” and eventual commander describe different predicted stations; the paragraph is not condensed into a single unqualified military outcome.

[^105]: **Several commercial models coexist.** Traveling Horse, Wealth meeting Robbery, Indirect Wealth with a strong body, and directional phase conquest are separate explanations. The geographic language also denotes regions of the stem–branch cycle, not necessarily a command to relocate physically. The last sentence makes Wealth’s strength decisive for profit.

[^106]: **Two time divisions, not an omitted modern clock conversion.** The marginal note assigns Wei and Chou Noble differently to the earlier and later halves of the year and says to reverse the Six Ren procedure. It does not supply enough detail for a standalone reconstruction of that procedure. The English retains the note rather than importing another text’s complete day/night calculation.

[^107]: **Different vocational interpretations.** The rhapsody expressly says some adverse influences injure laypeople but not monks or Daoists. It also distinguishes religious prestige, disciples, poverty, dependence on donations, and return to secular life. The two complete quatrains are preserved; the purple robe is a sign of religious distinction, not a newly named astrological spirit.

[^108]: **Natal provision versus temporary support.** The text contrasts Wealth and Officer already rooted in the chart with a rise supplied by later courses alone. Its condemnation of the latter as “name without substance” remains alongside more favorable accounts of late rescue elsewhere. It is not silently reconciled into a universal prohibition of beneficial luck cycles.

[^109]: **Three life-periods.** 三限 divides the life-course, rather than adding three more natal pillars. The paragraph combines broken roots, seasonal decline, and named adverse influences in describing departure from home. Its conclusion is a prediction within the historical method, not evidence that an actual migration occurred.

[^110]: **Phase strength and family support are separate questions.** The Metal example explicitly gives Si, Shen, and You in both S and G627; all three remain. The passage first infers numerous brothers from shared phase strength, then asks whether they actually help, requiring freedom from Void, Solitary, and Widow. The English does not turn a count of siblings into a guarantee of support.

[^111]: **Relational assignments and household hierarchy.** The passage uses phase correspondences for spouse and offspring and then discusses repeated stem combinations as repeated wives or secondary chambers. These are historical forms of marriage and concubinage, not neutral modern relationship categories. The text’s counts are retained without supplying missing charts or calendar rules.

[^112]: **The source explicitly changes the child assignment by sex.** For men it takes what controls the stem; for women, what the stem generates. Other parts of the collection offer other assignments. The five phase examples are translated without silently forcing all of them into the generated-child model familiar to some modern readers.

[^113]: **Fosterage imagery, not medical treatment.** The passage first says “borrowing a wife to settle a child” and then explains wet nurses, foster mothers, and a mother’s phase generating the child’s. Both expressions remain. This is not a medically supported way to prevent miscarriage or ensure a child’s survival, nor an instruction to exchange spouses.

[^114]: **Nayin in a compressed example.** “Wood Snake” naturally suggests Jisi’s Wood nayin, but the source does not spell out the stem; it remains Wood Snake in English. The subsequent Ji/Metal-Rooster example and Earth/Wood husbands use different reference relationships. No composite full chart has been invented to make them uniform.

[^115]: **Historical judgments of female conduct.** The text equates particular combinations with chastity, illicit attachments, servitude, and prostitution. Those evaluations are retained as part of its historical social world, not endorsed as valid inferences about a woman’s character. Its final rhetorical question about blame belongs to the author.

[^116]: **A substantive variant and a distinct marriage form.** S gives 丙寅, Bingyin, in its compressed departure clause; G627 has 丙壬, Bing and Ren. S remains. 入贅 denotes a husband entering the wife’s household, not simply any remarriage. The claims about depending economically upon a wife are the source’s, not modern generalizations.

[^117]: **The closing household passage is compressed.** 命配成婚休敗克滯 and 支多克滯定應知汝 do not provide complete examples or unambiguous punctuation. G627 transmits substantially the same wording. The English gives a conservative connected reading—an obstructed marriage, subsequent generation, an outside son-in-law—without reconstructing missing stems or a supposed lost chart. The distinction between a manifest transformation and a merely apparent combination is retained in the final paragraph.

[^118]: **The Siku title uses 元.** 元機 is rendered “Hidden Mechanism,” a title for the pivotal workings of creation, without changing the Chinese heading to 玄機. The source gives no separate author’s name here. This is a new work, not additional commentary on the preceding rhapsody.

[^119]: **A broken string means remarriage.** 弦斷再續 is the conventional image of losing a spouse and marrying again. S’s image-only 弦 glyph is translated through its source mapping, not omitted. The opening kinship judgments differ from those elsewhere in the volume and are retained as this work’s claims.

[^120]: **Removing Wealth or Seal is conditional.** The text first distinguishes the strong body’s need for Wealth and the weak body’s need for Seal, then says to remove the other when it obstructs the chosen use. It is not a blanket command to discard every chart containing both. Other works in this juan explicitly praise Wealth and Seal in appropriate sequence or with mediation.

[^121]: **Year and course are differently weighted.** The text gives the branch priority in the course and the stem priority in the year. This is not permission to disregard the other component in every analysis. Its general praise of Companions with flourishing Wealth is likewise part of the surrounding strength-and-capacity argument.

[^122]: **Direct assignment of relatives to the pillars.** This passage assigns father/mother, self/wife, elder/younger brother, and son/daughter to the stems and branches of year, day, month, and hour. These are positional assignments, distinct from deriving relatives solely through generation and control. The verse’s “before” and “behind” are therefore preserved rather than replaced by a single modern kinship scheme.

[^123]: **A shared difficult maxim.** S and G628 both read 財旺梟衰因財喪命, flourishing Wealth and declining Owl bringing death through Wealth. That is not silently reversed to make Owl flourishing or Wealth declining. The text supplies no local explanation that would establish a certain correction, even though other passages welcome Wealth’s restraint of Owl.

[^124]: **An abbreviated recurrence rule.** “A stem year” and “a branch year” translate 干年 and 支年 in parallel with adverse stems and branches. The text does not spell out a complete list of qualifying years. The English therefore does not manufacture one or treat the rule as an independently verified prediction of death.

[^125]: **You and Yin are retained.** S and G628 both give 酉寅刑害, although You–Yin is not one of the usual six harm pairs. The transmitted pairing is preserved, not silently corrected to Shen–Hai or Yin–Si. The adjacent 巳卯, Si and Mao, is explicitly Wind and Thunder and has been read accordingly.

[^126]: **Two metaphors of service.** “Salt and plums” seasoning a tripod and “boat and oars” crossing a great river evoke the minister’s work of assisting a ruler. They are not additional dietary or travel prescriptions. The passage places them alongside Wood–Fire clarity and Water–Earth depth as images of governing capacity.

[^127]: **Transformation and restraint are not merged.** The source distinguishes a transforming spirit from a restraining one and evaluates their relative force. Its final contrast between development in declining versus flourishing courses is stated without a worked example. The translation preserves that contrast rather than inventing a universally applicable timing algorithm.

[^128]: **The monthly command and Day Master are necessary but not sufficient.** Their simultaneous strength is judged adversely here when the relevant Wealth, Officer, or configuration is lacking. The passage is not silently reconciled with the surrounding praise of a strong body. The collective title Gold and Jade is literary; it does not mean that this entire work concerns only the Metal phase.

[^129]: **Longevity Star can be Food God.** The context pairs an existing Indirect Seal with a later encounter with 壽星, Longevity Star, describing the Owl–Food conflict. It is not necessarily the South-Pole deity or a separate astronomical planet. The source’s newborn, bodily, and mortality claims remain historical rather than clinical.

[^130]: **Different combinations for yang and yin days.** The text explicitly distinguishes Food secretly combining Officer on a yang day from Food’s association with Seal on a yin day. It gives no full list of examples here. No omitted ten-stem table is supplied, and the two clauses are not collapsed into one rule.

[^131]: **爵星 is not silently changed.** The source writes Rank Star, after contrasting Owl and Food and shortly after using Longevity Star. Food God can bear both associations elsewhere in the book, but the English preserves the present title rather than replacing it with one uniform label. The two statements concern different functions within the argument.

[^132]: **Marriage language remains historical.** The clear wife-star is compared to established trees seeking one another, an image of matched families. The distinction between openly manifest and concealed Wealth also underlies the contrast between wife and secondary chamber. Red Luan is the named auspicious marriage influence; the text does not give its calculation here.

[^133]: **A measure of subsistence.** 升合 are small measures of grain, so 日求升合 means seeking daily subsistence, not seeking promotion or a technical stem combination. The surrounding line explicitly contrasts flourishing Food with plentiful Rob Wealth. The same characters can have technical meanings elsewhere, but not all occurrences are astrological terms.

[^134]: **An enumeration invoked, not supplied.** This paragraph mentions eighteen configurations without listing eighteen names. It then returns to the Five Phases and strength, decline, and changing qi. A table compiled from other chapters would be an editorial addition, not part of this source, and has not been inserted.

[^135]: **The wife–child connection is methodological.** The passage begins its inquiry into the child’s position from the wife’s palace, consistent with the argument that the wife’s phase generates the male chart’s child influence. It does not thereby establish every child-assignment elsewhere in the book; the other systems remain distinct.

[^136]: **Wild geese and linked calyxes.** 雁行 evokes siblings ordered like a line of wild geese; 連萼 evokes brothers flourishing together like connected blossoms. These are literary kinship images, not additional bird or flower sha. The subsequent mother/father longevity judgments belong to the source’s phase relations and are not independent family histories.

[^137]: **Several place-names remain uncertain.** S and G628 give the difficult sequence 庚坐燕寅、己入巽乾、乙臨雙女. Yan–Yin and Double Maiden are retained rather than replaced with conjectural modern branch labels. Xun and Qian are recognizable trigram directions, but that recognition does not repair the entire sequence. No missing day or hour stems are invented. The paragraph itself calls the positions Rest and Imprisonment and contrasts Killings with Longevity Star.

[^138]: **Moral agency is explicitly admitted.** After saying ingenuity cannot escape Heaven’s bounds, the source immediately says 陰功, unobtrusive or unseen good deeds, can prevail over creation. Both assertions are preserved. The sentence is not suppressed to make the work uniformly fatalistic, nor expanded into a modern doctrine that any hardship is caused by insufficient virtue.

[^139]: **Two allusions to adversity without loss of purpose.** Yuan Xian distinguishes poverty from failure to practice what one knows in [*Zhuangzi*, “Yielding Kingship,” revision 9855961](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=9855961). The [*Records of the Historian*, juan 79, revision 2549307](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=2549307), describes Wu Zixu beating his belly and playing a chi flute while begging in Wu. S names him Wu Yuan and calls the instrument xiao; that wording remains. 鼓腹 is not translated as “hungry belly,” even though the historical story concerns hunger.

[^140]: **A verse work with its own calculation language.** 心鏡 is Heart-Mirror, an image of discerning reflection. The opening contrasts the positions of celestial stars with the procedure of placing the hour upon the monthly establishment and seeking the fate position. It does not provide a complete diagram or modern software algorithm. The two-line English units preserve the source’s paired verse clauses, without attempting to reproduce Chinese character counts mechanically.

[^141]: **The three pairings are not conflated.** The text gives the seated-Food and stem-combination maxim, then Jiazi/Jisi, then Bingzi/Guisi. The latter two do not use an identical ordinary stem-combination mechanism, and the source supplies no explanatory chart beyond the phrases themselves. Both examples and the claim that they follow the same law are retained.

[^142]: **A difficult social label.** 裹頭 literally means a wrapped or covered head. In this pairing with monks it may indicate a religious or other head-covered practitioner, but the short verse does not establish a unique office or sect. The English keeps “one with a wrapped head” rather than assigning an unsupported identity.

[^143]: **The source defines its own image.** The small-character note says that the daily branch itself as seat is called 盤足, “cross-legged.” This is a technical seating metaphor, not a physical instruction for posture. 的煞, Target Sha, is a named adverse influence; it is not translated as the ordinary possessive particle 的.

[^144]: **The sequence is preserved.** Zi–Mao is explained through Gui and Yi’s generation; Wei→Chou→Xu→Wei gives the three Earth-branch punishments. The English follows the direction actually written rather than silently rearranging the branches to match another mnemonic’s order.

[^145]: **An opaque maxim and a specific exception.** The verse’s 祿馬克生 is compressed; “controlling and generating” preserves its two terms. The commentary then calls Jia/Yi encountering Yin/Mao a case of extinguished Lu and Horse, while expressly excluding Jiashen and Yiyou. This distinction is retained rather than rewritten into a single phase-wide rule. The missing explanatory steps are not invented.

[^146]: **A conclusion about method, not verified accuracy.** The closing lines move from individual favorable influences to the need to distinguish yin, yang, high station, low station, and many forms of fate. Their assurance that the trained reader can grasp a thousand changes is the author’s claim. The translation does not certify the predictions as empirically established.

[^147]: **A heading embedded in the source paragraphing.** 造微論 begins a separate work after the Heart-Mirror verses even though the repository transcription does not give it the same Markdown heading level as many earlier titles. It is separated here for reading. “Conception and breath” translates 胎息, an additional pair of technical origins, not an instruction to perform breath exercises.

[^148]: **Administrative penalties and formal images.** 徒流 denotes penal servitude and exile; 編配 denotes compulsory assignment or registration for service. They are distinct historical outcomes, not generic metaphors for a disappointing career. “Sword at Horse’s head” and the repeated punitive sha describe symbolic combinations, not real weapons supplied by the translator.

[^149]: **鎮星 is Saturn.** The phrase uses the traditional Earth planet alongside Wu and Ji, while the adjoining examples identify character through phase and stem. Strings rotting and grain aging are images of accumulated abundance. The text is not introducing a new astronomical ephemeris calculation at this point.

[^150]: **One supported repair, one unresolved phrase.** S’s 學若 before the overflowing-Water clause is read 粵若, “as for,” with G628; the supplied English is bracketed. Both witnesses retain the awkward 火強燥而微眇, strong/parched Fire yet slight or subtle. That wording remains visibly difficult rather than being reversed into weak Fire or expanded into an unstated chart. The subsequent Completion clause gives the required balanced outcome.

[^151]: **Bereavement and conception imagery.** “Beating the basin” evokes Zhuangzi singing and beating a basin after his wife’s death; see [*Zhuangzi*, “Perfect Happiness,” revision 9853848](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=9853848). Here it names a bereavement-related sha. Conception’s palace as White Tiger is the source’s distinct technical association, not identical with every earlier use of the prenatal origin. G628 has “in later years” where S says repeated injury in the following child-palace clause; S remains controlling.

[^152]: **Twenty-two signs.** The number is ten heavenly stems plus twelve earthly branches, not twenty-two pillars. The Human Mirror develops a model in which the birth day is master and the elder year sovereign. Its repeated insistence on both sides, beginnings and endings, is preserved instead of reducing the work to a list of isolated configurations.

[^153]: **Some names are secure, others not.** Yi Yin and Fu Yue exemplify ministerial advancement; Jia Yi and the Grand Duke exemplify unfavorable and favorable turns in a career. 范子, “Master Fan,” is not securely identified in this passage and is not silently expanded to a particular Fan. 老彭, Old Peng, retains the conventional longevity name rather than supplying an asserted historical lifespan. No full charts accompany these comparisons.

[^154]: **S and G628 disagree at Hui.** S reads 回也不愚, Hui is not foolish; G628 reads 回也不遇, Hui is not favored by opportunity. S’s wording is retained. The following 子之好學 is rendered “your love of learning,” preserving the brief address rather than supplying an unnamed historical person. The quotation-like form is part of the work’s literary fabric.

[^155]: **Classical language woven into chart judgments.** “I pray for you to the spirits” recalls the prayer discussion in [*Analects*, “Shu er,” revision 10602118](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=10602118). “Why not die swiftly?” is the severe language of the *Songs*’ “Rat.” Sima probably evokes Sima Niu’s anxiety over having no brothers in [*Analects* 12.5](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=10602324), but the astrological phrase about kindred harmony and lack of position is the rhapsody’s own. The identification is probable, not proof of a lost natal chart.

[^156]: **Songs, leaves, and legendary lovers.** “Meeting me among the mulberries” recalls the *Songs*’ “Among the Mulberries”; writing on a leaf evokes the literary motif of lovers linked by a leaf-borne poem. Xi Shi and Green Pearl are named as contrasting beauty and fatal misfortune. The text supplies no dates or charts that would independently substantiate these causal claims. The Nine Palaces’ strength is therefore not translated as moral approval of every ensuing action.

[^157]: **Trees and reeds are lifespan images.** 椿松 contrasts enduring trees with 蒲柳, plants proverbially quick to decline. The exact tree-and-reed botanical identifications are not needed for the stated contrast and have not been used to invent years of life. The final emphasis remains the relation between root and stream, root and leaves, and the visible and hidden forms of the chart.

[^158]: **Alternative titles are part of the source.** The parenthesis names 碧淵賦, Rhapsody of the Jade Abyss, and 千里馬, the Thousand-League Horse. The English does not treat these as additional works to be translated a second time. The Siku heading 元妙 is retained in Chinese, while “Subtle Mystery” conveys its contextual sense.

[^159]: **The preference for inferred configurations is the work’s own.** The opening says actual Wealth and Officer are difficult to obtain and emphasizes inviting Lu and Horse through empty positions. Other treatises in this collection prefer plainly rooted Wealth and Officer. That disagreement remains visible; the translation does not harmonize the authors into one school.

[^160]: **Seasonal assemblies and triple combinations.** Hai–Mao–Wei and Shen–Zi–Chen are not the same kind of grouping as Hai–Zi–Chou or Shen–You–Xu. Both types are present in the text, with their appropriate phase stems. “Completing” a configuration therefore does not always refer to the same three-branch procedure.

[^161]: **The context is controlling apparent excess.** The spring-Wood and summer-Fire clauses are followed by a preference for control and a dislike of further generation. S’s rare character in 有𡒦 corresponds to G628’s 有壓, having pressure or restraint. “When restraint is present” makes that contextual reading explicit without changing the preceding strong-Fire description into weak Fire. The passage does not say all seasonal excess is harmless.

[^162]: **Two small repairs and several unverified examples.** S’s 官勝四 is read 官勝印, Officer overcoming Seal, with G628 and the parallel contrast with Kui overcoming Officer. S’s 甲膀 is likewise read 甲榜, the higher examination list. The named failures and successes are literary illustrations; the text supplies no independently verifiable charts for Liu Fen or Li Guang here. Its applications of Kui and Officer should not be imposed on every other paragraph without considering context.

[^163]: **“Ailment” is chiefly a technical obstruction here.** 病 denotes a flaw or impediment in the configuration, not necessarily physical disease. The verse can call Kui itself the ailment that must be removed, yet elsewhere praise Kui as the examination star. Both uses are retained rather than assigning Kui one universally favorable role.

[^164]: **Seal year supported by the parallel.** S’s 官年四歲 is read 官年印歲, Officer year and Seal year, with G628; “[Seal]” marks the supplied reading in the main text. Dark Warrior and Vermilion Bird identify symbolic configurations in the passage. No new astronomical positions for Kui, Jade Hall, or Taiyi are calculated by the translator.

[^165]: **The same sign may change function with the season.** The text expressly says that Bing/Ding for autumn Jia/Yi should not simply be called Injury, and that Wu/Ji can rescue Metal amid Fire. These are functional qualifications to a label, not changes to the ordinary generating and controlling cycles themselves. The Water-, Wood-, Fire-, Metal-, and Earth-based examples are preserved individually.

[^166]: **Dragon, Tiger, Hooked Array, and Vermilion Bird.** Here Azure Dragon is Wood, White Tiger Metal, Hooked Array Earth, and Vermilion Bird Fire. Their joining another phase’s complete configuration need not have the same effect: the text distinguishes, for example, Wood overwhelmed by Metal from Metal releasing into Water. The resulting social judgments belong to the source.

[^167]: **A deliberately sweeping line.** The statement that everyone develops through punishment or clash of the four Storehouses is transmitted as a general maxim. Elsewhere the book requires useful stored qi, suitable strength, and freedom from destructive excess. Those conditions are not silently inserted into this line; its breadth is a real feature of the collected text.

[^168]: **Spouse is also a phase relation.** Rootless autumn Wood following the wife and summer Metal paired with a flourishing husband are explanations through following and transformation, not literal instructions about whom to marry. The subsequent repeated praise and warnings concerning Metal, Fire, Water, and Wood are retained even when their conditions overlap awkwardly.

[^169]: **The nine in the fifth place.** 九五 evokes the sovereign’s position in the *Changes*, not a count of nine or five additional pillars. Great Ocean is a named nayin image; Heaven’s Gate a directional/branch image. The final appeal to infer many cases from a small indication is the author’s methodological claim, not a guarantee supplied by the translation.

[^170]: **Sixfold classification rather than a new list.** The opening invokes the six major relationships around Wealth, Officer, Seal, Killings, Food, and Injury. Its “seven in ten” and “nine in ten” are rhetorical numerical judgments in the historical text, not estimates derived from a stated sample or modern validation study.

[^171]: **A seasonal exception is stated.** S and G628 welcome Officer for Hurting Officer in the twelfth month. The maxim is not changed into the opposite rule merely because other passages warn against Hurting Officer Meeting Officer. The accompanying warning about damaged Seal and excessive Injury remains, as does Dark Warrior’s role in the preceding line.

[^172]: **A caution against superficial strength labels.** The examples of scant Water in blazing Fire and extinguished Wood in heavy Earth explicitly reject two simplistic body/Officer classifications. The next lines separately address Wealth’s weight, Seal’s abundance, and different ways of obtaining office. Bribery and treasure are translated as stated, not softened into ordinary business skill.

[^173]: **Different kinds of authority.** The rhapsody distinguishes military and penal command, local civil government, provisioning, and close attendance at court. 官 is consequently not always the technical Direct Officer relationship: it can also be the office held. The English preserves these differences rather than translating every instance as either “government” or “Officer Star.”

[^174]: **Names can conflict when combined.** Day Virtue meeting Kuigang and Kuigang meeting Wealth or Officer are explicitly treated as reduced configurations here. The Ren and Gui examples also invoke repeated stems and branch patterns, not simply an auspicious birth day in isolation. “Even though properly grounded” is retained beside the modest clothing-and-Lu outcome.

[^175]: **A real disagreement about women’s fates.** This work praises Hurting Officer with Returning Lu for a woman while other works condemn Hurting Officer under different or less explicit conditions. It also singles out Metal Spirit, Established Lu, and Well-Railing as unsuitable for women. These statements remain recognizable as the author’s historical rules; they are not merged or endorsed as judgments of actual women.

[^176]: **The craft description is compressed.** S reads 金弱火強土木銷鎔之匠, combining weak Metal and strong Fire with an artisan working Earth and Wood through melting or shaping. The English retains the awkward material sequence rather than replacing Earth/Wood with an invented trade. The following needlework clause is distinct and concerns abundant Earth with shallow Water.

[^177]: **Eight methods and eight evaluative terms.** The male list gives kinds, affinities, following, transformation, illumination, return, ghosts, and concealment. The female list uses paired assessments of purity, harmony, distinction, turbidity, and sexual conduct. These are not interchangeable lists of eight modern configurations. 貴全財煞 in the preceding passage remains compressed as “nobility completes Wealth and Killings”; the text supplies no missing formula that establishes a unique expansion.

[^178]: **Twenty-two signs expressed through professions and character.** The work moves rapidly among symbolic configurations, official careers, trade, religion, and bodily fate. Suspended Needle and Kuigang are technical names, whereas carving faces and working iron are occupational images. The distinction is kept rather than turning every vivid phrase into a separately computed sha.

[^179]: **The repeated day is transmitted twice.** S and G628 both read 戊午戊午, Wuwu with Wuwu. It is not silently changed to Wuxu/Wuwu or expanded into a four-pillar chart. The statement that Blade transforms to generate the body recalls the Fire/Seal interpretation of Wu seated on Wu found elsewhere, but this brief line supplies no further conditions.

[^180]: **A short, incomplete specification.** The first sequence gives Bingshen, Gengyin, and Jia/Ji without completing the latter into stem–branch pairs. The second gives Jiazi, Jisi, and Renchen for medicine and divination. The English preserves this difference in specificity. It does not invent missing branches or imply that all three are necessarily present in one natal chart.

[^181]: **An awkward qualification remains.** S calls much Earth dull and turbid, then says 化生須福, transformation and generation require blessing. This compressed qualification is retained instead of changed to an unconditional promise of wealth. The adjoining warnings about excess Horse, Lu, punishment, and breaking are likewise the text’s separate assertions.

[^182]: **Religious and official dress.** Round/shaven head and square robe are images of Buddhist life; the three islands, jade steps, golden halls, and red shoes evoke high or privileged station. The English distinguishes literal occupational descriptions from these allusive images. Their use does not establish a biographical identification of an unnamed individual.

[^183]: **Bent Legs is a named indicator.** 曲腳 is kept as a technical designation rather than translated only as a symptom. The resulting descriptions of disability and dwarfism are historical claims, not supported medical deductions. The text’s attribution of bodily conditions to fate is not an explanation accepted by the translator.

[^184]: **The closing qualification matters.** Even a very weak day stem can contain renewed birth; even flourishing and mutual generation can conceal injury. These two clauses prevent the preceding catalogue from being reduced to “strong always good, weak always bad.” S’s 身弱食多則天 is read 夭, early death, with G628 and the sentence’s parallel construction.

[^185]: **A complete seven-character song.** The source gives 114 seven-character lines, translated here without abbreviation. 明津先生 is retained as Master Mingjin; no unsupported personal identity is supplied. The opening distinguishes the Five-Planet method’s life-periods from Ziping’s luck courses rather than claiming that they are identical techniques.

[^186]: **Shared Wealth and exclusive Wealth differ.** The song contrasts a directly or indirectly designated Wealth held exclusively with Wealth contested by brothers. It also warns about entering another Wealth influence, but does not give a full chart for each alternative. The repeated direct/indirect distinctions are preserved instead of condensed into a single generic warning about greed.

[^187]: **A rule stated in the song’s own terms.** Seal-Ribbon’s prosperous courses and its danger from Wealth are set out before the later configurations. The song can therefore praise body-strengthening luck here while rejecting it for following configurations elsewhere. No additional unstated condition is inserted into the verse to reconcile all schools.

[^188]: **Inverted Food and reversed Injury are retained as named phrases.** 倒食 and 倒傷 are not silently collapsed into a single word, though the context concerns Food’s injury. The song places one in the course and the other in the year. It gives no separate definition of 倒傷 at this point, so the English preserves the distinction and its uncertainty.

[^189]: **A shift to exceptional formations.** Day Noble, Day Virtue, Flying through Heaven, and Arching Lu have their own aversions to clash, filling, binding, and manifest Officer. The subsequent line that an encounter makes no distinction of age belongs to this timing discussion, not to a claim that every appearance of Wealth or Officer causes death in every chart.

[^190]: **Injury is not automatically death.** The song expressly says not to make calamity from injury immediately, but to consider its weight. It then lists retirement, dismissal, loss of property, injury to descendants, and mourning before returning to survival. These alternatives are all translated and not replaced by a single fatal prediction.

[^191]: **The enemy is relational.** “Internal” and “external” describe competing influences in the chart/course discussion, not actual enemies the reader is instructed to identify. The song distinguishes their relative severity, then turns to mixed qi in the seasonal-ending branches. It does not provide a fully explicit procedure for assigning every internal and external position.

[^192]: **An incomplete and compressed timing catalogue.** The Zi, Yin, Si, Mao, Wu, Shen, You, and Hai examples are retained in their transmitted order, including their interwoven stem names and the statement about eight palaces and four seasonal endings. No missing Chen, Xu, Chou, or Wei formulas have been fabricated. The final assertion that the course is heavier and the year follows it is the author’s rule, not a modern probability estimate or validated timing method.

[^193]: **Shen and Shang are separated stars.** 參商 is the traditional image of relatives who cannot meet, not an extra pair of earthly branches. The Three Mounds and Five Tombs likewise carry inherited technical names; the poem does not enumerate three new hills or five additional natal pillars.

[^194]: **A social criticism is explicit.** 不做人 is rendered as neglecting human relations while closely guarding wealth, rather than literally not being human. The verse criticizes miserliness; it is not a neutral recommendation for accumulation. The hidden Blade remains a distinct technical danger.

[^195]: **Historical reproductive claims.** False pregnancy, miscarriage, menstrual illness, wasting disease, and difficult childbirth are translated as the poem presents them. They are not established consequences of Owl, Food, or Seven Killings and should not be used for diagnosis or reproductive decisions. The poem itself also asks the reader to examine the courses rather than treating the natal label as the only condition.

[^196]: **A compressed first line remains uncertain.** 丙丁子丑戊己春 juxtaposes stems, branches, and spring without unambiguous pairing. The English retains that compression instead of inventing four complete days or changing “spring” into another branch. The following clearly named day pairs are translated individually. The full song contains 124 seven-character lines.

[^197]: **Repeated days receive different judgments.** Jisi, Guisi, Jiashen, and other days recur across favorable and unfavorable lists. The verse introduces different surrounding conditions, but not always enough to reconcile every case securely. Repeated examples remain, and no apparently conflicting day has been removed as a supposed duplicate.

[^198]: **The final warning is part of the poem.** The day lists culminate in the instruction to distinguish strength and weakness and not reduce yin and yang to one saying. The translation preserves that qualification together with the preceding severe family judgments. It does not endorse day-pair labels as sufficient evidence about a spouse or child.

[^199]: **Complete four-character sequence.** The source supplies 109 four-line stanzas: 81 in the opening sequence, 15 under “The weak body,” and 13 under “Abandoning the fate.” All are translated in order. Earlier Heaven and Later Heaven are retained as conceptual distinctions; the opening provides no diagram identifying a unique later numerological arrangement.

[^200]: **Historical subdivisions of the day.** The hundred ke belong to the timekeeping scheme named by the text. They are not silently converted to a modern 96-quarter-hour day. The verse contrasts year-root, monthly command, and changing conditions within the day; it does not provide a modern time-zone or clock-correction rule.

[^201]: **Three uses within Human Origin.** The stanza points to the three concealed influences in each seasonal-ending Storehouse branch. It does not enumerate them here. “Exposed and flourishing” qualifies their usefulness; the English does not turn every concealed stem into a manifest heavenly stem.

[^202]: **A skeptical maxim is retained.** The verse speaks of transforming spirits as a shadow without form and says their blessing is hard to rely upon. Elsewhere the book praises true transformation. The contrast is not erased by inserting “false” into this stanza, since that word is not present in S.

[^203]: **Roots belong to Killings.** The stanza contrasts unrooted hourly Killings, which can use a flourishing-Killings course, with heavily rooted hourly Killings, for which further flourishing is adverse. It does not say the Day Master should always be rootless. The owner of the roots matters to the argument.

[^204]: **An implied reference stem is left implied.** Officer in You month fearing Mao and Ding naturally recalls Jia Wood’s Xin Officer, but the stanza does not write Jia. It is not inserted into the main verse. The final 有情無情 remains a compressed contrast of affinity and its loss, not a complete independent rule.

[^205]: **A local comparison, not universal exclusion.** Wealth, Officer, and Seal are said to dislike Companions, while Injury and Killings receive their assistance. Other stanzas allow Companions to rescue Seal from Wealth or support a weak body. The different cases remain separate instead of being rewritten as one absolute preference.

[^206]: **Jia and Ji are deliberately contrasted.** The poem says Jia’s Metal Spirit particularly needs Fire’s restraint and asks why Ji’s would need it. This is an actual contrast in S, not an omitted negative to be repaired. Earlier descriptions of the Metal-Spirit configuration should not be used to remove it silently.

[^207]: **A witness-supported character repair.** S reads 二且刑煞. G628 reads 二巳刑煞, “two Si punishing Killings,” and the character 巳 is visible on [scan page 105](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?title=Page:Gujin_Tushu_Jicheng,_Volume_472_%281700-1725%29.djvu/105). “[Si]” marks the repair in English. This is a local correction, not a claim that every adjoining difficult verse has been established against the Siku facsimile.

[^208]: **The line division is clearer than the syntax.** 乙日卯月／金神剛烈／富貴比肩／旺橫死絕 is transmitted in both S and G628. The four-character meter identifies the four lines, but the last two remain syntactically compressed. The English retains wealth/distinction, Companions, flourishing, and violent death rather than inventing a lost conditional clause or another branch.

[^209]: **Gen remains named.** 艮祿 is rendered “Gen’s Lu,” retaining the northeastern trigram designation. The stanza does not give enough detail to establish a complete four-pillar case from two Yin, exposed Ren, and Shen. Its warning about Water is not silently reversed merely because Ren has just appeared.

[^210]: **Wu, not Xu.** S’s 六戌 is read 六戊, the six Wu days, with G628 and [scan page 105](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?title=Page:Gujin_Tushu_Jicheng,_Volume_472_%281700-1725%29.djvu/105). “[Wu]” marks the emendation. A “six Xu” reading would be a different and unexplained branch enumeration.

[^211]: **Two seasonal references are retained.** “Autumn Metal born at Wu” juxtaposes Metal’s autumn association and a Wu position without specifying whether Wu is day, hour, or another seat. The English does not silently change autumn to summer or supply a missing pillar. The verse’s southern-course warning remains as transmitted.

[^212]: **Wu, Wei, and winter are not silently reconciled.** The stanza gives Geng born at Wu, Xin born at Wei, balanced exposed Killings, and winter birth as most noble. Because it does not specify the reference pillar for the first two positions, no complete calendar reconstruction is added. Its distinctions remain available for comparison with the surrounding stanzas.

[^213]: **Month in S, Lu in G628.** 官印用月 is the Siku transcription’s reading; G628 gives 官印用祿. The main text retains use through the month and records the alternative here. This substantive difference is not resolved solely by which doctrine appears more familiar.

[^214]: **Opposite Earth judgments.** The poem says Earth with Xin and Fire makes punishment, while Earth with yang Metal and Fire brings a name. That yin/yang contrast is preserved rather than reduced to “Earth always rescues Metal from Fire.” The stanza itself provides no additional season or chart.

[^215]: **拘印 remains a compressed relation.** S and G628 both read 財官拘印, here “Wealth and Officer hold Seal.” The verb is not silently replaced by 俱, “both,” or another more familiar term. Si’s concealed stems allow the commentary tradition to discuss all three relations for Gui, but the stanza itself does not expand them.

[^216]: **The year and month labels are the verse’s.** The poem calls the arrangement 年煞月劫, year Killings and monthly Robbery, after specifying Gui, Shen, Mao, and Hai. It does not supply exposed stems sufficient for a complete reconstruction. The English preserves the labels and the stated secluded outcome rather than manufacturing a four-pillar chart.

[^217]: **An unresolved colloquial-looking ending.** S and G628 both give 朝劫暮巴. “Robbed at dawn, clinging at dusk” preserves a plausible literal reading of 劫 and 巴, but the exact intended idiom is uncertain. The preceding contrast between Officer and Killings with Peach Blossom is secure; the doubtful ending is not presented as a recovered standard proverb.

[^218]: **Month restored from the parallel.** S has 壬日亥日, repeating “day.” G628 reads 壬日亥月, Ren day in Hai month, parallel to the Gui/Yin monthly thread. “[Month]” marks the supported repair in the English.

[^219]: **Clear Metal versus sinking Metal.** S reads 土厚金清, thick Earth and clear Metal; G628 reads 土厚金沉, thick Earth and sinking Metal. S’s “clear” remains in the main text. The disagreement is substantive and cannot be settled simply by applying the common maxim that much Earth buries Metal.

[^220]: **Metal Spirit versus Food God.** S reads 金神生旺, Metal Spirit at Birth and flourishing; G628 reads 食神生旺, Food God at Birth and flourishing, confirmed on [scan page 105](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?title=Page:Gujin_Tushu_Jicheng,_Volume_472_%281700-1725%29.djvu/105). The main text retains S and records the strong parallel alternative here. Both give 清之則垣, whose final 垣 is rendered through the enclosure image but remains awkward; it is not silently changed to 貴, noble.

[^221]: **Rootlessness is not uniformly favorable.** The fifteen stanzas under “The weak body” distinguish yang and yin Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, and Water, and differing seasons and configurations. Their favorable and adverse rootless cases are retained individually. The following “Abandoning the fate” rubric begins a separate group, so it is not retroactively inserted as the rule governing every preceding stanza.

[^222]: **Generation does not erase the adverse judgment.** The stanza says Xinyou in the eighth month receives birth in Wei hour yet has much calamity. That wording is not reversed to blessing or treated as an accidental duplicate of the preceding Guisi stanza. The surrounding account repeatedly distinguishes excess from useful assistance.

[^223]: **A major difference in the final clause.** S’s 無根是宜 says that without roots the condition is fitting. G628 instead gives 無根寅危, without roots Yin is dangerous. S remains in the translation. The next stanza’s 遇庫, meeting a Storehouse, likewise differs from G628’s 二庫, two Storehouses; no missing number is supplied in the main text.

[^224]: **Following Wealth and following Killings differ.** Their different preferences are stated explicitly, together with the need for the relevant party to gather and the danger of renewed roots. The final promise of immortal understanding and prohibition on selling the formula belong to the poem’s rhetoric of transmission, not an assertion by the translator about secrecy or supernatural attainment.

[^225]: **A separate work begins inside a source line.** 五言獨步 follows the end of the four-character poem within S’s line 964. It is separated as its own heading here. All twenty-three five-character quatrains are translated, including the concluding ten-year-course stanza. The transition and ending are also visible on [scan page 106](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?title=Page:Gujin_Tushu_Jicheng,_Volume_472_%281700-1725%29.djvu/106).

[^226]: **A difficult first line.** 寅卯多金丑 is shared by S and G628. The relation of Chou to the preceding Wood and Metal is not fully stated. The English preserves the signs rather than supplying an unrecorded stem or changing the stanza into a prose rule of the translator’s own. Its north/south warnings are likewise retained.

[^227]: **Coming to Office, not a second palace word.** S’s 臨宫 is read 臨官, Coming to Office, with G628 and the standard technical phrase. Si remains the named position. The stanza’s avoidance of Hai and the northwest is its own rule; other passages’ different Earth growth-cycle conventions are not silently imposed upon it.

[^228]: **Avoiding complete Metal is explicit.** Yi born in You is told not to complete Si and Chou, while Kan and Li are favorable and Kun and Gen adverse. This contrasts with some earlier following-Metal cases. The wording is preserved, not rewritten to make all Yi/You charts follow one rule.

[^229]: **Named form and intrusive Metal.** The stanza retains Bending and Straightening in spring together with exposed Geng and Xin, then distinguishes Li from Kan. It does not explain in full whether Metal alters the configuration’s formal status. The English leaves that tension visible rather than removing the Metal stems.

[^230]: **An intrusive opening character.** S begins 巫甲乙生三月, which makes a six-character line in this five-character quatrain. G628 and [scan page 106](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?title=Page:Gujin_Tushu_Jicheng,_Volume_472_%281700-1725%29.djvu/106) read 甲乙生三月 without 巫. The extra character is therefore omitted from the English. The remaining compressed lists of stems and branches are not expanded into an invented chart.

[^231]: **A short contrast of time, weight, and direction.** 時分西與北 and 輕重辨東南 are translated as the verse gives them. The text does not provide a table assigning every month or weight to one direction. The north/west and east/south pairs are not converted into unsupported geographical advice.

[^232]: **Four explicit negatives.** S and G628 state that two Zi do not clash with Wu, two Yin do not clash with Shen, and the two reciprocal cases likewise do not clash. All four negatives are preserved. Many other passages use repeated branches to intensify or summon a clash; this stanza’s contrary rule is not silently corrected to agreement.

[^233]: **One useful influence, not necessarily Tianyi alone.** 時上一位貴 is immediately explained as concealed in the branch and requiring a strong Day Master. “Single Noble Influence” preserves that broad wording; the verse does not name Tianyi or provide its calculation. The claim that bodily strength lets gain and reputation possess qi is retained.

[^234]: **Ten years, then two fives.** The final stanza divides the course into upper and lower five-year portions and directs attention first to the passing year and then to the periods that come and go. It does not explicitly assign the two fives to stem and branch in this stanza. 旬 in the closing phrase is rendered “periods,” not the modern English ten-year “decade.” The separate Siku editorial preface is translated in [preface.md](preface.md).
