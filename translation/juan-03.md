# Sanming Tonghui · Juan Three
## A Comprehensive Gathering of the Three Fates

**Compiled by Wan Minying of the Ming dynasty.** From the imperially commissioned *Siku Quanshu*.[^01]

*Reading conventions.* Terminology follows the preceding juan. **Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, and Water** are the Five Phases; *qi*, *yin*, *yang*, *nayin*, and **Lu** retain their technical meanings. Stem–branch names are in unaccented pinyin. Angled brackets **〈 〉** identify the source's small-character commentary; square brackets identify supplied words or a reading repaired in the notes. The numbered section headings are editorial. The endnotes distinguish textual evidence from conjecture and explain where an apparently familiar term belongs to a different method.[^02]

## 1. On the Lu of the Ten Stems
### 論十干祿

<!-- source: L14-L22 -->
〈Two branch positions ahead of Lu lies the Golden Carriage: the image is that one who receives emolument must ride in a carriage. Its discussion therefore follows this one.〉

**Lu** means rank and emolument: one obtains a position of strength and enjoys its benefits. From the first differentiation of the ten stems and twelve branches, Jia and Yi were paired with Yin and Mao in the east; Bing and Ding with Si and Wu in the south; Geng and Xin with Shen and You in the west; Ren and Gui with Hai and Zi in the north. The ten stems take their Lu in the corresponding branch influences: Lu follows flourishing. Thus Jia's Lu is Yin; Yi's, Mao; Geng's, Shen; Xin's, You; Ren's, Hai; Gui's, Zi; Bing's, Si; and Ding's, Wu. Wu Earth lodges at Si, Ji Earth at Wu. Si and Wu are Fire's flourishing region: the child follows its mother and receives Lu. Among the remaining branches, Chen and Xu are Kui and Gang, called the remote and unfavorable borderlands, where the Lu origin does not lodge. Chou and Wei are the gates through which the Tianyi Noble Person enters and leaves; the Lu origin avoids them. Thus these four palaces have no Lu. Another explanation says that the four season-ending months contain mixed qi: their Lu would not be exclusive, so they are not taken.[^03]

<!-- source: L24-L42 -->
**Jia 甲: Lu at Yin 寅.** If Jia meets Bingyin, Jia Earth controls Bing Water as Wealth: this is **Blessing-Star Lu**. Wuyin, through the mutual generation of Fire and Earth, is **Hidden-Horse Lu**. Both are auspicious. Gengyin is **Broken Lu**, half auspicious and half ominous. Renyin is **Proper Lu**, carrying Road-Blocking Void: one is destined for Buddhist or Daoist religious life. Jiayin is **Birth Lu**, greatly auspicious.[^04]

**Yi 乙: Lu at Mao 卯.** Yimao is **Joy-Spirit Flourishing Lu**, auspicious. Dingmao is Road-Blocking Void, ominous. Jimao is **Advancing-Spirit Lu**. Xinmao is Broken Lu and also Crossing Spirit, half auspicious and half ominous. Guimao carries Taiyi and **Dead Lu**: even with distinction, one ends in poverty.

**Bing 丙: Lu at Si 巳.** Jisi is **Nine-Heavens Storehouse Lu**, auspicious. Xinsi is Road-Blocking Void. Guisi is **Hidden Noble-Spirit Lu**, half auspicious and half ominous. Yisi is **Flourishing-Horse Lu**, Dingsi **Storehouse Lu**: both are auspicious.

**Ding 丁: Lu at Wu 午.** Gengwu is Road-Blocking Void, ominous. Renwu is **Virtue-Combination Lu**, Jiawu **Advancing-Spirit Lu**: both are auspicious. Bingwu is **Joy-Spirit Lu**, meeting Yang Blade, partly auspicious. Wuwu is **Hidden Yang-Blade Lu**, mostly ominous.

**Wu 戊: Lu at Si 巳.** Jisi is Nine-Heavens Storehouse Lu, auspicious. Xinsi is Road-Blocking Void. Guisi is **Noble-Spirit Lu**: Wu and Gui combine and transform, bringing high official position. Yisi is **Horse and Lu in One Region**, Dingsi **Flourishing Storehouse Lu**: both are auspicious.

**Ji 己: Lu at Wu 午.** Gengwu is Road-Blocking Void, Renwu **Dead-Ghost Lu**: both are ominous. Jiawu is **Advancing-Spirit Combining Lu**, an image of eminence. Bingwu is Joy-Spirit Lu. Wuwu is **Hidden-Spirit Yang-Blade Lu**, ominous.

**Geng 庚: Lu at Shen 申.** Renshen is **Great-Defeat Lu**, Jiashen **Road-Blocking Void Lu**: both are ominous. Bingshen is Great-Defeat Lu, bringing many successes and failures. Wushen is Hidden-Horse Lu, bringing much obstruction; with a Blessing Star it is noble and auspicious. Gengshen is Birth Lu, greatly auspicious.

**Xin 辛: Lu at You 酉.** Guiyou is **Hidden-Spirit Lu**: Water and Fire offend one another, ominously. Yiyou is Broken Lu, bringing success and failure. Dingyou is **Void Noble-Spirit Lu**: Ding Wood receives qi while Xin Water bathes, indicating illicit sexual affairs; meeting Joy Spirit makes it auspicious. Jiyou is Advancing-Spirit Lu, Xinyou Proper Lu: both are auspicious.[^04]

**Ren 壬: Lu at Hai 亥.** Dinghai is **Noble-Spirit Combining Lu**; Yihai, **Heavenly-Virtue Lu**; Jihai, **Flourishing Lu**; and Xinhai, **Lu in the Horse's Region**. All are greatly auspicious. Guihai alone is Great-Defeat Lu, indicating poverty and scant resources.

**Gui 癸: Lu at Zi 子.** Jiazi is Advancing-Spirit Lu, indicating success in the examinations and advancement. Bingzi is **Lu Meeting Yang Blade**, carrying Blessing Star and Noble Person, with authority. Wuzi is **Hidden Yang-Blade Combining-Noble Lu**, partly auspicious. Gengzi is **Seal Lu**, auspicious. Renzi is **Proper Yang-Blade Lu**, ominous.

<!-- source: L44-L68 -->
There is **Generated-and-Completed Lu**, as when Jia and Yi people obtain Jiayin and Yimao; and **Lu of Name and Position**, as when a Jia person meets Bingyin. There is **True Lu**, as when a Jia person meets Bing or Si, or an Yi person meets Ji or Wu: all are noble configurations. There is **Advancing and Retreating True Lu**: Wuchen meeting Dingsi, Wuwu meeting Dingsi; Bingchen meeting Guisi, Bingwu meeting Guisi; Guihai meeting Jiazi, Guichou meeting Jiazi; Renxu meeting Guihai, Renzi meeting Guihai. Advancement brings ease, retreat difficulty. With auspicious influences in addition, these can form noble fates; repeated encounters are feared.[^05]

There is **Lu Meeting Combination**, as when Jia, whose Lu is Yin, obtains Gengxu. There is **Food God Carrying Lu**: Ren takes Jia as Food God and obtains Jiayin; Gui takes Yi as Food God and obtains Yimao; Wu takes Geng as Food God and obtains Gengshen; Ji takes Xin as Food God and obtains Xinyou. These are auspicious. There is **Food God Combining with Lu**: Jia takes Bing as Food God and obtains Bingshen or Bingyin; Yi takes Ding as Food God and obtains Dingwei or Dingmao; Geng takes Ren as Food God and obtains Renchen or Renzi; Xin takes Gui as Food God and obtains Guisi or Guichou. All eight positions are auspicious.[^06]

There is **Wealth Above Lu**, called **Richly Spread Sha**: a Jia person meeting Wuyin, an Yi person meeting Jimao, and so forth. It indicates wealth and reputation. There is **Ghost Above Lu**, called **Red-Mouth Sha**: a Jia person meeting Gengyin, an Yi person meeting Xinmao, and so forth. It indicates disputes, punishment, and reprimand. There is **Lu Within the Ten-Day Round**, as Jiashen meeting Gengyin or Wuwu meeting Dingsi, indicating pure distinction and important office. There is **Heavenly-Lu Noble Spirit**: Ding's Lu is Wu; derive the stem at Wu and obtain Bing. Bing's Noble Persons are You and Hai. Obtaining Xinyou or Xinhai, one finds that Xin's Noble Person in turn appears at Wu. Entering a configuration, this reaches the highest rank.[^07]

There is **Stem-and-Branch Combining Lu**: Jia's Lu is Yin, and it obtains Jiayin with Jihai; Yi's Lu is Mao, and it obtains Yimao with Gengxu. It indicates exalted and weighty office. There is **Exchanged Noble Lu**, as Gengyin meeting Jiashen in day or hour. There is **Lu Paying Court to the Origin**, as a Yin person meeting Jia in day or hour; if it pays court in the conception position, it is especially noble. There is **Paying Court to the Origin Through Flanking Combination**: Guisi meeting Wuchen and Wuwu. The two Wu combine with Gui and flank Si, where Wu has its Lu. This indicates enfeoffment and rank.

There is **Lu Entering the Hall of Lu**. The *Liyu Song* says, “Lu entering the Hall of Lu calls for a great appointment.” Li Xuzhong takes a Jia person obtaining Jiaxu as the example: Jia is the year stem, and deriving Jia's own position to Xu gives the Hall of Lu. Xin and Ren have two positions each: Xin has Xinmao and Xinchou; Ren has Renyin and Renzi. If the Five Phases do not control them and the other positions assist, the blessings that unfold must be great.[^07]

<!-- source: L70-L74 -->
The ancients said: “The two positions ahead of Lu turn their backs upon it; the two behind face toward it.” Shen Zhi takes four terms as favorable: **Established**, **Facing**, **Near**, and **Combining**. Established is inferior to Facing, Near inferior to Combining. Obtaining two of these four brings distinction. Thus, with Jia's Lu at Yin, Yin is Established, Chou Facing, Mao Near, and Hai Combining. Apply this to the others; each of the remaining cases has its respective position. Punishment or a ghost in a triple or sixfold combination is avoided. For Jiaxu, a ghost at Chou is fearsome because Chou punishes Xu; at Wei it is not feared because Xu punishes Wei. For Yiyou, a ghost at Si harms through the triple combination; at Chen it diminishes blessing through the intimacy of the Six Combinations.[^08]

**Established Lu** indicates full flesh and substantial qi, a physique not finely refined, and a life of ease with sufficient wealth and profit. This is naturally so in Birth and flourishing. In Death and Extinction, the qi is turbid and the spirit sluggish; the person toils for a living, miserly and mean. Joined with Yuanchen, it gains money through *chupu* gambling, then loses it the same way. Joined with Official Talisman, it gains through the gates of officialdom or becomes embroiled in many lawsuits. Joined with Robbery Sha, it favors lowly skills, petty trading, and unrighteous windfalls. Joined with Heavenly Void, it brings repeated loss and dissipation of wealth. Joined with Lu's ghost and Inverted Food, it gains through credit, lending, and brokerage: a person unmoved to the day of death, thinking only of money.

It is also said: “A **Storehouse** gathers Lu: for Jia and Yi it is Wei; for Bing and Ding, Xu; for Wu, Ji, Ren, and Gui, Chen; for Geng and Xin, Chou. A Jia or Yi person with many Hai positions who obtains Wei has abundant and substantial Lu. **Combination** brings Lu unexpectedly: Jia's Lu is Yin, and obtaining Hai makes a manifest combination; obtaining Hai without Yin makes a hidden combination. Though Lu is not visibly present, this too counts as meeting Lu and often brings unlooked-for blessings. **Arching** honors Lu: if Jia's Lu at Yin is absent, but Chou and Mao stand on either side and arch toward it, this is empty arching. If Yin is present as well, it is actual arching. Arching toward the actual is inferior to arching toward the empty, which indicates great wealth and distinction.”

<!-- source: L76-L84; displaced Horse material in L76-L78 is translated in section 3 -->
When a fate carries Lu, it may be auspicious or ominous, noble or lowly. One cannot rely upon Lu alone and immediately pronounce it auspicious. The *Subtle Purport of Heaven's One* says: “Do you not see that Lu, Horse, and Noble Person offer no certain support? Examine the good and ill of the Five Phases. A weak Heavenly Origin is not yet disaster; firm Earth qi can provide ample happiness.” The *Song of the Marrow of the Source* says: “Lu and Horse have many further explanations: their own Decline and Death, Defeat and Extinction. Without auspicious influences to assist them, ancestral property is certainly broken, and much is rootless and inferior.” Clearly, one must discuss the Five Phases first, then Lu and Horse. The Five Phases want Birth and flourishing; Lu and Horse fear Decline and Extinction. Sima Jizhu says: “Much Lu and little Horse wear down the spirit; little Lu and much Horse give skill in managing and bearing burdens.”

The *Classic of Penetrating Mystery* says: “Jia takes Yin as Lu. Geng and Ren are not its proper mounts: mounting Lu may bring ascent, yet at times it serves no purpose.” Thus Lu within a fate should be simple, not overcomplicated. The stem above Lu should not turn back to injure the natal ruler, nor offend Owl Spirit: that is best.[^09]

It is also said: “A fate carrying Lu most fears a clash, called **Breaking Lu**. Jia taking Yin as Lu and meeting Shen, or Yi taking Mao as Lu and meeting You, scatters the qi instead of gathering it. Those of rank are suspended or stripped of office; ordinary people lack sufficient clothing and emolument.” The *Song of the Marrow of the Source* says, “Breaking Seal, breaking Wealth, and breaking Lu; breaking Horse has seldom brought me blessing.” This is why clashes are disliked. Falling into Void is especially feared and is called **Idle Lu**: in the Jiachen round, for example, Yin and Mao are void, yet Yin is obtained as Jia's Lu. The ancients said: “Wealth gathers and scatters when Lu dwells in Void; it is feared in conception and birth hour. Poor and lowly, serving others or begging food, one wanders east and west.” Shen Zhi says: “What can be known of Lu entering Void? Empty name and empty reputation, worthy only of mockery.” This is why Void is disliked.

In the end, whether a fate enters a configuration and accords with creative transformation does not depend upon Lu alone.

## 2. On the Golden Carriage
### 論金轝

<!-- source: L86-L88 -->
*Yu* means a carriage; *golden* expresses its preciousness. A gentleman holding office and receiving Lu must sit in a carriage to be conveyed. Thus **Golden Carriage** ordinarily lies two positions ahead of Lu: a Jiazi person's Lu is Yin, and Chen is the Golden Carriage. This influence is the banner of Lu and fate, the command tally and battle-axe of the Three Powers. It indicates a gentle disposition, an honest countenance, and conduct both warm and restrained. A woman meeting it becomes wealthy or distinguished; a man obtaining it has many wives and concubines and receives their supporting blessings. Meeting it in the birth day or hour is best. Close kin remain peaceful and secure throughout life; one obtains worthy wives and concubines and flourishing descendants. Members of the imperial family often carry it. In an ordinary configuration, if the person is born where the body lacks qi, it indicates entering the wife's household as a son-in-law.[^10]

The *Purple Void Scheme* says: “Two places before Lu is called Golden Carriage; the person meeting it receives exceptional blessings. It especially indicates intelligence, wealth, and distinction, a lifetime of clear peace without anxiety.” The *Golden Book of the Eight Characters* says: “Two positions before the Traveling Horse, the Golden Carriage lies within. Born in this place, or meeting it in the luck course, one continues to advance in office even in old age.” This latter account instead takes two positions before **Horse** as Golden Carriage. If Officer and Noble Person flank and support it, with Golden Carriage leading and attending, great distinction results. Golden Carriage meeting Blessing and Noble Person together with the General Star is excellent.

## 3. On the Traveling Horse
### 論驛馬

<!-- source: L90-L92, with the displaced portions of L76-L78 restored here -->
〈One position behind is **Mounting the Saddle**: the image is that a rider must put a saddle upon the horse. Thus discussion of Horse requires Mounting the Saddle.〉

What is called **Traveling Horse** arises from the Earlier-Heaven numbers of the triple combinations. Yin is seven, Wu nine, Xu five: together they make twenty-one. Counting forward from Zi to Shen takes twenty-one positions, establishing the Fire configuration's Horse. Hai, Mao, and Wei have the numbers four, six, and eight, totaling eighteen. Counting forward from Zi reaches Si at eighteen, establishing the Wood configuration's Horse. Wood and Fire are yang configurations and move forward from Zi, the first yang. Metal and Water are yin configurations and move backward from Wu, the first yin. Shen, Zi, and Chen have the numbers seven, nine, and five, totaling twenty-one. Counting backward from Wu reaches Yin at twenty-one, establishing the Water configuration's Horse. Si, You, and Chou have the numbers four, six, and eight, totaling eighteen. Counting backward from Wu reaches Hai at eighteen, establishing the Metal configuration's Horse. This is how the method was founded.[^11]

Another account says that Horse has the qi of transmitting and receiving and performs the service of taking over another's labor: when a person is ill and cannot attend to a duty, a child is awaited to take it up. Thus meeting the child at the position of Sickness is called Traveling Horse. It is also said: “Traveling Horse is a name provisionally given to the Five Phases' qi poised for action, awaiting employment. Yin and yang lie concealed within each other; the qi of command circulates. It resembles the relay stations of the present day, transmitting orders, receiving those who come and sending onward those who depart. Qi at rest is like the station; qi in motion, the horse.”

“Yin–Wu–Xu belongs to Fire, yet Water is concealed within it. Meeting Shen, where Water is born, releases that Water; yin then moves within yang and transformation occurs. Shen–Zi–Chen belongs to Water, yet Fire is concealed within it. Meeting Yin, where Fire is born, brings it into harmonious circulation; yang then moves within yin and life arises. Hai–Mao–Wei belongs to Wood, yet Metal is concealed within it. Meeting Si, where Metal is born, works it like the bellows: what moved becomes still, and what gathered disperses. Si–You–Chou belongs to Metal, yet [Wood] is concealed within it. Meeting Hai, where Wood is born, spreads its flourishing: what gathered disperses, and what bent stretches forth.”[^09][^11]

“Thus Water, Fire, Wood, and Metal interweave and pass to and fro, moving or resting with the time, inner and outer responding and serving one another. Advancing, they proceed with the season; retreating, they reach their limit with it. What the ancients provisionally called Traveling Horse follows this same pattern. One corner alone has been shown: infer the other three, and the principle returns to a single measure. One need not insist that only Shen for Yin–Wu–Xu or Yin for Shen–Zi–Chen can be Horse. Whenever Fire rises within Water or Water descends within Fire, yin and yang communicate in Peace and hardness and softness transform into one another: all belong to the category of Horse.”

Xiyin says: “The ancients said that in moments of replacement, alteration, movement, sudden advance, coming, and going, Traveling Horse alone acts in this way. The Fire configuration places it at Shen, Water at Yin, Metal at Hai, and Wood at Si. The Five Phases' qi begin to strike and stir when they reach their opposites. Fire's Horse therefore lies at Water's Birth, Water's Horse at Fire's Birth; Wood and Metal follow the same principle. This is its meaning. Xu Ziping's taking Wealth as Horse likewise follows the ancients: he names it from the place of control.”

The *Classic Illuminating the Spirits* says: “Traveling Horse in Birth and flourishing indicates a composed, elevated bearing, adaptability, and responsiveness to the time, with much reputation throughout life. In Death and Extinction, the disposition begins but does not finish, veers between right and wrong, achieves little, and wanders without a settled place. In the same region as Lu, it brings ample and unhurried enjoyment of blessings. Clashing with sha, or joined with Lonely Spirit, Mourning Guest, or Mourning Gate, it makes someone who leaves home and native well: perhaps a Buddhist or Daoist, perhaps a merchant. Carrying Inverted Food and Lu's ghost, it indicates lifelong miserliness, calculation, and opportunism, with the mean habits of the marketplace. Meeting Food God in clash or conjunction makes a person of reputation. In the progressing year, Horse together with Sickness Talisman brings alarm through illness; together with Official Talisman, alarm through official affairs. Entering the dwelling brings disputes and alarm. Judge these only through that year's auspicious and ominous conditions.”[^12]

<!-- source: L94-L100 -->
**For those born in Yin, Wu, or Xu, Horse is Shen, ridden by one of the five yang stems.** Jiashen is **Road-Blocking Void Horse**; Bingshen, **Great-Defeat Horse**; Wushen, **Blessing-Star Hidden Horse**; Gengshen, **Horse Meeting Heaven's Pass**; Renshen, Great-Defeat Horse. 〈Also called **Inferior Horse**: it indicates lifelong rushing about, with wealth repeatedly exhausted. The four Horses Renshen, Jiashen, Bingshen, and Wushen bring many illnesses, repeated sinking and rising, and repeated entanglement in right and wrong.〉 These respond in years, months, days, and hours of Si, You, Chou, or Shen.

**For Shen, Zi, or Chen people, Horse is Yin, ridden by one of the five yang stems.** Jiayin is **Proper-Lu Literary-Star Horse**; Bingyin, **Blessing-Star Horse**; Wuyin, **Hidden Horse**; Gengyin, **Broken-Lu Horse**; Renyin, **Road-Blocking Horse**. These respond in years, months, days, and hours of Hai, Mao, Wei, or Yin.

**For Si, You, or Chou people, Horse is Hai, ridden by one of the five yin stems.** Yihai is **Heavenly-Virtue Horse** 〈also called Inferior Horse〉. The branch within Horse generates its stem: this indicates being submerged and achieving nothing, cleverness alone proving futile. 〈Also called **Extinction Horse**.〉 Dinghai is **Heaven's-One Horse** 〈Horse at Arrival at Office〉; Jihai, **Flourishing-Lu Horse** 〈Birth Horse〉; Xinhai, **Proper-Lu Horse** 〈also Sickness Horse〉; Guihai, **Great-Defeat Horse** 〈also Arrival-at-Office Horse〉. These respond in years, months, days, and hours of Shen, Zi, Chen, or Hai.

**For Hai, Mao, or Wei people, Horse is Si, ridden by one of the five yin stems.** Yisi is Proper-Lu Horse; Dingsi, **Flourishing-Qi Horse**; Jisi, **Nine-Heavens Lu-Storehouse Horse**; Xinsi, Road-Blocking Horse 〈one account says that, meeting Noble Person, it is partly auspicious〉; Guisi, **Heaven's-One Hidden Horse** 〈one account says that, meeting Noble Person, it is auspicious〉. These respond in years, months, days, and hours of Yin, Wu, Xu, or Si.

Whenever a pillar carries Horse, unless it meets Void, breaking, Defeat, Crossing, Retreating, or Hidden Spirit, honor and distinction should follow. If exchanged Lu and the Tianyi Noble Spirit share its position, with the other influences gathering there, one holds great authority in office and a distinguished place at court. The hour gives the highest distinction, the day the middle, the month an ordinary condition. **Storehouse Horse** brings the joys of youth; **Flourishing Horse** supports the honors of one's prime; **Birth Horse** succeeds only in old age, with lower office and distant appointments. Thus Wood is born at Hai, flourishes at Mao, and stores at Wei; apply the others by analogy. Luoluzi says: “Horse at Birth need not truly possess Horse; turning one's back on Lu need not mean the absence of Lu. Examine flourishing and Storehouse, not merely turning away or Birth.” The subtlety lies in weighing fullness and emptiness.

It is also said: “Traveling Horse is an influence of effective action and joyful occasions within the Three Fates. The gentleman meeting it commonly occupies an honored position; the ordinary person receives abundance. Major or minor luck, or the progressing year, reaching it brings the joy of obtaining office or moving to another appointment. When minor progression or the progressing year combines with Horse, it likewise brings promotion and Lu: a Jiazi person's Horse is Yin; minor progression or Tai Sui reaching Hai combines Hai with Yin.”

<!-- source: L102-L104 -->
There are also **twelve kinds of Horse**.[^13]

**1. Plodding Horse 欵叚.** A Si, You, or Chou person obtains Renhai 壬亥; a Hai, Mao, or Wei person obtains Bingsi 丙巳; a Shen, Zi, or Chen person obtains Jiayin; a Yin, Wu, or Xu person obtains Wushen. [The first two compounds are transmitted in this defective form.]

**2. Stumbling Hoof 蹶蹄.** Although the Four Pillars carry Traveling Horse, the birth day meets an influence of Void. **3. Broken Leg 折足.** The conception month carries Horse, while day and hour carry Bathing. If Shen, Zi, and Chen are all present and meet Yin as Horse, three people ride one horse: hence Broken Leg. If Hai, Mao, and Wei are all present and meet Horse, then even with Officer and Noble Person, the end is lowly. If only one branch occupies it, youth may be peaceful, but poverty returns later.

**4. Without Fodder 無糧.** The birth day meets Horse, Horse takes Tai Sui as its Food God, and the hour falls into Void: for example, a Jiazi person obtaining a Renyin day under that condition. **5. Never Leaving the Stable 不出㕔廐.** Conception or month carries Horse, but it meets neither Noble Person nor the Hall of Lu and enters Void. **6. Neighing in the Wind 嘶風.** 〈Text missing.〉 **7. Hurrying Along the Road 趨途.** Traveling Horse is present, but Lu lies in Void.

**8. Carrying a Corpse 䭾屍.** Of the twelve Horses, this is the most ominous: obtaining Lu immediately makes a corpse. Within the Jiazi ten-day round, Si, You, and Chou births have Horse at Hai: Yichou obtains Dinghai, Jisi obtains Yihai, and Guiyou obtains Guihai. A Yichou person obtaining Hai Horse avoids Yin in month, day, or hour; Jisi obtaining Hai Horse avoids Shen; Guiyou obtaining Hai Horse likewise avoids Yin. One such occurrence is called Carrying a Corpse. Similarly, for Hai, Mao, and Wei within the Jiawu round, Yiwei obtains Xinsi, Jihai Jisi, and Guimao Dingsi. Yiwei obtaining Si Horse avoids Yin in month, day, or hour; Jihai obtaining Si Horse avoids Shen; Guimao obtaining Si Horse likewise avoids Yin. All are Carrying a Corpse. Apply the other rounds by analogy.

**9. Eating Grass 食蒭.** Horse controls the hour: for example, Horse belongs to Metal and the birth hour obtains Wood. **10. Riding the Dignitary's Carriage 乘軒.** Conception, month, or birth day carries Lu and Horse: the example gives a Jiashen person obtaining Gengyin, a Jiayin hour, and a Gengyin conception month. **11. Riding the Light Chariot 乘軺.** Heaven and Earth obtain combination, and Noble Person and Horse are encountered in the natal year, month, day, or hour. For example, a Dinghai person born in the fourth month, on a Renyin day at a Jiyou hour: the month sits on Horse, and You is Noble Person. **12. Without a Bridle 無轡.** Noble Spirit falls into Void and Lu dwells in Extinction.

Weigh these twelve Horses according to their meaning, and blessing and calamity become apparent. Plodding Horse gives a lifetime of difficulty and no more than a junior appointment; Without Fodder receives no heavenly stipend; Never Leaving the Stable never reaches the place of appointment; Broken Leg is a permanent loss, whereas Stumbling Hoof rises again. Without a Bridle gives lifelong solitude and poverty; Eating Grass can reach the sixth official rank; Neighing in the Wind has only empty renown; Hurrying Along the Road labors vainly in pursuit of Lu. Riding the Light Chariot carries an official appointment; Riding the Dignitary's Carriage reaches the Three Dukes; Carrying a Corpse dies on obtaining office.[^14]

<!-- source: L106-L118 -->
There is **Stem-and-Branch Combining Horse**: for Shen, Zi, or Chen, Horse is Yin; Jiayin combines with Jihai, Bingyin with Xinhai. This indicates exalted and weighty office. There is **Horse Wearing a Sword at Its Head**: Geng or Xin appears above Horse, or its nayin is Metal. This gives a name renowned upon the frontiers.

There is **Horse Galloping to the Heavenly Court**: a Wood person obtains Hai and meets Xinhai, while the stem above Horse also meets Lu. The example gives a person of one of the six Ren births occupying Yin, Wu, or Xu: starting from Jia, the derived stem is Wu; Wu's Lu is Si, and Si is the Heavenly Court. Si appears again and obtains You in combination. This indicates the highest official rank. Two positions behind Horse is **Nine-Earths Horse**, indicating service close to the royal court.[^15]

There is **Heavenly-Horse Noble Spirit**. Horse need not appear in the natal year: use the Five-Tigers derivation to reach Horse and determine its stem. That stem meets Tianyi; the stem on which Tianyi sits must then find its Noble Person back at Horse. The distinction is no lower than the third rank. There is **One Stake Tethering Two Horses**: Yin, Wu, or Xu repeatedly meets Bingshen; Shen, Zi, or Chen repeatedly meets Gengyin; Si, You, or Chou repeatedly meets Jihai; Hai, Mao, or Wei repeatedly meets Guisi. The stem above Horse controls its branch, indicating many alarms and dangers. If four Horses gather in the hour or year, they indicate enfeoffment and rank.[^15]

A person born under Horse whose month, day, and hour supply both **Rein** and **Whip**, and whose Horse controls the body, is assured of distinction. One position ahead of Horse is Rein, one behind is Whip. For example, a Jiazi Metal fate born in the first month, on a Xinchou day at a Mao hour: Zi's Horse is Yin, which meets Lu. Natal Zi and the birth day's Chou are behind Horse as Whip; the Mao hour is ahead as Rein. Moreover, Horse is Bingyin Fire, which controls Jiazi Metal: hence distinction.[^16]

<!-- source: L120-L136 -->
There is **Having Both Station and Horse**, indicating rank as duke or marquis. The stem is Horse, the branch the station. A Wuxu person, whose Horse is Shen, obtains Gengshen: its stem and branch are both Metal, reaching Arrival at Office at Shen; Wuxu's stem and branch are both Earth, reaching Birth at Shen. The natal stem and branch and those of Horse all have qi: station and horse are both present. A Renwu person has Horse at Wushen: Wu Earth reaches Birth at Shen, and natal Ren Water likewise reaches Birth there, but Wu Fire declines there. The natal stem flourishes while the branch declines: this is **Horse Without a Station**. A Dingchou person has Horse at Xinhai: Metal at Hai is sick, natal Ding Fire at Hai is extinct, while Chou Earth reaches Arrival at Office there. The natal stem declines, the branch flourishes, and Horse's stem also declines: this is **Station Without a Horse**. Another interpretation takes a Yin, Wu, or Xu person meeting Geng without Shen, or Shen without Geng, as Horse Without a Station or Station Without a Horse. This, too, is intelligible.[^17]

There is **Horse Controlling the Body**, where Horse's branch controls the birth month. For Yin, Wu, or Xu, Horse is Shen; Shen Metal can control the Wood of Yin and Mao months. A Jiazi person born in Chen, Xu, Chou, or Wei is another example. It indicates easy attainment of office and Lu, unobstructed official advancement, early success, and pure distinction in office; an ordinary person meeting it obtains modest wealth. There is **Horse's Wealth Storehouse**, taking the Tomb of the phase Horse controls: Horse at Shen is Metal; Metal controls Wood, and Wood stores at Wei. This indicates a lifetime of travel in all directions and abundant acquisition of wealth.

There is **Brilliant Spirit Pervading Horse**, when Horse meets the Birth position of the Five Phases' true qi. It indicates an inspecting envoy bearing the insignia of delegated authority. There is **Horse of Southern Li's Brightness**: Wei's Horse is Si, Chou's Hai, Chen's Yin, and Xu's Shen. Chen, Xu, Chou, and Wei are Earth positions, and Birth supplies their Horse. Thus these people have a flying Horse at Wu, the Horse of the southern gate of Li. It welcomes the Zi–Wu clash as **Flying Horse Meeting Whip and Rein**; entering a configuration, it brings distinction.[^17]

Horse also has **clear and turbid forms**. Jiazi obtaining Bingyin puts Lu and Horse in the same region; moreover, Bing is Food God riding Birth Horse. Dingchou obtaining Dinghai puts Tianyi, Heaven's Officer, upon an Arrival-at-Office Horse. Riding Horse at Birth or Arrival at Office, or carrying Food, Lu, and noble qi, one encounter is worth a hundred. Riding Horse in Sickness, Extinction, or Void, with breaking, Defeat, Crossing, Retreating, or Hidden Spirit as well, one encounters it yet effectively does not. Even an official appointment is coarse and lowly, not a post of pure distinction and importance.

Another account says: “In judging Horse, distinguish the Four Exclusive Positions, Name and Position, Birth and flourishing, Sickness and Extinction, Carrying Treasure, Bearing Flowers, Inverted Food, and exchange. Their favorable and unfavorable qualities, honor and disgrace, must be examined minutely through the whips and reins of year and luck course. The **Four Exclusive Positions** are these: for Shen, Zi, and Chen, Horse is Yin, where Jiayin is met; Shen meets Gengshen, Si Dingsi, and Hai Guihai. **Name and Position** means Food God at Horse, as Jia meeting Bing or Yi meeting Ding: Horse obtains food. The **Four Births** are Xinsi, Jiashen, Jihai, and Bingyin, whose nayin occupy their own Birth. The **Four Sicknesses** are their own Death or Extinction—for example, a year with Metal nayin, Metal becoming extinct at Yin.”

“**Carrying Treasure** is Jiazi meeting Wuyin: Heavenly Wealth lies above Horse. Or the natal nayin controls Horse as Wealth, resembling the Food-God arrangement: Jiayin meeting Bingshen, or Jiashen meeting Bingyin in day or hour. **Bearing Flowers** is nayin at Arrival at Office meeting Horse: Gengshen, Renzi, and Wuchen all have Wood nayin and meet Yin Horse, Wood's Arrival at Office. Generated-and-Completed Horse and Horse of Name and Position follow the earlier discussion of Lu and likewise indicate distinction.”[^18]

Those meeting Horse welcome exclusive flourishing and dislike Void and mixed impurities, which prevent attainment. They dislike Death and Extinction and welcome Food and Wealth, which are beneficial. Merchants especially favor Carrying Treasure; women especially fear Bearing Flowers. Carrying Treasure brings wealth; Bearing Flowers, sexual misconduct. Bearing Flowers is especially feared when a Wood person meets Gengyin, Yihai meets Yisi, Dingmao meets Dingsi, or Jiwei meets Jisi. Men are often dissolute, women prone to secret attachments. In the luck course, judge as above.

It is also said: “Horse particularly fears **Inverted Food in the stem**. Yiyou meeting Guihai obtains Horse, but Gui turns back and consumes what nourishes the reference: hence the saying, ‘Yi and Gui do not belong to the same category.’ When whip and rein set it moving, examine exchange. A Chen person's Horse is Yin: if Tai Sui is Shen and clashes with Yin, Tai Sui is whip and rein; if the minor progression is Shen, that progression is whip and rein. Movement usually follows. Examine further how Shen and Yin exchange: Gengshen meeting Jiayin, for example. Gengshen belongs to Wood, whose Arrival at Office is Yin; Jiayin belongs to Water, whose Birth is Shen. Mutual harmony is auspicious, mutual control obstructive. If both exchanged positions lack qi, movement is empty or disappointed; with qi, it brings wealth. Four Horses Paying Court to the Origin bring honor and distinction when favorable, but destruction of household and occupation when adverse. A Buddhist or Daoist with this arrangement delights in wandering.”

“Children and old people do not benefit from Horse. Above the age of three and before twelve, Horse clashed by minor progression or Tai Sui, or an encounter with an Arrival-at-Office Horse, often brings fright, illness, falls, and tumbles. Beyond fifty, luck or Tai Sui riding it indicates weak qi and pains in the waist and feet. It resembles old people whose Lu meets Sickness and who often vomit food; younger people encountering it also often fall ill. Old and young alike cannot bear riding Horse, because among the Five Phases it is the influence of leaping and movement.”[^18]

## 4. A General Discussion of Lu and Horse
### 總論祿馬

<!-- source: L138-L140 -->
Lu is the source that nourishes life; Horse the foundation that supports the body. The two most welcome meeting one another. Yin, Wu, and Xu have Horse at Shen, while Jia has Lu at Yin. Deriving from Jia, Yin receives Bing and Shen receives Ren: Bing is **Heavenly Lu**, Ren **Heavenly Horse**. When they appear reciprocally in day and hour, this is called Heavenly Lu and Heavenly Horse. A Jia person deriving to Xu obtains Jiaxu, called **Living Lu**; a Jiazi person deriving Bingyin obtains **Living Horse**.[^19]

Again, Yin, Wu, and Xu have Horse at Shen and obtain Geng as the hour stem; Hai, Mao, and Wei have it at Si and obtain Bing; Shen, Zi, and Chen have it at Yin and obtain Jia; Si, You, and Chou have it at Hai and obtain Ren. Seek the hour stem from the day branch and the day branch from the hour stem. Their reciprocal exchange is **Lu and Horse Crossing at Full Gallop**. Jia's Lu is Yin, and the Horse of Shen, Zi, and Chen is likewise Yin: Jiazi, [Jiashen], or Jiachen birth obtaining a Bingyin hour gathers Lu and Horse at the imperial seat, called **Lu and Horse in One Region**. Again, the place after Lu and before Horse—Xinsi obtaining Xu in day or hour, Yihai obtaining Chen—lies between them. Xin's Lu is You and Horse Hai; Yi's Lu is Mao and Horse Si. Neither Lu nor Horse appears, yet both are enclosed within: **Flanking Lu and Flanking Horse**.[^19]

The *Writings Bequeathed by Guigu* says: “The hour occupies the day's Lu: a path must open into the blue clouds. Five Horses crossing at full gallop can bring one to the Yellow Pavilion.” Luoluzi says: “Turning one's back on Lu to pursue Horse, one remains anxious upon a road of poverty. Lu and Horse in one region: if not the Three Terraces, then the Eight Seats.” Han's *Book of Fate* gives an account of **Solitary-and-Widowed Lu and Horse**: Jiazi meeting Bingyin, or Gengwu meeting Renshen. Although Lu and Horse share a region, Jiazi is the first yang, Yichou the second, Bingyin the third—pure yang without yin. Gengwu is the first yin, Xinwei the second, Renshen the third—pure yin without yang. These two are called Solitary-and-Widowed Lu and Horse: sharing a region does not make them auspicious.[^20]

The *Liyu Song* says: “Lu and Horse fly to Heaven without punishment or control; born in flourishing and support, they must attain distinction.” Lin Kai says: “Horse at Birth calls for wealth and learning; Lu at Imperial Prosperity provides ample money. If instead there is death by stabbing or banishment to labor, Hanging Needle and Robbery Sha have come.” He also says: “Lu reaches the hour but Horse does not: this person has money alone. All his life he disdains talk of welcoming worthy scholars; in Death and Extinction he shuts his door and swears not to open it.” Examining these explanations shows that possessing both Lu and Horse, riding Birth and flourishing, is excellent.

## 5. On the Tianyi Noble Person
### 論天乙貴人

**Tianyi, Heaven's One**, is a celestial spirit outside the Changhe Gate of the Purple Tenuity Enclosure, standing beside Taiyi and serving the Great Emperor of Heaven. It descends to travel through the three celestial divisions. Its home is Jichou, in the station of Dipper and Ox; it emerges at Jiwei, in the mansions Well and Ghosts. Holding the Jade Balance, it weighs the affairs of Heaven and humanity; hence its name. This spirit is supremely honored. Wherever it arrives, every ominous sha quietly withdraws.[^21]

The *Classic of Penetrating Mystery* says: “In Earlier Heaven, Kun occupies the northern Zi position. The **yang Noble Person** begins from Earlier-Heaven Kun, placing Jia at Zi. The yang Noble Person proceeds forward.” The successive assignments are these:[^22]

“Chen is Heaven's Net and is not visited; Wu therefore skips Chen and places its virtue at Mao. Yin stands opposite [Shen] and is called Heavenly Void: Noble Person has no opposite, so Ji skips Yin and dwells at Chou. Xu is Earth's Mesh and is not visited; Ren therefore skips Xu and places its virtue at You. Shen is the original Later-Heaven starting place, not visited a second time; Gui skips Shen and places its virtue at Wei. Thus the dwelling of Jia, Wu, and Geng's Noble Person at Chou and Wei, and its yin–yang beginnings in Earlier and Later Heaven, are clear.”[^22]

Both begin at Kun because Noble Person's home is Jichou and its place of emergence Jiwei: both belong to Earth. They are the combined qi of Kun's second and fifth places, the yellow center. The stems match the branches and the qi of virtue combine: their agreement arises naturally. In this account, “yang Noble” is used after Winter Solstice, “yin Noble” after Summer Solstice; it is not a distinction of day and night. On this explanation, Tianyi is the refined qi of the ten stems, not a celestial star.

I have heard a practitioner say: “The sun rises at Yin, and all the stars disappear; it sinks at Shen, and all the stars emerge. Thus day Noble begins at Yin and night Noble at Shen. Count to Chou or Wei, Tianyi's dwellings: a stem meeting Tianyi there obtains Noble Person. Jia's Lu is Yin: place Yin over Yin and count forward to Chou, reaching its own house; Jia's Noble Person is therefore Chou. Yi's Lu is Mao: place Mao over Yin; when the count reaches Zi, Chou is encountered, so Yi's Noble Person is Zi. Bing's Lu is Si: place Si over Yin; when the count reaches Xu, Chou is encountered. But Xu is a weak and unfavorable place, where Tianyi does not descend, so advance one position: Bing's Noble Person is Hai. Ding's Lu is Wu: place Wu over Yin, and at You encounter Chou; Ding's Noble Person is You.”[^23]

“Wu Earth lodges in Gen, so it also begins from Yin and follows Jia. Ji Earth lodges in Kun: place Wei over Yin, and at Shen encounter Chou; Ji's Noble Person is Shen. Geng's Lu is Shen: place Shen over Yin, and at Wei encounter Chou; Geng's Noble Person is Wei. Xin's Lu is You: place You over Yin, and at Wu encounter Chou; Xin's Noble Person is Wu. Ren's Lu is Hai: place Hai over Yin, and at Chen encounter Chou. But Chen is a weak and unfavorable place, where Tianyi does not descend, so advance one position: Ren's Noble Person is Si. Gui's Lu is Zi: place Zi over Yin, and at Mao encounter Chou; Gui's Noble Person is Mao. Night Noble begins from Shen by the same method, encountering Wei as the former method encountered Chou.”

Here the allotment of Noble Person has fixed positions, whereas merely repeating the song “Jia, Wu, and Geng: Ox and Goat” is rigid and fails to penetrate the method. This explanation reasons directly from the celestial stars and is particularly persuasive. The Six-Ren method, date selection, and other arts now distinguish either yin from yang or day from night, drawing on these two explanations. Huzhongzi says: “Noble Person divides its administration between day and night, each exercising sole authority. A daytime birth meeting day Noble, or a nighttime birth meeting night Noble, receives its effective strength.” Some call the time after Zi day and after Wu night; others call sunrise day and sunset night. These are conjectures. Better simply to divide yin and yang at Yin and Shen, using yang Noble after Winter Solstice and yin Noble after Summer Solstice. A fate receiving yang Noble after the birth of the first yang, or yin Noble after the birth of the first yin, receives effective strength.[^23]

The *Comprehensive View of the Three Vehicles* says: “Jia is yang Wood, riding Lesser Yang and born in the east. At Si its work is finished, so it withdraws into concealment at Wei and obtains Noble Person. Geng is yang Metal, riding Lesser Yin and born in the west. At Hai its work is finished, so it withdraws at Chou and obtains Noble Person. Wu is yang Earth, blending harmoniously at the center and spreading through the four seasons. Through it Jia generates the myriad things and Geng completes them: the principle of generation and completion is thereby fulfilled. Yi is yin Wood, Ji yin Earth. These two positions lack qi, lose their kind, and have nowhere to dwell. They must await the flourishing Water and Earth of Shen and Zi to nourish, fill, and supplement their deficiency; hence they welcome Shen and Zi as Noble Person.”

“Bing and Ding Fire in high summer are so fierce that they harm the myriad things. Their nature is extinguished at You and concealed at Hai; the completing qi of the northwest moderates them. They therefore take the harmonizing yin qi of You and Hai as Noble Person. Ren and Gui Water at the depth of winter become severe and kill the myriad things. They are restrained at Mao and hidden at Si; the warming qi of the southeast moderates them. They therefore take the harmonizing yang qi of Si and Mao as Noble Person. Xin is yin Metal, holding a fixed form and unable to transform itself. It must borrow the Fire of Yin and Wu, in Birth and flourishing, to temper its rigidity and reshape it into a vessel: this makes it noble.”

The *Extensive Record* says: “Jia yang Wood, [Wu] yang Earth, and Geng yang Metal all welcome Earth positions. Wei is Earth's proper position, Chou its place of stillness; hence Ox and Goat are Noble Persons. More precisely, Jia especially welcomes Wei and Geng especially welcomes Chou, each returning to its own Storehouse. Wuzi, Wuyin, and Wuwu welcome Chou, the region of Gestation and Nurturing for Fire people. Wuchen, Wushen, and Wuxu welcome Wei, Wood's Storehouse and Earth's place of Birth and flourishing. Yi is yin Wood and Ji yin Earth. Yin Earth welcomes Birth and flourishing; yin Wood loves yang Water: hence Rat and Monkey are Noble Persons. Yi especially welcomes Zi, Water's flourishing region; Ji especially welcomes Shen, Kun's proper position.”[^24]

“Bing and Ding belong to Fire, whose Tomb is Xu; Ren and Gui to Water, whose Tomb is Chen. Chen and Xu are Kui and Gang, where Noble Person does not descend. Fire's Noble Person is therefore lodged at You and Hai, Water's at Mao and Si: all are regions of returning to the root and recovering life. The six Xin are yin Metal and welcome yang Fire at Birth and flourishing, so Horse and Tiger are Noble Persons. Investigate further through the exchange of nayin. Equality and harmony are required for nobility to become blessing. If Bing Fire obtains You, Fire dies there: how could that alone be noble?”

Yan Dongsou says: “Tianyi must be judged through the Five Phases' preferences and aversions. Jia people, and those of Wu and Geng, obtaining Guiwei and Yichou meet two auspicious influences and carry Seal: this is best. Deriving Dingchou or Xinwei is next best. These three yang stems favor Seal's Storehouse. Yi people obtain the flourishing Earth of Wushen and Gengzi; Ji people the flourishing Water of Jiashen and Bingzi: yin Wood and yin Earth welcome prosperous Wealth. Bing and Ding obtain Dingyou and Yihai; Ren and Gui obtain Yimao and Guisi: Water and Fire do not object to Death and Extinction. The six Xin obtain Bingyin and Bingwu: yin Metal does not object to the victory of its ghost. Obtaining two is best, one next best.” The *Purple Void Scheme* calls this **Noble Person Entering Its Temple**, giving the distinction of gold insignia and purple robes. The *Precious Mirror of the Jade Heavens* instead calls it Entering the Temple when the Five-Tigers derivation reaches Noble Person's own position and the derived stem is actually seen. Without Void or mixed impurities, it indicates pure distinction.[^24]

The *Golden Book of the Eight Characters* further distinguishes better and worse Noble Spirits according to the favorable and unfavorable qualities of the sixty pairs already discussed. The essential point is to share a seat with Lu and Horse, avoid Crossing, Retreating, and Hidden Spirit, and have stem and branch combine auspiciously. Combination of stems and branches in month, day, and hour is particularly important.

Lin Kai discusses **Tianyi in Combination**. Jiazi meeting Jiwei, without Death, Extinction, clash, breaking, or Void, and with auspicious influences helping, gives the highest distinction. The prohibited conditions diminish its force and add difficulty without blessing. Wuzi meeting Jichou, free from those conditions and assisted by blessing, is the second configuration. Death and Extinction weaken it; clash, breaking, and Void make an official buffeted by the dust of worldly affairs. Xinwei meeting Gengyin, free from those conditions and assisted by blessing, is the third. Death and Extinction weaken it; clash, breaking, and Void bring many lifelong difficulties and only a low, undistinguished office.

Yan Dongsou discusses **Noble Combination** and **Noble Food**. Jia obtains Jichou or Jiwei; Wu, Guichou or Guiwei; Geng, Yichou or Yiwei; Yi, Gengzi or Gengshen; Ji, Jiazi or Jiashen; Bing, Xinyou or Xinhai; Ding, Renyin or Renchen. These are called Noble Combination. Jia takes Bing as Food God, Yi takes Ding, and Bing and Ding's Noble Persons are You and Hai: Jia obtains Bingyin or Bingchen, Yi Dingyou or Dinghai. Geng takes Ren as Food God, Xin takes Gui, and Ren and Gui's Noble Persons are Mao and Si: Geng obtains Renshen or Renxu, Xin Guimao or Guisi. These are called Noble Food. Noble Combination brings honor and eminence in office; Noble Food brings ample and satisfying Lu. Possessing all three gives high office and weighty emolument.[^25]

<!-- source: L154-L166 -->
The *Essentials of the Three Fates* places Tianyi upon the Six Combinations of the Noble-Person positions. For Jia, Wu, and Geng, these are Zi and Wu: Zi combines with Chou, Wu with Wei. For Yi and Ji, they are Chou and Si; for Bing and Ding, Yin and Chen; for Ren and Gui, Shen and Xu; for Xin, Wei and Hai. All indicate great blessing; obtaining two or more such combinations is especially noble.

The *Precious Mirror* has **Tianyi Supporting the Body**, taking Noble Persons flanking and supporting Tai Sui. A Renyin person obtains Jiayin in day or hour: Ren's Noble Person is Mao and [Jia's Noble Person] Chou, flanking Yin. Bingshen obtaining Wushen follows the same pattern. Entering a noble configuration, without further punishment or clash, it indicates little illness throughout life and early enjoyment of blessing. An ordinary configuration obtaining it avoids penal imprisonment throughout life.[^26]

There is **Stems Flanking Noble Spirit**: a Jia, Wu, or Geng person obtains Chou or Wei in day or hour, with one of Jia, Wu, or Geng carried above it. This indicates exceptional eminence. The *Guide to the South* has **Flanking Noble Person and Meeting Sixfold Combination**: a Ren or Gui person sees Chen and obtains Guiyou in combination; a Bing or Ding person sees Xu and obtains Dingmao in combination. Tianyi stands on either side. With Lu and Horse coming to the body as well, great wealth and distinction result.

The *Purple Void Scheme* has **Living-Lu Noble Person**: turn from the stem above Noble Person to further stems and branches, then examine birth hour, conception, and month. Jia meets Yin and Wu; Yi and Xin, Chou and Wei; Bing, You and Hai; Ding, Shen and Zi; Wu and Gui, Monkey and Rat; Geng and Ren, Hare and Snake; Ji, Mao and Si. Their full occupation in day and hour indicates great distinction. One example is **Wuxu / Jiazi / Xinsi / Bingshen**. There is **Blessing-Star Noble Person**: in **Renzi / Guimao / Gengxu / Gengchen**, Ren rides the Dragon's back; its Noble Persons are Mao and Si, and obtaining Chen is Blessing Star supporting Noble Person. There is also **Noble Person Supporting Horse and Looking Forward to Blade**, indicating authority and distinction.[^26]

Chen Xilie says: “The stem is Tianyi's general; the branch is Noble Person's commander. A Chou or Wei person obtaining Jia, Wu, or Geng in month, day, or hour meets **Proper Tianyi**. A Jiazi person born in the twelfth month meets Noble Spirit; a Jisi day and Yihai hour give two Tianyi in a fate of combined Noble Persons. Meeting Tianyi in the natal Tai Sui stem's four honored positions or upon Canopy gives a chancellor's fate. Meeting two Tianyi in the four neutral positions gives pure distinction as a close attendant; meeting two in the four avoided positions gives an ordinary official appointment.”[^27]

In judging noble fates, a Chou or Wei person who obtains just two of Jia, Wu, and Geng, a Zi or Shen person who obtains both Yi and Ji, or a You or Hai person who obtains both Bing and Ding, doubles the blessing if the natal year's stem-Lu also provides Direct Officer and Direct Seal. Shen Zhi says: “Tianyi Noble Person appearing in an interwoven arrangement through birth month, day, and hour, with all four characters complete, gives the utmost distinction”—for example, You and Hai together with Bing and Ding. Commentators cite the Duke of Lu's fate, **Jihai / Bingyin / Yiyou / Dingchou**, as the complete four-character interweaving. I say that he reached the chancellorship because the hour did not meet an avoided influence. Had he been born in a You month, Ji's Lu reaching You would meet the avoided condition of Defeat: even with all four characters, the highest distinction would not follow.[^27]

The ancients all said Noble Person brought blessing, without discussing its strength or weakness. Now distinguish its force according to position. Jia meeting Chou and Geng meeting Wei are each at Lu's Storehouse; Wu meeting Chou or Wei obtains Earth branches; Ji meeting Shen shares a position with Birth and flourishing. These four influences, met in birth day or hour with Direct Officer and Direct Seal, bring doubled blessing when upper and lower agree with Proper Tianyi and the Noble Spirit of its own house combines. Yi meeting Shen encounters avoided Extinction, though there is hidden combination; meeting Zi encounters avoided Defeat. Bing and Ding meeting Hai encounter avoided Extinction, though Ding has hidden combination; meeting You encounters avoided Death. Ren and Gui meeting Si encounter avoided Extinction, though Gui has hidden combination; meeting Mao encounters avoided Death. Xin meeting Wu encounters avoided Defeat; meeting Yin, avoided Extinction. These six Noble Spirits share positions with avoided influences: meeting them in birth month, day, or hour gives only two or three parts of blessing. If they share Horse's position and are assisted by Proper Tianyi, their own house's Lu, Direct Officer, Direct Seal, or stem combination, the blessing doubles. Yet this classification treats Death and Extinction as adverse to Noble Person, forgetting that Bing, Ding, Ren, and Gui obtain nobility precisely through Death and Extinction.[^28]

The *Classic Illuminating the Spirits* says: “Tianyi Noble Person in Birth and flourishing gives an elevated appearance, an intelligent and perceptive nature, clear understanding of principle and righteousness, no liking for miscellaneous tricks, and the purity of a great vessel. The person bears the Way and virtue within and is admired and loved. In Death and Extinction, it gives stubborn self-approval and a liking for the company of the eminent. Joined with Robbery Sha, it gives substantial and imposing appearance, many plans and ample strategy. Joined with Official Talisman, it gives graceful writing, lofty discourse, and powerful debate. Joined with Established Lu, it gives pure and substantial writing, generous assistance, and wide association: the character of a gentleman. Falling into Heavenly Void, combining with it, or forming a string of pearls with it, it gives a musician's manner, fondness for song, and skill in the performing arts.”

It is also said: “Tianyi is the most auspicious influence of the Three Fates. Meeting it brings honor, early achievement, and easy advancement in office and Lu. If all Three Fates ride flourishing qi, one eventually reaches the rank of general, chancellor, duke, or marquis. Major and minor luck or the progressing year reaching it also brings promotion and increased wealth: whatever descends upon it becomes an auspicious sign. Noble Person's position generally wants Birth and flourishing, no clash or breaking, an orderly relation of principles, freedom from Void, and harmony between stem and nayin. Better still are Lu and Horse without a conflict of day and night; exchanged Noble Persons between year and hour, as Jiawu meeting a Xinchou hour or Bingshen meeting Jihai; stems gathering to meet Noble Person in one branch, as Chou or Wei birth obtaining Jia, Wu, and Geng; or the four stems sharing a noble place in the branches, forming the Five Phases' gathering of nobility. Heavenly and Monthly Virtue together improve it further.”

The *Liyu Song* says: “If Noble Person falls into Void, with Lu and Horse contrary, it is as though never met.” The *Precious Mirror* says: “Noble Person without qi: present, yet as though absent.” Lin Kai says: “Noble Person in Death and Extinction becomes the sha of mean miserliness.” The *Classic of Penetrating Mystery* says: “When Noble Person grows angry, misfortune comes.” Thus possessing Noble Person in a fate does not justify an immediate judgment of good fortune: the particulars must be examined closely.

## 6. On the Three Wonders
### 論三奇

<!-- source: L174-L176 -->
Luoluzi says: “The wondrous is the noble.” A wonder is something exceptional: a thing is called wondrous because it is precious. **Yi, Bing, and Ding** arise from the subtle pairing of Noble Person's stem virtues with the branches. Yang Noble begins with Jia's virtue at Zi; Yi's is then at Chou, Bing's at Yin, Ding's at Mao: three stems joined without an interval. Yin Noble begins with Jia at Shen; Yi is at Wei, Bing at Wu, Ding at Si: again three joined without an interval. Because they accompany Noble Person in Heaven, they are called the **Three Wonders in Heaven**. Among the ten stems, these alone are exceptional. Others are separated by Net and Mesh or Heavenly Void, cannot revisit a position, or fail to adjoin; they cannot be called Wonders.[^29]

The *Precious Mirror of the Jade Heavens* says that the ancients took the first month as the year's beginning. The sun rises at Yi, so Yi is the **Sun Wonder**. The appearance of the Old Man Star is an auspicious sign, and it appears in Ding's region, so Ding is the **Star Wonder**. The moon illuminates the night; on reaching Bing's region, all beneath Heaven becomes bright, so Bing is the **Moon Wonder**. **Jia, Wu, and Geng** are also called the Three Wonders in Heaven: all descend at Chou and Wei, Noble Person's home in Dipper and Ox and its place of emergence in Well and Ghosts. Beginning Noble Person through Earlier and Later Heaven brings precisely these three stems there, unlike the others. This explanation, too, is intelligible.

The *Comprehensive View of the Three Vehicles* calls Jia the chief of yang Wood, Wu the lord of yang Earth, and Geng the essence of yang Metal. Earth possessing these three things makes them the **Three Wonders of Earth**. That explanation is forced. The *Classic of Taiyi* takes Xin, Ren, and Gui as Water Wonders and calls them the **Three Wonders Among Humanity**. That explanation lacks foundation. Yet Xin, Ren, and Gui form a string of pearls among the heavenly stems, called the **Three Terraces**, and are indeed difficult to obtain. The *Purple Void Scheme* also speaks of Four Wonders. But *qi* means an odd number, whereas four is even: can four be called odd?[^29]

The Three Wonders want forward arrangement, not inversion and disorder. Yi–Bing–Ding or Jia–Wu–Geng distributed in forward sequence through year, month, day, and hour is auspicious. The *Extensive Record*, however, says that Yi birth with a Bing month and Ding hour means Yi generates Bing and Ding, with refined qi descending: ordinary standing results. Yi in the hour, Bing in the day, and Ding in the year lets refined qi rise, bringing distinction. This seems not to judge simply by forward and reverse. For Jia–Wu–Geng, forward order is noble; reverse order gives blessing slowly; disorder gives short life. Clear qi brings distinction, turbid qi wealth. The classic says: “Each of the Five Phases has its Wonders and Instruments: distinguish reverse and forward. If day and month are inverted but the Three Wonders have forward order, this is not called inversion.”[^30]

A fate with the Three Wonders must obtain their proper form and ground, not miss their season. Yi–Bing–Ding born at night, Jia–Wu–Geng by day, obtain proper form. Yi–Bing–Ding with Hai in the pillars gives the three lights somewhere to rest; Jia–Wu–Geng with Shen gives the three substances a support. Yi–Bing–Ding obtaining Chou–Yin–Mao or Wei–Wu–Si, or Jia–Wu–Geng obtaining both Chou and Wei, obtains proper ground. The Wonders again meeting triple combinations—Yi–Bing–Ding obtaining Metal or Wood configurations, Jia–Wu–Geng Water or Fire configurations—and also meeting the **Six Instruments**, are all auspicious. The Instruments are Wu in the Jiazi round, Ji in Jiaxu, Geng in Jiashen, Xin in Jiawu, Ren in Jiachen, and Gui in Jiayin. Luoluzi says: “Repeatedly encountering Wonders and Instruments, one contains the capacity of an exceptional vessel.” *Concealing Jia* says: “Arrange the Three Wonders forward, the Six Instruments backward.” A fate emerging from the Jiazi or Jiashen round and meeting Jia–Wu–Geng repeatedly encounters Wonders and Instruments.[^30]

The Three Wonders indicate an extraordinary spirit, an outstanding breadth of mind, love of the unusual and aspiration toward greatness, wide learning, and many abilities. With Tianyi, achievements surpass the crowd; with Heavenly and Monthly Virtue, calamities do not intrude; with the Six Instruments, intelligence excels; with triple combinations completing a configuration, one becomes a pillar of the state. With Official Talisman and Robbery Sha, capacity and vision are broad. With Void in Birth and flourishing, one leaves dust and convention behind, neither corrupted by wealth and distinction nor bent by power. Joined with Yuanchen, Salty Pool, clash, breaking, Heaven's Net, or Earth's Mesh, the Wonders are judged ineffective. If Tai Sui does not carry them but month, day, and hour do, solitude results. The poems say:[^31]

Read these poems together, and the Three Wonders' preferences and aversions become evident.

## 7. On Heavenly and Monthly Virtue
### 論天月徳

<!-- source: L180-L182 -->
**Virtue** means benefiting things and helping people, covering over evil and bringing forth good. **Heavenly Virtue** is explained as follows. Heaven's circuit contains 365 du and 25½ fen. The twelve palaces each take thirty du, totaling 360. The remaining five du and 25½ fen are distributed among the twelve assisting palaces—Jia, Geng, Bing, Ren, Yi, Xin, Ding, Gui, Qian, Kun, Gen, and Xun—called **Spirits Concealed and Sha Submerged**. Each palace receives forty-four fen. Thus Zi, Wu, Mao, and You contain Jia, Geng, Bing, and Ren 〈Zi contains Ren; Mao Jia; Wu Bing; You Geng〉. Chen, Xu, Chou, and Wei contain Yi, Xin, Ding, and Gui 〈Chou contains Gui; Chen Yi; Wei Ding; Xu Xin〉. Yin, Shen, Si, and Hai contain Qian, Kun, Gen, and Xun 〈Yin contains Gen; Si Xun; Shen Kun; Hai Qian〉. These twelve palace positions can turn ill fortune into good: hence Heavenly Virtue.[^32]

**Monthly Virtue** is the direction illuminated by a triple combination, the position where sun and moon meet. Shen–Zi–Chen meets at You, emerges at Geng, and enters its enclosure at Ren. Hai–Mao–Wei meets at Wu, emerges at Bing, and enters at Jia. Yin–Wu–Xu meets at Mao, emerges at Jia, and enters at Bing. Si–You–Chou meets at Zi, emerges at Ren, and enters at Geng. Thus Ren, Jia, Bing, and Geng are Monthly Virtue; Heavenly Virtue in the four months Chen, Wei, Xu, and Chou belongs to these same stems. In palaces illuminated by sun and moon, all celestial luminaries and earthly sha can be restrained, turning ill fortune into good.

Huzhongzi says: “Heavenly Virtue is yang's virtue. In the first month it begins one position before Qian, at Hai, and proceeds forward. Monthly Virtue is yin's virtue. In the first month it begins one position behind Kun, at Wei, and proceeds forward.” The resulting positions are these:[^33]

| Second | Zi | Shen |
| Third | Chou | You |
| Fourth | Yin | Xu |
| Fifth | Mao | Hai |
| Sixth | Chen | Zi |

| Eighth | Wu | Yin |

| Tenth | Shen | Chen |

Five-Planet astrology bases its Heavenly and Monthly Virtue on this latter explanation, while taking the former as **Heavenly- and Monthly-Virtue Noble Persons**: a distinction between branches and stems.

Yan Dongsou says: “When Noble Spirit occupies its place, the sha lie concealed; when the Two Virtues support, the many evils disperse. A fate carrying ominous sha is spared their worst effects if the Two Virtues support and transform them. They should appear in the day and be seen in the hour, free of control, clash, punishment, or breaking, to be auspicious. Those obtaining them enjoy ease throughout life, avoid punishment and robbery, and find that even encountered disasters disperse of themselves. With the Three Wonders and Tianyi as well, they are especially fortunate. If Wealth, Officer, Seal-Ribbon, or Food God becomes Virtue, each receives double the blessing according to its transformation. In a noble configuration, they bring examination success, the ruler's favor and appointment, or eminence inherited through ancestral privilege. In a lowly configuration, they bring lifelong food and warmth, blessing and longevity together. Even amid obstruction, one keeps one's station and remains steadfast in poverty, not ceasing to be a gentleman. Women obtaining them often become the wives of eminent men.”

The *Key to the Three Fates* says: “Heavenly Virtue is the Five Phases' position of blessing and virtue. Meeting it indicates ascent to the chancellorship. Monthly Virtue accompanying it is better still: even with ominous sha, pure distinction results.” The *Ziping Rhapsody* says: “Seal-Ribbon together with Heavenly Virtue: official punishment does not offend, and old age is free of calamity.” This makes Heavenly Virtue superior to Monthly Virtue.

<!-- source: L184-L202 -->
The *Great Unity Calendar* also has **Heavenly-Virtue Combination** and **Monthly-Virtue Combination**, positions at which the Five Phases accord. Monthly-Virtue Combination: in the first month Bing combines with Xin; in the second, Jia with Ji; in the third, Ren with Ding; in the fourth, Geng with Yi. Continue by analogy. Heavenly-Virtue Combination: in the first month Ding combines with Ren; in the second, Kun with Xun; in the third, Ren with Ding; in the fourth, Xin with Bing. Continue by analogy.[^34]

There is **Monthly Void**: Ren in Yin, Wu, and Xu months; Geng in Hai, Mao, and Wei; Bing in Shen, Zi, and Chen; Jia in Si, You, and Chou. There is **Monthly Aversion**: Xu in the first month, You in the second, Shen in the third, Wei in the fourth, Wu in the fifth, Si in the sixth, Chen in the seventh, Mao in the eighth, Yin in the ninth, Chou in the tenth, Zi in the eleventh, and Hai in the twelfth. There is **Monthly Sha**: Chou in Yin, Wu, and Xu months; Xu in Hai, Mao, and Wei; Wei in Shen, Zi, and Chen; Chen in Si, You, and Chou.

There is **Year-Stem Virtue**: Jia for Jia and Ji; Geng for Yi and Geng; Bing for Bing and Xin; Ren for Ding and Ren; Wu and Gui lodge at Wu. Thus Jia, Geng, Bing, and Ren are taken as Stem Virtue [with Wu's lodging as just stated]. Month-Stem Virtue follows the same rule.[^34]

There are **Heavenly Pardon Days**: Wuyin in spring, Jiawu in summer, Wushen in autumn, and Jiazi in winter. Heaven's concentrated seasonal qi nurtures the myriad things and pardons guilt and fault. A fate gathering Monthly Virtue, Refinement, combination, and Void, born in one of the four greatly auspicious hours, is especially excellent when it also meets a Heavenly Pardon day. There is **Heavenly Joy Spirit**: Xu in spring, Chou in summer, Chen in autumn, Wei in winter. Meeting it brings joy and gladness.

There is **Banner-of-Virtue Sha**: Yin, Wu, or Xu meeting Bing in day or hour; Hai, Mao, or Wei meeting Jia; Shen, Zi, or Chen meeting Ren; Si, You, or Chou meeting Geng. There is **Banner-and-Battle-Axe Sha**: Yin, Wu, or Xu meeting a Yin hour; Hai, Mao, or Wei a Hai hour; Shen, Zi, or Chen a Shen hour; Si, You, or Chou a Si hour. Again, Yin, Wu, or Xu meeting Xin, Hai, Mao, or Wei meeting Ji, Shen, Zi, or Chen meeting Ding, and Si, You, or Chou meeting Yi is also called Banner of Virtue. The classic says: “One influence presides as Banner of Virtue: five generations do not become poor. Within it lies Banner and Battle-Axe, giving generals, chancellors, and the Three Dukes.” Virtue and Battle-Axe meeting together give distinction or wealth.[^35]

Another **Banner-and-Battle-Axe Sha** places Guiyou for Yin, Mao, and Chen people; Guimao for Si, Wu, and Wei; Wuzi for Shen, You, and Xu; Wuwu for Hai, Zi, and Chou. It is the influence exclusively administering execution in the four seasons. For ordinary people it indicates penal servitude or banishment; controlling the natal fate, it indicates a dreadful death. Another **Three-Dukes Sha** places Renzi for Yin, Wu, and Xu people; Bingwu for Si, You, and Chou; Yimao for Shen, Zi, and Chen; Xinyou for Hai, Mao, and Wei. Seated in the concentrated qi of the four directions, it comes to control the birth year: the Five Phases' poisonous qi. Ordinary people encountering it suffer violent, unnatural death. But if Banner and Battle-Axe meets Three-Dukes Sha in the same position, exceptional distinction results. Those who discuss fate today speak of Monthly Virtue while omitting these influences: that is a partial view. I have therefore included them here.

## 8. On Great-Ultimate Noble Person
### 論太極貴

<!-- source: L204-L214 -->
〈Also called the **Examination-Renown Star**.〉

The Great Ultimate means the Great Beginning, the origin: things are first created, and this is the Great Ultimate. It also means completion and gathering: when a thing finds its return, this is its ultimate. Jia and Yi Wood first arise at Zi, generated with the assistance of Kan Water, and end at Wu, burned to death by Li Fire. Ren and Gui Water first obtain Shen and are born, then obtain Si and are received there. The classic says: “Earth sinks in the southeast; the four great waterways all flow toward Xun.” All express beginning and ending. Wu and Ji are Earth: they welcome Birth at Shen and obtain Chen, Xu, Chou, and Wei as their proper Storehouses. The *Liyu Song* says: “The Four Storehouses complete in the hour make the utmost distinction, a place among the highest ranks and command of the balance.” A fate entering a configuration, with auspicious qi and Noble Spirit assisting—how could that not be excellent?[^36]

## 9. On Study Hall and the Literary Academy
### 論學堂詞館

<!-- source: L216-L218 -->
〈Chief Star and examination renown are appended.〉

**Study Hall** is like a place where a person studies books; **Literary Academy** is like the Hanlin office of the present day. The names express concentrated learning and writing that surpasses the ordinary. Birth is Study Hall's proper position: a Metal fate meeting Xinsi, for example, has Metal's Birth at Si, while Xinsi's nayin is also Metal. Arrival at Office is Literary Academy's proper position: a Metal fate meeting Renshen has Metal's Arrival at Office at Shen, while Renshen's nayin is also Metal. Apply the others by analogy.[^38]

Huzhongzi says: “Literary Star enters the fate, and one laughs at Ban and Ma's lack of talent.” The commentary says: “Yihai and Dingsi are Literary Stars,” taking Wood and Fire's Birth and Arrival at Office. Repeated Wood and Fire produce flames, red and green: the image of literary brilliance. Some explain through nayin, saying “Fire encloses yet does not enclose: only Yihai obtains it; Earth encloses yet does not enclose: only Dingsi obtains it.” I fear this is not so.[^39]

Yan Dongsou says that Jia, Yi, Bing, and Ding distributed through year, month, day, and hour, linked without interruption, make blue-green and red into writing: examples are **Jiayin / Yihai / Dingyou / Bingshen** and **Jiazi / Bingyin / Jiayin / Bingyin**, indicating literary brilliance beyond the ordinary. The *Precious Mirror of the Jade Heavens* instead requires Wood, Metal, and Fire complete, red and white forming a pattern. Bingyin meeting Jihai and Xinsi gives Bingyin Fire, Jihai Wood, and Xinsi Metal, all seated at Birth, indicating distinguished literary talent. It also takes Shen, Zi, and Chen complete, all meeting Bing: **Bingzi / Bingyin / Bingchen / Bingshen**. Bing is true Water, and Shen–Zi–Chen Water's proper positions; stem and branch appear in full. With Metal, this brings distinction; even without Metal, it indicates deep waves in the sea of learning. The *Essential Instructions of Guigu* also speaks of repeated Wu and Ji in two positions, carrying flourishing and original Lu: a Wuzi person meeting Wuwu, Wuxu, Jiwei, and Jiyou, for example, indicates splendid writing.[^39]

<!-- source: L220-L226 -->
Various authorities speak of **Study Hall Meeting Lu**. Metal is born at Si and reaches Arrival at Office at Shen; Jia and Yi people obtain these. Water and Earth are born at Shen and reach Arrival at Office at Hai; Bing, Ding, Ren, and Gui people obtain these. Wood is born at Hai and reaches Arrival at Office at Yin; Wu and Ji people obtain these. Fire is born at Yin and reaches Arrival at Office at Si; Geng and Xin people obtain these. Also called **Officer-Noble Study Hall**, it takes Officer/Noble Person's Birth as Study Hall and its Arrival at Office as Literary Academy.

There is **Study Hall Meeting Food**: Jia takes Bing as Food God and obtains Bingyin; Yi takes Ding as Food God and obtains Dingsi; Bing takes Wu as Food God and obtains Wushen. Officer, Seal, and Horse accompanying it deepen its blessing; Lu, Noble Person, Wonders, and Virtue clear its qi; punishment, control, clash, and breaking muddy its qi. Clarity brings lofty examination renown; depth brings honored and illustrious office; turbidity makes blessings and Lu scant, office low and mean.[^40]

There is **Meeting Control at Birth**: Jia and Yi people meeting Xinhai, Ding and Bing people Renyin, Wu and Ji people Jiashen, Geng and Xin people Dingsi. This is called **Officer-Star Study Hall**, indicating examination success and entry among the ruler's attendants. There is nayin meeting Imperial Prosperity with Tianyi above it: a Jiyou person obtaining Bingzi or Gengzi in day or hour, or a Renwu person obtaining Xinmao. This is **Study Hall Meeting Noble Person**, indicating pure distinction.

Study Hall and Literary Academy must avoid Void, clash, and breaking; their stems, branches, and nayin must not meet control, if they are to be effective. The *Classic of Invoked Victory* says: “Jiachen with Bingyin: Study Hall is not genuine. Perhaps only wealth or an inherited privilege, with low office and poverty; reading and study bring nothing but an empty name.” This says Study Hall fears Void. The *Three Vehicles* says, “Study Hall without qi benefits only teachers and scholars”: Study Hall needs flourishing. The *Liyu Song* says, “Study Hall also paying court to Horse brings the highest position and achievements that overshadow the world”: Study Hall needs Horse. It also says: “Born with Lu, Horse, and true Study Hall, one has literary talent if Literary Academy accompanies them. Who understands encountering without truly encountering? Free of clash and control, blessing and emolument flourish.” Again: “Where Literary Stars gather, a marvel among people arises, whose brilliant reputation crowns the heroes. Without true Study Hall at birth, how could talent and learning stand above the crowd?” This says Study Hall fears clash, breaking, and control. Yet a fate entering a configuration and according with creative transformation does not attain this through Study Hall and Literary Academy alone.[^40]

Ziping says: “Study Hall is the clear and refined qi of Heaven and Earth, yin and yang: the influence of the Five Phases' Birth. Examples are Jia meeting Hai and Yi meeting Wu. One occurrence in month or hour suffices; all positions need not be present. Add Tianyi, as a Ding day meeting a You hour or a You month. A scholar obtaining this is intelligent and skillful, with high examination achievement and literary gifts. If the qi is led into a suitable place, without a controlling or suppressing influence, and Virtue and Refinement also appear, the scholarly work surpasses the age. The Wealth, Seal, and Food within the fate stand as its outer and inner supports.”[^41]

The classic says: “Living-qi Study Hall gives writing, wealth, and distinction that crown the age.” It also says: “Study Hall need not be judged exclusively through Officer Star; literary talent need not be judged exclusively through Seal-Ribbon.” The ancients used Birth as Study Hall to express living qi approaching learning. One need not confine it to the day stem: Yin, Shen, Si, or Hai anywhere in the pillars gives learning. These four palaces gather Heaven and Earth's clearest qi. Within them lie the Four Horses and Four Robbery Sha, all expressing the stirring of life. Zi, Wu, Mao, and You are the Four Defeats, with turbid qi; Chen, Xu, Chou, and Wei the Four Storehouses, with mixed qi. When the monthly season reaches Yin, Shen, Si, or Hai, it is neither cold nor hot, and wind and qi are naturally clear, elegant, warm, and harmonious. Their occurrence in year and month is most powerful.

The day stem obtaining its own Birth does not, however, welcome this in the monthly command: if it appears there, forward-moving luck immediately enters Bathing. If the Eight Characters lack Study Hall but at five or seven years the day stem's Birth luck arrives, there is still literary learning. Though not a thoroughly learned scholar, the person does not remain entirely illiterate. If Study Hall is absent, entering Defeat or Death luck at five or seven is especially feared: even a stern teacher finds it difficult to instruct. Both kinds of Study Hall within a fate fear being damaged by their own punishment or control. With punishment, clash, breaking, or injury, study fails to come to fruition. Repetition need do no harm, yet a single clash destroys purity.[^41]

It is also said: “A fate does not welcome wealth flourishing to the utmost. Wealth is a heavy and turbid thing: once it appears, desire and greed arise, and purpose is lost. If Officer Star was already present to suppress the partiality of one's endowment, the disposition is still attached to old forms and cannot adapt. If Wealth flourishes without Officer Star, all is muddy and unclear; even the wealthy and eminent remain foolish.” An old song says: “The Five Phases' Birth positions are Study Hall: examine the forward and reverse movement of yin and yang. Led toward flourishing with support, literary learning becomes noble; meeting control or suppression is unfavorable.” The commentary explains that a ghost above Study Hall constitutes control and suppression.

Those with examination renown have Five Phases naturally clear and pure: Study Hall and Horse in Birth and flourishing upon Metal and Water; Three Wonders and Canopy gathering in day and hour; or punishment and clash in Birth and flourishing, Water and Fire generating one another, auspicious spirits gathering in the hour, and no warfare in the Heavenly Origin. All indicate a clear reputation and lofty examination success.

It is also said: “The Jiachen ten-day round, [here described as] twelve positions, is called **Chief Star**. Within it, four positions are singled out: Jiachen, Dingwei, Gengxu, and Guichou. Jia Wood flourishes in the east, Ding Fire in the south, Geng Metal in the west, Gui Water in the north. The flourishing qi of each direction is placed upon Chen, Xu, Chou, and Wei to make the **True Chief Stars**. When positions from Jiachen through Guichou in this round appear in day and hour, none fails the examination. Two or three such positions ensure a leading place.” It is also said: “Dinghai, Xinmao, and Gengxu are Chief Stars. Meeting them in day and hour often wins first place at the provincial, metropolitan, or palace examination.”[^42]

Within the Four Pillars, two occurrences of stem combination are called **Year-Beginning Star**. For a Zi-born person it lies at Zi; the month established from the natal fate is called **Year's Seat**, indicating abundant literary learning and conspicuous examination renown. The four positions Yin, Shen, Si, and Hai especially welcome the stems derived from the natal year as Chief Stars. A Guisi person derives from Yin to obtain Jiayin, Dingsi, Gengshen, and Guihai; a Dingmao person derives Renyin, Yisi, Wushen, and Xinhai. Apply the rest by analogy.[^42]

Metal and Earth riding refined qi, Metal and Wood riding refined qi, Fire and Wood riding refined qi, or Water and Wood riding refined qi all indicate examination renown. Refined qi means the refined qi of monthly command. In general, stems and branches with qi riding the month's refinement indicate a fate that passes the examinations. An old song says: “The day flourishes in the governing framework; Fire is bright and Wood refined; Metal is white and Water clear; Earth and Metal repeat; Water and Fire achieve completion; Bing and Ding exchange; root and seedling meet Heaven's One; Metal and Water contain one another.” So it is.[^43]

Again, Wood born in spring meets Food and Hurting Officer, with Wealth and Seal both light and Officer and Killings heavy; or Killings are heavy, the body light, and Seal-Ribbon appears; or Chief Star and Officer/Killings are clearly distinguished and exceptional. These, too, are fates of examination success and years in which the examinations are passed.

Huzhongzi says: “Horse together with Wealth in combination presents the lone osprey's commendation at Qin's court; Officer together with Lu in welcome passes the three tiers of waves at Yu's Gate.” The commentary says that the progressing year's Horse clashes with, and does not combine with, the natal Horse, while Heavenly Wealth, Literary Star, or similar influences also appear. The person must rise. If they combine, the rise does not occur. Meeting Officer and Lu ensures completion; without them, there is merely a beginning of advancement. It is also said: “In the year a candidate completes the matter, Tai Sui must above all harmonize with the established month and make blessing. Other combinations, such as Jia–Wu–Geng or Yi–Bing–Ding, are not decisive. Judge through the progressing year's Tai Sui in relation to the natal monthly establishment.” Again: “The major luck must occupy Officer's position, while Tai Sui carries Direct Seal, Proper Tianyi, or the Lu of its own house. This is a year of examination success.”[^44]

## 10. On Direct Seal
### 論正印

<!-- source: L238-L240 -->
**Direct Seal** is the Five Phases' proper Storehouse: a Metal fate meeting Yichou; Wood, Guiwei; Fire, Jiaxu; Water, Renchen; Earth, Bingchen. The *Conversations* says, “Born with Direct Seal, one must receive appointment in the Jade Hall.” The *Subtle Selection* says, “The Five Phases enter their enclosures: office lies among the five administrations.” Thus obtaining the Direct Seal of one's own house is noble. Agreement in virtue with natal root and ruler is highest; its place at the imperial seat is middle; at conception and month, lower. It indicates a substantial, impressive person whose achievements and name are manifest. The Seal of one's own house is finer still when a noble configuration supports it.[^45]

Wood obtaining Water's Seal or Fire obtaining Wood's Seal often acquires additional authority and outside wealth. If the body controls Seal, or Seal controls the body, there is dismissal followed by restoration. Water people obtaining Fire's Seal or Fire people carrying Water's Seal rank below those obtaining their own house's Seal; but the natal root and ruler must have flourishing qi to make them auspicious. If control and breaking occur without an auspicious influence to rescue them, or if Void intervenes, they make only an unoccupied Buddhist or Daoist, or an unsuccessful examination candidate. If the Five Phases have clear qi, the person is an extraordinary recluse; with sha, poor and lowly.

<!-- source: L242-L248 -->
There is **Noble Person Flanking Seal**. A Bing or Ding Fire fate takes Jiaxu as Direct Seal and obtains You and Hai on either side: these are Bing and Ding's Noble Persons. A Ren or Gui Water fate takes Renchen as Direct Seal and obtains Mao and Si on either side: these are Ren and Gui's Noble Persons. There is **Canopy Seal**, as Hai, Mao, or Wei obtaining Guiwei.

There is **Literary Seal**: Wuyin meeting Guiwei; Xinsi Jiaxu; Gengshen Yichou; Guihai Bingchen; Yihai Renchen. Nayin controls the body while the stem influence in turn exerts restraint. Wuwu obtaining Guiwei, Gengzi Yichou, Dingyou Renchen, Jimao Jiaxu, or Xinyou Bingchen gives control between the stems and combination between the branches.[^46]

These Seals require Tomb or Storehouse. With Birth and flourishing supporting, exchanged Lu, Horse, and Noble Person accompanying, and mutual combination, they make the highest distinction. Punishment, clash, breaking, harm, and a ghost upon a triple or sixfold combination are most feared. A Jiaxu day obtaining a Guiyou hour, for example, loses strength. A Water fate gaining its own house's Seal receives no benefit; gaining Wood's Seal loses qi; gaining Earth's Seal gives **Officer Seal** if stems and branches have a relationship, **Ghost Seal** if they do not. Seal appearing in year and hour is called **Phoenix Bearing the Seal**: the minister Yu's chart, **Guiwei / Jiayin / Wuwu / Bingchen**, is the example.[^46]

<!-- source: L250-L262 -->
There is **Blessing-Gathering Seal**. Year, month, day, hour, and conception all lack qi or are in Decline and Defeat, but obtain Direct or Indirect Seal. At that Seal their qi are either stored or flourishing; even sha reaching it are restrained. This is Blessing-Gathering Seal.

There is **Calamity-Gathering Seal**. A Guisi person carries Renchen Seal and repeatedly meets Water in the pillars. All this Water enters its Tomb at Chen, so Guisi receives sha. Si is taken as the fate; when Si Fire meets the excess [of Water], the natal fate becomes sick. This is Calamity-Gathering Seal.

There is **Seal Breaking Blessing into Calamity**. A Water person obtains Water's Seal, while month, day, hour, or conception carries much Earth: the Seal of one's own house meets a flourishing ghost. This breaks blessing into calamity. There is **Seal Carrying Sha**: noble sha appear within Seal, as Renzi meeting Renchen or Bingchen. From Zi to Chen is Canopy; Ren meeting Chen is noble. But if the Seal turns back to control the natal fate and no auspicious influence passes between them, it is called Carrying Sha and is ominous.

There is **Seal Facing Void**: Seal falls into Void and its branch has no Six Combination. Even meeting Officer and Noble Person, it is extremely lowly and achieves nothing. There is **Self-Punishing Seal**: a Gengxu person carries Yichou. Metal meeting Metal's Seal would be good, but Chou and Xu punish one another, Metal punishing Metal. Better not to have such an arrangement: even with a little blessing, it ends lowly. Apply the other cases by analogy.[^47]

In judging Seal, true Five-Phase qi sharing the same affiliation as nayin is especially excellent. Yet it indicates little ease, is unfavorable to close kin, and makes children difficult.[^47]

## 11. On Virtue and Refinement
### 論徳秀

<!-- source: L264-L266 -->
**Virtue** is the virtue of Birth and flourishing in the natal month. **Refinement** is formed when the Five Phases transform through the harmonious, balanced qi of Heaven and Earth. Another explanation calls Virtue the yin–yang spirit that dissolves misfortune, and Refinement the clear, excellent qi of Heaven and Earth, the influence flourishing in its proper season. The assignments are therefore these:[^48]

| Months | Virtue | Refinement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Yin, Wu, Xu | Bing and Ding. | Wu–Gui. |
| Shen, Zi, Chen | Ren, Gui, Wu, and Ji. | Bing–Xin and Jia–Ji. |
| Si, You, Chou | Geng and Xin. | Yi–Geng. |
| Hai, Mao, Wei | Jia and Yi. | Ding–Ren. |

A person whose fate obtains Virtue and Refinement, without breaking, clash, control, or suppression, is endowed with intelligence, warmth, substance, and harmonious qi. Meeting Study Hall and carrying Wealth and Officer indicates distinction. Clash and control diminish their power.

## 12. On Robbery Sha and Loss Spirit
### 論刼煞亡神

<!-- source: L268-L270 -->
**Robbery** means seizure: being deprived from outside. **Loss** means disappearance: losing from within. Robbery lies at the Five Phases' Extinction; Loss at their Arrival at Office. Both are found among Yin, Shen, Si, and Hai. Water becomes extinct at Si, so Shen–Zi–Chen takes Si as Robbery Sha: Wu Earth within Si robs Water. Fire becomes extinct at Hai, so Yin–Wu–Xu takes Hai: Ren Water within Hai robs Fire. Metal becomes extinct at Yin, so Si–You–Chou takes Yin: Bing Fire within Yin robs Metal. Wood becomes extinct at Shen, so Hai–Mao–Wei takes Shen: Geng Metal within Shen robs Wood.[^49]

An old song says:

> Robbery Sha makes a calamity hard to withstand;  
> Vainly one runs upon the field of profit and fame.  
> Guard against the utter loss of ancestral property:  
> How can wife and children long remain secure?

Another says:

> Birth is met in the four positions, and Robbery comes again:  
> At court one establishes achievements, foremost among scholars.  
> With Officer and Noble Person also in the hour,  
> Upright integrity wins a name in the Censorate.

Another says:

> Enclosed by Robbery Spirit, one meets Officer Star:  
> One holds military power to assist the sagely ruler.  
> Commanding awe without anger, admired by all,  
> One must bring peace and honor throughout the realm.

Another says:

> Robbery Sha is originally the chief of sha;  
> Body Palace and fate ruler should not come here.  
> If it forms the chief configuration, death should follow;  
> A baleful luminary descending leaves no doubt.  
> If no star occupies this position,  
> Examine the triple combinations in detail.  
> An ominous star added by the heavenly plate  
> Makes life a lamp in the wind, soon extinguished.[^50]

Water generates Wood: Shen, Zi, and Chen take Hai as **Loss Spirit**, because Jia Wood in Hai drains Water. Fire generates Earth: Yin, Wu, and Xu take Si, because Wu Earth in Si drains Fire. Metal generates Water: Si, You, and Chou take Shen, because Ren Water in Shen drains Metal. Wood generates Fire: Hai, Mao, and Wei take Yin, because Bing Fire in Yin drains Wood.

An old song says:

> Loss Spirit, the Seventh Sha, brings no light misfortune:  
> Every device is tried, and nothing succeeds.  
> Children harmed, wife punished, ancestral property gone—  
> Even an official fears an empty name.

Another says:

> If the Life Palace meets Loss Spirit,  
> Birth and Noble Person must be encountered.  
> With Heaven and Earth combining in day and hour,  
> Selfless and steadfast, one becomes the ruler's minister.

Another says:

> All call the Seventh Sha Loss Spirit;  
> Do not say its calamities are light.  
> If body and fate occupy this place,  
> Poverty and obstruction fill a lifetime.  
> If ominous stars and harmful luminaries descend,  
> The major limit is like treading on thin ice.  
> Examine the triple combinations clearly:  
> Sha coming to flank and arch make progress difficult.

### The Sixteen Forms of Robbery Sha

<!-- source: L272-L276 -->
〈Also called **Great Sha**.〉 When auspicious, it gives intelligence and quick response, exceptional talent, action without delay, a mind embracing the myriad forms, clarity and swiftness, martial virtue, and unexpected wealth. This is when it is in Birth and flourishing and joins noble influences and Established Lu. When ominous, it gives obscurity, depravity, extravagance, a heavily malicious nature, chronic illness, punishment and penal servitude, wounds from weapons, stubbornness and inward cruelty, acquisitiveness, and lack of feeling. This is when it is in Death and Extinction and joins harmful sha. With Yuanchen and Void it makes a thief. With Metal Spirit and Geng or Xin, it favors cutting, carving, engraving, and chasing. With Void and Metal–Fire, it makes a smith, butcher, broker, hunter, or keeper of caged animals. If Robbery Sha controls the body and Metal Spirit and Yang Blade control it as well, it indicates overturned carriages or falls from horses. In the birth hour, it gives foolish and unworthy descendants.[^51]

<!-- source: L278-L292 -->
**1. Robbery Sha Gathering Treasure 刼煞聚寶**, also called **Precious-Pearl Robbery Sha**. 〈I control the other.〉 The year controls Robbery with strength, and the relation with the day ruler is harmonious: this indicates wealth and abundance.

**2. Robbery Sha Fit for Authority 刼煞宜權**, also called **Cap-and-Robes Robbery Sha**. 〈Arrival at Office and Void. Void is reckoned through the ten days and nights; Arrival at Office is apportioned through the ten day-positions.〉 It gives a person of proper dress and distinguished appearance, a noble person—how much more so with Birth and flourishing in the day! [The commentary's reckoning is compressed.][^52]

**3. Robbery Sha of Worthy Counsel 刼煞嘉謨**, also called **Ordering-Principles Robbery Sha**. 〈The body is not controlled by sha, while sha fails to obtain its position.〉 The ruler is strong and sha weak; the day has nobility from another position. The person's bearing is never rash, accords with propriety, and rests in constancy.

**4. Robbery Sha Proclaiming Commands 刼煞奏號**, also called **Banner Robbery Sha**. 〈Noble Person arrives together with it.〉 Robbery sits upon Noble Person and offers assistance to the day ruler. In every affair difficulty becomes ease; others naturally admire the person, whose blessings are exceptional.

**5. Robbery Sha Displaying Auspicious Signs 刼煞呈瑞**, also called **Pendant-Crown Robbery Sha**. 〈The true Birth position.〉 Robbery occupies Birth and possesses the day ruler's Wealth, Officer, or comparable Lu: this indicates distinction.

**6. Robbery Sha Becoming Timely Rain 刼煞為霖**, also called **Salt-and-Plum Robbery Sha**. 〈Interwoven Noble Persons.〉 The hour carries this sha; its stem and branch exchange Noble Persons with the year's ruler. If the day also has a sympathetic relation, fame and profit become conspicuous.[^53]

**7. Robbery Sha Generating Above 刼煞生上**, also called **Storehouse-Hall Robbery Sha**. 〈What is below generates what is above.〉 The hour carries this sha in its own Birth or flourishing and assists the year. A single occurrence is best; the two ends also welcome clash and breaking. If year and hour combine through their stems while sha lies beneath in the branches, it indicates the destruction of house and occupation—especially when its relation with the day is unsympathetic.[^53]

**8. Robbery Sha Contending with Its Own Kind 刼煞類爭**, also called **Strife Robbery Sha**. 〈A Fire person meeting a Fire-kind sha.〉 Without restraint or governance, it gives butchers, wine sellers, spirit-mediums, and healers. With restraint and governance, it forms a noble configuration of blessing and longevity. Examine the day's good and ill and adjust the judgment accordingly.

<!-- source: L294-L308 -->
**9. Robbery Sha Forming an Intention 刼煞造意**, also called **Holding-a-Child Robbery Sha**. 〈I go to generate the other.〉 The year generates sha as a mother generates a child. If Lonely Spirit or punishment joins it, it indicates loss of family and harm to children, or wealth consumed by sons and daughters. If it also controls or drains the day ruler, there is no doubt.

**10. Unwholesome Robbery Sha 刼煞非良**, also called **Greedy-Indulgence Robbery Sha**. 〈Sha comes to control me.〉 It indicates acquiring wealth unrighteously, harming oneself through wealth, or suffering calamity through greed and miserliness. If it constitutes an ominous sha for the day, this must occur; if it provides noble qi, matters may be somewhat better.

**11. Robbery Sha of Destruction and Burning 刼煞毁焚**, also called **Mulberry-Shade Robbery Sha**. 〈House-Breaking Sha controls the year.〉 It additionally commands the influence that breaks the dwelling and comes to control the year. Not only is there no house to live in: poverty lets dust gather in steamer and cooking pot. If Loss Spirit also controls and drains, there is no rescue.[^54]

**12. Robbery Sha Drunk with Desire 刼煞酣慾**, also called **Amorous Robbery Sha**. 〈Combination rouses it: I am weak, the other strong.〉 Day or hour encounters a single or double combination; sha is neither subdued nor controlled, and the day has no favorable influence to help. Drink and sex ruin the household; shame and integrity are unknown. The person spends life drinking deeply and delighting in music.

**13. Light-and-Graceful Robbery Sha 刼煞輕盈**, also called **Pipes-and-Strings Robbery Sha**. 〈Sha meets Death or Extinction; I generate it, and combination also rouses it.〉 It gives a captivating manner and beautiful form, making a daily livelihood amid spring breezes, flowers, and willows. This is because the day is “not extinct,” yet auspicious spirits provide no support.[^54]

**14. Robbery Sha Massing Its Allies 刼煞黨衆**, also called **Heavenly-Prison Robbery Sha**. 〈Two sha come together to control the year.〉 Even with distinction, life is short; one may die abroad in military service, or otherwise become a thief. If stem and nayin also control and drain the day, and enter Void or Extinction, the result is beyond doubt.

**15. Fierce Robbery Sha 刼煞暴厲**, also called **Sword-and-Spear Robbery Sha**. 〈Loss and Robbery are both complete, with further control, Yang Blade, and repeated combinations.〉 It indicates violent conduct and a dreadful death. If stem and nayin control or drain the day ruler, escape is certainly difficult.[^55]

**16. Robbery Sha of Empty Leisure 刼煞空閒**, also called **Mists-and-Sunset-Clouds Robbery Sha**. 〈Lonely Spirit and Widow Lodging arrive together.〉 This sha encounters two among the four positions of loneliness, widowhood, punishment, and separation, together with the qi of Death or Extinction controlling or draining the day ruler. The person becomes a Buddhist or Daoist. Distinguish by Water and Fire images: much Water makes a Daoist, much Fire a Buddhist. An ordinary layperson is exceptionally solitary and impoverished; noble qi mingling with it offers some relief.

### The Sixteen Forms of Loss Spirit

<!-- source: L310-L312 -->
〈Also called **Official Talisman**, and also **Seventh Sha**.〉[^56]

When auspicious, it gives severity and authority, strategy and calculation, insight as though inspired, and plans whose workings remain hidden. In war it employs deception and strives for victory from beginning to end; in speech it analyzes and distinguishes, and in the prime of life it gains employment. This is when it occupies Birth and flourishing and joins noble sha. When ominous, it gives impatience and narrowness, deceit and wild presumption, rootlessness and disputes, drink and amorous pleasure, official trouble and imprisonment, ulcerous illness and disorders of qi and blood, a manner lacking humility, loss of position and of support below, and punishment or reprimand through military and judicial affairs. This is when it occupies Death and Extinction and joins harmful sha. With Noble Person and Established Lu, it devotes itself to brush and inkstone, composing and embellishing words, building a household through public service and sharing in official profit, or serving as a clerk. With Fire controlling the body, it gives stammering, weak breath, and frequent illness in the waist and feet.[^56]

<!-- source: L314-L326 -->
**1. Loss Spirit Storing Wealth 亡神富藏**, also called **Noble-Station Loss Spirit**. The year controls sha: Loss Spirit submits to the year's control. I must prevail over the other to receive its surrender. If it becomes the day ruler's Wealth, Officer, or noble qi, or comes to generate and assist the day ruler, it indicates great wealth and distinction.

**2. Loss Spirit at Birth 亡神長生**, also called **Ritual-Jade Loss Spirit**. Loss Spirit occupies Birth and also carries noble Lu from another position related to the day: it soars upward and advances early.

**3. Loss Spirit at Arrival at Office 亡神臨官**, also called **Carriage-and-Cap Loss Spirit**. Loss Spirit enters Arrival at Office but falls into Void, which instead makes it auspicious. If stem and nayin can also generate the day, it indicates the distinction of riding an official carriage and wearing a ceremonial cap, although two or three parts of indulgence in drink and sex remain.

**4. Loss Spirit of the Imperial Carriage 亡神鑾輿**, also called **Great-Cauldron Loss Spirit**. Loss Spirit occupies the hour's seat, while year and hour exchange Noble Persons passing back and forth. If the stem influence assists the day ruler, distinction follows.

**5. Self-Possessed Loss Spirit 亡神自如**, also called **Compass-and-Square Loss Spirit**. Sha occupies weak ground while the year enters a strong palace. Even without controlling sha directly, sha lacks its proper position and submits. With additional noble qi belonging to the day, this indicates a worthy, capable, modest, careful, upright, and publicly diligent recipient of official emolument.

**6. Loss Spirit Generating the Root 亡神生本**, also called **Parental Loss Spirit**. Loss Spirit generates the year and occupies Birth or flourishing. Only one occurrence is appropriate. If Wealth, Officer, or another noble influence relates to the day, it indicates an abundance and gathering of essence and spirit.

**7. Loss Spirit of the Righteous House 亡神義門**, also called **Silk-and-Gauze Loss Spirit**. Noble Person arrives with it. Day or hour encounters this sha, and its stem and branch do not injure the day: blessing and splendor follow.

<!-- source: L328-L342 -->
**8. Loss Spirit of the Brocade Quarter 亡神錦里**, also called **Sons-and-Daughters Loss Spirit**. The year goes to generate the other. Meeting loneliness, separation, or Robbery Sha is ominous; carrying Lu and Noble Person is auspicious. A single occurrence is excellent. Combine this with the day's governance and judge good and ill together.

**9. Unsubdued Loss Spirit 亡神未降**, also called **Halted-Power Loss Spirit**. Year and sha share one qi, as a Wood person meeting a Wood sha. Neither controls nor subdues the other, and the day provides no governing restraint. Without noble qi, this gives only butchers, brokers, spirit-mediums, healers, painters, and the like.

**10. Reckless Loss Spirit 亡神妄作**, also called **Plundering Loss Spirit**. From below, this sha turns back to control the year and drain the day's qi. It indicates acquiring wealth without right and speaking wildly: a practitioner of healing, ritual, or other arts who talks of Heaven and Earth.

**11. Loss Spirit Howling in the House 亡神嘯宅**, also called **Ditch-and-Ravine Loss Spirit**. Loss Spirit becomes House-Breaking Sha, also controlling and draining the year, without a sympathetic relation to the day. Its plans are clumsy and its schemes exhausted: a beggar in life, without inner or outer coffin in death.

**12. Loss Spirit Dancing in the Crowd 亡神舞羣**, also called **Drum-and-Music Loss Spirit**. The sha is neither subdued nor controlled, but an influence of combination rouses it. The person makes a livelihood amid noisy crowds, flowers, and wine. Without noble qi belonging to the day, judge in this manner.

**13. Loss Spirit of Shallow Malice 亡神薄惡**, also called **Spurting-Blood Loss Spirit**. Loss and Robbery are both present; year and hour combine through their stems, while nayin controls the year. This must bring punishment, or else serious illness. When the day is controlled or drained and no noble qi is present, it certainly manifests.

**14. Entranced and Drowning Loss Spirit 亡神迷溺**, also called **Flower-and-Willow Loss Spirit**. This influence comes alone to combine; sha's qi is hard and strong, while my body is soft and weak. It certainly indicates a life passed in singing, flowers, and wine. If it also draws out the day's ominous sha, food and clothing become hard to obtain, and consumption brings premature death.

**15. Wayward Loss Spirit 亡神乖張**, also called **Cangue-and-Chains Loss Spirit**. Loss Spirit appears twice in day and hour and also carries combination. It gives tattooed faces and skin, penal servitude or exile, or subordinate clerical service. Control and depletion of the day ruler make it worse.[^56]

<!-- source: L344-L350 -->
There is **True Loss and Robbery**: Yin, Wu, or Xu people meet Guisi and Guihai; Si, You, or Chou people Bingshen and Bingyin; Shen, Zi, or Chen people Dinghai and Dingsi; Hai, Mao, or Wei people Renyin and Renshen. An isolated occurrence indicates misfortune.[^57]

There is **Wealth Above Robbery**: a Yin, Wu, or Xu person with a Jia stem meets Jihai; a Si, You, or Chou person with an Yi stem meets Wuyin. Wealth in the stem appears above Robbery Sha. It indicates great accumulations of riches, engrossing others' property without scruple, and cruel harm to living things. Two occurrences instead indicate poverty. There is **Ghost Above Robbery**: a Shen, Zi, or Chen person with a Jia stem meets Xinsi; a Hai, Mao, or Wei person with an Yi stem meets Gengshen. Robbery meets Officer-Ghost. It indicates no office, or dismissal for some incident even if office is obtained; entry into close attendance on the ruler is difficult. Ordinary people are repeatedly robbed and experience gathering and scattering throughout life.

There is **Robbery Sha in Combination**: Jiayin meets Jihai; Bingyin Xinhai; Wuyin Guihai; Gengyin Yihai; Renyin Dinghai; Jiashen Jisi; Bingshen Xinsi; Wushen Guisi; Gengshen Yisi; Renshen Dingsi. These ten arrangements in the day are called **Banner Sha**, in the hour **Hero Sha**. They indicate martial affairs.

<!-- source: L352-L356 -->
There are **Divided Robbery** and **Gathered Robbery**. Divided Robbery is a Jiazi person obtaining a Jisi month and a Jisi hour: Zi's one Robbery is lodged in two Si positions, which share its reception, so the calamity is lighter. Gathered Robbery is a Jiazi person obtaining a Bingzi month and a Jisi hour: two Zi gather their Robbery at one Si, which receives it alone, so the harm is heavier.[^58]

It is also said: “Robbery Sha governs killing; living qi governs life. If living qi occurs twice and Robbery once, life is stronger than Robbery. If Robbery occurs twice and living qi once, Robbery is stronger than life. When living qi is more abundant, judge through living qi; when Robbery is more abundant, judge through Robbery. One cannot generalize. If the Four Pillars meet Birth and encounter one another reciprocally, they indicate the offices of censor, remonstrator, general, or commander.” Apply the same reasoning to Loss Spirit. In general, both sha should be controlled by the body, not control the body. Sha in the day harms the wife—or a woman's husband; sha in the hour harms children.

There are also **Heaven Sha**, **Earth Sha**, **Year Sha**, and **Punishment Sha**. Heaven Sha lies two positions ahead of Robbery; Earth Sha five positions ahead, at Chen, Xu, Chou, or Wei. Year Sha lies three positions ahead, likewise at Yin, Shen, Si, or Hai. Punishment Sha lies seven positions ahead, sharing General Star's positions at Zi, Wu, Mao, or You. All four indicate authority. Without controlling the body, they do not make calamity; controlling it, they cause grave harm, like Loss and Robbery. Carrying Metal and Earth makes a military official; Water and Wood, a civil official. A civil official carrying Earth and Metal as sha also commands military power.[^58]

## 13. On Yang Blade
### 論羊刃

<!-- source: L358-L360 -->
〈The opposite palace is called **Flying Blade**, also **Tang Talisman**.〉

The *Three Vehicles* says: “*Yang* expresses hardness; *blade* takes the sense of slaughter and cutting. When Lu exceeds its proper measure, Blade arises. Once achievement is complete, one should withdraw. Failing to withdraw oversteps one's allotted share, like a sheep brought beneath the blade: injury is implied. Thus Yang Blade always lies one position ahead of Lu.”[^59]

Xiyin says: “The principles of yin and yang and the myriad things all abhor excessive flourishing. At the extreme, Fire scorches and goes out; Water surges and runs dry; Metal snaps and chips; Earth collapses and splits; Wood breaks and falls. Completion that has not reached the extreme brings blessing; the extreme is about to reverse into misfortune. The ten stems' central, proper positions are precisely these places of utmost flourishing. Mao is Jia's proper position, the extreme of yang Wood; Chen is Yi's, the extreme of yin Wood. Wu is Bing's, the extreme of yang Fire; Wei is Ding's, the extreme of yin Fire. You is Geng's, the extreme of yang Metal; Xu is Xin's, the extreme of yin Metal. Zi is Ren's, the extreme of yang Water; Chou is Gui's, the extreme of yin Water. At their extremes the qi are hard, fierce, violent, and discordant. Hence the position ahead of Lu is Yang Blade, and its direct opposite Flying Blade. Flourishing before the extreme is gentle, harmonious, and free-flowing: hence the position behind Blade is Lu.”

Huzhongzi says: “Whoever possesses Lu must be granted a blade to guard it.” This is the meaning. Yixing's *Book of Fate* says: “Repeated Yang Blades, with Lu as well: wealth and distinction, gold and jade in abundance.” The *Classic of Penetrating Mystery* says: “Officer and Seal assist one another, and blessings lend support.” Thus Yang Blade carrying Lu, further supported by Officer and Seal, is especially to be judged auspicious.

Yang Blade alone indicates exposed eyes, impatience, violence, harm to things, and closeness to wicked companions. Birth and flourishing are somewhat tolerable; Death and Extinction are worse. Meeting it where the Five Phases are in Defeat often brings swollen glands, pestilential illness, or injury by metal blades. Noble or lowly, the person is burdened by confused affairs and urgent labor, with little ease. The *Classic of Taiyi* says: “For the six Jia births, Yimao and Dingmao are **True Yang Blades**. Repeated offenses indicate disability, loss and decline in office and Lu, and ruin in old age. The other Mao positions are **Partial Blades**, with lighter effects.”[^60]

<!-- source: L362-L370 -->
The *Guangxin Collection* has **Wealth Above Blade**, as a Jia person meeting Jimao, called **Melting Sha**. It indicates exhausted wealth. Ordinary people make their living through butchery, selling wine, knives, saws, and similar work, or lose their lives through robbery. There is **Ghost Above Blade**, as Jia meeting Xinmao, called **Blade-Holding Sha**. It indicates an unhappy end. Even in a noble configuration, the outcome cannot be confidently reckoned; it is especially pressing for Jia and Yi people, who often die of carbuncles upon the head or back.

The *Golden Book's Instructions on Fate* has **Yang Blades Devouring One Another**: Jiayin with Tiger and Hare, Jiaxu with Dog and Hare, and so forth. Encountering the devouring relation in year or month is somewhat tolerable; in day or hour it is gravely dangerous. If it occurs twice and also meets Void, even without mutual devouring there is banishment or penal exile, and no good end in old age.[^61]

Shen Zhi's *Marrow of the Source* has **Yang Blade Paying Court to the Origin**, as a Mao year with Jia in day or hour, indicating misfortune. If the day stem forms Blade in the hour, it indicates scarring or illness; otherwise the children carry it, or children are few. If the hour stem forms Blade in the day branch, it indicates a wife's dreadful death and an ill-endowed nature; otherwise the person is a soldier or bears scars or illness. If the year stem forms Blade in the hour branch, it often indicates a dreadful death for the parents; with Heaven Sha above it, there is no doubt. Blade in the conception position, also carrying punishment of the year, indicates an unhappy origin, a dreadful death of the parents, or harm to a woman. The year stem forming Blade in the day branch indicates a dreadful death for the parents. Judge the remaining cases through their respective year, month, day, and hour positions.[^62]

An old poem says:

> Blade concealed in the hour may enter conception;  
> The day's Blade may come to pay court in the hour.  
> If stem and branch also punish and control one another,  
> Calamity must answer in the wife, the body, pregnancy, or birth.

If a woman's fate is like this, one may pronounce concern for danger in childbirth. Thus mutually punishing Yang Blades make the gravest calamity.

There are also **String-of-Pearls Blades**: Gengxu with Xinyou; Wuwu with Jiwei; Bingwu with Dingwei; Jiachen with Yimao; Renzi with Guichou. All are ominous images. Metal acts urgently, Wood slowly. In a woman's fate, they certainly harm husband and children and indicate unchastity. The *Liyu Song* says:

> Inverted Yang Blade traveling together again:  
> The body cannot escape filling ditch or ravine.

It also says:

> Flying Blade and Reversed Spear end in discord;  
> The ordinary person obtaining them meets calamity.  
> If Void and Road-Blocking appear together as well,  
> How can this body rise out of the dust?

Again: “Yang Blade together with Reversed Spear must make a headless ghost.” Yang Blade carrying other harmful sha is thus particularly ominous.

<!-- source: L372-L374 -->
In the luck course, Yang Blade is especially feared: undertakings are delayed. Scholar, farmer, artisan, or merchant—all dislike it. The classic says, “Luck passing through Yang Blade: possessions are consumed and scattered.” This is the meaning.

The *Precious Mirror of the Jade Heavens* has the configuration **Taking the Reins to Make the Realm Clear**. Noble Person rides Horse and looks forward toward Yang Blade, like Horse Wearing a Sword at Its Head. A Gengwu person obtaining Yiyou or Jiyou in day or hour, and carrying Jiashen, enters the configuration. Wu's Horse is Shen; Geng's Lu is Shen; Yi and Ji's Noble Person is Shen; Geng's Yang Blade is You. Yi and Ji's Noble Person rides Jiashen as Horse and looks forward toward Blade: hence the name. This configuration often makes a pure and stern official. With further auspicious influences, it often makes a harsh official capable of restraining criminals.[^63]

Ziping, however, gives Blade only to the five yang stems Jia, Bing, Wu, Geng, and Ren, not to the five yin stems Yi, Ding, Ji, Xin, and Gui. For the latter, Hurting Officer brings calamity like Yang Blade. Here *yang* means the yang of yin and yang, not the sheep of oxen and sheep. Its meaning is explained later under the Yang-Blade configuration.[^59]

## 14. On Void
### 論空亡

<!-- source: L374-L376 -->
〈Also called **Heavenly-Center Sha**; the positions opposite Void are **Lonely-and-Empty Sha**.〉

*Empty* stands against *full*; *absent* against *present*. The *Shenbai Classic* says: “Void, Void—how many kinds! Where the ten stems do not reach, reckon emptiness.” The *Classic of Penetrating Mystery* says: “When derivation is exhausted, absence arises.” Thus the end of a Jia round is called Void. A position without Lu is empty; a branch without a stem is absent. In the Jiazi round, the ten stems are complete on reaching You, leaving no stem for Xu or Hai. Apply the other five rounds by analogy: this is Void. Yet emptiness can have substance, and absence can retain presence. One cannot immediately pronounce it ominous.[^64]

Luoluzi says of Void: “The five yang positions use one yang; the five yin positions use one yin.” Jiazi, Bingyin, Wuchen, Gengwu, and Renshen therefore use Xu, not Hai; Yichou, Dingmao, Jisi, Xinwei, and Guiyou use Hai, not Xu. Yang is allotted to yang years, yin to yin years. Another explanation takes Jiazi through Wuchen as using Xu, Jisi through Guiyou as using Hai, dividing the earlier and later groups of five years. Within this, Jiazi through Wuchen meeting Renxu is heavy, meeting Wuxu or the like light; Jisi through Guiyou meeting Guihai is heavy, meeting Yihai or the like light. Jiazi birth at a Jiaxu hour directly encounters it in the hour but is relatively light; Jisi birth at a Guihai hour encounters it there most heavily. The *Rhapsody Pointing Through Delusion* says: “Lu entering Void must be divided between the preceding and following positions.” This distinguishes yin and yang and clarifies degrees of weight.[^65]

The *Golden Book of the Eight Characters* says: “Ren and Gui fall into Void in the Jiayin round; Jia and Yi in Jiachen; Bing and Ding in Jiashen; Geng and Xin in Jiaxu.” It discusses stems through the two branch positions. Some call this **Ten Evils and Great Defeat**: birth on the offending day indicates poverty and low standing. Yet many fates encounter Void and still form effective configurations.

The *Classic of Penetrating Mystery* says: “Deep and clear, yet empty and without qi” 〈much Void is called the **Deep-and-Clear Configuration**〉; “rounded in resourcefulness, establishing a school of one's own” 〈Void upon Lu, Horse, Noble Person, or Officer makes the **Nine-Schools Configuration**〉; “words have pattern and adorn the name” 〈Study Hall meeting Void〉; “the nature is uncontrived and limpid” 〈one's own Death or Extinction meeting Void〉; “a fine endowment worthy of love” 〈many Noble Persons meeting Void make one lovable〉; “achievement and reputation worth treasuring” 〈many Canopies meeting Void make these precious〉; “unhurried, quiet, and free from burden; accomplished in carving and elegant embellishment” 〈auspicious spirits in rest, confinement, flourishing, or support, meeting Void〉; “possessing exceptional talent and the courage to decide” 〈spirits and sha point secretly toward Void: though surpassing others, one lacks blessing〉. Examined closely, these contain auspicious possibilities too.[^66]

<!-- source: L378 -->
Those carrying this sha in Birth and flourishing have broad bearing, acts that win an empty name, a tall and full physique, and many unexpected, unsought blessings. In Death and Extinction, life alternates between success, failure, and wandering. Yet where I have qi, it cannot make calamity. Stems or branches combining with Heavenly Center are particularly avoided: this is called **The Petty Person Obtaining Position**, with deceit and trickery stopping at nothing. If I control it, this is **Heavenly Center Receiving Calamity**, which instead brings exceptional blessing. 〈For example, a Wuwu Fire person meeting Jiazi Metal.〉

This influence has no constant nature. Joined with Official Talisman, it gives flattery and elaborate writing; with Robbery Sha, cunning courage; with Loss Spirit, wandering like windblown tumbleweed; with Great Consumption, confusion and disorder; with Established Lu, lifelong loss and dispersal; with Salty Pool and the Six Harms, repeated misfortune and sudden death. Only with Flanking Noble Person, Canopy, the Three Wonders, or Study Hall does it make a person of great intelligence beyond ordinary convention.

In discussing Void, do not speak only of Tai Sui: the birth day's ten-day-round Void is especially important. Reciprocal Void between Tai Sui and the day is worse. If substance is made empty, substance can still reflect emptiness; if emptiness is made empty, there is no substance to reflect. Thus reciprocal Void brings deep calamity. A Jiazi year with a Renxu day, for example: Jiazi's proper Void is Renxu; Renxu belongs to the Jiayin round, whose Void in turn lies at Zi. It indicates lifelong consumption and scattering of wealth and great destruction of the dwelling. Apply the other cases by analogy.[^67]

With exchange between day and hour, the hour acts urgently and the day slowly. If the day offends but the hour punishes, harms, clashes with, or breaks it—or the hour offends but the day does so—it can also bring blessing, although difficulty remains. It is also said: “Heavenly Center cannot be called wholly ominous. If the pillars contain harmful spirits or sha where calamity gathers, Void is needed to dissolve them. Void should then avoid combination: combination prevents emptiness. If Lu, Horse, Wealth, Officer, and the qi where blessing gathers are present, Void is feared for scattering them. Void then welcomes combination: combination prevents emptiness.” Without clash, combination, or punishment, it is **True Void**. In the four corner positions it is especially harmful and makes only a practitioner of minor skills and clever techniques.[^67]

<!-- source: L380-L382 -->
Another account gives **True Void** as Water and Earth in the Jiazi round; Metal in Jiaxu; Fire and Earth in Jiashen; Fire and Earth in Jiawu; Wood in Jiachen; Water and Earth in Jiayin. It also says: “All resonance that has sound comes from a hollow center.” Thus the fate of a great person needs the virtue of inward emptiness. Void flourishing in itself and effective in use makes a vessel of great sound and great response. Void in all three positions of month, day, and hour does no harm and makes a very distinguished person; in only two positions, any office obtained is not great.[^68]

It is also said: “Void in the hour often gives obstinacy and undertakings that are lofty but empty. With Canopy as well, children are certainly few. Void in the day often indicates birth from a secondary wife, or separation among wives and concubines. Meeting a pairing combination often gives sexual abandon.”

<!-- source: L384-L388 -->
An old song says:

> In conception at birth, meeting Void is feared;  
> Meeting it leaves the season clouded and obscure.  
> Though nine of every ten steps have a plan,  
> One cannot escape wandering east and west.

〈This says Void is avoided in conception.〉

Another says:

> Established Lu facing Void has only an empty name;  
> A lifetime bent on study comes to nothing in old age.  
> Even should Horse and Noble Person come to rescue,  
> An appointment gained is suspended again.

〈This says Established Lu avoids Void.〉

Another says:

> Jiayin, Wuwu, and Gengshen:  
> Heavenly Center at Chou is most unkind.  
> By one's original allotment one should receive Lu,  
> But meeting Five Ghosts brings decline and poverty.

Jiayin Water meeting Xinchou Earth receives a ghost that controls the fate. Wuwu meeting Dingchou and Gengshen meeting Yichou are the same: Void is feared when it controls the natal fate.[^69]

Another says:

> The two positions after each of the six rounds are Heavenly Center;  
> Meeting combination, Birth, and flourishing is not ominous.  
> Clash and control descending, with punishment of Lu as well,  
> Can raise official rank and make the position still more exalted.

For example, a Si, You, or Chou person with a Dingchou month, Guiwei day, and Wuwu hour has Wu and Wei in Void. Yet the Fire of Wu–Gui flourishes at Wu, making a noble configuration. Empty Fire has flame.[^69]

<!-- source: L390-L394 -->
Another says:

> Void appears within the star of Seal-Ribbon;  
> It follows into Tomb and Storehouse, blessing upon blessing.  
> If it flanks Noble Person and returns to the origin's position,  
> Carrying sha, one must become a supervising secretary.

In **Jiawu / Guiyou / Bingzi / Renchen**, for example, the hour is empty, yet it forms a Six Combination with Guiyou; Tomb/Storehouse also flanks Noble Person, so the configuration is complete. Empty Water flows. The classic says: “Empty Metal rings; empty Fire shines; empty Water is clear; empty Wood breaks; empty Earth collapses.” This is the meaning.

It is also said: “Void in a fate is originally unfavorable, but a clash or punishment in the luck course can make the sha empty instead. Scholars encountering it gain swift and widespread fame.” **Jiashen / Dingchou / Yihai / Jiashen**, moving through Renwu luck, attained a position of authority; **Yiwei / Yiyou / Yichou / Dinghai**, moving through Xinsi luck, became an inspecting official with a resounding reputation. But if Renyin is Void and Renshen of the same kind clashes with it, it does not move. This is especially harmful.[^70]

<!-- source: L396-L404 -->
There is **Road-Blocking Void**, like a traveler encountering Water on the road, unable to advance or cross. It is found only by deriving the hour from the day. On Jia and Ji days, Ren and Gui appear above Shen and You in the twelve-hour derivation. Thus Jia and Ji meet it at Shen and You; Yi and Geng at Wu and Wei; Bing and Xin at Chen and Si; Ding and Ren at Yin and Mao; Wu and Gui at Xu and Hai. Both hours encounter Ren or Gui Water. This Void is not only unfavorable in a natal fate: departures and returns, seeking wealth, trading, assuming office, marriage, and every other undertaking avoid it.[^71]

There are **Four Great Voids**. Among the six Jia rounds, Jiachen and Jiaxu contain all Five Phases. Jiazi and Jiawu alone lack Water; Jiayin and Jiashen alone lack Metal. These four rounds are incomplete in the Five Phases. A person born in Jiazi or Jiawu meeting Water, or in Jiayin or Jiashen meeting Metal, directly offends them. Even without the offense in the natal year, luck reaching Water or Metal also counts as an offense. Carrying this gives lifelong obstruction: poor or wealthy, lowly or eminent, all die prematurely. Three repeated encounters bring the end in an instant. Huzhongzi says, “Yan Hui's early death was caused solely by the Four Great Voids.” This is what he means.[^72]

The *Classic of Penetrating Subtlety* has **Five-Ghosts Void**: Jia and Ji people meeting Si or Wu; Yi and Geng Yin or Mao; Bing and Xin Zi or Chou; Ding and Ren Xu or Hai; Wu and Gui Shen or You. A life-limit reaching this region indicates poverty. There is **Controlling-and-Harming Void**: Jia and Yi meet Wu; Bing and Ding Shen; Wu and Ji Si; Geng and Xin Yin; Ren and Gui You or Chou. It indicates harm to wife and children. There is **Ancestral-Destruction Void**: Jia, Yi, Bing, and Ding follow the preceding list; Wu and Ji meet Xu, Geng and Xin Zi, Ren and Gui Yin. Meeting it indicates the destruction of ancestral property. These must be considered together.[^71]

## 15. On Yuanchen
### 論元辰

<!-- source: L406-L408 -->
〈It belongs to the **Hairy-Head Star** and is also called **Great Consumption**.〉

**Yuanchen** names separation without union. Yang before and yin behind creates a bending, and what is bent cannot extend into affairs. Yin before and yang behind creates a straightness that cannot achieve its purpose: violent and disorderly in affairs, difficult to work with. Hence Yuanchen. For men of yang years and women of yin years, it lies one branch position ahead of the natal clash; for men of yin years and women of yang years, one position behind. A Jiazi-born man directly clashes with Jiawu, so Yiwei is the proper position. An Yichou-born man directly clashes with Yiwei, so Jiawu is the proper position. The other stems at Wu and Wei have half the force.[^73]

It is ominous because it stands where qi collide: stir on the left and the wind-sha is on the right; stir on the right and it is on the left. Hence men and women, yin and yang, differ in taking the place before or after the clash. When year or luck arrives there, one is like a thing exposed to wind, shaking and overturned, unable to find rest. If not internal illness, then external trouble. However high one's wealth and rank or blazing one's power, a major luck course meeting it gives ten fearful years. At court one must suffer expulsion; at home one must encounter calamity. Even with auspicious spirits supporting, fortune and misfortune remain intertwined. Favorable beginnings followed by an unfavorable end are especially feared: after reaching flourishing, at the moment of being about to leave yet not having left, calamity is especially certain.

Meeting it at birth gives a coarse and ill-favored form, prominent cheekbones, a low nose and broad mouth, menacing corners to the eyes, a protruding back of the head and high buttocks, stiff hands and feet, and a deep, turbid voice. In Birth and flourishing, the person is dissolute and expansive, unable to distinguish right from wrong or good from bad, confused and muddled. In Death and Extinction, there is poverty, meanness, an ungainly appearance, confused speech, no sense of shame, repeated ruin and obstruction, a taste for drink and sexual pleasure, and ready acceptance of low habits. Joined with Official Talisman, it brings repeated unmerited trouble. Carrying Robbery Sha, it neglects propriety in small things, acts in ways that invite danger and shame, and becomes poor, lowly, and shameless.

Women obtaining it have a masculine voice and turbid disposition, engage in adultery and secret intercourse with servants and lowly people, are beset by spirits, disregard propriety and law, and suffer repeated calamities. Even their children are stubborn and unfilial. The popular song “Rat fears Goat's head” does not distinguish men and women and cannot be relied upon.[^74]

Luoluzi says that even the Master Kong feared his Yuanchen. Lin Kai treats Yuanchen as a baleful sha that causes very heavy calamity, with reciprocal encounters especially unfavorable. Yet if combination suddenly appears, it is judged auspicious. The *Classic of Penetrating Mystery* says, “Yuanchen meeting combination brings great success.” The *Guangxin Collection* cites the charts of vice-councillor Wu Ji, **Jimao / Jiashen / Jisi / Jiaxu**, and privy councillor Teng Kang, **Yichou / Renwu / Yichou / Renwu**. Do these not offend Yuanchen? Li Jifu says, “Noble fates generally must encounter sha before the ruler unexpectedly raises them.” Lin Kai's view is one-sided.[^75]

Xu Ziping says: “Yuanchen is the position that originally harms a fate: Jia meeting Shen or Geng, Yi meeting You or Xin, and the like. If Seven Killings were already present in natal year, month, day, or hour, that is the original harmful position. Meeting it again in year or luck is called offending Yuanchen and is especially harmful; if absent originally, the harm is lighter.” Yet Yuanchen belongs to the same class as Loss, Robbery, Yang Blade, and Void, as is evident in Luoluzi's *Rhapsody on Waning and Waxing*. The *Snow-Heart Rhapsody* says, “Yuanchen Water departs,” likewise using the name of this spirit-sha. The ancient explanation is correct.[^75]

## 16. On Hidden-Gold Target Sha
### 論暗金的煞

<!-- source: L410-L412 -->
〈Date-selection specialists now derive it from the month and call it a **Red-Sha Day**. Every undertaking is ominous; travel is especially avoided.〉

These three sha arise from the four opposed positions in the Earlier-Heaven numbers. Zi and Wu each number nine, Mao and You six: together thirty. Counting forward from Zi to the thirtieth position reaches Si, the proper sha of the four middle positions. Yin and Shen each number seven, Si and Hai four: together twenty-two. Counting forward from Zi to the twenty-second reaches You, the proper sha of the four initial positions. Chen and Xu each number five, Chou and Wei eight: together twenty-six. Counting forward from Zi to the twenty-sixth reaches Chou, the proper sha of the four season-ending positions. Thus they arise from number. All spirits and sha arise from number and belong to the same class as Lu and Horse.[^76]

This one sha has three names: **Groaning**, **Shattering**, and **White Garments**. Zi, Wu, Mao, and You place it at Si; Yin, Shen, Si, and Hai at You; Chen, Xu, Chou, and Wei at Chou. Si is Metal's Birth. Fire at Arrival at Office within Si subjects Metal qi to control, indicating flogging, punishment, imprisonment, and groaning under calamity: hence Groaning. You is Metal's flourishing place, where its power to kill is timely, aided by Xin Metal. Nothing encountering it escapes shattering; it indicates dismemberment and bloodshed: hence Shattering. Chou is Metal's Storehouse and Tomb, occupying a season-ending position at the Ghost Gate. It indicates injury, mourning dress, and weeping: hence White Garments. Of the Five Phases, Metal alone can kill things; together these are therefore called **Hidden-Gold Target Sha**.

In Birth and flourishing, this influence gives broad capacity, the qualities of a great vessel, decision and action, and a clear, stern appearance. If not early death or penal injury, there must be leprous illness or paralysis. In Death and Extinction, it gives cruelty and malice, a red-and-white complexion, clever words and pleasing looks, a knife concealed within a smile. Joined with Official Talisman, it brings unexpected official trouble; with Robbery Sha, sudden death or bereavement; with White Tiger and Yang Blade, bloodshed and disabling injury. With Noble Person and Established Lu, its effects are somewhat slower. If the Five Phases flourish and are supported, auspicious spirits rescue it, and it enters a noble configuration, it does no harm. In a lowly configuration, further ominous spirits make it worse.

It is also said: “At Metal's Birth, it indicates severe wind illness and paralysis; at Metal's flourishing, disorders from Water, *gu*, or poison; at Metal's Tomb, anxiety through harm to children or a dreadful death. Carrying the Three Punishments, Virtue, and Noble Person, it indicates high office and military command.” In general this sha is unfavorable and should not be met in a natal fate. Meeting it in the year also indicates mourning garments, cries of grief, intrusion by thieves, disputes, losses, and consumption of property. Children offending it suffer from hot water or fire, or bear scars and bodily damage. The classic says: “Nothing is more perverse than punishment and harm, yet at times they are auspicious. Nothing is more discordant than clash and breaking, yet they are not necessarily all ominous.” This is the meaning.[^76]

## 17. On Calamity Sha
### 論災煞

<!-- source: L412-L414 -->
〈Also called **White-Tiger Sha**. In my observation, only all of Shen, Zi, and Chen meeting Wu constitutes White Tiger; it differs slightly from Calamity Sha.〉

**Calamity Sha** is fierce and forceful in nature. It ordinarily lies ahead of Robbery Sha and clashes with and breaks General Star. For Shen–Zi–Chen, General Star is Zi, and Wu clashes with it; for Yin–Wu–Xu, General Star is Wu, and Zi clashes with it; for Si–You–Chou, General Star is You, and Mao clashes with it; for Hai–Mao–Wei, General Star is Mao, and You clashes with it. This is Calamity Sha.

The *Shenbai Classic* says:

> Water's kind meets a star's illumination;  
> The empty sky fears the sun's smoke.  
> Geng and Xin [fear the Scorpion's assault];  
> Wood does not lead the Rooster to sleep.  
> When these meet across the Four Pillars,  
> Blessing is scant and calamity unbroken.[^77]

This sha indicates bloodshed and violent death. In Water and Fire, guard against drowning and burning; in Metal and Wood, rods and blades; in Earth, falls and pestilence. Controlling the body, it is greatly ominous. With auspicious spirits assisting, it often instead gives military authority. Like Robbery Sha, it is best when it meets Officer Star and Seal-Ribbon at Birth and flourishing. The *Shenbai Classic* says: “Calamity Sha fears control; at the place of generation it instead becomes auspicious.” This is precisely the meaning.

## 18. On the Six Obstructions
### 論六厄

<!-- source: L416-L418 -->
An **obstruction** is an encounter with difficulty. It ordinarily lies one position ahead of Horse and two behind Robbery. Death without Birth is called obstruction. In the Shen–Zi–Chen Water configuration, Water dies at Mao; in the Yin–Wu–Xu Fire configuration, Fire dies at You; in the Hai–Mao–Wei Wood configuration, Wood dies at Wu; in the Si–You–Chou Metal configuration, Metal dies at Zi. Hence obstruction. Rescue, protection, support, Birth and flourishing, and noble qi assisting make it auspicious; nevertheless, life remains obstructed. Huzhongzi says: “Six Obstructions strips away office: Li Guang was not enfeoffed as marquis.” This is the meaning.[^78]

## 19. On Hook and Strangling
### 論勾絞

<!-- source: L420-L422 -->
〈Also called **Claw-and-Fang Sha**.〉

**Hook** means entanglement; **Strangling**, binding and constraint. The two sha always oppose one another, like Loss and Robbery. For men of yang years and women of yin years, the third position ahead of the natal branch is Hook, the third behind Strangling. For men of yin years and women of yang years, the third ahead is Strangling, the third behind Hook. Thus a Jiazi yang-fate person takes Mao as Hook and You as Strangling; an Yichou yin-fate person takes Chen as Strangling and Xu as Hook.[^79]

An old song says:

> Claw-and-Fang Sha stands three positions from the fate;  
> Metal Spirit and Yang Blade descending are greatly feared.  
> Sha flanking and controlling the body, with no blessing to rescue,  
> Must bring injury from snake or tiger.

This sha especially fears joining Metal Spirit or White Tiger: then it is ominous. Injury by ox, horse, dog, or other domestic animal also belongs here, not only tiger and wolf. Without control of the body, or sharing a palace with blessing, it is not employed in this judgment. In general, if the body controls sha, the person has many mental paths and clever plans, holding responsibility for penal law or becoming a commander with authority to execute. If sha controls the body, it indicates an unnatural death. Ordinary people encountering it meet unforeseen calamity; the progressing year reaching it also brings disputes, punishment, and imprisonment. It is also said that both positions make the calamity heavy, one light; with a ghost the calamity is heavy, without one light.

## 20. On Lonely Star and Widow Lodging
### 論孤辰寡宿 · 隔角煞

<!-- source: L424-L426 -->
〈Separated-Corner Sha is included.〉

An earlier worthy says: “Old and without a husband is widowed; young and without a father is orphaned.” This is the meaning. *Chen* and *xiu* refer to stars and stellar lodgings, naming the influences. A fate offending these stars becomes lonely or widowed accordingly. Take the three positions in a direction, such as Hai, Zi, and Chou: advance one position to Yin for Lonely Star; retreat one position to Xu for Widow Lodging. Passing beyond the corner makes loneliness; retreating behind it makes widowhood. Apply the same reasoning to the other three directions. It expresses yin and yang's desolation. Yin begins spring and Chen ends it; Si begins summer and Wei ends it; Shen begins autumn and Xu ends it; Hai begins winter and Chou ends it. All are influences of yin and yang's separation, directions where the seasons replace one another.[^80]

The *Three Vehicles* says: “Within creative transformation, what generates me is mother; what controls me, husband; what I control, wife. Hai, Zi, and Chou belong to northern Water. Water uses Metal as mother; Metal becomes extinct at Yin, so the mother is extinct. Water uses Fire as wife; Fire's Tomb is Xu, so the wife is entombed. Shen, You, and Xu belong to western Metal. Metal uses Fire as husband; Fire becomes extinct at Hai. It uses Wood as wife; Wood's Tomb is Wei. Si, Wu, and Wei belong to southern Fire. Fire uses Wood as mother; Wood becomes extinct at Shen. It uses Water as husband; Water's Tomb is Chen. Yin, Mao, and Chen belong to eastern Wood. Wood uses Water as mother; Water becomes extinct at Si. It uses Metal as husband; Metal's Tomb is Chou.” Thus the mother's Extinction makes Lonely Star, and the husband's or wife's Tomb makes Widow Lodging. This explanation is especially pertinent.[^80]

Luoluzi says: “Close kin part midway: loneliness and widowhood still fear the separated corner.” The *Jade Gate Collection* says: “Yin, Shen, Si, and Hai are the corners; Chen, Xu, Chou, and Wei the separations. Advance is yang and unfavorable to the father; retreat is yin and unfavorable to the mother.” It also says: “In a yang palace it harms the father; in a yin palace, the mother.” For Yin, Mao, and Chen people, Si is Lonely Star and Chou Widow Lodging; Yin and Chen are yang positions, Chou and Si yin positions. Men and women meeting them find that even their own sons and daughters are often inharmonious and disobedient.

Master Wang says: “A man's fate born where the wife is extinct and meeting Lonely Star has lifelong difficulty in marriage. A woman's fate born where the husband is extinct and meeting Widow Lodging may marry repeatedly without growing old together.” A Xinchou person obtaining Gengyin illustrates this: Yin is Chou's Lonely Star and Chou Yin's Widow Lodging. Yin and Chou reciprocally make loneliness, widowhood, and separated corner, because Gen lies between them; Qian, Kun, Gen, and Xun are like the four corners.[^81]

<!-- source: L428 -->
The *Classic Illuminating the Spirits* says: “A fate offending Lonely Star and Widow Lodging has a solitary, bony appearance, an ungenial face, and little benefit among close relations. Birth and flourishing are somewhat tolerable; Death and Extinction are worse. With Horse, one wanders abroad; with Void, one has no support in youth; with Mourning Gate and Mourning Guest, parents die in succession. Throughout life, bereavement and calamity repeat, leaving kin isolated, poor, and unsupported. In a noble configuration, one becomes a son-in-law in the wife's household; in a lowly configuration, displacement and wandering cannot be escaped.”

The *Writings Bequeathed by Guigu* says: “With continuous connection, do not speak of loneliness and widowhood.” Hai obtaining Yin and Xu, or Yin obtaining Chou and Si, for example—or stems and branches gathering in attendance and enclosing Noble Person—may offend the named positions without being judged lonely or widowed. The *Extensive Record* says: “Well-Railing Oblique Clash is Lonely Star and Widow Lodging. If the heavenly stems also carry Inverted Food, it is called **Well-Railing Inverted Food**. Si, Wu, or Wei people also born in the three summer months carry two layers of loneliness and widowhood. They harm wife and children, have few close relations, fail to gather wealth, and often have daughters. With other ominous sha, the end is not good.”[^82]

Luoluzi says: “Relying on yin, examine yang's calamity: the year star must not offend Lonely Star. Relying on yang, examine yin's calamity: Heaven's years must avoid Widow Lodging.” This means that minor progression and Tai Sui should not offend them. For yang, Lonely Star is heavier; for yin, Widow Lodging is heavier.

Monk Ying says: “Zi, Wu, Mao, and You contain dead qi; Chen, Xu, Chou, and Wei are the four regions of Tomb. A person meeting these is lonely within loneliness.” Ordinarily Yin, Mao, and Chen reside at Wu; Si, Wu, and Wei at You; Shen, You, and Xu at Zi; Hai, Zi, and Chou at Mao. These are Lonely Star and Separated Corner; Chen, Xu, Chou, and Wei follow the same reasoning. Yin, Shen, Si, and Hai are Heaven and Earth's corners; these arrangements lie separated beyond them, hence Lonely Star.

Another account calls a Yin day with Chou hour, Si day with Chen hour, Shen day with Wei hour, or Hai day with Xu hour **Desolation Sha**. It indicates sighing and dissatisfaction, even in wealth and distinction. A gentleman suffers disgrace and reprimand; an ordinary person punishment and servitude—not loneliness and widowhood alone. A Xu day with Zi hour, Chou day with Mao hour, Chen day with Wu hour, or Wei day with You hour is considered reciprocally: the gentleman dies of an ulcerous illness, the ordinary person through bloodshed. In day and hour it harms wife and children; in conception and year, parents. This is **Bloodshed Sha**.[^82]

Shen Zhi also takes Zi meeting Hai and Hai Zi; Chou meeting Xu and Xu Chou; Yin meeting You and You Yin; Mao meeting Shen and Shen Mao; Chen meeting Wei and Wei Chen; Si meeting Wu and Wu Si. Reckon each pair in succession. Meeting them in day and hour certainly indicates loneliness and widowhood and early loss of kin; in year and hour, unless adopted into another household, one harms the parents. This is yet another explanation.

## 21. On Heaven's Net and Earth's Mesh
### 論天羅地網

<!-- source: L430-L432 -->
The meaning of **Net and Mesh** is plain. But why are Xu and Hai Heaven's Net, Chen and Si Earth's Mesh? Heaven tilts toward the northwest, and Xu–Hai is the end of the six yin; Earth sinks toward the southeast, and Chen–Si is the end of the six yang. At yin and yang's extremes, obscurity replaces clarity, like a person caught in a net: this is the meaning.[^83]

Huzhongzi says: “Dragon and Snake mingled are particularly unfavorable to Chen birth; Pig and Dog encroaching especially dislike Hai.” Dragon is Chen, Snake Si. Chen obtaining Si or Si obtaining Chen is called **Dragon and Snake Mingled**. It does no harm in a man's fate, but in a woman's it breaks marriage, harms children, and gives an ill-starred, sickly life. Chen obtaining Si is heavy, Si obtaining Chen light: a Dragon born in a Snake's hole retreats, a Snake born in a Dragon's hole advances. Pig is Hai, Dog Xu. Xu obtaining Hai or Hai obtaining Xu is called **Pig and Dog Encroaching**. It does no harm in a woman's fate, but in a man's it brings obstruction, discord, damage to ancestry, and harm to the wife. Xu obtaining Hai is light, Hai obtaining Xu heavy: a Dog entering a herd of Pigs advances; a Pig entering a pack of Dogs is injured.

The books likewise say: “Dragon and Snake mingled: always guard against danger to women. Pig and Dog encroaching: continually consider hardship for men.” Thus men fear Heaven's Net, women Earth's Mesh. Further distinction gives Heaven's Net to Fire fates and Earth's Mesh to Water and Earth fates; Metal and Wood fates do not have them. Carrying this usually indicates obstruction. With harmful sha and no qi in the Five Phases, it certainly indicates a dreadful death. The luck course reaching it is judged the same way.

For example, birth on the first day of a Xu month in a Xu year offends one year of Heaven's Net; birth on the fifteenth day offends fifteen years. A Xu birth hour increases this to thirty years. A Xu year with Hai month, or Hai hour with Xu day, encountered reciprocally, is a repeated offense and the calamity does not cease. Earth's Mesh follows the same account. Heaven's Net and Earth's Mesh repeated together do especially grave harm. The *Liyu Song* says: “At birth, Earth's knot joins Heaven's plate: how can the parents long remain secure?” Jiachen meeting Jiaxu, or Jiaxu meeting Jiachen—these two natal positions alone—are spoken of within Net and Mesh.[^83]

## 22. On the Ten Evils and Great Defeat
### 論十惡大敗

<!-- source: L434-L440 -->
**Ten Evils** is a comparison with the ten grave crimes of the law, for which pardon is not granted. **Great Defeat** is a comparison with warfare in which an army is utterly defeated and not one soldier returns alive. They signify the utmost misfortune. In the six Jia rounds, ten days place Lu in Void. Jiachen and Yisi, for example: Jia's Lu is Yin and Yi's Mao, while the Jiachen round makes Yin and Mao void. Renshen takes Hai as Ren's Lu, while the Jiazi round makes Hai void. Apply the same reasoning to Bingshen, Dinghai, Gengchen, Wuxu, Guihai, Xinsi, and [Jichou]. In a natal fate, the offense is properly judged when it appears as the day; the other positions are not counted. Moreover, those offending it are not necessarily all unfortunate. Auspicious spirits supporting and noble qi assisting require an auspicious judgment.[^84]

The *Yuanbai Classic* says: “The Ten Evils together make ten positions; distinguish their sha according to the year.” The following year–day encounters are meant:

| Year | Day |
| --- | --- |
| Gengxu 庚戌 | Jiachen 甲辰 |
| Xinhai 辛亥 | Yisi 乙巳 |
| Renyin 壬寅 | Bingshen 丙申 |
| Guisi 癸巳 | Dinghai 丁亥 |
| Jiachen 甲辰 | Wuxu 戊戌 |
| Yiwei 乙未 | Jichou 己丑 |
| Jiaxu 甲戌 | Gengchen 庚辰 |
| Yihai 乙亥 | Xinsi 辛巳 |
| Bingyin 丙寅 | Renshen 壬申 |
| Dingsi 丁巳 | Guihai 癸亥 |

The year's stem and branch clash with the day's stem and branch, and Lu is absent: this is what is avoided. All other cases do no harm.

The Buddhist *Classic of Dark Heaven and Yellow Earth* further specifies months. In Jia and Ji years: a Wuchen month with Wuxu day, Renshen month with Guihai day, Yihai month with Bingshen day, or Bingzi month with Dinghai day. In Yi and Geng years: a Xinsi month with Renshen day, or Jiaxu month with Yisi day. In Bing and Xin years: a Renchen month with Xinsi day, Wuxu month with Gengchen day, or Jihai month with Jiachen day. In Wu and Gui years: a Jiwei month with Jichou day. Ding and Ren years have none. Thus in Jia or Ji years, births in the third, seventh, tenth, or eleventh month must actually meet the corresponding four days. Distinguish further a Jiazi-round birth actually meeting Renshen or a Jiachen-round birth actually meeting Yisi: only their appearance in the day counts. This explanation is particularly precise.[^85]

<!-- source: L442-L444 -->
There are **Four Discarded Days**: Gengshen in spring, Renzi in summer, Jiayin in autumn, and Bingwu in winter. The *Concordance of Three Calendars* adds Xinyou, Guihai, Yimao, and Dingwei respectively. Discarded means confined or dead and without use. A fate carrying this begins undertakings but does not complete them.[^86]

There are **Heaven-and-Earth Turning Days**: Yimao and Xinmao in spring; Bingwu and Wuwu in summer; Xinyou and Guiyou in autumn; Renzi and Bingzi in winter. Stem, branch, and nayin possess exclusive flourishing in their seasons. Born on such a day in its corresponding season, with the natal fate also reaching flourishing there, one achieves completion without retreating. Excess brings damage and indicates premature death. The *Rhapsody on Hidden Subtlety* says, “Han Xin's execution came solely from the harm of Heaven-and-Earth Turning Sha.” The example of Master Yan is **Jichou / Xinwei / Bingwu / Wuzi**: summer birth encounters Bingwu, and the natal fate additionally reaches flourishing there. Even for one second to the Sage, creative transformation went too far; his life ended in a Gengshen year. Another chart is **Bingxu / Dingyou / Xinyou / [Yiwei]**. Autumn birth encounters Xinyou; though the natal fate is Earth, life ended in a [Renshen] year and Gengxu month.[^87]

Creative transformation most welcomes balanced harmony. Excess and deficiency are both unfavorable. The Four Discarded Days above encounter Death and confinement to excess: this is deficiency. Heaven-and-Earth Turning Days encounter strength and flourishing to excess: this is overabundance. Neither is balanced harmony. If deficiency in the pillars has generation and support, or excess has restraint and suppression, these judgments do not apply.

## 23. On Miscellaneous Spirits and Sha in the Stem and Branch Characters
### 論干支諸字雜犯神煞

<!-- source: L446-L448 -->
When the ancients made characters, each took its meaning from something. The graph [柛] is explained as a tree falling of itself. Water and Earth become extinct at Si; hence the *Explanation of Characters* explains 汜 as a dead-end watercourse and [圮] as a bank collapsing or overturning. Fire declines at Xu, hence 烕 means extinguishing. Metal declines at Chou, hence 鈕 means a fastening that closes. The seed's core 核 takes Hai 亥, Wood's root 根 takes Gen 艮; Metal 金 meeting ten 十 makes needle 針, white 白 meeting ten 十 makes black 皁, Water 水 meeting ten 十 makes juice 汁. Can these be left undiscussed? In creating Jia and Yi and the ten stems, Zi and Chou and the twelve branches, the ancients must have had profound meanings.[^88]

Consider the written shapes. **Jia 甲, Bing 丙, Ding 丁, Ren 壬, and Chen 辰** are called **Flat-Head Sha**. Three or four of them, carrying Void, certainly make a Buddhist or Daoist. The *Book of Fate* says: “Jia, Yi, Bing, and Ding in an uninterrupted sequence indicate entrapment and injury: a man harms his wife, a woman her husband.” **Jia 甲, Gui 癸, Wei 未, Shen 申, and You 酉** belong to **Broken Characters**. Jia, Gui, and You must damage the eyes; Wei and Shen bring ailments of the chest and abdomen. Examine further whether the hour enters a harmful region and the stem influence is controlled: then the judgment is not empty.

**Jia 甲, Xin 辛, Mao 卯, Wu 午, and Shen 申** belong to **Hanging Needle**. If the Five Phases originally lack qi and no Virtue Spirit is met, the person is certainly a soldier. The *Collected Configurations of the Three Fates* says:

> Three or four Jia and Xin are called Hanging Needle;  
> Eye disorders abound, and danger descends.  
> Punished and carrying sha, one must face penal exile:  
> Often a soldier with a tattooed face.

**[Wu 戊], Geng 庚, and Xu 戌** belong to **Punishment by the Rod**, **Keen Blade**, and **Reversed Spear**. With Yang Blade and a devouring influence, they certainly bring penal servitude, exile, or a dreadful death.[^89]

**Yi 乙, Ji 己, Chou 丑, and Si 巳** belong to **Missing-Character** and **Bent-Leg Sha**. Repeated offenses must bring a cleft lip, pierced ears, or incomplete limbs. Without Virtue and combination, and with no qi in the Five Phases, grain and cloth are insufficient and one eats at another's door. The *Book of Fate* says, “Jisi, Yisi, and Dingsi encountered as the natal day indicate harm to the first wife.” Li Jiuwan says: “Si, You, and Chou complete in triple combination, with Ji in the heavenly stems, indicate incomplete lips or teeth and a lifetime exposed to other people's tongues. Jisi in the Four Pillars is called Bent-Leg Sha. Repeated occurrences indicate raising another person's child as one's own, or being raised oneself as another's child.”[^89]

**Bing 丙, Ren 壬, Yin 寅, and You 酉** are **Deaf-and-Mute Characters**. Repeated offenses, injury in conception, or the hour devouring the day must cause deafness and muteness. A You day with Xu hour also indicates these afflictions and severe sores on head or face: the hour breaks the day. The song says:

> Flat Head must make a Buddhist or Daoist;  
> Broken Characters end by losing the eyes' light.  
> Hanging Needle with sha requires a tattooed face;  
> Penal servitude and exile often join Punishment by the Rod.  
> Hunger and poverty meet unfortunate emptiness and deficiency;  
> One knows the appearance will not be wholly formed.  
> If Deaf-and-Mute Characters meet,  
> Void and lack of qi make the response exact.  
> If many Bent Legs are carried,  
> Father and son must come from two different surnames.[^90]

Again, Ren or Gui people obtaining You, or You people obtaining Ren or Gui, join Water with You 酉 to form wine 酒. With auspicious influences, wine establishes the household; with ominous ones, it ruins the household or brings death through drunkenness. **Yi 乙** is **Disheveled Head**: repeated occurrences give a ritual master, spirit-medium, singer, or performer; with Tianyi and Heavenly Virtue, a high official. **Bing and Wu** above Yin or Mao are called **Enmity and Obscuring Qi**. They invite slander and resentment and much contention with others.

**Yi, Ji, and Gui** all present bring either injury to limbs or eyes, or punishment after middle age. **Jia, Yi, and Geng** all present indicate loss of sight or damage to the eyes from eruptive sores in youth. **Yi and Gui** must not meet Chou together: they indicate wealth and distinction but short life. **Ding and Xin** must not meet Si together: they indicate harm to parents. The classic says: “Yi and Gui together with Ox: not an inch of time can be assured. Ding and Xin: much harm to parents.”

**Bing and Xin** must not meet Si; **Ding and Ren** must not meet Wu or Shen. This is called **Response of Kind**, indicating lifelong interruption and decline. **Yi and Xin** must not share Wei; **Geng and Ren** must not share Xu. This is called **Returning Injury**, indicating death amid wealth and distinction, or penal execution for the ordinary person. The classic says:

> Yi and Xin meeting Wei make Heavenly Prison;  
> Four positions in one palace encounter calamity.  
> Geng and Ren meeting Xu require penal exile;  
> When calamity arrives, there is no escape.[^90]

## 24. A General Discussion of Spirits and Sha

Reckon each pair in this succession. A gentleman offending it has unusual illnesses, intestinal wind, or foot-qi disease; an ordinary person suffers injury or loss of limbs. These are the days Yisi, Dingsi, Xinhai, Wushen, and Jiayin, and also Bingwu, Wuwu, and Renzi. Men harm wives, women husbands.[^101]

**Yin–Yang Dislocation Sha 陰陽差錯煞.** These are twelve days: Bingzi, Dingchou, Wuyin, Xinmao, Renchen, Guisi, Bingwu, Dingwei, Wushen, Xinyou, Renxu, and Guihai. A woman meeting them has little harmony with parents-in-law, inadequate relations with sisters-in-law, and decline or coldness in the husband's household. A man meeting them brings decline to the maternal or affinal family and has disputes and little harmony with his wife's family. For either sex, two or three occurrences in month, day, and hour are extremely heavy; the day alone offending is especially serious. It indicates no support from the wife's family. Even wealth through the wife becomes an empty blossom, and eventually the two households become enemies and cease visiting.[^101]

Jia and Yi meeting Wu; Bing Yin; Ding Wei; Wu Zi; Ji Chen; Geng Xu; Xin You; Ren Si; Gui Shen: these make **Crimson Beauty**. A woman's fate especially avoids them.[^102]

Among all the preceding sha, **controlling the body** means that the nayin beneath an ominous sha controls the nayin of the natal year's Tai Sui. **Descending upon the body** means that the ominous sha carries Tai Sui's nayin in its own position. Tai Sui's nayin is the body. If that body is controlled or descended upon at Death, Defeat, or Extinction, unforeseen calamity follows. If it happens where the body has qi, control makes the calamity heavy and mere descent makes it light. Only Tai Sui's nayin controlling sha is auspicious.[^103]

If a fate has already entered a noble configuration and carries sha at an important point, with auspicious spirits assisting, this is called **Authority**. Without auspicious assistance, with sha's qi flourishing and exchanging back and forth, or punishing and controlling natal root and ruler, it gives low standing and a dreadful death. It is also said: “The positions occupied by all auspicious spirits should have Birth and flourishing: these bring splendor and distinction. The positions occupied by all baleful spirits should have Death and Extinction: these bring a good end.” Again: “Auspicious spirits should receive flourishing qi, and whatever defeats them is avoided. Ominous spirits should receive declining qi, and whatever helps them is avoided.”

It is also said: “If a fate contains clashes, breaking, triple combinations, or Six Combinations, examine how the Five Phases obtain one another. Blessing may arise within calamity, or calamity within blessing. Death and Extinction returning to life, Void receiving breaking, control turning into completion—these bring blessing from calamity. The reverse brings calamity from blessing.” The *Book of Fate* says: “Do lowly officials upon the roads lack Traveling Horse and Mounting the Saddle? Even gamblers in the marketplace possess the Three Wonders and Flanking Noble Person.” Ziping says: “A gentleman's configuration may offend Seven Killings and Yang Blade; a petty person's fate may contain Direct Seal and Officer Star.”

From this, auspicious and ominous spirits cannot be fixed rigidly. Weighing their relative force requires adaptable understanding. In general, the stem influence upon an ominous sha should not carry a true ghost that controls and injures the body. Even Officer Star can turn into a ghost; how much more a true ghost! ## Translator’s endnotes

[^01]: **Base text and witnesses.** **S** is the repository’s [juan three](../source/juan-03.md), from [Wikisource revision 657369](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=657369), whose checksum appears in the metadata. **G599**, **G600**, and **G601** refer to the parallel *Sanming tonghui* passages in the *Gujin Tushu Jicheng*, Arts volumes [599](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/欽定古今圖書集成/博物彙編/藝術典/第599卷), [600](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/欽定古今圖書集成/博物彙編/藝術典/第600卷), and [601](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/欽定古今圖書集成/博物彙編/藝術典/第601卷). **U** is the shorter [unlabelled Wikisource juan three](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/三命通會/卷三), revision 2184219. The underlying G599/G600/G601 work-page revisions retrieved were 1942562, 1942563, and 1942564; their transcluded text was saved at consultation. **W**, cited for specific parallel quotations below, is the related *Wuxing jingji* in [revision 10494296](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=10494296), not another edition of S. These are online transcriptions, consulted on 25 September 2026, not independently verified facsimiles. Agreement can support a reading without proving independent textual descent. S controls the translation; departures and unresolved defects are recorded below. No Chinese source file has been altered, and the separately transmitted Siku preface is not translated.

[^02]: **The historical claims and changing frames.** The names of stages—Birth, Bathing, Arrival at Office, Death, Tomb, Extinction, and so forth—denote technical positions, not medical events in themselves. The juan also contains explicit claims about disease, death, sex, disability, servitude, and social status. They are translated as historical assertions, not endorsed as facts about present-day people. “Sha” is not invariably evil: the text repeatedly permits authority and achievement through an appropriately supported sha. Reference may shift among birth year, day stem, branch, transformed qi, and nayin; no single modern Day Master framework has been imposed throughout.

[^03]: **Lu and the Earth stems.** Lu combines the sense of official emolument with a technical branch position. Here Wu and Ji follow their generating phase, Fire, to Si and Wu. Elsewhere Earth follows Water in its lodged-life cycle; the two accounts are not silently harmonized. Obvious confusions among 己, 已, and 巳 are normalized when syntax unambiguously requires the Ji stem or Si branch. “Two positions ahead” is illustrated by Yin → Mao → Chen: it means two advances, not two places counted inclusively from Yin.

[^04]: **Why Jia becomes Earth and Bing Water.** These are transformed-qi affiliations: Jia–Ji transforms into Earth, Bing–Xin into Water, Ding–Ren into Wood. They are not the ordinary elemental affiliations of the individual stems or the nayin of Bingyin. The same distinction is required by the Xin/Dingyou judgment. The entry for Guimao actually names 太乙, Taiyi; it has not been silently changed to 天乙, Tianyi. Some judgments here conflict with later classifications, and their transmitted distinctions are preserved.

[^05]: **True Lu and its abbreviated examples.** S’s first “Bing or 己” is read Bing or Si, supported by G599’s 巳. The following Yi example reads 己或午; it is retained as Ji or Wu, not conjecturally rewritten into a different Lu formula. G599 shares the abbreviation. The eight advancing/retreating examples remain as given. Their paired forward/backward branch motion explains the labels, but does not settle every underlying affiliation.

[^06]: **Food God and feeding.** 食 marks the influence produced by the reference stem in these examples: Ren → Jia, Jia → Bing, and so forth. “Takes as Food God” expresses the technical relationship without the misleading suggestion that the reference stem literally consumes the generated phase. All eight Food-God-combining examples in S are retained; G599 abbreviates the list and contains a reversed Ding/Yi clause, so it is not followed there.

[^07]: **Special Lu names.** 絪縟 is retained as the name of a richly spread or cushioned auspicious arrangement; its written form is not silently replaced with another familiar sha. In the ten-day-round example, S gives Wuwu/Dingsi, while G599 has Wuwu/Gengshen; S is retained, since both pairs occur within the Jiayin round and that fact alone does not establish a correction. “Hall of Lu” has its own derivation and is not simply another name for the ordinary Lu branch. G599’s 戊 where S gives 戌 in the Jiaxu explanation is not adopted.

[^08]: **Facing, nearness, and punishment.** The transmitted general expression about “two positions” is less precise than the ensuing individual examples, which clearly give Chou/Yin/Mao/Hai for Jia. These examples govern the translation without erasing the discrepancy. The direction of punishment matters: Chou punishing Xu is not treated like Xu punishing Wei. “Ghost” means an adverse controlling relationship in its local frame, not necessarily an apparition.

[^09]: **A substantial displacement restored.** S’s Lu discussion is interrupted by material belonging to Traveling Horse. Its line beginning 洞玄經曰位生火 combines the Lu quotation’s attribution with the middle of the Horse exposition; the following 燭神經 paragraph runs back into the Lu quotation at 與甲以寅. Conversely, the opening Horse paragraph breaks at 遇寅 and jumps to 官符同. G599 supplies continuous, corresponding sequences. The translation restores the Lu quotation after Sima Jizhu, and the Horse exposition after 遇寅, then joins 與 to 官符同 within the Horse judgment. Every affected passage is translated once; source-line comments identify the displaced material. G599 also supports 興騰, ascent, against S’s 興馬. The compressed Geng/Ren clause remains uncertain and is not expanded into a new rule.

[^10]: **Two Golden Carriage definitions.** The main explanation counts from Lu, while the quoted *Golden Book* counts from Horse. Both are retained as attributed alternatives. 旌旗 and 節鉞 are images of banners and delegated command, not additional chart variables. 作贅 denotes a husband entering his wife’s household, a social arrangement whose negative valuation belongs to the historical source.

[^11]: **Horse’s derivations.** The twenty-one/eighteen counts include the starting position and may pass through a full twelve-branch circuit. They yield Shen/Si going forward from Zi and Yin/Hai going backward from Wu. The source then offers two further explanations, including a broader theory of qi in motion. In the restored Metal-triad passage, S has “Water concealed within,” while G599 has “Wood concealed within”; the latter fits the immediate clause about Wood’s Birth at Hai and is adopted. This is not a claim that all these historical derivations are mathematically equivalent.

[^12]: **Movement and its context.** The direct *Classic Illuminating the Spirits* quotation was among the material displaced in S; see note 9. 衝併 has a broader range than simple harmonious union and is rendered “clash or conjunction.” The ensuing lists classify twenty Horse pillars, five in each branch, and retain their alternative titles and stated response times. Those titles sometimes use transformed qi or nayin, not the untransformed stem alone.

[^13]: **Twelve Horses, including a lacuna.** S and G599 both transmit 壬亥 and 丙巳 in Plodding Horse. These are not valid pairs in the alternating-polarity sixty-pair cycle. They are retained and identified, rather than silently changed to Guihai and Dingsi. The sixth definition is explicitly missing; its name and the later judgment about empty renown survive. No replacement definition has been invented. The tenth example’s compressed sequence of Gengyin/Jiayin/hour/conception is also preserved without claiming that its punctuation yields a uniquely determined complete chart.

[^14]: **Offices, not modern grades of worth.** 選人 denotes a lower official appointment or selection category in this context; 六品 and 三公 belong to historical ranks and institutions. “Carrying a Corpse” is a transmitted divinatory label, not an established predictor of death upon appointment. The text’s distinction between permanent brokenness and a stumble from which one rises explains the two horse metaphors.

[^15]: **Derived and visible Horses.** The Five-Tigers derivation was set out in juan two and uses a stem to assign the successive stems of the monthly branches. Here it generates an otherwise unmanifest Horse/Noble Person relationship. The Heavenly-Court passage remains compressed: it should not be taken as a complete new calculation algorithm. One Stake Tethering Two Horses concerns the stem’s controlling relation to the branch; its metaphor is not a instruction to count every visible 木 as another literal stake.

[^16]: **Rein and Whip.** 御 and 策 describe positions bracketing Horse. The example places both natal Zi and day-branch Chou behind Yin even though the initial rule speaks of one position: that extension is retained. Its controlling relation is Bingyin Fire nayin over Jiazi Metal nayin. “Controls the body” must not be indiscriminately rewritten as controls the day stem.

[^17]: **Earth’s other Birth scheme.** The station/horse examples explicitly give Earth Birth at Shen and Arrival at Office at Hai. This differs from the Fire-following Earth scheme at the start of the juan and is not corrected away. The southern Flying Horse account likewise gives both the individual Earth-branch Horses and an additional Wu position; it has not been collapsed into one lookup table. “Having both” is a technical configuration, not a universal guarantee of rank.

[^18]: **Named Horse states and age claims.** The Four Sicknesses heading extends to Death and Extinction in its own explanation. Carrying Treasure and Bearing Flowers depend on their stated relationships, not merely on the English associations of the names. The gendered and age-based judgments are historical claims. Ages three, twelve, and fifty have not been converted into modern clinical thresholds. The body’s distinction between natal configuration and activation through annual or minor-luck clashes remains essential.

[^19]: **Exchange and a malformed pair.** S’s 甲子甲甲甲辰 is read Jiazi, Jiashen, Jiachen: the middle item is malformed, and the preceding explicit Shen–Zi–Chen grouping establishes the intended branch. “After Lu and before Horse” follows the illustrated You–Xu–Hai and Mao–Chen–Si sequences; the text’s directional wording is not a modern map convention. Living Lu, Heavenly Lu, and ordinary Lu are separate terms in this exposition.

[^20]: **Han’s name and the court metaphors.** S has 韓 followed by the uncompleted ideographic descriptor ⿰; G599 has 韓《命書》 without resolving the name. “Han’s Book of Fate” therefore avoids inventing a personal name. Blue clouds, Yellow Pavilion, Three Terraces, and Eight Seats are images of advancement and high office. The pure-yin/pure-yang explanation uses successive six-pair half-rounds, not the alternating yin/yang classification of individual stems. 林開’s 富學 is retained as “wealth and learning,” without an unsupported emendation.

[^21]: **Tianyi and Taiyi.** 天乙 and 太乙 are different written names and remain distinct. The opening locates a celestial spirit, whereas the first derivation makes Tianyi the refined qi of the stems. The compiler himself notices the difference. Enclosure, gate, mansions, and Jade Balance belong to the source’s celestial-administrative imagery; the translation does not identify every expression with a modern astronomical coordinate or a single physical star.

[^22]: **The forward and backward derivations.** The two tables unpack every stem assignment in the prose. Skipping Chen, Xu, the opposed starting place, and the already-used starting place is part of the stated procedure. S’s 寅與甲對衝 is corrected to Yin opposing Shen, following G599’s 申 and the explicit Shen starting point. Animal names stand for branches. These assignments should not be conflated with an unordered two-branch mnemonic that erases which is yin and which yang.

[^23]: **Day/night versus solstitial halves.** The text successively offers a solstice-based explanation, a sunrise/star-based derivation, and a recommendation involving Yin/Shen and the solstices again. It does not resolve all their differences into one operational convention. The assertion that the sun rises at Yin and sinks at Shen belongs to that historical account, not to a latitude-independent modern timetable. The full ten-stem day derivation is retained; no missing night-by-night table has been invented.

[^24]: **Several frameworks, not one element table.** G599 supports 戊, Wu, against S’s 戌 in “yang Earth.” Guiwei and Yichou, Dingyou and Yihai, and the other complete pillars involve nayin as well as stems. The opening phrase “Jia … Wu … Geng” is read as the three yang-stem classes indicated by the following 三陽. The source itself first questions Fire’s Death at You and then allows Fire and Water to gain nobility at Death or Extinction; these arguments are preserved as competing explanations.

[^25]: **Noble Combination and Noble Food.** Several entries do not transparently follow the introductory formulas: Ding obtains Renyin/Renchen rather than Renyou/Renhai; Jia obtains Bingyin/Bingchen; Geng obtains Renshen/Renxu. G599 transmits these same examples, so they are not silently standardized. S ends with “all three,” G599 “both”; S is retained, with the antecedent unresolved rather than a third category invented. The source’s 天乙在六合 list for Yi/Ji is Chou/Si; G599’s Chou/Shen would not match the stated combination rule and is not followed.

[^26]: **Flanking and derived Noble Persons.** In Renyin/Jiayin, S’s 甲癸在丑 is read 甲貴在丑, Jia’s Noble Person at Chou, supported by G599. “Living-Lu Noble Person” gives a further derived set, not a replacement for the ordinary Tianyi list. G599 names historical owners for the two sample charts, whereas S only says “one fate”; those names are not imported. Their four pillars are preserved as examples without claiming independent historical verification.

[^27]: **Strength of blessing and the Duke of Lu example.** The four honored, four neutral, and four avoided states refer back to the graded lodged-life conditions discussed in juan two. The example is an attributed chart, not a verified biography. The compiler’s objection depends on the actual month and hour rather than the mere presence of four desired characters. 二三分 means a small number of comparative parts, not a calibrated probability or percentage of favorable outcomes.

[^28]: **A contradiction explicitly challenged.** Jia at Chou and Geng at Wei are called “Lu’s Storehouse” here, although earlier ordinary phase-Storehouse assignments point the other way. G599 shares the wording; it is retained. More importantly, the author explicitly criticizes classifying Noble Person through an undifferentiated fear of Death and Extinction, since the preceding Water/Fire account uses those very places. The concluding qualifications are integral to the teaching, not optional additions.

[^29]: **Wonders, odd numbers, and rejected systems.** 奇 can mean exceptional, wondrous, or odd-numbered. “Three Wonders” preserves the established technical name; the author’s objection to “Four Wonders” exploits its numerical sense. Yi–Bing–Ding, Jia–Wu–Geng, and Xin–Ren–Gui are not treated here as equally accepted sets: the text argues over their grounds. The Old Man Star’s auspicious appearance is translated as the source’s account rather than furnished with an unverified astronomical identification.

[^30]: **Order and the Six Instruments.** The source juxtaposes a preference for chronological forward order with an account favoring the upward movement of refined qi; neither is suppressed. The Six Instruments are explicitly assigned to the six ten-day rounds, rather than simply treated as six additional Wonders. The term 遁甲, Concealing Jia, is retained at the point of quotation; it is not used to import the complete rules of another system into natal interpretation.

[^31]: **The concluding poems retain the debate.** The final poem names Gui–Ren–Xin despite the earlier criticism of Xin–Ren–Gui as Wonders among humanity. Its reverse order is retained, not silently rearranged. Penglai’s three isles and golden halls contrast transcendent retirement with high worldly office. Verse translations preserve each judgment and image without manufacturing English rhyme at the cost of the technical conditions.

[^32]: **The celestial remainder.** S and G599 both give 365 du and 25½ fen, then five du and 25½ fen distributed as forty-four fen to each of twelve positions. If a fen is one hundredth of a du, these are not exactly equal: twelve times forty-four is 528, not 525½. The figures remain as transmitted. The du belongs to the historical celestial-circle model, not a modern 360-degree coordinate. The twelve embedded positions are this argument’s diagrammatic correspondences, not a substitute for the complete hidden-stem lists.

[^33]: **A separate branch-based virtue scheme.** Huzhongzi’s two twelve-month series are given in full and kept distinct from the preceding triad/stem Monthly Virtue. The compiler expressly assigns the latter discussion a place in Five-Planet astrology. Readers should not merge these tables into the modern four-stem Monthly Virtue rule without specifying which scheme is being used.

[^34]: **Associated virtues and abbreviated clauses.** The second-month Heavenly-Virtue Combination is Kun with Xun, as S gives it; it is not replaced by a heavenly-stem combination. The Year-Stem Virtue statement lists four stems after expressly adding Wu as a lodging for Wu/Gui; that inconsistency is left visible. The Heavenly Pardon clause compresses Monthly Virtue, Refinement, combination, Void, and four auspicious hours without supplying all their definitions. The translation does not turn this into a fully specified modern date-selection recipe.

[^35]: **More than one Banner-and-Battle-Axe.** The same label is attached to a birth-position arrangement and then to a seasonal punitive arrangement. The text announces the second as “another kind,” so the tables are not collapsed. The distinction between the ordinary person’s calamity and an official’s delegated authority belongs to the book’s historical social interpretation, not to an intrinsic moral evaluation of a present-day chart.

[^36]: **Great-Ultimate Noble Person.** The explanation depends on the double force of 極: beginning at the ultimate source and ending at the place of return. Its Metal-at-Yin and Earth-at-Shen statements are retained rather than harmonized with every earlier Birth table. Four Storehouses and the four cardinal phase beginnings are part of this argument; no new universal definition of the Great Ultimate is being advanced by the translation.

[^37]: **Four literary indicators, not one standardized star.** The source distinguishes 文昌貴, 文譽貴, 文星貴, and 天印貴. Their formulas are preserved separately, even where the Wenchang assignments differ from a familiar later lookup table. The first Heavenly-Seal line actually reads 甲子在寅中, “Jiazi lies within Yin”; the extra 子 is not silently deleted. The verse’s animal names stand for branches, whereas 文昌 and 文星 name different indicators here.

[^38]: **Study Hall and Academy.** The opening definition uses the natal phase’s Birth or Arrival at Office together with matching nayin: Xinsi Metal and Renshen Metal for a Metal fate. Later paragraphs offer Officer-based and Food-God-based versions, then a broader theory of the four corner branches. These are related but not interchangeable definitions. 翰林 supplies the historical literary-office comparison; “Academy” should not be read as a modern university.

[^39]: **Unresolved wording and examples.** S and G599 both have 火包而不包 and 土包而不包, translated literally as enclosing yet not enclosing. Their precise force remains uncertain; neither phrase is silently replaced by a hypothetical Death/Birth rule. “Ban and Ma” is the source’s allusive shorthand for exemplary writers. The second sample chart does not display all four Jia/Yi/Bing/Ding stems, despite the preceding formulation; it is retained. Bing as “true Water” uses transformed qi, not Bing’s ordinary Fire affiliation. Sample charts are transmitted examples, not independently established historical births.

[^40]: **Control is not uniformly adverse.** Officer-Star Study Hall specifically involves control at Birth, while the later general warning asks that Study Hall avoid control and breaking. Both accounts remain visible. The author ultimately makes effective configuration and creative transformation more important than the mere presence of a named literary indicator. The English does not turn the juxtaposed rules into an unqualified promise of academic success.

[^41]: **Two kinds of Study Hall.** The later Ziping passage uses the day stem’s Birth and then extends learning to any Yin, Shen, Si, or Hai position, with year and month especially influential. It subsequently dislikes the day stem’s Birth in monthly command because forward luck would enter Bathing. These shifts are preserved. The statements about instruction at five or seven and stern teachers are the source’s historical judgments, not a valid assessment of an actual child’s capacity to learn.

[^42]: **Chief Star and a numerical discrepancy.** 魁星, Chief Star, is not simply Kui-Gang. S describes the Jiachen ten-day round as “twelve positions,” although Jiachen through Guichou contains ten pairs. The discrepancy is explicitly retained. Its four selected pairs are Jiachen, Dingwei, Gengxu, and Guichou. The alternative Dinghai/Xinmao/Gengxu group and the year-derived corner positions remain separate. The Year-Beginning and Year’s-Seat clauses are compressed and do not supply a complete independent computational method.

[^43]: **Refined qi is qualified by the month.** Here 秀氣 is explicitly the refinement supplied by monthly command, not a free-standing auspicious object whose name alone settles the chart. The following Wood/Food/Hurting-Officer clauses are densely unpunctuated; the English separates successive conditions without inventing omitted stems or combining them into one fully specified example. The source’s distinction between a natal potential and a year of success is preserved.

[^44]: **The examination allusions.** The lone osprey evokes an exceptional recommendation, and passing the waves at Yu’s Gate evokes successful ascent. The commentary then expressly requires a clash rather than a combination between the annual and natal Horses, despite the verse’s language of joining. The difference is left intact. The later annual and monthly qualifications do not establish a statistical or unconditional prediction of examination results.

[^45]: **Direct Seal means a proper Storehouse here.** 正印 in this section is first defined through the five nayin Storehouses: Yichou Metal, Guiwei Wood, Jiaxu Fire, Renchen Water, and Bingchen Earth. It is not automatically the generating-phase relationship called Direct Seal among the Ten Gods. Natal root, day ruler, hour as imperial seat, and conception/month are distinct reference positions in the ensuing grading.

[^46]: **Literary Seals and competing judgments.** All ten Literary-Seal examples are retained. The first series concerns nayin control combined with stem restraint; the second stem restraint with branch combination. The later assertion that Water gains no benefit from its own Seal sits beside the opening praise of the proper Seal and has not been harmonized away. The minister Yu’s chart is given as the source reports it; no uncertain identification or independent verification is supplied.

[^47]: **Unusual Seal terminology.** “Self-Punishing Seal” is the source’s name for its Chou–Xu example, not the ordinary repeated-branch self-punishment rule. In Calamity-Gathering Seal, “[of Water]” supplies the object from the preceding repeated-Water clause; it is an interpretive clarification, not a newly attested reading. The final 少安逸 is rendered “little ease,” not an assertion of peace in youth. Its family and offspring judgments remain historical claims.

[^48]: **Virtue and Refinement.** The source expressly includes Wu and Ji in the Water-triad Virtue group and Jia–Ji in its Refinement group. These are not deleted to create a simpler four-phase table. Virtue and Refinement are here conditioned by month, combination, and freedom from injury; they should not be conflated with the Heavenly and Monthly Virtue schemes discussed earlier.

[^49]: **Robbery from outside, Loss from within.** The paired definitions turn on 奪, seizure, and 失, loss. Robbery’s explanation uses a controlling influence at the triad phase’s Extinction; Loss’s uses the phase produced at its Arrival-at-Office position. These are local phase/branch arguments and need not refer to the modern day stem in every example. The verses immediately juxtapose calamitous and authoritative manifestations: both sides are part of the transmitted exposition.

[^50]: **Stars, plates, and palaces in the Robbery verses.** The language of Body Palace, fate ruler, luminaries, and the heavenly plate belongs to an astrological framework broader than a simple four-pillar lookup. “Chief configuration” follows the transmitted 魁局, whose exact technical scope is not defined here. The translation does not invent a missing star-placement procedure. All three supplementary Robbery verses and the three Loss verses are retained, including their contrasting favorable judgments.

[^51]: **The auspicious and ominous preambles.** These are not two independent sha lists: they explain how the same influence changes with strength and companions. 刓刻雕鏃 covers several cutting and decorative crafts; 籠養 concerns keeping creatures in cages. 聲氣 and medical descriptions elsewhere in these lists remain historical categories rather than newly supplied diagnoses. The source’s disparagement of particular occupations is translated without adopting it as the translator’s judgment.

[^52]: **An abbreviated calculation.** The commentary to 宜權 reads 空亡准十日夜推臨官准十日辰折 in both S and G600. It gestures toward reckoning Void and Arrival at Office by ten-day positions, but does not fully specify an algorithm. The English retains its distinctions and marks the compression. It should not be taken as a replacement for the more explicit Void procedures that follow later in the juan.

[^53]: **Ministerial metaphors and an unresolved contrast.** Timely rain and salt with plum evoke a minister’s nourishing and harmonizing service; 羅文 is rendered interwoven, in keeping with the following exchanged Noble Persons. Generating Above explicitly welcomes clash and breaking at the two ends, then condemns a stem-combination/sha arrangement. The shift is present in S and G600 and is not smoothed into an invented exception. The relationships to year and day must remain distinct.

[^54]: **Mulberry Shade and the Light-and-Graceful crux.** S’s 醫桑 is read 翳桑, “mulberry shade,” following G600. The latter supplies an intelligible name within the poverty imagery; it is an attested textual repair, not a new sha invented from literal 醫, healing. The Light-and-Graceful explanation has 日辰不絕, “the day is not extinct,” in both witnesses. This is retained despite the strained connection with its unassisted condition. Flowers, willows, song, and wine are the text’s recurring vocabulary for entertainment and sexual commerce, not merely gardening.

[^55]: **The Ferocity commentary.** 亡劫雙全又剋羊刃重重來合 is unpunctuated and permits different attachments of 又剋 to the succeeding words. The English keeps the elements—Loss and Robbery complete, control, Yang Blade, and repeated combinations—without pretending the line supplies one unequivocal mechanical test. The main clause explicitly turns on control or depletion of the day ruler.

[^56]: **“Sixteen” Loss forms, but fifteen definitions.** S supplies fifteen separately named Loss-Spirit definitions under its heading “sixteen”; G600 has the same sequence. No sixteenth has been invented. The title and the discrepancy are both preserved. Official Talisman and Seventh Sha are given as aliases here; this does not mean every occurrence of those names elsewhere is identical. The preamble’s 失勢失下, literally losing position and what is below, remains compressed. Cangue-and-Chains combines penal tattooing, exile, and clerical service in its transmitted list; the translation does not resolve these into one occupation.

[^57]: **True Loss and Robbery and relational Wealth.** The four paired examples use complete stem–branch pillars. S’s following 獨者主凶, an isolated occurrence being ominous, is retained without changing it into a requirement for both. Wealth Above Robbery uses the stem’s Wealth relationship—Jia with Ji, Yi with Wu—rather than every occurrence of Earth having the same significance. The ten combining examples are all retained in their source order.

[^58]: **Dividing a sha is not always doubling it.** The text distinguishes one source distributed between two recipients from two sources gathered into one recipient; this is why its judgments differ from a crude count of visible Si branches. The following relative-strength argument further qualifies counting by living qi. The four additional sha are defined by successive advances from Robbery’s position. Their martial/civil classification and family predictions remain the compilation’s historical claims, not modern risk measures.

[^59]: **Sheep Blade and Yang Blade.** The writing 羊 means sheep, while 陽 means yang. The opening exploits the sheep-under-the-knife image and gives Blade to yin as well as yang stems. The concluding Ziping paragraph explicitly rejects the sheep reading and restricts Blade to the five yang stems. “Yang Blade” is retained as the running English term so that readers can follow the discussion; the distinction in characters and doctrine is not erased. Tang Talisman is a transmitted alias of the opposite position, not supplied with an invented etymology.

[^60]: **True and Partial Blades.** Yimao and Dingmao are singled out for Jia, while other Mao pillars are lighter. This cannot be reduced to a statement that every Mao is identical for every Jia chart. The source’s bodily and illness descriptions are preserved without retrospective clinical diagnoses. Its favorable Lu/Officer/Seal qualifications precede, and constrain, the subsequent ominous generalizations.

[^61]: **Animals and mutual devouring.** The compressed Tiger/Hare and Dog/Hare examples are retained as written in S and G600. Hare is the image-only glyph in S and is unambiguously identified by the repository’s glyph asset. The passage does not spell out every stem–branch pairing or define a zoological rule; no missing chart has been invented to complete it.

[^62]: **Which stem forms Blade where.** Day stem at hour branch, hour stem at day branch, and year stem at hour or day branch are different tests in the source. Their family assignments have not been interchanged. The child clause is abbreviated in S; G600’s 子息帶災 makes its sense of children carrying the affliction clearer. The judgments about childbirth and parental death are translated historical claims, not advice or predictions concerning a reader’s family.

[^63]: **Taking the reins.** 乙巳貴在申 is read Yi and Ji’s Noble Person at Shen: the context specifically names Yiyou and Jiyou, and the preceding Noble-Person rules identify Yi/Ji with Shen. This normalizes 巳 to 己 rather than introducing a new position. The image is an official taking the reins to restore order throughout the realm. Clear and stern government and harsh officialdom remain distinct expressions in the source, even when they appear in the same judgment.

[^64]: **Void’s basic arithmetic.** Ten stems paired with twelve successive branches leave two branches without partners in each ten-day round. In the Jiazi round those are Xu and Hai. This is the basic xun-Void calculation, not a claim that those branches disappear physically. The source then offers several narrower and broader variants; their common name should not obscure their different reference points.

[^65]: **Two divisions of a round.** Alternating yin/yang days and the earlier/later five-day halves are different partitions. S presents both and then grades complete pillars such as Renxu and Wuxu. The English retains the distinction rather than merging them into a single rule. The phrase “other five stems” in the initial account is understood as the other five Jia rounds, since all six rounds use the same ten stems.

[^66]: **The aesthetic of emptiness.** The paired phrases and all eight small-character glosses are retained. “Nine Schools” refers broadly to the traditional arts or schools, not nine modern academic departments. The text can make Void a condition of refinement, technical ingenuity, or contemplative detachment while denying worldly blessing; neither side is suppressed.

[^67]: **Void can dissolve harm or scatter blessing.** The central distinction is what is being emptied. Combination is unwanted when emptiness removes calamity, but welcome when it preserves beneficial Lu, Wealth, or Officer. Reciprocal Void, as in Jiazi year and Renxu day, is a separate condition. The day and year reference points are both retained; the translation does not decree one universal reference for every use of 空亡.

[^68]: **The True-Void list is not reconstructed.** S and G600 both list Fire/Earth for Jiashen and Jiawu and Water/Earth for Jiazi and Jiayin. The repeated affiliations do not form a neat one-to-one table. They are retained rather than silently inferred from a different Void scheme. The following claim that three void positions may be better than two shows again that the author is not using a simple count of negatives.

[^69]: **The three Chou examples and a partial chart.** Jiayin Water is controlled by Xinchou Earth; Wuwu Fire by Dingchou Water; Gengshen Wood by Yichou Metal. These are nayin relationships. The ensuing example gives only the natal branch class plus month, day, and hour; a full year pillar is not invented to make its Void attribution checkable. The poem’s striking combination of clash, control, punishment, and advancement is retained as an alternative judgment, not quietly reversed.

[^70]: **Historical chart examples.** The two complete charts are reproduced in their source order, together with their reported luck courses and appointments. They are examples cited by the text, not independently authenticated biographies. “Of the same kind” in the Renyin/Renshen clause can involve the common stem as well as other affiliations; the English does not impose an unexpressed nayin equivalence, since their nayin differ.

[^71]: **Distinct Void systems.** Road-Blocking Void is derived through Ren/Gui hours from the day stem. Five-Ghosts, Controlling-and-Harming, and Ancestral-Destruction Void have their own lists and are not interchangeable with ordinary ten-day-round Void. In Five-Ghosts Void, S’s 己午 is normalized to 巳午, Si/Wu, supported by G600. The prohibitions on travel, marriage, and trade are part of the historical text, not present-day practical recommendations.

[^72]: **Four Great Voids use missing nayin phases.** The six ten-day rounds’ nayin distributions explain the two that lack Water and the two that lack Metal; this differs from the two missing branch partners of ordinary Void. The fatality statements and the attribution of Yan Hui’s death are preserved as claims of the quoted authority, not independently established causation. No calendar or biographical reconstruction is supplied to validate the example.

[^73]: **Yuanchen’s direction and complete pillars.** The main rule uses the branch immediately before or after the natal opposition according to sex and year polarity. The examples then specify Yiwei for Jiazi and Jiawu for Yichou, grading the other stems at the same branches at half strength. That grading is part of the source, not a statistical percentage. “Ahead” and “behind” must be read with the supplied examples, not a modern spatial diagram taken for granted.

[^74]: **Physical and moral portraits.** The text’s physiognomic traits, gendered judgments, allegations of spirit possession, and claims about children reflect its historical assumptions. They are not evidence that a particular appearance or natal position establishes character. The author himself rejects the popular Rat/Goat mnemonic for failing to distinguish its stated male/female cases; this objection is retained.

[^75]: **Two meanings of Yuanchen remain contested.** The branch-opposition method and Xu Ziping’s repeated natal Seven-Killings method are explicitly different. The compiler defends the older sha reading after citing both. His two official charts and the claim about the Master Kong—Confucius—are transmitted illustrations, not independently verified natal records. Their names and offices follow S without an added biographical reconstruction.

[^76]: **Hidden Gold and the three names.** 的 has the sense of a target; “Hidden-Gold Target Sha” preserves the written name rather than substituting a later alias. Inclusive counts from Zi yield Si at thirty, You at twenty-two, and Chou at twenty-six. Groaning, Shattering, and White Garments correspond respectively to these positions; white garments evoke mourning. The illness labels and date-selection warnings remain historical statements. The final quotation deliberately qualifies the surrounding negative claims.

[^77]: **A damaged Calamity verse and a parallel quotation.** S and G600 read 庚辛夏蠋戰, whose “summer” and 蠋 make poor sense here. A parallel quotation from the *Shenbai Classic* in the *Wuxing jingji*, juan 23, “White-Tiger Sha,” reads 庚辛忧蝎战, “Geng and Xin fear the Scorpion’s assault,” and explicitly glosses it as Si/You/Chou meeting Mao. The bracketed English follows that parallel, consulted in [Wikisource revision 10494296](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=10494296). This is a related textual witness, not another edition of S. The other lines remain close to S; the precise astral force of “star,” “empty sky,” and “sun’s smoke” is unresolved. The parallel’s additional final couplet is not imported.

[^78]: **Six Obstructions and four listed positions.** The transmitted name is 六厄 although this explanation gives four triad-derived Death positions. Both are preserved. The reference to Li Guang’s failure to receive a marquisate is the source’s explanatory allusion, not proof that an independently verified chart caused that outcome. Its statement that assistance can make the indicator auspicious is followed by a residual warning of obstruction; neither is omitted.

[^79]: **Hook and Strangling.** These lie three branch advances on either side of the natal branch and therefore oppose each other. The male/female and yin/yang direction rule is part of the historical method. The examples are read within that rule, not as an exception erasing it. The text expressly includes ordinary domestic animals under its claw-and-fang imagery, and also says the harmful judgment is not used when the body is not controlled or blessing shares the palace.

[^80]: **Lonely Star’s several derivations.** The first scheme takes positions immediately beyond and behind each seasonal triad. The next explains them through the extinction of a mother or husband and the tomb of a spouse. The western Metal example uses the husband’s Extinction, whereas the concluding generalization names the mother’s Extinction; this is the source’s compression and is not silently rewritten. 孤辰 and 寡宿 are technical star names as well as expressions of orphanhood and widowhood.

[^81]: **Separated corners.** The comparison of Chou and Yin depends on Gen between them on the compass. S’s awkward 四猶之角 is rendered as a comparison with the four corners, without pretending it establishes a different count. The assertions about marriage and kinship remain conditional judgments within these source schemes, not a claim that such a pair prevents actual partnership.

[^82]: **Exceptions and alternative loneliness schemes.** The source explicitly suspends the lonely/widowed judgment where continuity or Noble Person’s enclosure intervenes. The subsequent monk’s cardinal-position scheme, Desolation Sha, Bloodshed Sha, and Shen Zhi’s six reciprocal pairs are distinct accounts, all retained. No single modern lookup table is substituted for their differences. The occupational and medical consequences are translated as stated.

[^83]: **Net and Mesh are asymmetric here.** The source distinguishes Xu meeting Hai from Hai meeting Xu, Chen meeting Si from Si meeting Chen, men from women, and Fire from Water/Earth fates. Its one-, fifteen-, and thirty-year illustration is retained without extrapolating an unstated formula for every day of the month. The final Jiachen/Jiaxu exchange is another specific illustration, not proof that all Chen/Xu clashes are identical to Heaven’s Net and Earth’s Mesh.

[^84]: **Jichou, not Yichou.** S’s opening list has 乙丑, Yichou, but the same section later gives 己丑, Jichou. The *Wuxing jingji*’s “Ten Evils and Great Defeat” verse also gives Jichou, and Jichou’s Lu at Wu is void in the Jiashen round, whereas Yichou’s Lu at Mao is not void in its own Jiazi round. “[Jichou]” therefore has both textual and internal support. The legal and military comparisons explain the names; they are not current legal guidance or independently verified military history.

[^85]: **Three levels of qualification.** The source first names ten offending days, then gives ten particular year/day pairs, then adds month conditions from another authority. These are not collapsed into one universal prohibition. The supplied month stems are retained from S; G600 abbreviates that list to month numbers. The author’s closing remark favors the more specific account but does not delete the opening statement that auspicious support can reverse the judgment.

[^86]: **Discarded days and the added partners.** S’s added winter day is 丁未, Dingwei, which is retained even though related lists elsewhere give 丁巳, Dingsi. The seasonal lack of effective qi explains “discarded”; the translator does not replace the source’s exact day list with a modern standardized version.

[^87]: **Two repaired characters in a reported death example.** In the second chart, S gives a Jiwei hour and the malformed year 丑甲. G600 gives 乙未, Yiwei, and 壬申, Renshen; both are adopted in brackets. Yiwei also accords with the hour-stem derivation for a Xin day. The named historical deaths and the chart attributed to Master Yan are the compilation’s reports, not independently authenticated birth records or established causes of death. Its final excess/deficiency qualification is retained in full.

[^88]: **Character lore, not an asserted modern etymology.** S and G600 write 神 where the gloss is “a tree falling of itself.” The *Erya*, “Explaining Trees,” gives 木自斃曰柛; “[柛]” follows that parallel rather than translating 神, spirit, as though its ordinary meaning were a fallen tree. See the [primary-text quotation under 柛 in the Chinese Text Project](https://ctext.org/dictionary.pl?char=%E6%9F%9B&if=gb). S’s 圯 is read 圮, collapse, supported by G600. The other graphic and phase associations are presented as the author’s reasoning, not endorsed as historical derivations of the characters. Chinese graphs are retained because much of the argument cannot survive in English words alone.

[^89]: **The glyph-based sha lists.** Flat Head, Broken Characters, Hanging Needle, Missing Character, and Bent Leg are interpreted from written shapes, not simply phase relations. S’s 戌庚戌 is read 戊庚戌, Wu/Geng/Xu, following G600. The distinction between being adopted and adopting another’s child is preserved. The medical and physiognomic consequences are the source’s claims, not implications of the glyphs for actual people.

[^90]: **Wine and abbreviated combinations.** The 酒 argument depends on the water component beside 酉; it cannot be conveyed by the English word “wine” alone. The source’s condensed three-stem and paired-stem rules remain distinct. “Four positions in one palace” in the final verse is not expanded into an invented chart. Where the compilation gives severe judgments without conditions, the translation preserves that severity as the author’s language rather than moderating or endorsing it.

[^91]: **The compiler’s opening reservation.** The author calls many of the purported 120 sha explanations forced and fragmented before listing those he regards as having some basis. This is his distinction, not the translator’s authentication of the remaining systems. The full introductory criticism and final insistence on adaptable judgment are retained; removing either would exaggerate the compilation’s confidence in isolated indicators.

[^92]: **Repairs in the drowning/hanging material.** S has 金刃, Metal Blade, where G601 and the parallel *Wuxing jingji* passage give 金神, Metal Spirit; the latter is adopted. The verse’s 丑未, Chou–Wei, conflicts with the preceding reciprocal list’s Wu–Chou; the parallel verse in *Wuxing jingji* juan 23 gives 丑午 and supports the bracketed Wu. The anatomical, fatality, and self-hanging labels are historical assertions, not guidance or an assessment of any living reader.

[^93]: **The author’s fire report.** 育吾 is the compiler’s self-designation Yuwu. His own report is retained in first person, including the Gui month stem, Wuwu luck, and Jiaxu year and month. It is his interpretation of a reported event, not an independently tested causal account. The five positions in this exposition include the conception position alongside the four natal pillars; they should not be silently reduced to four.

[^94]: **Heavenly Slaughter and Punishment.** The first scheme uses reciprocal day/hour branch pairs; the second uses natal branches with hour stems. Those are different operations. S’s Yi hour for Zi/Chou is retained although the analogous list in *Wuxing jingji* gives Ji. 腸風 and 腳氣 are historical disease categories, kept as intestinal wind and foot-qi disease rather than automatically diagnosed as a particular modern illness. S’s final Wu hour for Hai is retained against G601’s malformed Xu hour.

[^95]: **Thunder ritual, not a civil magistracy.** 法官 here is a ritual officer: the following words explicitly concern commanding thunder, talismans, and consecrated water. S’s 墮於天眞 is defective; G601’s 天嗔, Heaven’s anger, supplies the bracketed reading. The two month/branch verses give the same six-position cycle and are both retained. No instruction for conducting a ritual has been added.

[^96]: **Engulfing’s difficult Hare line.** S, G601, and U have 兔趕蛇歌走遠鄉. The word 歌, song, fits poorly. The parallel “吞餤殺” verse in *Wuxing jingji* juan 23 has 兔赶蛇踪配远乡, Hare following Snake’s tracks and being banished afar. “[Tracks]” follows that parallel, while the rest of the line retains S’s less specific “travels far from home.” This is a supported conjectural repair, not a claim that the two recensions are identical. Rooster/Rat in 雞子 and repeated Dragon are retained as the transmitted branch images. S’s explicit birth-year reference is not changed to a day-based method.

[^97]: **Counting the annual spirits.** The mnemonic calls Tai Sui the first palace and places Official Talisman fifth, Death sixth, Sickness twelfth, Mourning Gate third, and Mourning Guest eleventh. “Five positions ahead” for Official Talisman is therefore clarified by inclusive counting; *Wuxing jingji*’s parallel explicitly says to count from Tai Sui to the fifth. Mourning Gate/Guest’s “two ahead/behind” are advances from that starting place. The translation does not assume every occurrence of a number in these lists uses one uniform convention.

[^98]: **Palace calculations and Sword/Edge.** S’s 日上數甲子本日干住 is read “count Jia … to the natal day stem,” following G601’s 數甲至本日干住. The instruction remains abbreviated and does not specify every detail of establishing the palaces. Sword and Edge are two separately listed positions, not the nayin name Sword-Edge Metal. S gives the fifth palace and children; G601 instead gives the seventh and wife/children. S is retained, since the witnesses reflect a substantive difference rather than an obvious single-graph correction.

[^99]: **Floating/Sinking and Breaking.** The Floating/Sinking instruction gives both a backward count from Xu and a subsequent count from the year’s palace. Neither is omitted, but no missing palace algorithm is invented. The unusual list Yin/Wu/Xu/Shen/Wei is retained. For Breaking Sha, S and G601 both say the four corner branches have breaking but also “triple combination”; this awkward formulation is not silently changed to a different theory of sixfold combination.

[^100]: **Eight Exclusives and “Nine” Uglies.** The Eight Exclusives list includes Jiwei, Dingwei, Wuxu, and Guichou as S gives them. The Nine Uglies list actually contains ten complete pillars in S and G601; all ten are retained, with no arbitrary deletion to make the name agree. Qin’s pleasure towers and Chu’s eight thousand are literary allusions in the quoted line, not newly supplied historical statistics. The gendered moral conclusions remain the source’s assertions.

[^101]: **Lonely birds and affinal relations.** Luan and swan are images of separated mates. All eight days in S’s list are retained, including Wushen, omitted in G601’s compressed version. In Yin–Yang Dislocation, 外家 can refer to the maternal or affinal household; the subsequent mention of wife and wife’s wealth makes the latter especially relevant, but the English preserves the wider possibility at its first occurrence. “Empty blossom” retains the metaphor for apparent wealth that fails to bear fruit.

[^102]: **Repeated names do not imply identical rules.** Arrival at Office meeting Robbery is called Peach Blossom here, immediately before the familiar triad/Bathing-based Peach Blossom list. Crimson Beauty then has a separate stem-to-branch formula. These are kept distinct rather than standardized. Short-Life Sha combines Returning Echo with Owl Spirit; its literal name is not an independently established lifespan prediction.

[^103]: **The decisive definition of body.** This paragraph explicitly defines body as the natal year’s nayin. “Controlling the body” is therefore not silently recast as controlling the day stem. The difference between controlling and merely descending upon the body is retained, as are the circumstances under which sha becomes authority. The comparisons with lowly officials and gamblers make the author’s final point: favorable labels alone do not prove a favorable fate, nor unfavorable labels an unfavorable one.

[^104]: **Sha enclosing sha.** The commentary defines this through Birth and Noble Person, expressly denying that it simply means one sha occupying two palaces. The four cardinal branches in the first example all direct their Shattering influence toward Si, where Xinsi Metal is born. G601 names owners for some anonymous charts; those names are not imported into S. Equal opposing power here means stalemate and strife, not automatically the harmonious balance praised elsewhere.

[^105]: **Concentration rather than equal distribution.** The verse’s “four weak and one strong” can encompass the conception position in addition to the Four Pillars. The commentary contrasts one shared center of Birth with several separate centers. Its four True Birth pillars and repeated-Yiyou example are retained. “A hundred” sha configurations is the verse’s expression, not a reconciled count against the earlier 120 names. Blending broth in great cauldrons is an image of ministerial service, not another literal food-related chart influence.

[^106]: **Authority repaired in the commentary.** S reads 神煞無權, spirits and sha “without authority,” awkwardly following a stanza about the cost of obtaining authority. G601 reads 神殺主權, “govern authority,” which supplies the bracketed repair. The resulting claim is that sha can bring authority without eliminating injury and solitude—not that authority guarantees an entirely fortunate life.

[^107]: **One, two, and three are qualified.** The source grades repeated Loss and Robbery, then qualifies a single occurrence by Noble Person and Birth and flourishing. 生不生 is compressed: it can ask whether Birth is obtained as well as evoke life and survival. The translation leaves that uncertainty visible rather than supplying an unexpressed condition. 決脊 denotes flogging on the back, not decapitation. The cited outcomes remain the compilation’s reports.

[^108]: **Commanding wealth through sha.** 殺財 in the verse and commentary is rendered “command wealth,” following the stated logic that controlling the hostile influence makes it submit as Wealth. It is not an instruction to kill for money. In the following example, 中風 remains wind illness, a historical category; a precise retrospective diagnosis is not asserted. Noble Person is said to mitigate the outcome without ensuring examination success, a distinction preserved in full.

[^109]: **Health and reported historical outcomes.** Seven injuries, five consumptions, bone heat, and the Nine Springs are retained as traditional medical and funerary expressions. The two named charts are translated as reports within S. G601 omits the Chu Chongyi example, which is therefore not omitted from the English. Neither the names nor the stated deaths establish an independently authenticated causal history of their charts.

[^110]: **Robbery with loneliness.** S’s 丑合 in the verse is read 丑命, “Chou fate,” following G601. In the gathering-host gloss, S’s 同八寡 is understood as joining loneliness and widowhood, consistent with the repeated neighboring lists; the malformed 八 is not treated as a new “Eight Widows” indicator. The partial Jiwei/Renshen/Shen-hour example is left partial: no missing day or hour stem is invented.

[^111]: **Direction of control and a differing interval.** S gives five days between the ox injury and death; G601 gives five months. S’s five days is retained, with no independent verification claimed. S’s 剋八 is read 剋入, controlling inward, supported by G601 and the fully explicit definition at the end of this section. “Before” and “behind” in the Guiyou/Gengshen example refer to the natal ruler and subsequent sha positions, not an assertion that a chart should be reordered geographically.

[^112]: **The two dwellings.** 命宅 and 祿宅 are technical dwellings associated with fate and Lu, repeatedly invoked in the concluding discussion. Here they are kept distinct rather than translated as two literal houses. S gives Jiayin both as the hour and conception in the military/beggar example; G601’s abbreviated version appears to omit the hour. S’s complete form controls the translation.

[^113]: **Upper Palace, age, and legitimacy.** The commentary explicitly identifies Upper Palace as the day and Imperial Seat as the hour. The verse’s 歲必低 is interpreted as fewer years of age in the remarriage context, not a statement that the year pillar must be weak; the phrase remains compressed. “The child is not true” preserves the verse’s 子未真, while the commentary instead calls the child stubborn. The two are not made artificially identical.

[^114]: **Astral names and legal examples.** Ji 計, Bei 孛, Fire 火, and Luo 羅 are retained as named astrological influences. Fire and Luo/Ji are not ordinary hidden stems, and the passage is not a complete modern astronomical specification. The reported imprisonment and killing examples illustrate the source’s contrast between activating sha by combination and protecting Lu/Horse; no biographical verification is implied.

[^115]: **Appointment and the reversed Shattering.** 拜麻 evokes receipt of the hemp-paper document of high appointment, not worship of hemp. The return-to-origin/Shattering definitions are given through complete branch groups and should not be reduced to one generic clash. The source’s contrast between high office and butchery depends on the different conditions of the sha, not the English occupational labels alone.

[^116]: **Double Maiden, Double Fish, and paired positions.** These are the source’s names for Si and Hai, explained through its snake/fish imagery. The compact 命帶巳亥寅申戌立命 is punctuated here as a fate carrying the four corner branches with the Life Palace at Xu; another division of the words is possible, so this is not presented as a securely reconstructed algorithm. The new/full-moon line remains obscure. Paired Positions then concerns repeated stem/branch arrangements, not simply the same rule about twins. King of Emptiness is a Buddhist epithet, and “forest” in 叢林 denotes a monastery.

[^117]: **Combination types and illness labels.** The enumeration of great/small, inner/outer, and forward/reverse combinations is retained without inventing missing definitions. Canopy’s place in the unpunctuated list is uncertain. 腸風 and 消渴 are translated as intestinal wind and wasting thirst, not equated without qualification with modern diagnoses. The following chart is the source’s illustration of its moral and medical claims, not evidence for them.

[^118]: **The concluding inward-control rule.** In the Dingsi/Renshen example, S’s 丁己 is normalized to 丁巳, supported by its explicit Si-punishes-Shen explanation and G601. The final gloss unequivocally says Noble Person, Lu, and Horse should control the reference person: this is controlling inward. It is intentionally contrasted with the earlier requirement to control harmful sha outward, not collapsed into a blanket rule that all control is either favorable or unfavorable.

[^119]: **Integrity-and-Constancy as a star name.** 廉貞 is translated literally here and retained as an astrological name, not the claim that Salty Pool’s moral character is intrinsically upright. “Overturning cities and kingdoms” is a conventional hyperbole of beauty. The four cardinal positions and the named Jia/Geng/Ren/Bing stems remain part of the technical conditions, rather than merely decorative verse.

[^120]: **Backward Peach Blossom.** The commentary reverses the usual relation: the natal year supplies Mao or You while the three later pillars supply its Fire or Water triad. That is why it is called inserted backward. The source’s portrait combines intelligence and worthiness with impatience and faults; the translation does not reduce it to either praise or condemnation alone. The following artistic reading is conditional on strength and assistance.

[^121]: **Year Sha is used differently here.** Earlier the text defined a Year Sha by its distance from Robbery. Here the commentary explicitly calls Salty Pool in day and hour Year Sha. The name is retained with that difference, not silently standardized. 火、水、土、木、金 assign the modes of calamity within the source’s theory; they are not established diagnostic or mortality categories.

[^122]: **Reported official charts and comparative rank.** The two anonymous examples and Su Dongpo’s attributed chart are preserved as S gives them. They illustrate the author’s hierarchy of blessing-qi, authority-sha, and refinement; no independent birth-record verification is claimed. The statements about chancellors and prefects describe the historical divinatory argument, not an evaluation of present-day political actors.

[^123]: **Advancement can advance something harmful.** 進 is not treated as an unqualified benefit: what is advanced may be Lu, Officer, Salty Pool, Needle, or Consumption. The enumeration of “four” influences and “two” Needles is retained as the commentary presents it, without inventing a missing comprehensive table. The successive verses deliberately qualify the auspicious-sounding name Advancing Spirit.

[^124]: **Beating the basin.** 鼓盆 alludes to a husband’s response to his wife’s death, hence its place among the source’s remarriage and bereavement judgments. It is not an additional musical occupation in this stanza. The reported death at forty-four is translated as an example, without presenting the chart as evidence that such an arrangement causes death at that age.

[^125]: **Another White Tiger.** Here White Tiger is explicitly the Five Phases’ Embryo Spirit. Earlier passages defined White Tiger through other positions, sometimes distinguishing it from Calamity Sha. Those differences remain visible. 焦牙爆齒 is rendered through darkened/protruding teeth rather than converted into a modern dental diagnosis; the implied qualification of marital harmony is not independently validated.

[^126]: **Marriage, mourning, and a compressed family report.** 因孝娶 refers to marriage connected with mourning obligations, not simply a morally “filial” marriage. 外祖 and 外家 can involve maternal and affinal kin, and the English retains that range. In the Dinghai example, 頭妻偏生女半年而死 is punctuated as the first wife bearing only a daughter and dying after half a year; the compact text does not securely specify every interval or exclude another attachment of “half a year.” The medical and marital reports remain those of the compilation.

[^127]: **Close-against-the-body Blade.** The example uses Bingwu/Jiawu and then the aggregation of Blades from several pillars; it should not be reduced to any one occurrence of 午. The verse’s 免得凌屍死血光 is read as escaping violation and bloody death; another punctuation would have escape from violation but death in blood. The following commentary explicitly preserves a reported death in childbirth despite escape from other violent ends. 割頸 reports a cut throat without securely establishing by whom; the English does not invent intent or a perpetrator.

[^128]: **The following-transformation exception.** The Gengxu/three-Yiyou chart is judged through following transformation and obtaining a configuration, not through a mere count of Blades. The subsequent contrast of scattered and concentrated sha likewise depends on their relations, not simply on how many names can be found. The source’s wealth-without-children example and its stated lifespan are retained together.

[^129]: **The already-married wife and Void.** The source gives the remarriage advice as a divinatory condition, not a recommendation by the translator. Its later Void verses distinguish harmful sha being emptied from beneficial Lu/Horse/Noble Person being weakened. “Empty gate” points to religious life, and “development from emptiness” preserves the verse’s paradox rather than eliminating it.

[^130]: **One round and the Dragon Gate.** The shared ten-day round is explicitly compared to one family and remains conditioned by the kind of influences present. Yu’s Gate, tiered waves, fish, and dragons express advancement and examination success; Peach-Blossom waves in that allusion are not automatically the harmful Salty-Pool indicator. The same Chinese image can work differently in adjacent technical and literary contexts.

[^131]: **Animal-fate speculation.** The final verses extend the compilation’s cosmological classification to animal births, using fur, horns, and a back turned upward as their imagery. This is a historical divinatory speculation, not a valid way to classify a human being or judge human worth. The text’s alternatives between injury, prenatal death, and animal fate are retained rather than recast as modern biological claims. The verse and gloss differ in how they connect prenatal injury to harm to the father; neither is suppressed.

[^132]: **Four Seals and official branch names.** Here the Four Seals are the four Earth/Storehouse branches, not four additional Ten-Gods Seals. Heavenly Gang 天罡, Great Auspiciousness 大吉, Lesser Auspiciousness 小吉, and River Chief 河魁 are names assigned to Chen, Chou, Wei, and Xu. Their auspicious-sounding names do not remove the following qualifications. The Golden Valley Garden is an image of great wealth; the stanza does not assert that the example chart belonged to its historical owner.

[^133]: **Canopy, its own Tomb, and astral Qi.** The word 氣 in 羅火孛與氣 occurs among astral names and is retained as Qi rather than silently reduced to ordinary phase qi. The commentary requires Canopy’s own Tomb or mutual generation; its Gengchen counterexample is preserved. The contrast of 華 and 夷 is rendered as central and foreign lands, retaining the image of separation without adopting the source’s cultural hierarchy as a judgment of peoples.

[^134]: **Blade and Flying Blade are not silently unified.** The verse names Flying Blade while the gloss discusses carrying Yang Blade. Both labels remain. The crucial qualification is relative strength: a weak harmful influence submits, a strong one overcomes a weak reference. The listed bodily harms and character traits remain historical assertions rather than contemporary diagnoses or personality assessments.

[^135]: **Hidden harm and a compressed ghost gloss.** Hidden Six Harms are explained through a branch combining with the branch that clashes with the natal reference. No unseen branch is inserted as though it were physically present. In the ghost gloss, the phrase concerning a Defeat palace and many ghosts is compact; the English retains the sequence without inventing a missing premise. Luoyang’s blooming flowers and waning spring convey prosperity that cannot last.

[^136]: **Ghosts, generation, and the seasonal mother.** “Ghost” remains a controlling influence, not necessarily a disembodied being. Its effectiveness is tested against the reference’s strength and the seasonal command. In the Gengxu example, the three Yiyou Waters and Bingzi conception make four Waters generated by Metal. The conception pillar is therefore essential to the arithmetic and has not been omitted. The explanation deliberately allows much generated qi without harm when the mother remains seasonally strong.

[^137]: **The Song’s stated limits.** The final gloss identifies the preceding material as the *Song of Responding to Heaven*, used as an aid to the Three Fates. Its explicit order is natal ruler/root, phase conditions and transformations, Wealth/Officer/Seal/Food, Noble Person/Lu/Horse, and only then the relative force of sha. The statement that exclusive reliance on sha is false is translated in full. The preceding body/hair/skin maxim invokes preservation of what one receives from parents, not a guarantee that insight prevents every calamity.

[^138]: **Two parties in battle.** S’s 疾寅 is read 庚寅, Gengyin, following G601 and the chart itself. In the Jisi/Guiyou/Gengyin/Xinsi example, weak and strong members on each side produce the stated equivalence. The husband/wife metaphor identifies the controlling and controlled parties; it does not mean that the chart necessarily describes an actual married couple. Its praise of balanced battle differs from earlier criticism of stalled combat and is preserved rather than harmonized into one rule.

[^139]: **Victory, the fish’s belly, and the rat’s teeth.** The images express drowning and litigation or punishment; they are not new animal-branch assignments. S gives death at thirty-eight, G601 at twenty-eight. S’s age is retained. The phrase calling Si/Shen/You a Metal configuration is likewise retained, although it is not the standard Si–You–Chou triple combination; the commentary’s broader grouping should not be silently rewritten.

[^140]: **Submission can favor either side.** The first two examples make successive restraints beneficial; the next makes the reference itself surrender to a stronger ghost. S labels that last case “Submission and Surrender Becoming Blessing,” but G601 has 禍, calamity, which fits both verses; “[Calamity]” follows it. S’s more specific Jiashen-round Metal Void is retained against G601’s abbreviated Xin-Void wording. The report of no calamity followed by death before fifty remains as transmitted, without an invented reconciliation.

[^141]: **The child rescuing the mother.** The relationships in the Wuwu example are principally nayin: Dingsi Earth restrains Water that attacks Fire. The contrasting Jiayin-hour/conception version is reproduced because changing those positions changes the stated outcome. S again writes 醫桑; “[Mulberry-Shade]” follows the earlier G600-supported 翳桑 reading, not a separate physician-related sha. Wang Can, Shi Chong, and Fan Dan are literary exemplars of writing, wealth, and poverty respectively; their inclusion is not an authentication of the anonymous chart reports.

[^142]: **Punishment that fails to penetrate.** These examples depend on competing influences in the same palace, Void, and support arriving at the ruler. The second chart includes Jiaxu conception, which must not be mistaken for a fifth ordinary natal pillar. “Does not penetrate” retains the technical distinction made in juan two and does not assert the physical absence of the branch relationship.

[^143]: **One against two or three.** The source’s 一位不能刑二三位刑一位乃真刑 is punctuated as one position unable to punish two or three, but two or three punishing one being genuine punishment. The bracketed subject in English makes that reading explicit; the compressed Chinese permits other punctuation. The ensuing Jichou-fire rescue is retained even though it follows a heading and verse about calamity: no adverse outcome is invented to make the example fit the heading.

[^144]: **Combination is not one uniform benefit.** The source refers back to its ten kinds without repeating their definitions. The Zhen Dexiu chart is a transmitted attribution, and the commentary’s particular judgments about Metal/Wood and absent Earth are retained without independently authenticating the birth. The timely-rain metaphor continues the image of effective ministerial service, while clouds without rain convey unrealized promise. The following prison examples are the text’s reports, not verified causal studies.

[^145]: **Restraint within flourishing.** The source can prefer a single controlling ghost when Birth and flourishing would otherwise be excessive. “Noble sha in the belly” and “an official in the womb” express the prenatal promise claimed in the example, not a literal prenatal appointment. 傳尸癆瘵 is retained as transmitted-corpse consumption, a historical conception of wasting disease; it is not automatically equated with one modern infectious diagnosis. King Yama is the funerary/religious image in the verse.

[^146]: **Death before a change of office.** S ends the Jiwei example with 未敗死亡, an unclear phrase. G601 gives 未改官而亡, “died before changing office,” which supplies the bracketed repair. *Digong lang* is retained as a historical rank-title without assigning an unverified modern equivalent. The following “red robe, black hair” contrasts early official distinction with youth; it does not identify a particular individual.

[^147]: **The closing qualifications are the conclusion.** The same states named Death and Extinction can be favorable with maternal support or renewed generation, and harmful with a strong controlling ghost and damaged Lu/dwellings. The final four-pillar example and closing statement about battles, submission, punishment, clash, breaking, and combination are translated completely. Their importance is the author’s conclusion; the English does not end with a simplified promise that any one named sha is always fortunate or always fatal.
