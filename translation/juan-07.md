# Sanming Tonghui · Juan Seven
## A Comprehensive Gathering of the Three Fates

**Compiled by Wan Minying of the Ming dynasty.** From the imperially commissioned *Siku Quanshu*.[^01]

*Reading conventions.* Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, and Water are the Five Phases. *Qi*, *yin*, *yang*, *nayin*, and **Lu** retain their technical meanings; stem–branch names use unaccented pinyin. Capitalized expressions such as Direct Officer, Seven Killings, Seal, Food God, Birth, and Extinction name technical categories. **〈 〉** marks the source’s small-character commentary. Square brackets identify supplied readings or marked repairs. Different authorities and changes of method remain distinct. The medical, physiognomic, sexual, and social judgments below belong to the historical text; they are not evidence about a living person’s health, character, fertility, or child’s sex, and are not instructions for medical care.[^02]

## 1. A Critical Examination of the Ziping Teaching
### 子平説辯

<!-- source: L14-L22 -->
Those who discuss fate today invoke the name Ziping at every turn. What meaning does “Ziping” convey? Heaven opens at Zi. Zi is Water’s exclusive seat, the first of the earthly branches; the origin of the Five Phases is born from Heaven’s One and corresponds to the north. On level ground, Water comes to rest; encountering a hollow, it flows. This explains the use of **Zi**. Again, when people weigh things on a steelyard, their standard is equilibrium. The slightest excess or deficiency of weight leaves it unbalanced. A person’s Eight Characters are the qi of the prior constitution, and may be likened to this balance: the year is its hook, the hour its counterweight, the month its suspension cord, and the day its measures of weight.[^03]

Among the Eight Characters, the day is ruler. If Wealth, Officer, Seal, and Food flourish and receive support, while the day stem likewise sits upon flourishing and supported ground, the load upon the hook answers to the counterweight: the fate is rich and noble. If Wealth, Officer, Seal, and Food flourish, but the day stem is at rest or in confinement, it is as though a heavy load hung from the hook without a corresponding counterweight. The scale is unbalanced; the fate is lowly and poor. If Wealth, Officer, Seal, and Food are at rest or in confinement, but the day stem flourishes, a light load likewise fails to answer to the counterweight. Again the scale is unbalanced; again the fate meets difficulty and obstruction. If the three things have no qi and the Day Master is at rest or in confinement, poverty and low station—or early death—follow. This explains the use of **ping**, “balance.”[^04]

〈The classic says:〉 “What is excessive in the prior constitution, diminish in the subsequent course; what is deficient in the prior constitution, supplement in the subsequent course.” Only when neither constitution nor course suffers excess or deficiency can balance be achieved. The luck periods constitute this subsequent course. Thus, when the natal Eight Characters give the day stem excessive strength, a course of rest and decline is appropriate, allowing its qi to discharge. When the day stem is deficient, at rest or confined, a flourishing and supportive course is appropriate, generating and sustaining its qi. Either arrangement can bring blessing, wealth, advancement, and free passage—like the physician’s methods of supplementation and draining. But if an already overstrong day stem continues through flourishing luck, or an excessively weak one through declining luck, excess and deficiency alike give rise to calamity, distress, and obstruction.

Ah! *Yun*, the course of fortune, means a turning. It turns once in ten years, and thereby difficulty and success may be known. The rise and decline of the major courses provide the basis for examining the annual ruler’s blessings and calamities. Thus, to discern rank and lowliness, flourishing and decay, examine Ziping; to understand Ziping, examine the relation between the prior constitution and the subsequent course.

This explanation of the two characters “Ziping” is certainly reasonable. Yet Ziping was the courtesy name of Xu [Juyi]; those who discuss fate today trace their method remotely to his and therefore call it Ziping. According to the *Notes on Washing the Cap-Strings*, his surname was Xu, his given name Juyi, and his courtesy name Ziping. He came from Donghai, used the additional name Master Shadi, and was also called the Old Man of Penglai. He lived in seclusion in a cave on Xitang Peak of Mount Taihua.[^05]

Ziping’s method inferred a person’s Lu and fate from the year, month, day, and hour of birth, and never failed to hit the mark. Its origin apparently lies with Luoluzi of the Warring States. A *Rhapsody on the Original Principle and Waxing and Waning* circulates under his name; judging from its language, however, it was probably fabricated by a later writer and is not Luoluzi’s authentic text. Guiguzi lived in Luoluzi’s time. The Han had Dong Zhongshu, Sima Jizhu, Dongfang Shuo, and Yan Junping; the Three Kingdoms had Guan Lu; the Jin had Guo Pu; the Northern Qi had Wei Ding; and the Tang had Yuan Tiangang, the monk Yixing, Li Mi, and Li Xuzhong. All took this art as their foundation. While traveling, Li Mi once obtained Guan Lu’s *Instructions on Heavenly Yang*, and also the *Essential Meaning of the Bronze Cymbals* transmitted by Yixing. His predictions of people’s good and ill were remarkably accurate. Li Mi passed these teachings to Li Xuzhong, who developed and applied them. Luoluzi used the year, whereas Xuzhong used the day: here the method underwent one change.[^06]

In the Five Dynasties there were the Hemp-Robed Daoist, Master Xiyi, Ziping, and others. Ziping obtained Xuzhong’s art, subtracting and adding to it; he made the Five Phases his exclusive basis rather than *nayin*. Here the method changed again. After Ziping’s death, during the Chunxi reign of Emperor Xiaozong of the Song, a practitioner from the Huai region called Chongxuzi mastered the art and was esteemed by his contemporaries. A monk named Daohong privately received his teaching, later entered Qiantang, and disseminated it. People did not know whence the teaching came and simply called it Ziping. Daohong subsequently transmitted it to Xu Dasheng. Works now circulating, such as the *Sources of the Three Fates* and the *Discourse on Establishing the True*, were all written by him. By then the original book had been transformed throughout.

Consider the *Wuxing jingji*, the *Lantai miaoxuan*, the *Comprehensive Survey of the Three Carts*, and the *Song of Responding to Heaven*: these differ from the *Yuanyuan* and *Yuanhai*. Observing patterns and examining change, ordering the calendar and making the seasons clear—all are revised according to their time. Even within a hundred years, therefore, the explanations of numerical arts cannot remain unchanged. How much more must this be so when more than three hundred years separated Dasheng from Ziping! Who knows how often the method changed in that interval? Some say that Dasheng obtained Ziping’s authentic transmission. Looking at chapters such as *Continuing the Good*, one finds nothing beyond the *Rhapsody on Penetrating Clarity*, though the wording has been changed; the *Rhapsody on the Original Principle and Waxing and Waning*, however, was Dasheng’s own particular attainment. The art of fate calculation practiced today is, moreover, an elaboration by Yuan writers who developed the methods of both Ziping and Dasheng. Yet today’s practitioners invoke Ziping at every turn without knowing the origin of the name. Hence I have explained the two characters and examined the matter in detail.[^07]

## 2. On Character and Appearance
### 論性情相貌

<!-- source: L24-L26 -->
Rank and lowliness are discerned in the Eight Characters; character and disposition answer to the Five Phases. Good and evil, benevolence, righteousness, ritual propriety, wisdom, and trustworthiness are governed by the heart. Joy, anger, sorrow, pleasure, love, hatred, and desire arise as its feelings.[^08]

In the east, at Zhen, Wood is called the Azure Dragon and named **Bending and Straightening**. Among the Five Constant Virtues it governs benevolence. Its color is blue-green, its taste sour, its nature upright, its disposition harmonious. Flourishing and supported, it indicates universal care and compassion, kindness and gracious goodwill: benefiting living things and helping people, caring for the orphan and remembering the widow, straightforward, unadorned, pure, and lofty; generous whether in public life or retirement. The bearing is elegant, the frame tall and slender, hands and feet fine and smooth, the mouth pointed, the hair beautiful, the face pale with a blue-green cast, the speech spirited and dignified. This is the meaning of “abundant Wood, abundant benevolence.” At rest or confined, it indicates a thin, tall figure, sparse hair, stubbornness, partiality, jealousy, and lack of benevolence: “declining Wood, scant affection.” At Death and Extinction, the brows and eyes are uneven, the disposition miserly and mean; the flesh is dry, the neck long and throat prominent, walking and sitting unsteady, the body often leaning to one side. Fire gives the complexion a reddish cast; Earth, yellow; Metal, white; Water, black. Apply this same principle to the other four phases.[^09]

<!-- source: L28-L28 -->
Fire belongs to the south and is named **Blazing Upward**. Among the Five Constant Virtues it governs ritual propriety. Its color is red, its taste bitter, its nature urgent, its disposition respectful. Flourishing and supported, it indicates modest deference, upright caution, reverence, humility, and harmony; an imposing dignity, simplicity, and honorable bearing. The face is narrow above and broad below, the head small and legs long, the space between the brows narrow and the brows thick, the nose-tip prominent and the ears small. Spirit flashes forth; speech is rapid. The nature is impetuous but not malicious, intelligent and capable. Excess produces a harsh, parched voice, a red face, shaking knees, and restless activity. Deficiency produces a yellow, thin, angular appearance, deceit, jealousy, malice, extravagant false speech, and beginnings without completion.

<!-- source: L30-L30 -->
Earth belongs to the center and is named **Sowing and Reaping**. Among the Five Constant Virtues it governs trustworthiness. Its color is yellow, its taste sweet, its nature weighty, its disposition substantial. Flourishing and supported, it indicates words and deeds that answer to one another, utmost loyalty and filial sincerity, reverence for spirits and buddhas, and faithfulness to promises and appointed times. The back is rounded, the waist broad, the nose large, the mouth square, the brows clear and the eyes handsome, the face full and yellow. Its capacity is generous and its conduct methodical. Excess makes it rigidly attached to one course, old-fashioned, dull, clumsy, and uncomprehending. Deficiency produces a worried, stagnant complexion, an uneven face and low nose, a heavy, turbid voice, and failure to understand affairs; cruel and contrary, alienating others, confused and untrustworthy, miserly and reckless.

<!-- source: L32-L32 -->
Metal belongs to the west and is named **Following Reform**. Among the Five Constant Virtues it governs righteousness. Its color is white, its taste pungent, its nature firm, its disposition fierce. Flourishing and supported, it indicates heroic courage, generosity in a righteous cause, integrity, and a sense of shame and moral aversion. Bone and flesh are well proportioned, the body vigorous and spirit clear, the face square, white, and clean, brows high, eyes deep, nose straight, ears red, the voice clear and resonant; the will is resolute and judgment decisive. Excess gives courage without strategy, greed, and lack of benevolence. Deficiency gives miserliness, acquisitive cruelty, repeated frustration, much deliberation and little decision, harshness and inward malice, lust and a taste for killing, with a thin, small body.

<!-- source: L34-L34 -->
Water belongs to the north and is named **Moistening Downward**. Among the Five Constant Virtues it governs wisdom. Its color is black, its taste salty, its nature intelligent, its disposition good. Flourishing and supported, it indicates far-reaching calculation, abundant resource and strategy, learning beyond others, and boundless cunning; the face is dark and lustrous, the speech clear and harmonious. Excess makes it contentious and restless, wandering and lustful. Deficiency makes the person short, inconsistent in action and disposition, timid, and without strategy. Though these are analogies drawn from the Five Phases, they truly concern human affairs. Judge the foregoing phase-dispositions together with the auspicious and ominous spirits and sha encountered in the fate. As a general rule, Birth and flourishing indicate a tall, large person; Death and Extinction a short, small one. If a sha is present, this rule does not suffice: judge from that sha. If there is control, judge from the phase being controlled. Also take the *nayin* of day and hour as a general basis, examine whether control is present and whether the spirits and sha occupy ground with or without qi, and determine form and character. Nothing fails to be borne out.[^10]

<!-- source: L36-L36 -->
〈Another account says:〉 In determining a person’s character and conduct, examine only the day and hour, taking the original Five Phases and not *nayin*. If the fate enters a configuration and meets Birth and flourishing, it indicates a naturally clear understanding, responsiveness rather than resistance to circumstances, and actions suited to the occasion. Speech is loud; the mind open and generous. The person judges decisively, is fair and free of suspicion, and does not fear danger or difficulty. Throughout life he is not miserly with possessions, gives freely rather than keeping everything for himself, delights in lavish enjoyment, and is affectionate and devoted to righteousness. There is nevertheless a danger of an unhappy end. If the fate enters a noble configuration but meets Death and Extinction, it indicates a solitary disposition, deep calculation and secret intentions, much suspicion and jealousy; movement bound by ritual, conduct guarded, appearance carefully ordered, constant self-restraint, and no action lightly undertaken. There is a danger of hidden scheming.[^11]

If a common person’s fate has already entered a lowly configuration and meets Birth and flourishing, the disposition is unstable and undisciplined, action heedless of danger and destruction. He delights in fighting, trusts in strength to oppress the weak, associates with evil companions, and neglects the household’s livelihood. In the end he cannot die well. At Death and Extinction, the disposition is lustful and crooked, every movement contrived and false; shrinking and obstinate, affected in behavior, devoted to manipulating words and displaying himself. Faced with affairs, he cannot decide; wrong far exceeds right, and throughout life he establishes nothing.

The *Essential Instructions of Master Zai* say: “High aspirations and far-reaching capacity arise because Water lies at a deep source. Steadfast trust and the keeping of benevolence arise because Earth forms mountains. Kindly, quick, and substantial, Wood completes the realm of Jia and Yi; swift in nature and clear in discrimination, Fire answers to Bing and Ding. High reputation and weighty righteousness come when Metal returns to unite with Geng and Xin. Those who occupy the center keep their proper nature unchanged; excess or decline alters character. Water riding decline and defeat makes a clouded and unprincipled nature. Earth too slight makes one closed, obstinate, and of little use. Wood in obstructed ground is overyielding and conducts affairs without order. Fire not yet risen gives petty cleverness, excessive injury, and no decision. Metal shallow and thin may possess righteousness, but begins without finishing.” Thus gaining or losing ground, excess or deficiency, can all make the Five Phases ominous.

The *Ziping Rhapsody* says: “Beauty of form comes from Wood born in spring or summer; absence of intelligence from Water constrained on Chou or Wei days. A lucid, intelligent nature comes from the refinement of the Water image; decisiveness in affairs from the firmness of Metal qi. When the Five Phases have ample qi, the body must be full; when the Four Pillars lack mutual accord, the nature is often stubborn and coarse.” The *Rhapsody on Pointing Out the Maze* says: “Brilliance and quickness in letters require abundant Fire; martial strength and fierceness come from much Metal. Abundant Wood cherishes compassion; much Water harbors inventive intelligence. Earth’s nature, above all, takes weight and substance as its distinction.”

The *Collection of Extended Trust* says: “Those whose Five Phases are in Birth and flourishing love splendid display, though there may be nothing in their hearts; they are also inclined to lust. This is especially marked in Fire fates. Those at Death or Extinction, under control, or in the Tomb often favor Chan and the Dao: a return to the root and recovery of the origin.” Master Zhang Bai says: “The five self-Extinctions have five kinds of disposition. Metal governs righteousness: self-Extinction makes righteousness scant. Wood governs benevolence: self-Extinction makes one unbenevolent. Water governs wisdom: self-Extinction loses wisdom. Fire governs ritual: self-Extinction lacks ritual. Earth governs trust: self-Extinction makes trust scant.” Whenever the Five Phases first encounter Birth and flourishing and afterward Death and Extinction, much baseness results.[^12]

Huzhongzi says: “Cunning speech comes when the birth hour combines with the Six Voids.” The dwelling of the Six Voids is called the **Spirit of False Speech**. Whoever obtains it delights in embroidering empty words. Repeated occurrences make cunning certain. Throughout life he harms his elders and parents, wanders in other lands, and accomplishes things chiefly in empty reputation.[^13]

## 3. On Illness: First Know Which Stems and Branches Belong to the Five Viscera and Six Bowels
### 論疾病先知五臟六腑所屬干支

<!-- source: L38-L40 -->
〈A song says:〉[^14]

> Jia is gallbladder, Yi liver, Bing the small intestine;  
> Ding is heart, Wu stomach, Ji the spleen’s domain.  
> Geng is the large intestine, Xin belongs to the lungs;  
> Ren is the bladder, Gui the kidneys’ store.  
> The Triple Burner also lodges within Ren;  
> The Enveloping Network returns with Gui to one domain.[^15]

Another says:

> Jia is the head, Yi its crown; seek Bing in the shoulders.  
> Ding is the heart, Wu the flanks, Ji the abdomen.  
> Geng is the navel’s wheel, Xin the thighs;  
> Ren the shins, Gui the feet: thus the whole body is assigned.

Another says:

> Zi belongs to bladder, urinary passages, and ears;  
> Chou to womb, belly, and the spleen’s domain.  
> Yin is gallbladder, hair, vessels, and both hands;  
> Mao the ten fingers, and within, the liver’s realm.  
> Chen Earth is skin, shoulders, and chest;  
> Si the face, throat, and teeth, below the buttocks and anus.  
> Wu Fire, essence and spirit, governs the eyes;  
> Wei Earth: stomach, wrists, diaphragm, and spine.  
> Shen Metal: large intestine, channels, and lungs;  
> Within You lie essence, blood, and small intestine.  
> Xu Earth: Gate of Life, thighs, ankles, and feet;  
> Hai Water: head and the kidney-sac.  
> Infer a person’s illness by this method:  
> Qibo and Leigong, too, would proclaim its fame.[^16]

Another says:

> Wu is the head; Si and Wei the paired shoulders.  
> Chen and Shen are the left and right arms.  
> Mao and You the two flanks, Yin and Xu the legs;  
> Chou and Hai belong to the feet, Zi to the private parts.

Another says:

> Qian is head, Kun abdomen, Kan the ears;  
> Zhen the feet, Xun thighs, Gen the hands.  
> Dui is mouth and Li the eyes: the Eight Trigrams are assigned.  
> In examining illness, seek it by this rule.[^17]

<!-- source: L42-L42 -->
All illness arises from disharmony among the Five Phases—that is, from disharmony among the human body’s five viscera. The Five Phases communicate with the five viscera, and the six bowels with the nine openings. Illness affecting the ten stems belongs to the six bowels; illness affecting the twelve branches belongs to the five viscera. Bing, Ding, Si, and Wu form the Fire configuration of southern Li and indicate illness above. Ren, Gui, Hai, and Zi form the Water configuration of northern Kan and indicate illness below. Jia, Yi, Yin, and Mao belong to Zhen and indicate illness on the left; Geng, Xin, Shen, and You belong to Dui and indicate illness on the right. Wu, Ji, Chen, Xu, Chou, and Wei belong to Kun and Gen and indicate illness in the spleen, stomach, and middle stomach region.[^18]

Wind disorders, dizziness and shaking, vision growing dim, blood failing to circulate smoothly, hair lost early, bluish sinews and withered nails belong to the Liver’s sphere: injured Jia–Yi–Yin–Mao Wood is responsible for the illness. Pain, pus and blood, sores and scabies, a bitter tongue, and loss of speech belong to the Heart’s sphere: Bing–Ding–Si–Wu Fire has been impaired. Puffiness, leg-qi, yellow swelling, foul breath, stomach reversal, cold in the spleen and heat at the diaphragm belong to the Spleen’s sphere: Wu–Ji–Chen–Xu–Chou–Wei Earth has been impaired. Blocked nose, wine-red nose, halting speech, knotted qi, cough, and cries belong to the Lung’s sphere: Geng–Xin–Shen–You has been impaired. White turbidity, white vaginal discharge, sudden vomiting and diarrhea, flux and dysentery, shan-qi and small-intestine disorders belong to the Kidney’s sphere: Ren–Gui–Hai–Zi has been impaired.[^19]

<!-- source: L44-L44 -->
**Jia and Yi encountering much Geng, Xin, Shen, and You:** internally this indicates liver and gallbladder disorders, fright and palpitations, wasting illness, stubborn numbness of hands and feet, and pain in sinews and bones; externally, dizziness of head and eyes, a crooked mouth or eyes, paralysis on either side, and injuries from falls or blows. If much Bing and Ding Fire appears without Water to mediate, there are phlegm and panting, coughing blood, wind-strike with loss of speech, dry skin, internal heat, and a dry mouth. In women it indicates disordered blood and qi; in pregnancy, miscarriage. In children it indicates acute or chronic fright-wind, crying at night, and cough, with a dusky blue-green face.[^20]

<!-- source: L46-L46 -->
**Bing and Ding encountering much Ren, Gui, Hai, and Zi:** internally this indicates pain of the heart-qi, derangement and seizures, a stiff tongue, sore mouth, a hoarse throat, acute or chronic fright-wind, and hesitant, obstructed speech; externally, tidal fevers and frenzy, dim eyes and lost sight, small-intestine shan-qi, sores discharging pus and blood, and painful, turbid urination. In women it indicates dry blood-qi and disordered menstrual channels. In children it indicates pox eruptions, scabies, and ring-shaped eruptions, with a red face.[^21]

<!-- source: L48-L48 -->
**Wu and Ji encountering much Jia, Yi, Yin, and Mao:** internally this indicates disharmony of spleen and stomach, stomach reversal, obstructed food, choking qi, gu-distention, diarrhea, yellow swelling, selective eating and drinking, vomiting, and nausea; externally, heaviness of the right hand, wandering accumulations of damp toxin, and fullness and blockage in chest and abdomen. In women it indicates food and drink losing their savor, sour regurgitation, weakness, yawning, weariness, and drowsiness. In children it indicates the five gan disorders and five flaccidities, internal heat, and frequent spitting, with a wan yellow face.[^22]

<!-- source: L50-L50 -->
**Geng and Xin encountering much Bing, Ding, Si, and Wu:** internally this indicates intestinal wind, hemorrhoids and fistulas, blood after the stool, phlegm-fire cough, panting and vomiting blood, loss of the soul to baleful spirits, empty agitation, and wasting illness; externally, withered dry skin, lung-wind and a red nose, deep swellings and carbuncles on the back, discharge of pus and blood, and weakness. In women it indicates phlegmatic cough and bleeding in childbirth. In children it indicates dysentery with pus and blood, and a yellow-white face.[^23]

<!-- source: L52-L52 -->
**Ren and Gui encountering much Wu, Ji, Chen, Xu, Chou, and Wei:** internally this indicates involuntary seminal loss, night sweating, dreams of intercourse with ghosts, white turbidity and depletion, shivering and grinding teeth, deafness and blindness, and cold-damage or externally contracted chills; externally, wind- or worm-toothache, one-sided downward dragging, kidney-qi disorders, pain in the loins and knees, dribbling urine, vomiting and diarrhea, and dread of cold. In women it indicates white discharge, ghost-fetus, and irregular menstruation. In children it indicates sores within the ears, small-intestine pain, and disturbance at night, with a dark, dusky face.[^24]

The rhapsody says: “Pain in sinews and bones comes from Wood injured by Metal. Darkened eyes must mean Fire controlled by Water. Earth deficient in flourishing Wood’s country: judge injury to the spleen. Metal weak amid blazing Fire: blood illness is beyond doubt.” It also says: “Wood controlled by Metal surely indicates calamity in loins and flanks; Fire injured by Water surely indicates eye disease. Panting and fullness in heart and lungs also concern Metal and Fire punishing one another; injury to spleen and stomach comes from Earth and Water battling and controlling. Water in the branch with Fire above in the stem brings abdominal illness and a clouded heart; Fire in the branch with Water above brings internal obstruction of sight and blindness. Blazing Upward scorches and steams the Earth influence: baldness and dim eyes. Moistening Downward, all dampness without Earth to restrain it: depleted kidneys and closed ears. Yinghuo flourishing at Li and Xun gives wind-strike with lost voice; Taibai hard and sharp at Dui and Kun brings the soul’s downfall before weapons.”[^25]

It also says: “When the heart is ill, the mouth cannot speak; when the liver is ill, the eyes cannot see; when the spleen is ill, the mouth cannot eat; when the lungs are ill, the nose cannot smell; when the kidneys are ill, the ears cannot hear. Follow the governing correspondence to establish deficiency and excess. Metal ghost is unsuited to needling; Fire ghost particularly avoids injury by moxa. Earth does not use pills; Wood must avoid powders. When Metal makes blessing, seek a physician in the west; when Wood comes to generate, take medicine in the east. Water at Extinction calls for needling; mineral medicines are appropriate to restore life. Weak Earth seeks Fire cautery; seek a physician of a zhi-tone surname and there is no misfortune. Fire ghost can be treated with decoctions; Water ghost particularly suits pills and powders.”[^26]

Ah! Human illnesses are a hundredfold, but their principle is not twofold. Inspection, listening and smelling, questioning, and pulse-taking are the physician’s subtle applications; generation, control, restraint, and transformation are the practitioner’s hidden subtleties. If one can investigate root and source, neither manifestation nor foundation departs from this method. The discourse says: “Health and ease arise from harmonious combination; disease arises from punishment and injury. Investigate the Five Phases’ strength and decline to infer the inner and outer workings of the hundred diseases. Within they answer to the five viscera; without they belong to the four limbs.”[^27]

Thus Wood qi at rest or confined gives thinning temples and hair that does not grow; Fire at Death or Extinction gives dark, lightless pupils. Earth hidden within Fire, with little Water to restrain it, leaves the heart-spirit confused and distracted. Metal concealed beneath Wood, without Water to gather it, brings injury to legs and feet. Jia and Yi born in Dui meet Ren and Gui and die in the country of drunkenness; Bing and Ding nurtured in Kan meet Geng and Xin and perish in ditches or waterways. Abundant Water floating Wood often gives diarrhea and dysentery; heavy Earth burying Metal often gives upward-rising qi.[^28]

Wind disorders, shaking, and dizziness arise when Yi Wood flourishes and Xin Metal declines; pain, itching, sores, and ulcers when Ding Fire is abundant and Gui Water weak. Blockage, swelling, and fullness arise from excessive Ji Earth; pent-up vexation and wasting weakness from insufficient Xin Metal. Flourishing Gui Water makes ears and eyes keen; a depleted Kidney channel lets cold evil contract the body. Jia and Yi injuring Wu and Ji without rescue give a cleft lip; Bing and Ding controlling Geng and Xin with little restraint give loss of speech. Earth within Fire gives swellings along the neck; Earth within Water gives gu-swelling in the abdomen.[^29]

The useful influence restrained and punished gives death beneath rods and staves; ghosts above and below, with no rescue, give death hanging from a beam. Repeated clashes in the Four Pillars bring many misfortunes and loss of the body far from home; the Five Phases declining, defeated, and insufficient bring death by epidemic. Water defeated bends the back: what use then are the methods of Xuan and Qi? Metal punished makes a turtle-back: how could the prescriptions of Lu and Bian be applied? Geng and Xin with refined qi in the west encounter Wood and die by weapons; Jia and Yi defeated or extinguished in Kun’s southern region, without Water, suffer scattering of bone and flesh.[^30]

Xinsi and Bingshen meeting punishment give weak arms and a sixth finger; Jimao and Wuyin meeting an enemy give a weak stomach and recurring sores and deep ulcers. Yiwei and Jiawu meeting Metal often give a turtle-shaped head. Guimao and Jichou punishing one another give illness in loins and knees. Jiashen and Yiyou give much illness of the Liver channel in childhood; Xinmao and Gengyin give late-life injury and exhaustion of sinews and bones. Bing Fire blazing upward is especially to be avoided in a man’s body and heart; Ding Fire damp below gives women wasting depletion and bleeding in childbirth. Metal and Earth at Yin or Mao give panting lungs and a cold spleen. Wu and Ji meeting defeated Fire give illness of the spleen from an old mass.[^31]

Geng and Xin meeting Fire’s punishment require concern over white discharge in women; Bing and Ding together facing Li require particular caution over uterine flooding. Yang Blade means lancing the upper arm and cauterizing the thigh; Suspended Needle, puncturing the face to tattoo the body. Day and hour declined and defeated make a grave illness hard to cure; stems and branches in punishment and harm leave even a small illness unhealed. Qi in mutual accord gives peace and harmony; qi opposing qi brings calamity and destruction. Disease does not depart from the six pulses, and life and death cannot pass beyond the Five Phases. Examine rise and decline closely: not one case in ten thousand will be missed.[^32]

An old song says:

> Wu and Ji at birth possess incomplete qi;  
> Month and hour both show Hurting Officer.  
> Head and face must suffer impairment;  
> Sores of pus and blood afflict the young.

> The Day Master is born as Wu or Ji;  
> Fire’s configuration in the branches steams its qi.  
> Clash, punishment, control, and breaking bring disability:  
> Baldness—and how can dim eyes be borne?

> A Bing or Ding day stem, the Five Phases weak;  
> Seven Killings descend, a triple combination comes.  
> Seeking a sheng or ge each day, short of food and clothes:  
> Deaf, disabled, the face covered in dust.[^33]

> Ren and Gui arranged again and again;  
> Should the hour encounter Heavenly Wealth,  
> Even without blotches or leprous marks on head and face,

## 4. On Poverty, Low Station, Misfortune, and Wickedness
### 論貧賤凶惡

<!-- source: L78-L80 -->
Fates of poverty and low station generally lack noble qi. The Five Phases may be at Death and Extinction, with stems and branches idle and disconnected. Lu may fall in Void while Great Depletion controls the body; Heavenly Center may descend upon the day; or the Five Phases may be at Death and Extinction and also fall in Void. All Lu and Horse, in Birth and flourishing, may gather at one position, only for Heavenly Center to occupy it. Another position may punish or harm it until its qi scatters. The place where blessing gathers may be unable to stand alone, its blessing divided among the other positions. Traveling Horse may control the body; repeated Robbery may encounter restraint. Chen, Xu, Chou, and Wei may control one another while the Five Phases lack qi and cannot be stirred into action. Every position may meet combination; or there may be neither combination nor clash, with the qi above and below belonging to different kinds. Stems and branches may be disordered, yin and yang one-sided and withered, the Eight Characters unsupported by any configuration and the nine fates mixed with punishment. Birth and flourishing may come first, followed by Death and Extinction; transformed qi may miss its season while the original fate lacks qi; borrowed sound may bring harmful control, ruler and root reversed, father and son at odds. All fates of these kinds indicate poverty and low station.[^47]

〈Another account says:〉 Poor and lowly fates commonly use Established Lu and Food God as rescuing spirits. With these two rescues in the fate, poverty does not reach starvation and lowliness does not reach slavery. When a favorable course develops, there is a little satisfaction; when it passes, poverty and lowliness return.

The *Surviving Writings of Guigu* include **Punishment Gathering at Utter Defeat**, such as [Jiashen] obtaining Dingsi, Jimao, and Jisi; **The Four Pillars Failing to Gather**, such as Jiazi obtaining Bingyin, Dingsi, Xinhai, and Renshen; **The Five Forms Incomplete**, such as Jiazi obtaining Gengzi, Jimao, and Guimao; **One Direction Before and Behind**, such as a Wood-fate person obtaining Jichou; and **Separated Angles in the Pillars**, such as Xinchou obtaining Xinmao or Jiazi obtaining Jiaxu. All indicate poverty and low station.[^48]

<!-- source: L82-L82 -->
Fates of misfortune and wickedness occur when the fate and the Five Phases lack qi and also control one another; when branches and stems are contrary, punishing and clashing, mutually overpowering and battling while carrying sha; or when True Clash and True Punishment scatter qi. Officer Talisman and Great Depletion may punish and control the original fate while the Five Phases lie at Death and Extinction without any rescue. Officer Talisman may control the body while two Woods punish and clash. Chen and Xu, the Kuigang positions, may clash with no Noble Person or Traveling Horse to assist. At places where the Five Phases die, become extinct, and control one another, evil sha may appear—Heavenly Fire, Drowning in Water, White Tiger, Self-Hanging, and the like.[^49]

Or all the Five Phases may flourish, yet every position controls another and qi disperses. Void, Solitary Time, and Widow’s Lodge may be exchanged among the positions; Year Breaker, White Tiger, Yang Blade, and other sha may appear. Heavenly Center, Great Depletion, Robbery Sha, and Loss Spirit may share a palace, repeatedly controlling one another. Many Separated Angles may carry sha; Robbery and Loss in day and hour, together with sha, may control the year. Suspended Needle, Reversed Spear, Metal Spirit, Seven Killings, Yang Blade, and similar sha may repeatedly control the original fate. Punishment may gather at utter defeat with no rescue in the Four Pillars; or the Four Pillars may punish one another while also occupying empty, exhausted ground. All such fates indicate misfortune and wickedness.

〈Another account says:〉 Fates of misfortune and wickedness commonly use Noble Person, the Three Wonders, Canopy, and Flanking Nobility as rescuing spirits. With these, ominous spirits may appear without inflicting injury. What is most feared is that the Five Phases remain idle, with neither Established Lu nor Food God, while the Three Powers punish and control, carry sha, and have no noble qi to resist them. Poverty, lowliness, misfortune, and wickedness are then beyond doubt.[^50]

## 5. On Long Life and Early Death
### 論夀夭

<!-- source: L86-L88 -->
As a general rule, in the allotment of long and short life, Birth and flourishing give longevity, Death and Extinction early death. Deep roots make a firm stem; a deep source makes a long stream. This too follows naturally from the principle. When month and day meet Birth and flourishing, Death qi in the hour does no harm. If the month is at Death and Extinction, then even if day and hour are in Birth and flourishing, the qi is not very vigorous, for that generation succeeds Death and Extinction. With month and day in Birth and flourishing but the hour at Death and Extinction, life does not exceed forty-five years. With month and day at Death and Extinction but the hour in Birth and flourishing, death comes before thirty. Further ominous sha and encroachment among the Five Phases make death in childhood certain. Ordinarily the month governs ages one through thirty; the day, thirty-one through forty-five; the hour, forty-five through one hundred. Examine Birth and flourishing or Death and Extinction in each period.[^51]

A person lives when qi gathers and dies when qi scatters. If qi in the two luck courses and Tai Sui assembles in the country of Death, Extinction, decline, and rest, while Tai Sui additionally punishes or controls and corresponds to a period of calamity, death is certain. Each takes effect in a month, day, or hour in which the body lacks qi.[^52]

<!-- source: L90-L92 -->
Much Birth and flourishing in the fate, without offending sha, indicates little illness and a good end: death in the space of a single thought. Much Death and Extinction with heavy punishment and sha indicate exhaustion and suffering, or illness lingering for years and difficult to cure. Death in a flourishing course, or upon Lu, means dying amid satisfaction. An old person whose annual course meets Birth and flourishing dies in suffering, for that continuing generation prolongs frailty and emaciation. A young person instead dies of a “gentle illness.” Repeated Loss Spirit and Great Depletion in the fate require mutilation of the corpse.[^53]

Heavenly stems in Birth and flourishing, without injury or control, give long life. Heavenly stems defeated or dead but receiving rescue and assistance give still longer life. Heavenly stems defeated or dead and harming one another give early death. Heavenly stems in Birth and flourishing but containing breaking and control give earlier death still.

For example, a Guihai Water-fate person born in the fourth month lacks longevity. In Wu and Gui years, the fourth month is Dingsi, whose *nayin* is Earth; Earth controls Water, so the birth month controls the fate. Moreover, for a Guihai person, the fourth month is the Extinction of Lu and Fate, hence the lack of longevity.

There are **The Two Extremes of Yin and Yang**. 〈A song says:〉 “Do you know the two extremes of yin and yang? For men and women, count from the original fate. A man meets nine positions, a woman three pairs; pass each position in forward order. If major and minor courses both lack qi, know that arrival here is the place of death. If the two courses possess qi, no harm follows; their conjunction within a year brings the suffering of a calamitous course.” 〈For a man, count forward from the original fate to the ninth position: the yang extreme. For a woman, count from the original fate to the sixth position: the yin extreme.〉[^70]

<!-- source: L120-L122 -->
〈The *Song of Source and Marrow* says:〉 “Wood and Water meeting Si, Metal meeting Yin: generating Fire and generating Metal injure the body itself. All Five-Phase fates must guard against this; know that those meeting it will not be crane-haired elders.”[^71]

All these accounts must be examined fully. First consider the Five Phases’ Birth, flourishing, Death, and Extinction; next, whether the configuration is damaged; afterward examine the spirits and sha, comparing them with the flowing year and Tai Sui. Nothing will fail to hit the mark.

〈One account says:〉 A person’s long or short life is received from the parents. The father’s essence and mother’s blood differ in strength and decline, and so people’s lifespans differ. When, at the beginning of life, the received qi of both is abundant, an upper or middle span should result. When only one is abundant, the span should be middle or lower. When both are weak, careful preservation and nourishment secure only the lower span; otherwise early death is frequent. Even so, one cannot cling rigidly to an ordinary rule. Wind, cold, summer heat, or damp may affect a person from outside; hunger, surfeit, toil, or service may injure within. How could everyone exhaust precisely the original qi received at birth and complete the full span of natural years? The gentleman who understands fate must cultivate himself and await what comes: that is all.[^72]

## 6. On Women’s Fates
### 論女命

<!-- source: L124-L126 -->
Someone asks: “Where does a woman’s advantage lie?” It lies in the husband-star. If the husband is favored, his wife must be favored; if the husband is distressed, his wife must be distressed. A woman follows her husband. First examine the husband-star to determine the rank or lowliness of her circumstances; then examine the child-star to discern honor or humiliation in later life. Officer, Killings, and Wealth gaining their proper ground favor the husband; Food God gaining its ground favors the child. A favored husband brings wealth and distinction in her station and the enjoyment of blessing throughout life. A favored child brings generous support in old age, praise, favor, and an honorific title by imperial patent. Some arrangements also strengthen the husband because Food generates Wealth and Wealth generates Officer. The reverse does not.[^73]

In a woman’s fate, what controls me is husband and what I generate is child. Both should obtain their season and ride the qi of Birth and flourishing; it is also usable if flourishing qi gathers only in the hour. When Officer is used as husband, Killings should not appear; when Killings is used as husband, Officer should not appear. One position is good. Two Officer positions without Killings to mingle with them, or pure Killings throughout the Four Pillars without Officer to confuse them, alike make a good wife. The body itself flourishing is still better, but its strength must not be excessive. Food represents offspring: draw it into the hour and encounter flourishing there. Add the Two Virtues supporting the body, and this is a fate of a noble husband and honored children.[^74]

Excessive bodily strength, repeatedly concealed husband influences, Hurting Officer, Seven Killings, Kuigang in mutual punishment, an overheavy Yang Blade, or many mutually attracted combinations are all undesirable. Years and luck courses follow the same judgment. There are eight methods and eight configurations to examine closely.

### The Eight Methods: Pure — 純
<!-- source: L128-L130 -->
Pure means single: a single, unmixed Officer Star or Killings Star, with Wealth and Seal, without punishment or clash, and without the two being confused. Take 〈one fate〉 **Guisi / Wuwu / Xinyou / Bingshen**. The body possesses Exclusive Lu and flourishes, so it does not follow transformation. Xin uses Bing Officer as husband-star. Fire flourishes in the fifth month, making the husband vigorous. Bing uses Gui as Officer, seated upon Noble Person; it sees Wu as Food, and both return to Lu at Si. Xin Metal generates Ren Water as child, drawn into the Shen hour, the ground of Birth. The heavenly stems—Gui, Wu, Xin, Bing—give Water and Fire Achieving Completion. The branches—Si, Wu, You, Shen—arch and flank the Wealth Storehouse. Thus she marries an official and enjoys Heaven’s emolument: a fate of an honored husband and noble children.[^75]

〈Another:〉 **Guihai / Jiayin / Bingxu / Jiawu**. Bing uses Gui as husband, with Coming to Office at Hai. Jia is Seal, seated on Yin’s Established Lu. The body itself sits upon the Storehouse. Ji Earth is child, returning to Lu at Wu, which occupies the hour, the position of offspring. Jia Wood is Ji Earth’s Officer. The Four Pillars are pure and unmixed; hence noble children bring reflected honor. Infer the other cases accordingly.[^76]

### Harmonious — 和
<!-- source: L132-L134 -->
Harmonious means calm and quiet. The body is yielding and weak, there is only one husband-star, and the pillars contain no influence of clash, breaking, or assault. Receiving the qi of balanced harmony is called harmonious. Take 〈one fate〉 **Renchen / Xinhai / Jimao / Jisi**. Ji uses Jia as husband. Hai is the ground of Birth, giving the season of Heaven and advantage of Earth. Jia takes Xin as Officer; Metal is born at Si. Ji takes Metal as child, also born at Si. This is called “the husband obtaining Officer Star and the child obtaining Birth”; it benefits the husband and strengthens the child. Although the body sits on Mao as Killings, Geng within Si restrains it. It is therefore judged as Removing Killings and Retaining Officer, a noble woman’s fate.

〈Another:〉 **Dingchou / Renyin / Dingyou / Jiyou**. Ding uses Ren as husband and Jia as Seal, which is the husband’s Food and Lu. Dingyou is Day Nobility and generates the Jiyou child. Ren Water obtains Ji Earth as Officer, indicating a noble husband; Ji Earth obtains Jia as Officer, indicating a noble child. Wealth flourishes within You: a configuration honoring the husband and sheltering the child. Infer the other cases accordingly.[^77]

### Clear — 清
<!-- source: L136-L138 -->
Clear means clean and pure. In a woman’s fate, a single Officer or a single Killings, without mixture, is called clear. The husband-star must obtain its season; Wealth in the pillars must generate Officer and Seal assist the body. Only with not a trace of turbid qi is there clear distinction. Take 〈one fate〉 **Jiwei / Renshen / Yiwei / Jiashen**. Yi uses Geng as husband, and Geng’s Lu reaches Shen. Ding is child, flourishing at Wei; Ren is Seal, born at Shen. The branch beneath the day is Yi Wood’s Wealth. Flourishing Wealth can generate Officer; the Four Pillars have no punishment, clash, breaking, or defeat. 〈The classic says:〉 “Wealth, Officer, and Seal-Ribbon: a woman’s fate encountering these three must strengthen her husband.” Hence this is the fate of a titled lady.[^78]

〈Another:〉 **Jiayin / Guiyou / Bingyin / Wuzi**. Bing uses Gui as husband, seated at You, its own Birth. Gui obtains Wu as Officer, and Gui’s Lu dwells at Zi. A husband obtaining Lu is noble. Bing Fire obtains Wu Earth as child, seated upon Wealth and encountering Killings and Seal: the child is noble. Infer the other cases accordingly.[^79]

### Noble — 貴
<!-- source: L140-L142 -->
Noble names honor and distinction. The fate contains Officer Star, aided by Wealth qi; the Three Wonders obtain their foundation, and the Four Pillars harbor no ghostly affliction. This is a Yao or Shun among women’s fates. 〈The classic says:〉 “A woman’s fate without Killings: one noble influence can make a good wife.” 〈It also says:〉 “A woman’s fate without Killings, meeting the Two Virtues, may receive the patent of two states.” The Two Virtues do not mean only Heavenly and Monthly Virtue: Wealth is one virtue and Officer another. Adding Seal and Food makes it nobler still.[^80]

Take 〈one fate〉 **Jiawu / Bingyin / Dingwei / Renyin**. Ding uses Ren as Officer; Ren takes Jia as Food, which is Ding’s Seal. Ren uses Bing as Wealth. Ren takes Hai as Lu, and Renyin secretly combines with it. Although the husband-star has missed its season, the northwest courses that strengthen the husband are welcome; hence great distinction. 〈Another:〉 **Yihai / Bingxu / Xinmao / Guisi**. Xin uses Yi as Wealth, flourishing at Hai. Bing is husband-star, seated on the Storehouse and returning to Lu at Si. Gui Water is the husband’s Officer. Xin Metal generates Gui as child, seated upon Si at the same position as the husband’s Lu. It is also Noble Person, and Wealth and Officer in Double Beauty. Husband and child are both noble, and she receives two honorific patents. Infer the other cases accordingly.[^81]

### Turbid — 濁
<!-- source: L144-L146 -->
Turbid means mixed. The Five Phases have lost their positions, Water and Earth injure one another, the body is too strong, the proper husband does not appear, and secondary husbands crowd together. The pillars contain many competing divisions but no Wealth, Officer, Seal, or Food. This makes a lowly, rustic, and coarse person, or a prostitute, maidservant, concubine, or woman of lustful cunning. Take 〈one fate〉 **Jihai / Yihai / Guichou / Jiwei**. Gui Water born in the tenth month floods abundantly. Gui takes Wu as husband, but Wu does not appear. The hour draws in Jiwei, a secondary husband; both Chou and Wei contain Earth, making an unwelcome mixture. There is no Wealth in the pillars. Yi Wood is Food God and strong in the stem, so Ji Earth receives control. The defeated ghost descends upon the body, and the Five Phases lose their positions. This indicates clarity first and turbidity afterward, with no enjoyment of blessing.[^82]

〈Another:〉 **Guiwei / Jiayin / Xinyou / Yiwei**. Xinyou is one of the Eight Specialists and flourishes in itself. Bing Fire is husband, born at Yin. The husband’s strength is originally good, but Xin covets Wealth in Yiwei’s Storehouse, stirring Ding Fire within Wei as a secret husband. The hidden husbands in two Storehouses outweigh the visible husband. The visible and hidden mingle: though she has a proper husband, she cannot avoid secretly taking a lover for gain. This is an image of turbidity and disorder. Infer the other cases accordingly.[^83]

### Indiscriminate — 濫
<!-- source: L148-L150 -->
Indiscriminate means greedy. Many husbands are openly present in the pillars, Wealth flourishes in secret, and stems and branches also carry many Killings. Wealth must come through drink, sex, and private liaisons. Such a fate may be that of a servant, or one who harms her husband and remarries. Take 〈one fate〉 **Gengyin / Bingxu / Gengshen / Dinghai**. Gengshen is one of the Eight Specialists and flourishes in itself. Bing Fire is husband, and Yin and Xu assemble its configuration. Ding also appears in the hour stem, adding further attachment to Fire. Gengshen Metal secretly controls Yin–Hai Wood as Wealth; Ren Water within Hai is Food generating Wealth. Though she is beautiful and fortunate, she cannot avoid acquiring wealth through indiscriminate conduct.[^84]

〈Another:〉 **Wuzi / Jiayin / Jiwei / Dingmao**. Jia Wood flourishes in the first month. Mao and Wei assemble a configuration, so proper and secondary husbands are many. Flourishing Wealth also lies upon Zi. Ji combines with Jia Officer, the proper pairing of yin and yang. Thus, though intelligent and beautiful, she cannot avoid falling into indiscriminate conduct. Moreover, Reversed-Insertion Peach Blossom carries a sister above it, not Officer Star. How could this make a good wife?

〈Another:〉 **Jiyou / Dingchou / Guichou / Renxu**. Ji husband is openly present in the pillars. Two Chou and one Xu conceal three husbands. Ding is Wealth, returning to its Storehouse at Xu and punishing Chou. The second yang has obtained the season, and Fire’s qi is advancing: husbands are many and Wealth flourishes. Ding and Ren are excessive. 〈Another:〉 **Jiachen / Guiyou / Bingzi / Xinmao**. Bingzi day offends Yin–Yang Sha, indicating men’s enticement. Bing takes Gui as husband; Chen and Zi gather Water, making many husbands. Day and hour have Bing–Xin combination and Zi–Mao punishment: branch punishment and stem combination offend Wantonness and Rolling Waves, and drink and sex cloud the mind. Wealth flourishes within You, where Gui husband has its exclusive seat. Both of these last two fates belong to prostitutes who obtain wealth by selling illicit intercourse. Infer the other cases accordingly.[^85]

### Prostitution — 娼
<!-- source: L152-L154 -->
Prostitution means the life of a courtesan. The body flourishes, the husband is extinguished, Officer declines, and Food is abundant. Perhaps neither Officer nor Killings appears in the pillars; perhaps they appear but Hurting Officer injures them completely; or Officer and Killings are mixed while Food God flourishes abundantly. This must be a prostitute’s fate; otherwise, that of a religious woman, nun, maidservant, or concubine who harms her husband or runs away in lust. Take 〈one fate〉 **Dinghai / Gengxu / Wuchen / Gengshen**. Wu takes Jia as husband. In the ninth month Jia has missed its season and lacks qi; Geng additionally controls it, and the hour draws it into Extinction at Shen. Geng serves as Food, with Established Lu at Shen. Wuchen is Kuigang, born at Shen and excessively strong; Ren Wealth within Hai also flourishes. This is called “a flourishing body encountering generation, greedy for Food and Wealth, while husband is extinguished”: a beautiful prostitute.[^86]

〈Another:〉 **Yihai / Bingxu / Jiazi / Bingyin**. Jia takes Geng and Xin as husband. In the ninth month Metal declines and its qi retreats. The hour draws Food God to Birth, while Wood’s ground assembles a configuration; Jia Wood returns to Lu and the body flourishes. Geng Metal, drawn to Yin, reaches Extinction without qi. Two Bing Food Gods are too strong and injure the Metal husband. This is called “self-flourishing with abundant Food.” Though food and clothing are good, she cannot escape the courtesan’s life amid wind and dust.

〈Another:〉 **Guichou / Gengshen / Wuchen / Gengshen**. Wu uses [Yi] as husband, extinguished at Shen. The [Wu] day obtains Gengshen as Food God, repeated in month and hour. This is called “flourishing Food and an extinguished husband,” hence prostitution. In general, many Food Gods in a woman’s yang-stem fate make a prostitute; many Food Gods in a yin-stem fate make a courtesan. Infer the other cases accordingly.[^87]

### Licentious — 滛
<!-- source: L156-L158 -->
Licentious means overflowing. The body obtains its ground while husband-stars mingle in the open and in concealment: the day stem flourishes in itself and the pillars are full of Officer and Killings. In the stems they are visible; in the branches, hidden. Excess throughout the Four Pillars—as one Ding meeting three Ren and many Chen and Zi—is called their mingling. She refuses no one.[^88]

The transmitted example reads **Wuchen / Renchen / Renxu / Guihai / Bingchen / Guihai**. Guihai itself obtains its ground. Wu Earth openly serves as the proper husband, while Chen and Xu conceal secondary husbands. 〈Another:〉 **Gengxu / Wuzi / Yiyou / Jiashen**. Yi takes Geng as visible husband, yet sits upon You and is further drawn to Shen in the hour as hidden husband. Luck travels west through flourishing Metal. In these two examples, visible and hidden husband-stars mingle, and the licentiousness is beyond description.[^89]

〈Another:〉 **Guihai / Renzi / Dingchou / Renyin**. Ding Fire lies wholly amid a multitude of Waters; husbands are many, both visible and hidden, making shameless sexual disorder. 〈The classic says:〉 “Ding encountering excessive Ren must fall into licentious confusion.” 〈Another:〉 **Guimao / Jiazi / Jimao / Yihai**. Ji uses Jia as husband. Jia is defeated at Zi; Mao is the secret husband seated beneath the day. With much Hai and Mao, the visible and hidden mingle. The proper husband cannot assert himself or impose restraint. The secret husband gains power and enters; the proper husband instead withdraws. Infer the other cases accordingly.

### The Eight Configurations: Strengthening the Husband, Injuring the Children — 旺夫傷子
<!-- source: L160-L162 -->
How does a woman strengthen her husband but injure her children? Infer this entirely from the hour, the place of final settlement. Draw the husband- and child-stars into the hour: the husband-star is in Birth and flourishing, while the child-star declines or is defeated. Take 〈one fate〉 **Bingxu / Bingshen / Dingsi / Xinhai**. Ding seated at Si flourishes in itself and takes Ren Water as husband. The hour is the husband-star’s Coming-to-Office ground; the Shen monthly branch is its Birth. Xin Metal serves as Wealth, and Metal flourishes in the seventh month. The two Bing are Companions, each seated upon the husband’s Wealth or Seal. Hence the husband is intelligent, refined, rich, and noble. Ding takes Wu as the offspring influence. Drawn into the hour, it meets Hai, whose Jia Wood controls Wu Earth. The child-star is controlled and children are difficult to obtain. Hence “strengthening the husband, injuring the children.” Infer the other cases accordingly.[^90]

### Strengthening the Children, Injuring the Husband — 旺子傷夫
<!-- source: L164-L166 -->
How does a woman strengthen her children but injure her husband? This method uses the month and hour specifically. What controls me is Officer and husband; possessing qi and obtaining its season, it brings blessing to the husband. But if its stem and branch lose their position, it receives none of the month’s qi, the pillars also clash with or control it, and the hour gives it no flourishing qi, while the child I generate is drawn into Birth, Coming to Office, or Imperial Prosperity in the hour without punishment or control, this is strengthening the children and injuring the husband. Take 〈one fate〉 **Jimao / Jiaxu / Yimao / Wuyin**. Yi uses Geng as husband; in the ninth month Geng Metal lacks qi. Yi uses Bing as child. Bing Fire is born at Yin, which assembles a configuration with Xu, both belonging to Fire. The monthly command provides no Metal qi, and the hour draws Metal to Extinction, where Fire also controls it. This injures the husband-star and strengthens offspring. Hence the name. Infer the other cases accordingly.[^91]

### Injuring the Husband and Controlling the Children — 傷夫尅子
<!-- source: L168-L170 -->
Here the husband-star loses its place in stem and branch and misses its season in the birth month. The pillars also clash with and control it, and the hour’s branch neither generates nor assists it. Repeated Seal-Ribbon further steals the husband’s qi and severely controls the child. Neither husband nor child can flourish; both instead reach Extinction in the hour. Take 〈one fate〉 **Bingzi / Gengzi / Yihai / Bingzi**. Yi Wood uses Geng Metal as husband-star. In the eleventh month, Metal is cold and Water chilly; Metal also dies at Zi. Hai and Zi in the branches drain Metal’s qi completely. There is no Earth in the pillars to generate and assist it, and there is much “Hurting-Officer Wood”; hence injury to the husband. Yi Wood takes Bing Fire as child. Drawn into Zi hour, it reaches flourishing Water’s ground, where Fire is extinguished. Though two Fire stems appear in year and hour, the many Waters control them. Husband and child both perish. Hence “injuring the husband and controlling the children.” Infer the other cases accordingly.[^92]

### Quietly Keeping One’s Station — 安靜守分
<!-- source: L172-L174 -->
The husband-star possesses qi, the day stem flourishes in itself, and the two are balanced without control. There is no punishment or clash, and Wealth and Food occupy their proper places. Take 〈one fate〉 **Guisi / Gengshen / Yimao / Dinghai**. Yi sits on Mao with Exclusive Lu and self-flourishing; Hai in the hour branch also combines into its configuration. Thus the body flourishes. Geng Metal is husband. In the seventh month Geng’s Lu reaches Shen; Si Fire in the year branch is also Metal’s Birth. Thus the husband-star flourishes. Ren Water within Hai is the husband’s Food God and Heaven’s Kitchen, so the husband enjoys Heaven’s emolument. Wife and husband-star do not injure one another; each rides flourishing qi, without mixture or mutual encroachment. This is the configuration of husband and wife living harmoniously and quietly keeping their station.[^93]

### Violent or Untimely Death in Youth — 横夭少年
<!-- source: L176-L178 -->
Violent or untimely death in youth comes when creative transformation is exhausted and the configuration changes abnormally. There may be hanging, drowning, early death through bleeding in childbirth, or killing by another person. Why does this happen? The body is weak and encounters heavy Killings; many Killings control the body while punishment, clash, breaking, and defeat are also present. Or Officer Star is originally present but injured, and luck again enters Officer’s country; or Officer is absent but Hurting Officer present, and luck then reaches Officer. Or an unrestrained Blade travels to ground combining with Blade, with Loss Spirit, Robbery Sha, and similar influences. All these belong to untimely death. They occur not only in women’s fates: men are judged alike.[^94]

Take 〈one fate〉 **Dingmao / Guichou / Gengchen / Bingzi**. Geng uses Ding as Officer. Repeated Gui Water and Zi–Chen Hurting Officer control it too heavily; much Water sinks Metal. On entering DingSi luck, Hurting Officer meets Officer, while Bing Killings also assembles to control the body. Hence death by drowning. 〈Another:〉 **Yiyou / Wuzi / Bingyin / Jihai**. Bing Fire, the day stem, is born at Yin, but winter birth brings excessive Officer and Killings at Hai and Zi. This is called “flourishing Fire cast into abundant Water”; she dies in childbirth. 〈Another:〉 **Renzi / Guimao / Jiaxu / Dingmao**. The monthly command is Yang Blade; Dingmao hour is Hurting Officer and Yang Blade. Zi punishes it and Xu combines with it. There is neither husband-star nor Wealth Star in the pillars. In Guiyou year, Yichou month, Jimao day, she is killed for adultery. In women’s fates, excessive Officer and Killings with an unfeeling Yang Blade give either indiscriminate lust or an ominous death. Infer the other cases accordingly.[^95]

### Blessing and Longevity Both Complete — 福夀兩備
<!-- source: L180-L182 -->
Blessing and longevity both complete arise from balanced harmony in creative transformation and purity in the configuration. Some enjoy their whole lives and receive the enduring gift of freedom from age’s decline. Why? The body sits in flourishing country and communicates with the month’s qi. Stems and branches assist one another; Wealth, Officer, and Seal-Ribbon each occupy their proper place. The course does not strip Wealth, damage Seal, or injure Officer. Food God and Heaven’s Kitchen are especially welcome. A flourishing body traveling through Wealth and Food’s country belongs to this class of fate.

Take 〈one fate〉 **Bingwu / Gengzi / Xinyou / Guisi**. Xin sits upon You with Exclusive Lu and self-flourishing. Gui in the hour returns to Lu at Zi as Food God and Longevity Star: the child-star has its ground. Xin uses Bing Fire as Officer, and Bing’s Lu returns to Si: the husband-star has its ground. Birth in the eleventh month also gives the image of Metal White and Water Clear. Above and below, stems and branches support one another without injury. The body does not follow transformation. Thus she is beautiful and upright in appearance, husband and child are balanced, and blessing and longevity are both complete. Infer the other cases accordingly.[^96]

### Principal and Secondary Wives Finding Their Places — 正偏自處
<!-- source: L184-L186 -->
What does this mean? Husband and wife combine, but Companions also appear to divide and compete. One husband-star combines with two wife-stars: this is **Competing Combination**. If my body flourishes while the other’s declines and the Four Pillars do not clash, I am the principal wife and she the secondary. If she flourishes and I decline, and the Four Pillars clash with me, she is principal and I secondary. My flourishing body possesses qi, so the husband follows me as principal; if my body declines and another position flourishes, he follows that position instead, and I become secondary. Her strength contends for and takes my husband; I can only occupy the secondary place. Excessive self-flourishing with no husband-star in the pillars likewise makes a secondary wife, as do mixed Officer and Killings or excessive Hurting Officer, with licentious and indiscriminate conduct besides.[^97]

Take 〈one fate〉 **Renzi / Bingwu / Xinyou / Xinmao**. Xin uses Bing as husband-star. I sit upon You with Exclusive Lu and self-flourishing. Though Xinmao Metal appears in the hour, she has no strength; hence I am principal and she secondary. This is two women competing for one husband, with principal and secondary finding their own places. 〈Another:〉 **Guiwei / Renxu / Guisi / Renzi**. Gui uses Earth as husband. Guisi Water is weak and Renzi Water strong; weakness cannot overcome strength. Ren Water contends for and takes Wu Earth, the proper husband. She wins and I decline, leaving me only the secondary place. But Ren Water is heavy and floods excessively, also carrying Peach Blossom, so she cannot keep her place. Infer the other cases accordingly.[^98]

### Unsettled in Taking or Joining a Husband — 招嫁不定
<!-- source: L188-L190 -->
How does this arise? A husband-star in the monthly command emerges in the stem and combines with me. My body follows and submits, but that husband-star lacks qi. The hour draws another husband-star or Killings Star onto flourishing ground, where it controls my body; I then follow and submit to the secondary husband. Hence “unsettled in taking or joining a husband.” If the husband-star does not flourish or is restrained, marriage must come late, or the husband’s position is unclear, or he is incapable, or there is an outside attachment.[^99]

Take 〈one fate〉 **Guiyou / Jiazi / Jiwei / Yihai**. Ji uses Jia as husband. Birth in the eleventh month gives him neither the proper season nor strength. Hai in the hour is Jia Wood’s Birth, so the husband does flourish there. Yet Yi Wood also controls Jiwei, and Wei is Yi Wood’s Storehouse: this is unsuitable. Jia born in Zi month is husband seated on Defeat and does not manifest himself. Yihai in the hour contains Jia at Birth, but while seeking Jia she also invites Yi. This is unsettled marriage. Infer the other cases accordingly.

### General Discussion of the Eight Methods and Eight Configurations
<!-- source: L192-L192 -->
The discourse says: In examining a yin fate, first infer the rise and decline of husband and child. To investigate flourishing and decay, next distinguish the relative strength of day and hour. Officer is husband; Wealth is father. Flourishing Wealth honors the husband. Food is child; Seal is mother. Abundant Seal weakens the child. The day stem should not be too strong; the month’s qi must receive balanced harmony. A flourishing, supported Day Master seizes the husband’s authority and suffers solitude; a resting, confined monthly command keeps its proper place and maintains the household. Officer Star obtaining its ground gives splendor to the husband; Hurting Officer free of control gives distinction to the child. Where Officer is present, Killings should not appear; where Killings is present, Officer should not occur. With Officer and Killings mixed, how could the person enjoy auspiciousness?[^100]

Officer Star without control, meeting the Two Virtues, may receive the patent of two states. Seven Killings restrained, meeting the Three Wonders, gives first-rank distinction. Food God is welcome for restraining Killings and generating Wealth; Hurting Officer is hated for controlling the husband and stealing qi. Coveting Wealth and damaging Seal—how could this make a good wife? Employing Killings and meeting Officer makes no chaste woman. Solitary poverty and lowliness arise because the child-star dies, rests, or is confined; towering wealth and distinction arise because husband prospers and child flourishes. Officer too strong makes the father-in-law’s life hard to prolong; repeated Wealth brings the mother-in-law an early death.[^101]

The body occupying flourishing ground may be wealthy, yet husband and child suffer punishment and injury. The day occupying declining country may be poor, yet husband and child remain together and intact. Self-flourishing gives skill in women’s work; a declining day gives clumsiness in needlework. A single noble spirit brings wealth or honor; repeated combining spirits make a nun or courtesan. Noble Person riding Traveling Horse surely indicates a beautiful courtesan amid wind and dust; Officer Star carrying Peach Blossom surely makes a good wife within the secluded courtyard. A single Food God gives peace, children, and longevity; repeated combinations with noble influences give seductive charm, much lowliness, and many attachments. Peach Blossom should not be inserted in reverse; Bath most fears Naked Form. Those offending these often become attendant maids; those meeting them become religious women or nuns.[^102]

The four midseason branches complete make a woman dissipated in drink and sex; the four seasonal beginnings complete make one intelligent and enterprising. Wei–Chou punishment is not avoided, but Xu–Chen clash is unfit. In general, the husband-star should be vigorous and flourishing, and the body should receive balanced harmony. Food God must not be punished or injured, and the child-star should approach Birth. One Seal-Ribbon to generate the body is enough; many Wealth spirits bringing blessing do no harm. Strong Wealth and a weak body cannot develop blessing; a strong body and weak Wealth—how could this make a good woman? Repeated Hurting Officer controls the husband-star and makes a woman who remarries. Repeated Seal-Ribbon makes a woman separated from her husband, whether by death or in life. Punishment and clash with Yang Blade give cruel ignorance; breaking and harm with Metal Spirit give bloodshed and difficult childbirth. ## 7. On Children
### 論小兒

<!-- source: L430-L432 -->
Examining a child’s fate is like cultivating flowers and trees. Skillful nurture makes roots and shoots flourish, flowers and fruit prosper; poor nurture does the opposite. Why? In planting flowers and trees, Earth must cultivate their roots: firm roots make vigorous shoots. Water must irrigate their bodies: strong bodies make abundant flowers. The Sun’s Fire must warm and illuminate the flowers: flowers setting well produce fruit. A Metal blade must prune their branches: clean branches make a firm trunk. But with loose Earth and shallow roots, little Water and withered shoots, scorching sun and burned flowers, or wind destroying fallen fruit, the balanced qi of nurture is lost. How could such flowers and trees not wither?

In a person’s Eight Characters, the year is root, month shoot, day flower, and hour fruit. The same principle applies. In judging a child’s fate, the day stem should possess qi, the monthly command generate and assist it, and roots be established in the year. Seal-Ribbon should be unharmed, Wealth and Officer restrained, Seven Killings transformed, and Hurting Officer combined. Qi should receive balanced harmony without punishment, clash, breaking, or harm. This is a fate easy to rear and long-lived. Heavy Killings and a light body, much Wealth and a weak body, repeated Hurting Officer or Food God, a day stem excessively strong without support or too yielding with little Seal, qi losing balanced harmony, and punishment, clash, breaking, or harm in the pillars make a fate difficult to rear and short-lived. The two cases resemble the methods of cultivation.[^188]

A weak day stem with much Wealth and Officer gives Sha and Pass together and is difficult to rear. What is a Pass? Indirect Officer is Pass; Indirect Wealth is Sha. No one dares cross without an official document; violating it must bring calamity. A child whose fate offends such a Pass is therefore disadvantaged. Someone asks: “When children happen to be born together from one mother, how can their rank and lowliness, flourishing and decay be distinguished?” The answer: Every hour has eight ke and twelve divisions, so earlier and later, shallower and deeper portions bring different good and ill. When one mother bears children in the same hour, distinguish the earlier and later portions and the yin or yang of day and hour. On a yang day and hour, the elder excels; on a yin day and hour, the younger. An early portion receives the preceding hour’s qi, a late portion the following hour’s.[^211]

〈An old song says:〉

> The method of twins has an exceptional gate;  
> To test their flourishing and decline, examine day and branch.  
> On a yin day the younger is strong, the elder weak;  
> At a yang hour the elder is noble, the younger poor.

〈Li Jiuwan says:〉 A child carrying many of the Four Births commonly belongs to twins. 〈The *Shenbai Classic* says:〉 In a yang fate, the later-born dies; in a yin fate, the earlier-born dies. This does not distinguish boys from girls. Another account divides an hour by direction. A Wood fate facing east receives generating qi; facing west receives controlling qi. Rank, lowliness, long life, and early death are distinguished thereby. I have heard that the Wang brothers of Sanhe were twins. The younger passed the examination first, the elder later. Achievement and lifespan were broadly similar, yet the elder ultimately did not equal the younger. The Li brothers of Yingzhou were twins separated by an hour: the younger attained the metropolitan degree, while the elder remained a licentiate. Examination of their Eight Characters showed day and hour to correspond to the preceding account.[^212]

## 8. On the Six Relations
### 論六親

<!-- source: L454-L456 -->
Someone asks: “How are yin and yang paired as husband and wife, forming the Six Relations?” 〈The answer:〉 Jia takes Yi as younger sister and marries her to Geng Metal; Bing takes Ding as younger sister and marries her to Ren Water. Wu pairs Ji with Jia, Geng pairs Xin with Bing, and Ren pairs Gui with Wu. One yin and one yang are paired as husband and wife. After husband and wife come father and child; after father and child come brothers. The Six Relations are parents, siblings, wife, and children.[^213]

The six Jia marry Ji as wife. Jia and Ji combine and produce Geng and Xin as children. A man takes what controls his stem as heirs; a woman takes what her stem generates as offspring. Thus Ji is mother of Geng and Xin, and Geng and Xin are Ji’s children. Geng marries Yi Wood as wife; Yi and Geng combine and produce Bing and Ding. Yi and Geng are therefore Bing and Ding’s parents: Geng father, Yi mother. Hence the yin stem that generates me is mother; the yang stem that I control is father. What controls me is Officer and child; what I control is Wealth and wife; what is of the same kind is brothers and sisters. The yin stem generating my wife is mother-in-law; the yang stem my wife controls is father-in-law. What controls my daughter is son-in-law; Food God is grandchild. The other relations are all derived from the transformations of the ten stems.[^214]
For someone born under any of the six Jia, Gui Water is mother and is Direct Seal; Ji Earth, if encountered, is Direct Wealth. Wu Earth is father—〈Wu combines with Gui〉—and is Indirect Wealth. Companions or Rob Wealth encountered mean injury to the father. Someone born under any of the six Yi likewise takes Gui as mother, though Gui is Indirect Seal, and Wu as father, though Wu is Direct Wealth. Jia and Yi both take Geng and Xin as children. Geng Metal is a son: Seven Killings for Jia, Direct Officer for Yi. Xin Metal is a daughter: Seven Killings for Yi, Direct Officer for Jia.[^215]

Ji Earth is wife and Wu Earth concubine, for Yi Wood controls Ji Earth. Although it is said that yin meeting yin does not make a marital pairing, yin Wood cannot control yang Earth, and a woman properly belongs to yin. Thus Jia and Yi both take Ji as wife and Wu as concubine. In a woman’s chart, Jia encountering Geng Seven Killings and Yi encountering Geng Direct Officer both find the husband-star. Geng is a yang male and the principal husband belongs to yang; yin Xin cannot be taken as husband. Yet yang meeting yang often lacks accord, whereas yin meeting yang gives husband and wife harmonious voices. 〈Another account says:〉 Direct Wealth is wife and Indirect Wealth concubine; for a Jia woman, Xin is the principal husband, following the proper pairing of yin and yang.[^216]

Jia and Yi both take Jia as elder brother or sister and Yi as younger brother or sister. Ding is paternal grandmother: what generates my father is my grandmother, and Ding generates Wu. Ren Water is paternal grandfather, since Ren combines with Ding. Ding Fire is also mother-in-law: what generates my wife is my wife’s mother. Ding generates Ji, who is paired with Jia as principal wife. Ren is father-in-law, since Ren combines with Ding. The wife’s brothers are brothers-in-law. Ji Earth is wife, so Wu Earth is her elder brother, the brother-in-law; Gui Water is that brother-in-law’s wife. Infer the other eight stems by analogy.[^217]

A woman’s assignments differ from a man’s: what I generate is child; what controls me is husband; what generates my husband is mother-in-law; what controls my mother-in-law is father-in-law. The remaining parents and siblings are judged as for a man, but yin and yang must be distinguished. For Jia and Yi stems, Bing is son, Ding daughter, Geng husband, Xin the husband’s sibling, Ji mother-in-law, and Jia father-in-law. 〈Another account says:〉 Food God is son and Hurting Officer daughter, taking the respective generation of yin and yang.[^218]

〈The classic says:〉 “The year is ancestral property; the month, parents, siblings, and the household’s gate; the day, wife, concubines, and oneself; the hour, offspring.” Examine where the stars of parents, siblings, wife, and children sit within the Four Pillars. Judge their good and ill through Flourishing, Assistance, Rest, and Imprisonment.

If the parents’ stars sit at Birth, flourishing, Storehouse, Lu, Horse, or Noble Person, they indicate parents with wealth, distinction, blessing, longevity, and honor. If they sit where Void, punishment, control, sha, Death, and decline converge, they indicate poverty, damage, injury, early death, death away from home, or an unhappy end. Punishment, breaking, or harm, even in Birth, flourishing, or Storehouse ground, indicate parents who live long but remain poor and lowly.

If the siblings’ stars obtain their season and command and sit at Birth, Storehouse, flourishing, Lu, Horse, or Noble Person, they indicate numerous siblings with wealth, distinction, and honor. If they sit in punishment, control, sha, Blade, Death, Extinction, decline, or Defeat, the siblings offer no support. Birth, flourishing, or Storehouse with punishment, clash, breaking, or harm means that siblings exist but are hostile or unhelpful.

If the stars of wife and concubines sit at Birth, flourishing, Storehouse, Lu, Horse, or Noble Person, or something generates them, they indicate wives and concubines with wealth, distinction, beauty, and many abilities. If they sit amid Void, punishment, control, sha, Yang Blade, Death, Extinction, clash, or Defeat, they indicate poverty, unattractive appearance, injury, early death, sexual disorder, disability, death in childbirth, or lack of support. Birth, flourishing, Lu, or Horse with punishment, clash, breaking, or harm gives a long-lived wife, but one with impaired appearance and little wealth. Lu, Horse, Noble Person, or Wealth Storehouse carrying punishment, clash, controlling influences, or sha gives a wife who is rich and noble but dies young.[^219]

If the children’s stars sit at Birth, flourishing, Lu, Horse, Noble Person, Officer, or Seal, with something generating them, they indicate honored, intelligent children, many of whom remain to care for one’s old age. Lu, Horse, or Noble Person at Death and Extinction may give intelligent and talented children, but they do not remain to see one through old age. Birth and flourishing damaged by punishment, clash, breaking, or harm gives children who are foolish, obstinate, or disabled, yet remain to attend one in old age. Death and Extinction with punishment, clash, breaking, harm, or Rob Wealth means no support from children; those who exist are disabled, disfigured, or without ability. In a woman’s chart, children’s stars at Birth and flourishing mean many children; Lu, Horse, and Noble Person give them wealth, distinction, blessing, and longevity. Void, punishment, control, sha, clash, Yang Blade, Death, or Extinction means that they are of no help. If the concubine’s position is at Birth and flourishing, children of a secondary union are favored. 〈The rhapsody says:〉 “In discussing the family, fear its Death and Extinction.” 〈The *Three Fates* says:〉 “Through the Four Pillars observe the nine degrees of kinship; through the Three Origins distinguish the Six Relations.”[^220]

Someone asks: “A Jia or Yi Day Master takes Wu and Gui as parents, but no Gui Water occurs in the Four Pillars’ stems or branches. Only Wu and Ren appear in the stems or lie concealed in the branches. Which, then, are the parents?” 〈The answer:〉 This is what the fundamental classic means by “When an exposed stem is present, take the exposed stem; when it is absent, seek within concealment.” If Gui is absent and only Wu is present, Wu is Jia or Yi’s father: seek the mother through the father. If no mother appears and Ren alone is taken as mother, Wu must still be taken as father. Wu and Ren cannot form a marital pairing. This is no ordinary marriage of young man and woman: it must be marriage during mourning, a mother older than the father, or a remarriage after an earlier union failed.[^221]

Another asks: “For a Jia Day Master, the year and month branches are Yi Wood, while the hour branch is Jia Wood. Yi comes first and Jia later. Which is elder brother, which younger?” 〈The answer:〉 Do not consider sequence: take the stronger as elder and the weaker as younger. In a person’s life, parents stand above, wife and children below, and siblings between. Their connections, separations, and unions are the workings of fate. To discuss fate without the Six Relations is itself a one-sided view. Yet people fail to understand the proper principle and take the five yang stems as mothers, wives, and daughters, the five yin stems as fathers, husbands, and sons. This is profoundly mistaken. The present method teaches that Jia takes Ji as wife, and Yi also takes Ji—not Wu, which is yang. Jia takes Geng as heir, Wu as father, Gui as mother; Yi does likewise. Do not be confined by Direct and Indirect: distinguish yin and yang. Yang male and yin female accord most fully with the principle.[^222]

〈The *Shenbai Classic* says:〉 “A Jia person takes Ding as father and Ren as mother; a Yi person takes Wu as father and Gui as mother.” Infer the remaining eight stems accordingly. This does not use yang male and yin female, but yin generating yin and yang generating yang. What generates me is mother, and the stem combining with mother is father: husband and wife combine before producing a child. For a yang stem, Hurting Officer is father and Direct Seal mother; for a yin stem, Direct Wealth is father and Indirect Seal mother. Seeing father, one develops only without father; seeing mother, one develops only without mother. Control of the year stem is adverse to father; control of the month stem, to mother. Stem ghost is adverse to father; Inverted Food, to mother. Conception crossing and converging with the Four Pillars gives different parents. This account, too, is intelligible.[^223]

〈Another account says:〉 Around the spring and autumn equinoxes, Mao or You in day or hour indicates separation from blood relatives and ancestral property. Xinyou day or hour is called **White Tiger Coming to the Courtyard**: in the day it harms wife, in the hour children, and is adverse to kin. Wushen and Wuyin are called **The Six Ways Dissipating into Emptiness**, adverse to relatives. Renxu day or hour is **Heavenly Empress Losing Her Course**, adverse to wife and children.

〈The *Extensive Record* says:〉 A birth hour of Chen, Xu, Chou, or Wei indicates harm to parents; numerous Robbery Sha, Loss Spirit, Original Time, or Yang Blades do likewise. Two Loss Spirits in day and hour harm mother. Yet Chen, Xu, Chou, or Wei in the hour without malignant sha does not cause such harm. 〈The *Book of Transformations of the Heavenly Origin* says:〉 Birth where the parents’ qi is extinguished or weak must bring injury; many sha indicate separation. This need not occur in the four season-ending hours. 〈*The Foot of Jade* says:〉 Robbery Sha and Yang Blade in the hour can cause injury even outside Chen, Xu, Chou, and Wei: the four corner positions affect mother, the four cardinal positions father. 〈The *Song of the Straight Way* says:〉 “Born in the seasonal endings, turning away from the Sun: surely father dies first.” 〈Huzhongzi says:〉 “The month seated upon Solitude and Emptiness: the brotherly blossoms wither.”[^224]

〈*The Foot of Jade* says:〉 Si, You, and Chou all present and all carrying Xin indicate kin abroad, unable to return for burial. 〈*Guigu’s Bequeathed Writings* says:〉 “The Five Tombs are places of covering and concealment: even a noble hour brings harm. The Four Beginnings are spirits of solitude and extinction: bearing sha, they must injure.” 〈Luoluzi says:〉 “Relatives are like Water and Fire meeting in Bathing’s country; blood relations part midway. Solitary Lodge particularly fears Corner Separation.” 〈An old song says:〉

### Illustrative Discussion of Wives and Concubines — 妻妾引例章

Direct Wealth is wife; Indirect Wealth is concubine. Take a Jia day: Ji is Direct Wealth and therefore principal wife; Wu is Indirect Wealth and therefore secondary wife. If the day stem is vigorous and Ji appears in the Four Pillars, the principal wife obtains her season and flourishing ground. A little Officer Star besides indicates a worthy, intelligent wife, endowed with both talent and beauty, through whom one meets distinction. Seal appearing in year or hour indicates that she brings property and a dowry. If Direct Wealth declines while Indirect Wealth flourishes and emerges, there is an affinity with a secondary wife. If Ji falls into weakness, sits at Death and Extinction, or is born in spring while the day ruler is vigorous—as with Jiayin and similar cases—the indication is failure to sustain the union and harm to the wife.[^226]

If the wife’s influence is born flourishing while the day sits in a declining configuration or at Death and Tomb, the man remains obstructed, is dominated by wife or concubine throughout life, or sees her remarry another. A Jiashen or Jiaxu day born in Jiayin or Yimao month has an excessively flourishing day ruler. Though there is a wife, Companions divide and seize her: she may marry another, be possessed by another, or form a separate attachment. Judge the remaining cases on this model.

### Illustrative Discussion of Offspring — 子息引例章
<!-- source: L462-L464 -->
The heirs are Officer Star. When Officer obtains the seasonal command, the Eight Characters contain no Hurting Officer clashing with or controlling it, and the Day Master itself sits in flourishing ground, children are filial, attend the parent’s end, and bring honor to later generations. Jia and Yi days, for example, take Metal as heirs. Flourishing Metal gives children in the corresponding numbers four and nine. If the Day Master is weak and sits upon Killings, while Officer Star is flourishing and exposed, with Three Punishments, Six Harms, Corner Separation, or a Killings configuration, the children are often unfilial, leave for distant lands, and abandon the family. If the Day Master is excessively strong, sits in Void, and carries Hurting Officer and Defeated Wealth while Officer has no qi, lifelong solitude follows, with no son to conduct the funeral; even children of secondary unions are difficult to obtain.[^227]

Seven Killings in the hour, whether excessively flourishing or excessively restrained, makes children difficult. Officer and Killings intermingled, without a clear removal or retention, may give two kinds of children. Exposed Killings means a daughter first: Killings is a secondary child or a daughter. Examining the charts of scholar-officials with children, I find that much Officer gives children of the principal wife, much Killings children of secondary wives; heavy Killings gives more daughters, heavy Officer more sons. Sons and daughters are also distinguished through stems and branches; repeated occurrences in both give many of each.[^228]

If the hour falls in Void but was originally Officer or Killings, two or three children may still exist. With Hurting Officer, neither does. Wealth or Seal requires a separate judgment: Seal gives daughters, Wealth sons. When Hurting Officer forms a configuration, as in Rat Nobility or Punishment-and-Combination, Officer or Killings in the pillars also indicates children; falling in Void means none. Hurting Officer seated upon Killings—as with a Bing day meeting Jihai hour—also gives children, but not harmonious ones.

To judge the years and luck courses in which children are born: heavy Officer and Killings point to Hurting Officer or Food God; light Officer and Killings to a Wealth year or an Officer-and-Killings year. With light Officer and Killings but heavy Food and Hurting Officer, the year must bring Direct or Indirect Seal. With heavy Officer and Killings and abundant Wealth, one needs Companions, Rob Wealth, Yang Blade, or a year of stem-and-branch combination, Triple Combination, or Six Combination. Apply these living methods in conjunction and none will fail.[^229]

### Illustrative Discussion of Parents — 父母引例章

Father is judged through Indirect Wealth and mother through Seal-Ribbon. Without injury, neither is harmed in one’s youth. A Geng day, for example, takes Jia as father. Another Geng in the pillars, or a Si–You–Chou Metal configuration, injures father; Seven Killings present in the fate prevents the harm. If the day stem is vigorous and Jia occupies Hai, Mao, Wei, or Yin, or the winter season, the parents are harmonious, or father receives an official title. Infer the rest by analogy.

A Wu day takes Ding Fire as mother and fears excessive Direct Wealth in the pillars turning to control Seal-Ribbon: this is the doctrine of Greed for Wealth Ruining Seal. Wealth originally present in both Heaven and Earth, followed by Wealth luck, brings early injury. Only earthly Wealth, with no Wealth course yet reached, brings later injury. A single Direct Officer in the Four Pillars prevents the harm. If a Wu day originally has two Ren, both established in their ground, the mother has two husbands. Direct Seal is mother; Indirect Seal is stepmother or a secondary mother. A fate with both parental influences complete receives a share of ancestral property throughout life, without injury or deprivation.[^230]

### Illustrative Discussion of Siblings — 兄弟引例章

Siblings are Rob Wealth and Companions: Jia meeting Yi, Yi meeting Jia, and the like. A Geng day sitting at Yin, Wu, or Xu, or in Death or Tomb ground, may have a younger brother represented by self-flourishing Xinyou. With Wealth and the season in his favor, the younger brother becomes distinguished in his own right, and the elder does not equal his blessing. Where brothers are harmonious, their stronger and weaker shares are differentiated by the same principle. Disharmony arises when the Four Pillars carry such stems as Geng, Ding, Xin, and Bing: the elder brother’s Officer Star controls the younger brother’s own body. The Five Phases are thus naturally at odds. 〈The fundamental classic says:〉 “Neither benevolence nor righteousness: Geng and Xin cross with Jia and Yi.” This is what it means. Infer the remainder by analogy.[^231]

〈Another account says:〉 In judging a man’s Six Relations, take the year as father, conception as mother, the month as brothers—for an official, as colleagues—the day as oneself, wife, and concubines, and the hour as descendants. For an official, the hour is the Imperial Seat and its good or ill. A man born on a Zi, Wu, Mao, or You day should marry a woman whose fate belongs to Zi, Wu, Mao, or You. A wife born under Shen, Zi, or Chen, under Chou, or under Jia or Ji will not remain with him to the end. After harm to the first wife and remarriage, this restriction does not apply. There are also repeated losses until one marries someone whose birth-day stem and branch differ, when the harm ceases. The Four Beginnings—Yin, Shen, Si, and Hai—and the four seasonal endings—Chen, Xu, Chou, and Wei—follow the same account.[^243]

Within the Four Pillars, what I control is wife. When nothing is controlled by me, this is called **No Wife within the Configuration**. Then examine the ground of the birth day. If it is flourishing Wealth’s country, a helpful wife should exist. With Noble Person, Lu, and Horse besides, the year indicates that the wife brings official standing into the marriage. But if the day falls where the original fate’s Wealth is in Death, Tomb, Extinction, or Defeat, harm to wife or lifelong widowerhood is certain.[^244]

If the day’s Lu appears in the hour, as a Bing day obtaining Guisi hour or a Ren day obtaining Xinhai hour, this is called **Renown Sha**. It indicates not only wealth but office through the wife. If the day carries the year’s Officer or the year’s Seal, the wife takes her husband’s authority, or his emolument comes through her protection, or she belongs to a powerful and noble family—as with consorts of imperial or princely daughters. The day seated upon the fate’s Wealth, in Wealth’s Birth and flourishing, gives the wife’s property and a worthy, intelligent wife. The day seated upon Noble Person gives her a distinguished family, or virtue and beauty.[^245]

If the fate carries punishment of the day, or the day carries clash or breaking of the year, the year’s Yang Blade, Robbery Sha, Six Adversities, Original Time, or Void, one may marry three or four times or have no wife. Self-Blade in the day is called **Day Sha**; this and the hour’s Yang Blade returning to the day both harm wife. Broken-Fragments Sha in the day brings wasting from sexual indulgence, blood disorders, or calamity. Chou days are particularly severe; Jiyou day somewhat slower in effect. The day carrying the year’s Tomb or Direct Seal harms the principal wife, although a favorable Five-Phase sequence is auspicious. The day carrying punishment, harm, clash, breaking, and malign sha, or sitting upon Floating-and-Sinking Sha, commonly means separation from the wife in life or her violent death.[^246]

When the day falls in the fate’s Wealth at Death, Tomb, or Extinction, it harms wife: a Metal fate whose day falls at Wu, Wei, or Shen is an example. Continue to judge through *nayin*. 〈An old poem says:〉

> For a Metal nayin fate, Wood is wife;  
> Wu and Wei are the palaces of her death and burial.  
> Advance one more position: wife is extinguished.  
> If she is not overthrown, injury comes first.

Canopy in the day commonly brings repeated harm to wives; the four season-ending days generally do so. 〈An old poem says:〉

> Canopy in the hour makes the person solitary;  
> If children exist, the year’s arrival must bring death.  
> If the day meets it in the hour, wives are harmed repeatedly;  
> One marries either a prostitute or a nun.[^247]

〈*The Inch of Pearl* says:〉 Canopy in the day indicates a wife who lacks integrity and filiality. Traveling Horse in the day indicates a wife who is often ill, indolent, or solitary. Five-Phase control also indicates injury. Xinyou day harms wife; Guisi day gives illness to both spouses, or indulgence in wine and sexual disorder. Year and day occupying the same position are called **Ruler and Root Sharing a Palace** and harm wife; marrying a woman of the same birth year alone avoids it. Popular usage calls this **Phoenix Pool**. 〈Shen Zhi says:〉 “Brothers sharing a palace, Phoenix Pool: it only leaves the heart ill at ease.” Day and hour clashing, breaking, punishing, or in Six Harms all indicate separation from spouse and offspring. This applies to men and women alike.[^248]

Shen day with Chen hour, Wei day with Hai hour, Yin day with Xu hour, and Chou day with Si hour are all **Well-Railing’s Oblique Clash**, making wife difficult to sustain. Food God added is called **Household-Extinguishing Sha**: many daughters and few sons. Jiachen and Renwu bearing Inverted Food are especially severe. 〈An old rhapsody says:〉 “Well-Railing’s Oblique Clash: Zhuangzi beats the basin and sings.” This is precisely its meaning. Self-punishment in the day makes the wife often ill; Bathing Sha in the day gives a beautiful wife, often lacking integrity.[^249]

For the number of wives, use the day’s *nayin*: Water one, Fire two, Wood three, Metal four, Earth five; in stronger cases, double it. The year clashing with the wife’s position is the year of marriage; wives are seldom encountered in a year forming Triple or Six Combination with the day. Further distinguish the circumstances with discernment.[^250]

〈The rhapsody says:〉 “The day affronts the parental year: an older wife lacks respect.” A Yichou person, for example, encountering a Xin day has the year stem injured; a Gui day inverts the Yi stem’s Food. Wu [午] day is Yi’s Birth, often indicating refusal to submit to the husband. The year is parents and the day wife or concubine; punishment, clash, and sha among the branches therefore give disrespect. But if generation through another stem intervenes, the result differs: Yi seeing Xin with Ren present has Xin Metal generating Ren Water, which generates Yi Wood.[^251]

〈Another says:〉 “Prior harm appears: zither and lute are out of tune.” Six Harms present at birth and met again by the annual progression and major and minor luck are **Prior Harm Appearing**. That year certainly brings disharmony between husband and wife. 〈Another says:〉 “The day branch suddenly stands ahead of Tai Sui: people laugh at the shameful reputation of fearing one’s wife.” The day is wife and concubine. Its branch ahead of Tai Sui means fear of wife or a wife with a forceful temper. Sha reaching the wife’s position usually brings injury; decline in her natal family can avert it.[^252]

〈Another says:〉 “Yin grieves and yang laments: Hai coming upon Zi harms husband. Corners flanking corners: Yin approaching Chou harms wife.” 〈Chen, Xu, Chou, and Wei do not make the Grieving-and-Lamenting Sha. It exists only where yin and yang stand together. A Hai person coming to Zi is yin lamenting its yang, like a wife crying for her husband; hence Hai coming to Zi harms husband. A Zi person obtaining Hai is yang lamenting its yin, like a husband crying for his wife: Zi coming upon Hai harms wife, understood without further explanation. Zi, Wu, Mao, and You do not have Corner Separation and Widow’s Lodge; these arise only at the four corners. A Yin person obtaining Chou has Corner Separation and Widow’s Lodge, adverse to wife; hence “Yin approaching Chou harms wife.” A Chou person obtaining Yin has Corner Separation and Solitary Time, adverse to husband; Chou approaching Yin harms husband, likewise understood without being stated.〉[^253]

<!-- source: L482-L482 -->
〈Another account says:〉 A man born in his wife’s Extinction should not father a daughter: a daughter’s birth brings loss of wife. A Jiazi Metal man, for example, takes Wood as wife; birth in the seventh month occurs at the wife’s Extinction. A woman born in her husband’s Extinction should not bear a son: a son’s birth brings loss of husband. A Jiazi Metal woman takes Fire as husband; birth in the tenth month occurs at the husband’s Extinction. Infer the remaining cases accordingly.[^254]

<!-- source: L484-L488 -->
〈Guihai, Bingyin, Jisi, Yisi, Gengshen, and similar days are **Beating-the-Basin Sha**, feared for the day’s flourishing strength. This is broadly the same as Solitary Luan. Another account calls Extinction’s palace Beating-the-Basin Sha.〉

Metal at Shen or You, Fire at Si or Wu, Water and Earth at Hai or Zi, and Wood at Yin or Mao are called **Looking-Homeward Sha**, disliked for the strength of the fate. This does not differ from Established Lu. Again, Hai, Wei, and Xu in spring; Si, Zi, and Chen in summer; Yin, Mao, and Wu in winter; and Shen, You, and Chou in autumn are called **Disarray Sha**. 〈The *Calendar of a Hundred Prohibitions* calls the first month Great Defeat, the second Disarray, and the third Entering Defeat. A man damages his wife’s family, a woman her husband’s.〉[^255]

<!-- source: L490-L490 -->
Again, those born under Yin, Shen, Si, or Hai in the seventh month; Zi, Wu, Mao, or You in Chou month; and Chen, Xu, Chou, or Wei in Mao month have **Household-Extinguishing Sha**. 〈The *Calendar of a Hundred Prohibitions* says:〉 “Call it upon the month through the twelve branches: a man harms wife, children, and parents; a woman harms husband and parents-in-law.”[^256]

The children’s position is what the wife generates. A Wood-fate man takes Earth as wife; Earth generates Metal, so Metal is child. A yin fate takes what it generates as child: a Wood-fate woman takes Fire. Children’s positions in Flourishing and Assistance give intelligent, loyal, and filial children who illuminate their ancestors. The reverse is otherwise. To know a child’s disposition, infer it through the Five Phases. A Wood fate takes Metal as child: the child is upright, unyielding, and self-willed. Fire takes Water: modest, harmonious, quiet, and unassuming, supporting those above and receiving those below. Earth takes Wood: compassionate, loyal, filial, yielding, and modest, favored in youth rather than old age. Metal takes Fire: greedy and indiscriminate, inwardly insubstantial and false, pursuing profit and advantage, beginning but not finishing. Water takes Earth: slow, composed, upright, and blessed in old age. Judge thus when the respective Five Phases occupy ground with qi; at Rest and Tomb, reverse it.[^257]

A man whose Four Pillars contain no ghost is said to have **No Child within the Configuration**. Examine the ground of the birth hour. A Wood fate takes Metal as child: though the configuration lacks a child, obtaining Shen, You, or Si hour must give children. This is **The Child Receiving Flourishing Qi**. But Metal dying at Zi or extinguished at Yin gives none. For the number of children, use the hour’s *nayin*: Water one, Fire two, Wood three, Metal four, Earth five. With qi and flourishing, double the number; without qi and out of season, reduce it. Some cases do not follow the number: only after fifty are their good and ill settled.[^258]

If a man has No Child within the Configuration and his hour is not in the ghost’s Birth and flourishing, but its stem and branch combine with the year, only daughters are reared successfully; sons do not survive. 〈An old poem says:〉

> Weeping for children: punishment beside Death’s position;  
> Many children, yet they die in youth.  
> Should stems and branches join in Virtue Combination,  
> Only daughters may be reared in pairs.

Take **Guiwei / Guihai / Bingchen / Wuzi**: No Child within the Configuration, also in Officer-and-ghost’s Death and Extinction, but Gui combines with Wu. Thus many daughters are born while sons fail to survive. A Jiazi instead would indicate sons. From the day stem, Bingchen takes Gui as child, drawn into Established Lu and Coming to Office in Zi hour, which should mean many sons; nevertheless, there are none. Thus judging from the year through *nayin* also proves accurate.[^259]

Another chart is **Guiwei / Jiazi / Xinmao / Guisi**: a child exists within the configuration but occupies Death ground. Fortunately, the hour draws Officer-and-ghost into Birth, giving several sons and few daughters. Another is **Gengchen / Renwu / Wuyin / Xinyou**: White Tiger Coming to the Courtyard; the person died at fifty-six without either sons or daughters.[^260]

〈*The Foot of Jade* says:〉 Many Hai indicate sons; many Si, daughters. The hour carrying the year’s clash, punishment, breaking, harm, Blade, Robbery Sha, or Original Time harms children. Heavy year-Void in the hour extinguishes offspring. Self-Blade and Flying Blade harm children; Canopy in the hour also harms them—after fifty, any who appear are insecure. Self-punishment in the hour gives children many illnesses; Six Adversities gives them many troubles; Floating-and-Sinking Sha gives death from diarrhea or dysentery. The hour seated upon the original *nayin*’s Tomb gives long life but no children to tend one’s old age, although grandchildren remain.[^261]

Breaking, Void, punishment, clash, Food, or Blade in the birth day all harm the first child. *Nayin* carrying Extinction while the hour also stands in Extinction’s country includes such hours as Guisi, Renyin, Gengshen, Yihai, and Dingsi. A Gengshen person obtaining Renyin hour, for example, certainly loses succession. Shen day with Hai hour or Si day with Yin hour, also encountered in reverse, is called **Cunning-Harm Sha**. It extinguishes heirs; a child of a secondary union or one adopted into the household is preferable. 〈An old poem says:〉

> Cunning Harm among the Five Phases is most unkind;  
> Husband and wife keep their chamber alone.  
> When they die, no son weeps before the grave;  
> Seeking one of another surname proves fitting.[^262]

〈*Guigu’s Bequeathed Writings* says:〉 A birth hour in which Lu and Horse move back and forth toward the natal fate, without Solitary Time or Widow’s Lodge, also gives descendants. The hour stem controlling the year stem, or acting as Inverted Food to it, indicates sons who are not obedient.

〈*Essential Discussions of the Five Phases* says:〉 Major and minor luck joining the year-fate in Triple or Six Combination indicates the joy of pregnancy and the birth of a son or daughter. More yang gives a boy; more yin a girl. Pure yang, however, reaches its extreme and turns into yin, giving a girl; pure yin reaches its extreme and turns into yang, giving a boy. Suppose a Jiazi fate belongs to yang, and major and minor luck reach Chen or Shen, making Triple Combination, while Xinsi Tai Sui outside them belongs to yin. Yang is then more abundant, giving a boy. Infer the others accordingly.[^263]

## 9. Determining Whether a Pregnant Woman Will Bear a Boy or Girl
### 定婦人孕生男女

<!-- source: L492-L494 -->
〈The instructions say:〉

> Set the parents’ years at the two ends;  
> Take the month of conception for the middle.  
> Qian, Kan, Gen, and Zhen signify a boy;  
> Xun, Li, Kun, and Dui all belong to girls.

Suppose both parents’ ages are even: each gives a broken line. The conception month is odd: it gives an unbroken line. These form Kan, determining a boy. If both parents’ ages are odd, they give unbroken lines, and an even conception month gives a broken line: these form Li, determining a girl. Father’s age is above, mother’s below, and the conception month between. Infer the rest accordingly.[^264]

〈Another method〉 calculates through the Number of the Great Expansion. 〈The instructions say:〉

> Seven sevens: forty-nine.  
> Ask in which month the mother conceived.  
> Subtract the mother’s years:  
> Single is odd, double is even.  
> If odd and even do not correspond,  
> The allotted life will not endure.

First set forty-nine on the counting board, then add the mother’s month of conception. Find the total: conception in the first month makes fifty. If the mother is thirty-one, subtract that, leaving nineteen. Nine is odd, and odd indicates a boy. If an odd result produces a girl or an even result a boy, it indicates early death. 〈One account says:〉 In addition and subtraction, subtract through Heaven one, Earth two, and Human three, then examine the remainder. 〈Another says:〉 Subtract one, subtract two, subtract three; when subtraction is finished, inspect what remains.[^265]

## Translator’s Endnotes

[^01]: **Controlling text and witnesses.** **S** is the repository’s [Chinese juan seven](../source/juan-07.md), exported from [Wikisource revision 2082208](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=2082208); the header records its checksum. **G601**, **G611**, and **G612** are the parallel *Sanming tonghui* extracts in the *Gujin Tushu Jicheng*, Arts volumes [601, revision 1942564](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=1942564), [611, revision 1942574](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=1942574), and [612, revision 1942575](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=1942575). **U** is the shorter, unlabelled [Wikisource juan seven, revision 2334571](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=2334571). Comparisons were made selectively, using the rendered texts retrieved on 26 September 2026. The G volumes transclude separately editable Page texts: pinning a parent-page revision does not freeze every underlying transcription. These are parallel textual witnesses, not three additional Siku editions, and agreement does not prove independent descent. The linked facsimile in the name note was also examined; no comprehensive facsimile collation is claimed. S governs coverage and sequence. All numbering and English subheadings are editorial; the separate Siku preface remains untranslated.

[^02]: **Reading the historical assertions.** 克 can mean technical control between phases and, in predictions, injury to a relative; the latter is not a demonstrated causal power. 身, “body,” often means the Day Master and its capacity to sustain a configuration. 用神 is the influence employed in that analysis, not necessarily a universal lifelong “lucky element.” The text moves between day-stem relationships, birth-year methods, *nayin*, and star/sha systems. Its sex hierarchy, descriptions of appearance and disability, claims about illness, and accusations of sexual misconduct are retained as the source’s assertions, not endorsed as reliable judgments. Its medicinal and pregnancy rules are historical material, not medical advice or a basis for diagnosis, treatment, or reproductive decisions. Unambiguous 巳/已 for 己, and the converse, are read according to context; consequential uncertainties receive separate notes.

[^03]: **Zi and ping; constitution and course.** The first explanation is a cosmological interpretation of the name, not its historical derivation; the compiler subsequently distinguishes the two. Zi is the first branch, associated here with Water; 平 means level or balanced. 坎 means a hollow and also evokes the Water trigram Kan. 先天 and 後天 are rendered “prior constitution” and “subsequent course” because this passage expressly identifies the latter with luck periods. They are not the names of two trigram diagrams here. The steelyard’s 提綱 is its suspension cord, an image behind the technical use of “framework” for the month; 銖兩 are traditional units of weight, not a modern conversion into kilograms.

[^04]: **An unreconciled count.** S and G601 say 三物, “three things,” after enumerating Wealth, Officer, Seal, and Food. All four are translated where listed; “three” is retained where transmitted. Supposing that Food has been subsumed under another class, or changing 三 to 四, would resolve the count only by interpretation or emendation.

[^05]: **Xu Juyi: supported repair.** S first gives 徐君易 and then, in its quotation, 徐居易. G601 reads 居易 at both points, supporting “[Juyi].” Compare the [Page transcription, revision 1898666](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=1898666), and its associated facsimile, *Gujin Tushu Jicheng*, digitized volume 470, image 85. The biography and named retreats are reported from the compiler’s account, not independently certified here. “Ziping” is the courtesy name; the names Shadi and Old Man of Penglai are additional sobriquets. The cited title is transmitted as 濯纓筆記, literally notes on washing the cap-strings.

[^06]: **A history claimed by the source.** The succession from Luoluzi through early diviners to Li Xuzhong and Ziping is the book’s retrospective lineage, not an independently established chronology. S includes a Warring-States attribution, a long list of predecessors, Guan Lu’s 天陽訣, and details about Yixing not all present in G601’s shorter account. They have not been omitted merely because that parallel is shorter. Conversely, the compiler’s suspicion that the attributed rhapsody is a later fabrication is translated without softening. 輅 in Guan Lu’s name is read *Lu*; 李泌 is Li Mi.

[^07]: **Titles and the changing tradition.** The Chinese titles include 五行精紀, 蘭臺妙選, 三車一覽, 應天歌, 三命淵源, 定真論, 繼善篇, and 明通賦. The text distinguishes 淵源 from 淵海, so the translation does not merge them into one normalized title. 元理消息賦 is treated here as the single title ascribed in this passage, without settling its relationship to works elsewhere called 元理賦 or 消息賦. S’s attribution of further elaboration to Yuan writers is retained; G601’s corresponding wording is less clear. The compiler’s argument is that transmission includes revision, not that every later text preserves an unchanged original.

[^08]: **Heart, nature, and feelings.** 心 is the heart as the seat of thought, feeling, and ethical judgment. 性 and 情 are distinguished as nature and disposition/feelings where the prose makes the distinction. The Five Constant Virtues are the source’s ethical correspondences, not psychometric categories. Its grouping of “good and evil” with the virtues is preserved.

[^09]: **Color and bodily description.** 青 spans blue and green; here “blue-green” preserves that range. The complexion colors are part of a correlative physiognomy, not ethnic classifications. The association of bodily form, beauty, illness, and moral worth belongs to that physiognomy and is not a trustworthy way of evaluating people. 行藏 contrasts engagement in public affairs with withdrawal or retirement, not simply walking and hiding.

[^10]: **Water’s contrary qualities.** The same favorable-strength paragraph calls Water both good in disposition and boundless in cunning. Both judgments stand; the translation does not remove the second to construct a consistently favorable personality profile. S’s 吉凶神然 is read as 吉凶神煞 with G611, “auspicious and ominous spirits and sha.”

[^11]: **An explicit disagreement about nayin.** The first account directs the reader to day-and-hour *nayin*; the next explicitly rejects *nayin* and uses the original phases. These are not editorial inconsistencies to be silently harmonized. The following contrast between noble and lowly configurations likewise makes the alleged disposition depend on the overall classification, not simply a phase’s strength.

[^12]: **A phase’s own Extinction.** 自絶 refers to a phase or *nayin* occupying its own Extinction position. The five ethical failings repeat the five correspondences stated earlier. Death and Extinction are technical stages here, not assertions that an actual person has died. The claim that some such placements incline people toward Chan or the Dao is the cited author’s interpretation.

[^13]: **Six Voids and false speech.** 六虛 is retained as the name of a technical grouping. Huzhongzi links it to 謾語神, the Spirit of False Speech. This paragraph supplies no calculation table for locating the Six Voids, and none is added. The prediction about elders and migration is translated as part of the quotation’s explanation.

[^14]: **Historical medicine, not a diagnostic manual.** This section translates a correlative medical theory and its prognoses, including treatments now unsafe to infer from a chart. Disease names do not map consistently onto present-day diagnoses. “Wind,” “cold-damage,” “gu,” “wasting,” “qi,” and “ghost” retain their historical categories. The violent predictions and associations between disability and astrological configurations are the source’s claims; neither disability nor illness establishes moral failings. No treatment here should be used in place of qualified medical assessment.

[^15]: **Bowels, viscera, and the two additional correspondences.** 臟 and 腑 are rendered “viscera” and “bowels,” the traditional paired categories. 三焦 is the Triple Burner, and 包絡 the Enveloping Network associated with the heart. The poem lodges these additional correspondences in Ren and Gui without replacing bladder and kidneys. “Kidneys’ store” reflects 藏 as well as completing the line; no new anatomical compartment is intended.

[^16]: **A different anatomical distribution.** The branch poem should not be silently forced to agree with the stem poem. S and G611 have 胃腕 at Wei: “stomach, wrists” is retained, though 胃脘, “stomach region,” is a plausible unconfirmed repair. 命門 is the named Gate of Life; 腎囊, literally kidney-sac, refers in this vocabulary to the genital/scrotal region rather than a modern renal capsule. Qibo and Leigong function here as exemplary medical authorities; the closing line praises the method through their names.

[^17]: **Several maps coexist.** The next branch arrangement places Wu at the head and Zi at the genitals, whereas the preceding poem assigned a larger set of correspondences. The trigram map is another scheme again. They are all translated, not combined into one supposedly uniform anatomical table. 陰 in the last branch line denotes the private parts, not the abstract polarity alone.

[^18]: **Functional spheres.** The text links organs, body regions, symptoms, stems, branches, and trigrams. “Liver’s sphere,” and the corresponding expressions for other viscera, render 肝家 and similar phrases without implying that every listed symptom proves disease of that anatomical organ. The statement assigning stems to bowels and branches to viscera is retained even though the preceding detailed lists include both classes.

[^19]: **Avoiding false modern precision.** 霍亂 is rendered as sudden vomiting and diarrhea, not automatically as the modern infectious disease called cholera. 癆瘵 is wasting illness, not a confirmed diagnosis of tuberculosis. 疝氣, retained as shan-qi, covers the source’s category of lower abdominal or genital afflictions and should not invariably be narrowed to hernia. 白濁, “white turbidity,” and 白帶, “white discharge,” remain distinct. G611, like S, transmits 喊 after coughing; “cries” preserves that uncertain item rather than supplying an unattested wheezing diagnosis.

[^20]: **Fright-wind and wind-strike.** 急慢驚風 distinguishes acute and chronic forms of the historical childhood fright/convulsion category; 中風 is wind-strike. Neither is silently equated with one modern neurological diagnosis. The clauses on miscarriage and a child’s complexion are translated as prognoses within the historical system, not reliable consequences of a natal arrangement.

[^21]: **Dry blood: a variant.** S reads 乾血氣, “dry blood-qi”; G611 has 乾血癆, “dry-blood wasting.” The main translation retains S rather than merging the two. 痘疹 and 疥癬 are rendered as eruptions, scabies, and ring-shaped eruptions without declaring which pathogen or modern skin condition is present.

[^22]: **Gu-distention and childhood categories.** 蠱脹 is left as gu-distention; its historical causal category is not replaced by a diagnosis of poisoning or a particular parasitic disease. 五疳 and 五軟 are named groups of childhood disorders: the five gan disorders and five flaccidities. The source supplies neither their individual definitions nor a treatment scheme here, so none is inserted into the translation.

[^23]: **Spirits and childbirth.** 魍魎失魂 is literally an affliction involving baleful spirits and loss of the soul; translating it does not recast a psychiatric or neurological condition as proven spirit possession. 血產 is kept as bleeding in childbirth rather than a precise obstetric diagnosis. The “internal/external” division follows the source’s ordering, not a modern clinical distinction.

[^24]: **Ghost intercourse and ghost-fetus.** 鬼交 and 鬼胎 are historical expressions retained as such, not evidence of a supernatural pregnancy or assault. 偏墜 is one-sided downward dragging, a symptom term rather than a confirmed anatomical lesion. S and G611 have 夜間作炒, literally an unsuitable “stir-frying” character in the nighttime clause; “disturbance at night” understands 炒 contextually as the sound-alike 吵, a minor conjectural reading rather than a newly attested variant.

[^25]: **Planetary names within phase language.** 熒惑, Yinghuo, is the traditional name associated with Mars and Fire; 太白, Taibai, with Venus and Metal. The passage uses these names within its phase correspondences. It does not provide celestial longitudes from which an actual planetary configuration could be reconstructed. “Soul’s downfall before weapons” keeps the martial force of 兵前落魄.

[^26]: **The treatment passage is retained, not recommended.** The text assigns needles, moxa, pills, powders, decoctions, minerals, directions, and even a physician’s surname to phase relations. These are historical prescriptions without a safe clinical basis supplied here; the translation does not endorse them. 徵姓 means a surname classified under the zhi tone and its Fire correspondence, not a physician literally surnamed “Zhi.” 金石 are mineral substances, not a recommendation to ingest metals. The rules are not harmonized where, for example, Fire ghost is first distinguished from moxa and later from a decoction.

[^27]: **Four examinations and six pulses.** 望聞問切 denotes inspection, listening/smelling, inquiry, and palpation of the pulse; 聞 includes smelling as well as hearing in this medical expression. 標本 contrasts manifestation and root. Later 六脈, the six pulses, belongs to that historical pulse framework. The comparison with phase calculation is the author’s argument, not verification that a birth chart diagnoses disease.

[^28]: **A Water/Fire variant left visible.** S says 木下藏金無水收, Metal beneath Wood without Water to gather it; G611 reads 無火救, without Fire to rescue. The main translation keeps S’s Water, and records the alternative rather than silently replacing the reasoning. “Qi rising upward” renders 氣高; it is not a diagnosis of a particular respiratory condition.

[^29]: **Punctuation controls the sense.** The unpunctuated sequence assigns dizziness to strong Yi/weak Xin, sores to strong Ding/weak Gui, fullness to excessive Ji, and wasting weakness to insufficient Xin. Some punctuation in G611 breaks these pairings after the wrong clause. The translation follows S’s parallel syntax instead of inheriting that punctuation. Minor graphic 上 for 土 is understood as Earth where the opposition clearly requires it.

[^30]: **Turtle-back and medical exempla.** 腰駝 and 龜背 are bodily descriptions, not a modern diagnostic classification. 軒岐 and 盧扁 invoke exemplary medical authorities. Fengdu, Mount Dai, rods, beams, and weapons elsewhere in these passages belong to the source’s imagery of death and its prognostic vocabulary, not instructions for causing harm.

[^31]: **Weak versus short arms.** S has 臂虛, weak arms; G611 has 臂短, short arms. S is retained. 鱉頭 is literally a soft-shelled-turtle head and is translated as a shape comparison rather than assigned a modern disorder. The day-pair lists and the differentiations between childhood and later life remain as transmitted; no chart-based medical inference is validated by their inclusion.

[^32]: **Needles, tattoos, and absolute claims.** The associations of Yang Blade and Suspended Needle include lancing, cautery, and facial tattooing. These are interpreted bodily events in the text, not recommendations. The final claim of never missing a case is translated in full, but does not establish diagnostic accuracy. The same applies to the lists predicting death from particular combinations.

[^33]: **Measures of grain.** 升 and 合, sheng and ge, are volume measures here, evoking the meager food sought each day; they are not a promotion and an astrological combination. 天財 is kept as Heavenly Wealth, the transmitted technical name. The last stanza’s strong Fire and resting/confined pillars are not reconciled by changing either adjective.

[^34]: **A quoted medical maxim.** The sequence 東方實西方虛瀉南方補北方 and the subsequent explanations are the author’s cited *Inner Classic* account. The translation preserves the directions and their phase relations. It does not turn “supplement the north” into a clinical instruction, and it does not supply a missing treatment protocol.

[^35]: **Both excess and deficiency can be harmful.** This qualification is essential: the author expressly rejects the view that illness arises from deficiency alone. His examples likewise allow both generation and control to produce illness. “Balanced harmony” renders 中和 without reducing it to equal numerical quantities of all five phases.

[^36]: **Four control pairs, not a completed table.** This paragraph gives Metal–Fire, Earth–Wood, Metal–Wood, and Water–Earth. It does not separately give Fire–Water here, and its Water–Earth entry does not repeat the Birth/Death subdivision. The translation does not add either missing symmetry.

[^37]: **Generation is not automatically favorable.** All five generative pairings appear. S and G611 both ascribe deficiency and little flesh to flourishing Metal–Earth generation. That counterintuitive wording is retained, not rewritten to make every instance of generation beneficial. “Intermittent fevers” renders 痁瘧 without diagnosing modern malaria.

[^38]: **Mao Wood “within Zi.”** S and G611 both transmit 刑起子中卯木為煞. The wording appears to place Mao Wood within Zi, which is not an ordinary hidden-stem assignment. The translation quotes the troublesome phrase rather than quietly removing 子中 or inventing a lost rule. It may be a damaged or compressed account of Zi–Mao punishment, but the consulted parallel does not establish a repair.

[^39]: **An important exception.** 傷官傷盡 refers to the complete removal or neutralization of Officer in the Hurting-Officer configuration, not to total physical injury. The author explicitly excludes that configuration from the general disease rule just given. This is another reason not to isolate a sentence about “Hurting Officer” as an unconditional medical claim.

[^40]: **Preserved compressed formulas.** The stanza-like prose beginning 申中無氣 and 乙丙有刑 is transmitted in both S and G611. Not every stem pairing matches a standard modern punishment table, and no such table has been used to rewrite it. The references to hearing music and distinguishing colors keep the source’s sensory imagery.

[^41]: **Named influences and an essential qualification.** 劫煞, 官符, 咸池, 大耗, 飛廉, and 亡神 are inherited sha names, not diagnoses. S’s unusual graph in 天𥌒 is supported by G611’s 天瞽, Heavenly Blindness. The final clause expressly requires a body-controlling sha before attributing illness from eating to Lu; it is not a blanket rule against Lu.

[^42]: **The pairs are retained in order.** The listed Jiachen/Jiaxu, Bingshen/Dingyou, and other pairs are described through their interacting stem and branch phases. They should not all be interpreted as organ assignments from the pairs’ *nayin*. 真衝 and 真刑 are kept as True Clash and True Punishment, the transmitted technical expressions.

[^43]: **A title displaced by commentary markup.** S divides 燭〈神經曰〉 across the small-character delimiters. G611 gives the full title 燭神經, supporting *Classic of Illuminating the Spirits*. 風癩 is rendered wind-sores rather than asserted to be a particular modern infection. 丹田 is the Cinnabar Field, a traditional bodily locus. All eighteen verse lines, including the repeated Water/Fire warning, are retained.

[^44]: **Life and Illness Palaces.** 命宮 and 疾厄宮 belong to the passage’s palace-based vocabulary. They have not been replaced by the day or hour pillars. No complete method of calculating the Illness Palace appears here. 白虎閉目, White Tiger Closing Its Eyes, is the title attached to Xinmao, not a literal celestial event.

[^45]: **The source identifies the influence.** Its own gloss equates 月陰 with 月煞 and supplies Yin–Wu–Xu meeting Mao. 陰命 is retained as “yin fate,” in a passage that immediately discusses women’s discharge and men’s bowel afflictions. The moral explanation of menstrual disorder is historical judgment, not a medical cause established by the translation.

[^46]: **The three older reference points.** Lu, Fate, and Body are the three named classes used in this paragraph; the passage does not simply say to count three modern Ten Gods. It further proposes looking at a relative’s chart for indications of illness or death. That method is translated, not offered as a reliable means of assessing a parent’s or spouse’s health.

[^47]: **Alternatives, not one composite chart.** The long paragraph lists different possible configurations, introduced repeatedly by 或. They should not be combined into a single checklist that every poor fate must satisfy. Heavenly Center is the Void-related name used in the source. “Nine fates” is retained without inventing nine additional natal pillars or a definition absent here.

[^48]: **Jiashen restored from the parallel.** S’s opening example begins 甲甲得丁己巳卯己巳, a malformed string. G611 has 甲申得丁巳己卯己巳, supporting “[Jiashen] obtaining Dingsi, Jimao, and Jisi.” The next example supplies a base Jiazi plus four further pairs; all are retained rather than forcing the string into four pillars. “One Direction Before and Behind” is named but not fully explained by either passage.

[^49]: **The range of 凶惡.** The heading joins adverse outcomes and moral wickedness. “Misfortune and wickedness” keeps both senses rather than making every negative configuration a claim about criminal character alone. S and G611 both have 兩木, “two Woods,” in the Officer-Talisman clause; it is retained, not conjecturally changed to two “roots.”

[^50]: **The rescue clauses qualify the judgments.** Established Lu and Food God mitigate the poverty configurations, while Noble Person, the Three Wonders, Canopy, and Flanking Nobility mitigate the ominous ones. These qualifications belong to the text itself. 三才, the Three Powers, retains its cosmological sense rather than being replaced by a modern three-factor scoring system.

[^51]: **Conflicting rules and an overlapping boundary.** S first says Death qi in the hour does not harm strong month and day, then limits such a life to forty-five. G611 preserves the same tension. The stated domains are month 1–30, day 31–45, hour 45–100: forty-five overlaps, and is not silently changed to forty-six. These are historical predictions, not dependable estimates of anyone’s life expectancy.

[^52]: **Major and minor luck.** 二運 refers to the two luck courses, read with the annual influence Tai Sui. The following 月日時 can be read as the months, days, and hours of manifestation, rather than three new natal pillars. The translation does not convert these general instructions into a calendar of predicted deaths.

[^53]: **The obscure “gentle illness.”** S and G611 both have 少者則死於善疾. 善疾, rendered in quotation marks, is unusual; a proposed 急疾, “sudden illness,” would be conjecture without support from these witnesses. The explanation that flourishing qi may prolong an elder’s suffering is preserved even though flourishing elsewhere signifies health and longevity. 破屍 means damage to the corpse and is not reduced to a peaceful death.

[^54]: **Birth-year nayin, not the modern Day Master.** The Guihai/Dingsi and Guichou/Bingchen examples explicitly compare the year’s *nayin* with the month’s *nayin*. Their phase relations are Water controlled by Earth and Wood controlling Earth. The title is shortened to 命鈴 in S here, whereas G611 gives 三命鈴. The different Lu/Fate life-cycle references are not collapsed into the day stem’s twelve stages.

[^55]: **Heavenly Longevity: a supported title repair.** S has 夭壽朝元, literally Early Death and Longevity Turning toward the Origin; G611 has 天壽朝元, Heavenly Longevity Turning toward the Origin. The translation follows G611’s 天. The example’s “true Water” concerns transformed phase relationships and is not the ordinary Fire identity of Bing. No contradiction is resolved merely by replacing all such phrases with ordinary stem phases.

[^56]: **Breaking and Establishing the Fate.** 破命 and 建命 are technical expressions in the quoted tradition, not simply destruction and creation of a person’s life. 裸形 and 夾煞 likewise retain their names. Fengdu and the office of Mount Dai are underworld images; 魂 and 魄 distinguish the heavenly and earthly soul in the translation without attempting a full theory of the soul from this short quotation.

[^57]: **The source’s own explanation remains partial.** The gloss names Jiazi/Jiawu rounds with no Water and Jiashen/Jiayin rounds with no Metal. Its ensuing “two occurrences” plus a completing annual or luck occurrence is not fully explained. The translation retains that count, and does not invent a third or fourth natal requirement. The claim about Yan Hui is an attributed prognostic illustration, not independent historical evidence for his chart.

[^58]: **Not Established Lu.** 建命 is not 建祿. The former is illustrated here by Zi and Chou encounters and given a nourishing qualification. The translation therefore keeps “Establishing the Fate” distinct from “Established Lu,” the monthly configuration discussed in earlier juan.

[^59]: **A marked gap.** The source itself inserts 原本無字, “the original lacks a character,” between 兼 and 死絶之氣. “[Carry]” makes the English construction readable while the transmitted gap notice remains visible. This is supplied syntax, not a recovered Chinese character. The alternatives involving early and late development are all retained.

[^60]: **Family imagery for technical relations.** Longevity Star is Food God. The stepmother represents the Indirect-Seal/Owl relation that can take its Food, and the favored concubine represents the rescuing Indirect Wealth. These are the source’s metaphors, not statements that a real stepmother endangers a child. The image of withered grass in frost remains in the verse.

[^61]: **The subsidiary pair list is essential.** Each branch group is accompanied by specific stem–branch pairs in the original small commentary; all are preserved. Their *nayin* relationships matter, so the prohibitions must not be generalized from the branch names alone. “Ghost Gate” and “Xiao Pass” are the text’s metaphors of dangerous passage; the translation supplies no geographical journey as a remedy.

[^62]: **The explanatory label belongs to the source.** S connects this additional stanza with the lesser ghost-sha and Ghost-Pass Sha. 受氣 is the receiving of qi, not necessarily the twelve-stage position Embryo in every occurrence. The three preceding phase-and-branch lists and this further stanza are distinct statements, not one reconstructed calculation.

[^63]: **The compiler disputes the automatic prognosis.** His observation that day–hour opposition often accompanies harm to relatives rather than an early death is part of the source’s own commentary. It must not disappear into the surrounding fatalistic verses. 衝而不破 distinguishes a clash from an effective breaking; the author requires the whole chart to be examined.

[^64]: **Sixteen and the first birthday are not reconciled.** 二八 is twice eight, conventionally sixteen, in the verse; the attached example says death before a completed year. Both appear in S and G611. The example is not rewritten as death at sixteen, nor is the verse’s number replaced by one.

[^65]: **Animal names encode branches.** Pig/Rat, Dog/Ox, Rooster/Tiger, Dragon/Goat, and Snake/Rabbit encode Hai/Zi, Xu/Chou, You/Yin, Chen/Wei, and Si/Mao. The concluding Si/Wu is retained even though it overlaps one named branch. The gloss explicitly allows escape through control of the sha: this qualification belongs to the transmitted rule.

[^66]: **Ding restored; a name variant retained.** S reads 丙子亥子, Bing–Zi–Hai–Zi; G611 reads 丙丁亥子, supporting “[Ding].” The resulting paired-stem sequence agrees with the rest of the stanza. S subsequently calls the influence 天鬼截路空, whereas G611 has 天鬼截路煞. The translation preserves S’s final “Void,” recording rather than silently erasing the variant.

[^67]: **A compressed stage list.** The line 金人沐浴火木胎 is parsed as Metal at Bathing, Fire and Wood at Embryo. It is followed by Earth at Death and Water at Tomb. Neither witness supplies a branch-by-branch derivation here, and the translation does not invent one. The imagery is a summons from the underworld, not an ordinary messenger’s timetable.

[^68]: **An unresolved number in the second stanza.** S has 五百年前, “five hundred years before”; G611 has 五日年前, an equally troublesome sequence involving “five days” and “before the year.” S’s wording is retained as a predestination-like expression, not treated as a usable age formula. The two glosses clearly distinguish the next branch ahead from the preceding branch behind; those relationships are secure even though the number is not.

[^69]: **Only the supplied periods are given.** S divides the lunar month into days 1–15 and 16–30, then gives Wood beginning on day 6 and completing on 10, Fire on 16–20, and Water on 26–30. It gives no matching Metal or Earth interval here. The example’s two Earths are the Ji stems; it does not imply that the year-pillar Jihai has Earth nayin.

[^70]: **Direction differs in the parallel.** The verse instructs forward counting. S’s gloss specifies forward movement for men but gives no direction for women; G611 explicitly has women count backward to the sixth position. The main text preserves S’s silence, while this note records the variant. The condition that both luck courses lack qi is essential to the stated adverse outcome.

[^71]: **A puzzling generative explanation remains.** S and G611 link the prohibitions to “generating Fire and generating Metal.” This does not form a transparent, uniform account of all the listed phase encounters. The words are translated rather than changed to match a modern generation table. 鶴髮, crane-white hair, is the literary image of advanced age.

[^72]: **A qualification grounded in circumstances and conduct.** The conclusion distinguishes the parents’ contribution, subsequent illness and hardship, and deliberate care of oneself. It does not reduce every death to natal configuration. 修身以俟之 means cultivating oneself while awaiting what comes; it is not a promise that cultivation cancels mortality or a prescription for treating disease.

[^73]: **The historical unit of judgment is the household.** Honor is repeatedly measured through a husband’s office, a son’s success, and titles conferred on their female relatives. 褒寵誥封 denotes official commendation and investiture, not a modern professional qualification. The passage’s unequal gender assumptions remain visible, but its chart classifications are not evidence of an actual woman’s character, conduct, fertility, or worth.

[^74]: **Plurality is not identical with mixture.** The opening expressly permits two Officers without Killings, or exclusively Killings without Officer. Later condemnations of “many husbands” therefore cannot simply replace this qualification. The husband-star and child-star are technical relational categories; their number is not an observed count of spouses or children.

[^75]: **The reference changes within the example.** After identifying Bing as Xin’s husband, the text treats Gui as Bing’s Officer and Wu as Bing’s Food. It then returns to Xin generating Ren as child. These are successive relational analyses, not all Ten Gods of the original Xin Day Master. “Both return to Lu at Si” is transmitted as written; Gui itself is not silently assigned Si as its ordinary Lu.

[^76]: **A transmitted stage inconsistency.** S and G611 give Gui husband “Coming to Office at Hai,” whereas other passages give Gui’s Lu at Zi. The translation retains the example rather than changing Gui into Ren. The later Jia-as-Officer relationship is reckoned from Ji child, not from the woman’s Bing day stem.

[^77]: **Two charts and shifting generations.** In the first chart, Jia husband takes Xin as Officer; in the second, Ren husband takes Ji as Officer and Ji child takes Jia. The text does not always restrict its child-star to the same-polarity Food God. The first chart’s Metal born at Si uses a phase-level Birth correspondence alongside other stem-specific judgments.

[^78]: **Clear means unmixed, not necessarily weak.** 清 concerns freedom from competing or disruptive influences. The example explicitly uses prosperous Wealth to support Officer. Ding flourishing at Wei is retained without redefining every 旺 as the named stage Imperial Prosperity. G611 adds a two-state title and expands the following example; those additions are not imported into S.

[^79]: **The source’s compressed ending.** S says the child “sits upon Wealth and meets Killings and Seal.” G611 instead invokes Dragon Pool and Phoenix Pavilion, followed by an explanation of those indicators. The main translation follows S’s shorter account; the alternative is a substantive expansion, not just punctuation.

[^80]: **Two meanings of the Two Virtues.** Here the text expressly extends 二德 to Wealth and Officer; elsewhere it specifically means Heavenly and Monthly Virtue. Neither definition should displace the other. The patent “of two states” is an honorific designation in this discourse, not territorial sovereignty. Yao and Shun serve as exemplars of supreme virtue.

[^81]: **Officer, child, and Lu are relationally reassigned.** Gui is first Bing husband’s Officer and then Xin mother’s child. The phrase 財官雙美 recalls the Guisi configuration treated in juan six. “Two patents” belongs to the narrative of husband and child both attaining distinction; the source supplies no independent documentary record of the investitures.

[^82]: **Turbidity combines physical and moral imagery.** Water mixed with Earth becomes the image for conflicting husbands and a decline in standing. The stated chart has Ji, not the preferred Wu husband, and its Yi Food controls Ji. These relations explain the author’s terminology without validating the sexual or social accusations drawn from them.

[^83]: **Visible and hidden do not have one strict meaning here.** Bing is called the proper or manifest husband although it is concealed within Yin rather than exposed in a stem. Ding lies within two Wei positions. The text’s contrast is retained, not rewritten into a modern rule requiring every “visible husband” to be a written heavenly stem.

[^84]: **A separate category from Licentious.** 濫 is explained through 婪, greed, and linked with gain through multiple attachments; 淫 is subsequently explained as overflowing. “Indiscriminate” preserves that distinction. The hierarchical categories of servant, concubine, and wife are the source’s historical classifications, not interchangeable modern occupations or identities.

[^85]: **The chart sequence is preserved.** The examples beginning Jiyou and Jiachen are two separate fates, and the concluding “both” refers to them. 二陽, “two yang,” belongs to the seasonal explanation of Chou and advancing Fire; it is not a claim that the chart has exactly two yang stems. No extra partners are inferred beyond the source’s assertions.

[^86]: **Occupational accusations and a conflicting Kuigang label.** 娼 and 妓 are translated explicitly, without treating their predicted occupation as an established fact. S calls Wuchen Kuigang here, despite the four-day list in juan six giving Wuxu rather than Wuchen. This inconsistency is retained. The judgment also draws on the husband-star’s state, not merely the presence of Food God.

[^87]: **Two repairs of different certainty.** S has 戊用巳夫, “Wu uses Ji as husband”; G611 has 戊用乙夫, supporting “[Yi].” Both transmit 戌日 later where the chart and syntax call for 戊日, “[Wu] day”; that second repair is conjectural. The claim that Yi is extinguished at Shen is preserved rather than harmonized with another stage scheme. 娼/妓 overlap semantically; the contrasting English labels do not establish two sharply separated historical professions.

[^88]: **Overflowing.** S explains 淫 through 沃, while G611 has 泆, overflow or excess. “Overflowing” conveys the comparison text’s clearer explanation and the ensuing account of excess. It is a gloss on the term, not a finding about the sexuality of people with strong natal stems.

[^89]: **A six-pair sequence is not silently made into a chart.** S and G611 both transmit 戊辰、壬辰、壬戌、癸亥、丙辰、癸亥 before the analysis of Guihai. The translation prints all six pairs and calls it a transmitted example, not a valid six-pillar birth chart. Deleting two pairs or rearranging them into separate charts would require evidence not established here.

[^90]: **The hour is the destination for two separate stars.** Ren husband reaches Lu at Hai; Wu child is opposed by Jia there. The prose thus evaluates the same hour differently according to the relation being assessed. “Harm to children” does not mean an intentional act by the mother, and the chart is not evidence of such harm.

[^91]: **Birth of the child-star, Extinction of the husband-star.** The example draws Bing and Geng into Yin hour and reads their different stages. The seasonal statement that ninth-month Geng has no qi is the author’s judgment in this example, not a universal exclusion of all Metal strength in Xu month.

[^92]: **“Hurting-Officer Wood” is left unresolved.** S has 傷官木多; G611 transmits the same phrase. Wood is not Yi’s ordinary Hurting Officer. It might be assessed from another reference, or 木 may be corrupt, but the comparison does not establish a repair. Quotation marks make the difficulty visible without inventing a lost explanatory clause.

[^93]: **Flourishing is explicitly favorable here.** Both wife and husband are strong, each supported without mutual injury. The example qualifies the later sweeping preference for a weak female day stem. 守分 is keeping one’s assigned station within the source’s household order; the translation does not turn it into a universally desirable limitation on women’s agency.

[^94]: **The author includes men.** The paragraph expressly extends the same types of violent or early death to male charts. 血產 denotes childbirth involving blood or bleeding in this vocabulary; it is not a confirmed modern obstetric diagnosis. The predictions are retained as textual content, not validated risk assessments.

[^95]: **Narrative reports, not independently verified cases.** All three charts and the last example’s year, month, and day are retained. 犯姦殺死 states killing in connection with adultery; it does not establish the truth of the allegation or identify who killed the woman. The translation adds neither a legal proceeding nor an assailant absent from the source.

[^96]: **Strong without following transformation.** The example explicitly says Xin does not surrender its identity through transformation. Bing husband and Gui child are separately drawn to their Lu positions, producing the author’s account of balance. The phrase 金白水清 recalls the configuration discussed in juan six; it is not a modern description of bodily chemistry.

[^97]: **Rank within a polygynous household.** 正 and 偏 distinguish principal and secondary wives or concubines. “Contending to combine” concerns two wife-influences seeking one husband-influence. The prose assigns rank through relative strength and injury; it does not describe equal-status contemporary marriage or establish that any real spouse is replaceable.

[^98]: **“I” is the day ruler’s position.** In the first example the two Xin are contrasted through You and Mao. In the second, Gui and Ren compete in relation to Earth. The first-person wording preserves the source’s reference point rather than making the reader personally the subject of a prediction.

[^99]: **Two directions of marriage.** 招 can mean bringing a husband into the household, whereas 嫁 is marrying out. The heading encompasses unsettled marital affiliation; it is not simply hesitation before a wedding. The explanation subsequently distinguishes a seasonally weak proper husband-star from a stronger rival influence.

[^100]: **The general account recapitulates, rather than replaces, the examples.** Its preference for a moderate female day stem sits beside earlier favorable strong-body charts. 傷官無剋 means Hurting Officer free from restraint, assessed here as a child-producing influence, whereas other clauses dislike it for injuring husband. The different relations must remain separate.

[^101]: **Gong and po.** 公 and 婆 in this women’s-chart discussion are father-in-law and mother-in-law, not a duke and an old woman in general. The relation is derived through the husband’s kin. The sentence is not silently reassigned to the woman’s own father and mother.

[^102]: **The same indicator can be judged differently with Officer.** The contrast between Noble Person with Horse and Officer with Peach Blossom is explicit. The source does not condemn every Peach Blossom encounter equally. “Wind and dust” is the conventional image for a life outside the sheltered household, here specifically courtesan life.

[^103]: **One support versus excess.** The prose permits one Seal and abundant undamaged Wealth, then warns against repeated Seal and a body too weak to sustain Wealth. These are conditional judgments, not a simple ranking of good and bad stars. 續室 is a wife who succeeds an earlier wife, distinguished from the secondary household position 偏房.

[^104]: **Images of separation and investiture.** Golden crown and brocade signify conferred status; broken mirror and parted hairpin signify marital separation. 玉筯, jade chopsticks, is an image of paired trails of tears. “Walking the moon” evokes examination success, not a literal nocturnal journey. These images are retained rather than flattened into a list of predictions.

[^105]: **The direct and inverted Seals are not harmonized.** The text makes Owl harmful in one configuration and useful in another. Cold orchid steps, a flourishing jade tree, the luan terrace, and the mandarin-duck curtain give the passage its domestic and literary register; they are not additional chart positions to calculate.

[^106]: **Flowers and guests.** Flowers withering in childbirth and leaves falling in spring are figurative deaths within the rhapsody. “Welcoming guests” in the surrounding sexual imagery implies entertaining clients. The Five Killings are not enumerated here; no five-item list has been supplied from another system.

[^107]: **Beauty and literary talent are separate exempla.** Consort Yang evokes famed beauty; Lady Xie evokes learned eloquence. The later willow-catkin allusion points particularly toward Xie Daoyun: compare the snow conversation in [*Shishuo xinyu*, “Speech”](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/世說新語/言語). These are retrospective literary comparisons, not birth charts independently known for the women named.

[^108]: **Do not literalize every number.** Five Horses, six Wealths, eight Officers, and seven Killings occur in a compressed parallel passage. The text does not give an algorithm for counting all of them within four natal pillars. “Seven Killings” is itself a technical name, not seven separate partners or adverse events.

[^109]: **A riddle-word for husband.** 藁砧, literally a straw-covered chopping block, is a literary substitute for husband through the word 鈇 and its resemblance in sound to 夫. Compare the explanation under “藁砧” in [*Changtan kaowu*, juan four](https://ctext.org/wiki.pl?chapter=4969038&if=en). The image is retained with its human referent made clear, not treated as a household object that the chart predicts will break.

[^110]: **Violent moral exempla.** The eye and arm passages invoke the historical ideal of bodily sacrifice to preserve chastity. They are not instructions or acceptable tests of virtue. The source names Empress Lü and Lüzhu but does not identify the women behind the following two images; the translation does not invent names or securely attested charts for them.

[^111]: **The commentary defines its own terms.** Here “noble” means Officer or Killings, and the Two Virtues are expressly Heavenly and Monthly Virtue. The definition differs from the earlier expansion to Wealth and Officer. All the small-character explanation is retained, including its gendered argument for weakness; it should not be taken as the translator’s endorsement.

[^112]: **Exceptions to the ominous names.** The four configurations cited in the gloss include Following Killings, Exclusive Lu with Food, Solitary Luan with Killings, and Peach Blossom with Officer. Their favorable treatment is an essential counterweight to the surrounding prohibitions. “Making the unfeeling possess feeling” describes a beneficial chain of phase relations.

[^113]: **Zidu is not another female chart-holder.** Zidu and Xizi are exemplars of beauty, paired rhetorically; the passage does not supply their charts. Compare the praise of Zidu’s appearance in [*Mengzi*, “Gaozi I”](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/孟子/全覽). The elder and younger Qiao are likewise literary names for exceptional beauty. The gender of an exemplar must not be used to alter the chart formula.

[^114]: **The source already records a variant.** The main line gives Gengyin and Wuyin, with its own alternate Wushen; the commentary then uses Wushen. Both are retained. The four days are not replaced wholesale with a modern normalized list. Mixed Qi in the second half is favorable because it supplies the relevant husband, child, and Wealth influences together.

[^115]: **An explicitly conditional warning.** The gloss makes the absence of all Officer and Killings the important restriction, and permits support to turn Solitary Luan or Yin–Yang Error favorable. 寡鵠, Widowed Swan, is identified with Solitary Luan by the source. 寒衾少怨 is transmitted with 少, “few”; a possible youthful-widow reading is not silently substituted.

[^116]: **Support changes the outcome.** The gloss explains “rich husband” as Officer carrying Wealth, which can restrain an excess of Seal. A strong “sister” is another stem of the woman’s kind, not necessarily a biographical sister. The unexplained 坑婢命, a fate that ruins a maidservant, is retained rather than replaced by a more familiar configuration name.

[^117]: **The examples are preserved, including the damaged gloss.** S’s second chart is Bingshen/Gengzi/Wuxu/Bingchen. Its ensuing 月丁庚食 is graphically confused; the translation reads the evident Geng Food in the month, controlled by the hour’s Bing. This is a contextual repair, not a new chart. The obstetric claims are historical interpretations, not reliable warnings or reassurances for a pregnancy.

[^118]: **Two passages clarified by G611.** S’s 天睢狀 is supported as 夫雖壯 by G611: the Indirect Officer is Wu’s husband, “although strong.” Later S gives 酉又五年至胎始壽, while G611 has 酉下五年甲胎為壽: the latter five years of You, with Jia at Embryo as life-source. The translation follows these clearer readings explicitly. The changes do not validate the prognostic method, and the distinct deaths of husband and woman are not conflated.

[^119]: **Three Wealths, three reference points.** The source defines them as what the year stem, year branch, and nayin respectively control. These must not be reduced to three appearances of the day stem’s Wealth. Dengming and Taiyi denote Hai and Si in the accompanying gloss; the text’s explanation in terms of approaching night is retained, not silently repaired into a conventional clock-time account.

[^120]: **Different definitions coexist.** Yin Blade is placed one branch behind Lu, distinct from the earlier definition of Yang Blade. Pure Yin is described both through all-yin stems and branches and through the half-year between the fifth and eleventh months. These are alternative symbolic criteria, not identical astronomical situations. The source itself equates Bone-Marrow Break with White-Clad Sha and Pillow-Offering with Cap and Belt.

[^121]: **A supported repair and two exclusions.** S’s 鴛鴦憚於女未 is obscure; G611 reads 鴛鴦憚於見水, “Mandarin Ducks fear encountering Water,” followed by a gloss about abundant Water. The main text adopts that reading in brackets. S has three branches and one stem in the next definition, whereas G611 has three branches and three stems; S’s count remains. The exceptions for Three Wonders and Void are translated in full.

[^122]: **The double signs are not silently modernized.** 雙魚 and 雙女, Double Fish and Double Maiden, are called licentious stars here. The passage supplies no positions or calculation rules, so the translation does not identify a modern zodiac chart or import a Western astrological interpretation. Yellow Springs and the separated mandarin pair are images of death and parting.

[^123]: **Kuigang is not invariably condemned.** This account credits it with adaptive cleverness and gives Day Nobility settled good fortune, even though other passages regard Kuigang as dangerous in women’s charts. The contrast remains part of the compilation’s plurality. “Heavy Seal” and “Exclusive Food” likewise receive different kinship judgments rather than a single fixed moral value.

[^124]: **Four animal-day names.** Metal Pig, Wood Tiger, Earth Monkey, and Fire Snake abbreviate stem-phase and branch pairings rather than four zoological images. The small commentary distinguishes the first pair’s supposed chastity from the second pair’s lack of it. No missing full list of sixty combinations is inferred from these four expressions.

[^125]: **The passage selects the monthly branch.** Its first criterion is not merely seeing Wealth, Officer, or Seal somewhere in the chart, but taking what is usable from the month. The three discussions retain their respective restrictions. Their Three Wonders are functional Wealth/Officer/Seal, not necessarily a sequence such as Jia/Wu/Geng.

[^126]: **The fifteen are not listed here.** The source rejects fifteen further configurations by broad association with Hurting Officer, Killings, Blade, Established Lu, and remote combinations, but does not enumerate fifteen titles. None has been reconstructed. The later favorable treatment of some of those configurations is retained rather than removed to make the chapter agree with this general prohibition.

[^127]: **A polarity rule explicitly different from the later kinship account.** Here same-polarity generation gives a son and opposite-polarity generation a daughter. The subsequent Six Relations discussion instead sometimes assigns all yang stems to males and all yin stems to females. Both systems are translated. The six-Ren/Renyin example is transmitted even though a standard day–hour stem calculation raises a separate difficulty.

[^128]: **A single restrained Killings is not sexual mixture.** The text expressly denies an automatic charge of sexual disorder in this case, while retaining its claim about authority over the husband. Seasonal decline, a missing support, and mixture are separate criteria. The translation does not extend the favorable exception to every occurrence of Killings.

[^129]: **Conditional within the source, not clinical reassurance.** Both the threatening configuration and the stated protective alternative belong to the original paragraph. A chart with Two Virtues cannot establish that childbirth is safe, just as Owl and Blade cannot establish that it is dangerous. The complete historical argument is preserved without supplying a treatment or prediction for a living person.

[^130]: **Two Ji positions, not two different people known independently.** The analysis identifies the day’s Jimao with the principal woman and the month’s stronger Jisi with her rival. Jia husband combines with Ji in the month. The domestic story is inferred within the source’s system; the supplied chart does not itself document a historical concubine’s conduct.

[^131]: **An extreme formulation preserved alongside its qualifications.** This account favors even Death and Extinction over flourishing and rejects Noble Person and Horse. Later prose explicitly warns against excessive Death and Extinction and welcomes those same favorable influences. The translation keeps the contradictory accounts rather than smoothing them into a uniform doctrine.

[^132]: **Two definitions are actually given.** The text first describes one stem with two combining partners, then two different stem pairs and corresponding branch pairs. Both are called Double-Mandarin-Duck Combination. Peach-Blossom Horse and Peach-Blossom Sha at Coming to Office are also distinguished from the Salty-Pool rule illustrated by Si–You–Chou meeting Wu.

[^133]: **Literary imagery and uncertain segmentation.** S gives 月籠華髮 and 空傷合; the translation preserves the moon-veiled hair image and reads Void, Hurting Officer, and combination as separate influences in the latter. 華髮 may also evoke gray hair, and 空 could be adverbial, “vainly.” The verse does not securely resolve these alternatives. Its broad condemnation is not allowed to erase the favorable Peach-Blossom examples elsewhere.

[^134]: **The husband’s chart enters the argument.** The first obstetric statement is expressly derived from a husband’s fate; the text then applies the analogous pattern to the woman. 朝元羊刃, Yang Blade Turning toward the Origin, uses the supplied Mao/Jia and Chen/Yi relationships. It is not simply another name for every Blade already present in a day pillar.

[^135]: **Conception is included as a separate reference.** The account assigns year to father-in-law/father, conception to mother-in-law/mother, month to sisters-in-law, day to husband and self, and hour to descendants. Its marital matching rules differ from a blanket preference for combinations. The translation preserves both the same-group recommendation and the contrary warning about combining partners.

[^136]: **Lack of attachment can be praised.** 無情 can mean absence of reciprocal attachment or entanglement among influences, not necessarily human cruelty. This author values separation of their functions in certain women’s charts. 榮神, Glory Spirit, is defined seasonally in the text and is not substituted for an unrelated Noble Person calculation.

[^137]: **Full or empty: the disagreement is explicit.** The passage first requires a substantial Storehouse and later quotes the opposite preference for an empty one. Both are translated. 奴婢宮, Servants Palace, belongs to palace-based analysis; no modern natal pillar is assigned to it. The prediction of beating a servant to death is a historical claim, not condoned behavior.

[^138]: **The second verse uses nayin.** A Metal nayin fate takes Fire as husband; this is not necessarily a Metal day stem’s relationship. The Xu/Hai Death-and-Extinction sequence follows that reference. An absent or ineffective husband-star is an interpretive condition in the text, not proof that a woman has no spouse.

[^139]: **Several classifications, not one scale.** 帶甲 names the repeated Jia condition, especially Jiawu year and day; 承旺 names a different list of days receiving flourishing. The fractions of blessing are the author’s qualitative rankings, not percentages from a measured scoring system. The alternative rankings of Wuwu, Guiyou, Xinmao, Bingzi, and Gengzi are all retained.

[^140]: **Keep the seasonal and hour conditions together.** Four birth-branch groups are each matched with a season and an hour. Dropping the season would produce a different rule. The source’s Eight Specialists, Nine Uglies, Ten Evils and Great Defeat, and Flowing-Water Sha are separate inherited classifications; their names do not establish the moral accusations attached to them.

[^141]: **The source itself separates fierceness from lust.** One arrangement is described as violent and contentious with kin, yet explicitly chaste. It is not translated as another example of sexual misconduct. 福在日時 explains how a woman deemed lowly may later gain honor through a child; this is a claim about the timing and location of blessing within the historical model.

[^142]: **Independent livelihood is disparaged in the original.** Market work, brokerage, medicine-selling, mediumship, and matchmaking are placed within its low-status rhetoric. The translation does not silently replace these occupations with prostitution or treat the author’s social judgment as a modern assessment of those who perform them.

[^143]: **Cord imagery and the number eight.** 子掛綆生 literally evokes a child born hanging by a cord; the translation does not supply a modern diagnosis. The abrupt statement that eight is yin’s final number is retained without inventing an omitted numerical derivation linking it to Great Depletion. Such numerology is not a basis for obstetric decisions.

[^144]: **Parity rules are stated at different levels.** All-yang and all-yin charts are judged by reversal at an extreme, whereas the sex of a first child is then assigned from the hour stem. The text also draws birth-order and twin judgments from branch categories. These are separate historical rules, not a reliable way to determine fetal sex or the number of children.

[^145]: **Numbers without a fully specified conversion.** The text gives one-, two-, and three-year intervals, then the phase numbers Water 1, Fire 2, Wood 3, Metal 4, Earth 5. It does not explain how every number yields an interval; none has been supplied. S’s 伏寅日 is literally “Repeated Yin day,” probably affected by the nearby 伏吟, Repeated Chant, but the main text retains the difficulty rather than treating a repair as certain.

[^146]: **The final prose corrects excess in both directions.** Too much strength threatens yielding; too much Death and Extinction is also rejected. Blessing located in the woman’s natal family is distinguished from blessing through husband or children. This passage is not equivalent to the earlier absolute instruction to prefer Death and Extinction.

[^147]: **Noble Person and Horse can be favorable here.** Their positive treatment in day and hour conflicts with earlier blanket warnings. The distinction is preserved rather than explained away by an invented condition. The concluding contrast between male and female strength is the source’s yin–yang argument, not an empirical claim about capacities or rights.

[^148]: **The collection restates earlier rules in verse.** The first three stanzas distinguish the three favorable classes, unsupported Hurting Officer, and husband/combination conditions. They are not abbreviated because prose has already covered similar ground. Later verses modify several of these formulations, so the collection should be read as a sequence of voices rather than a single set of axioms.

[^149]: **The exceptional configurations are named, not redefined.** Flying Heavenly Lu and Horse and Well-Railing Fork refer back to earlier juan. The stanza’s unfavorable judgment for women and its Wealth exception are both retained. The text does not give an entirely separate female calculation for locating either configuration here.

[^150]: **Star language, not purple-colored timber.** 紫、木、太陽 are read as Purple, Wood, and Sun in the astronomical register also used later in 紫木羅陽. They are not one Wood-phase adjective. The verse gives no positions or ephemeris for reconstructing these influences; the appended gloss returns to Lu, Horse, Noble Person, and the birth hour.

[^151]: **The small commentary expands the abbreviations.** The verse’s brief names stand for Loss Spirit, Robbery Sha, Solitary Time, Widow’s Lodge, Corner Separation, Level Head, Double Time, Canopy, Six Harms, and Three Punishments. The transmitted gloss, not an invented ten-star calculation, supplies that identification. Its loneliness is then expressed through incense, curtains, and moonlit bedding.

[^152]: **The star that bathes the sun.** 洗日星 is the poem’s epithet for Salty Pool. It is retained as a literary name, not converted into a modern physical account of the Sun. The contrasting claims about men’s acquaintances and women’s monks are the stanza’s own unequal judgments.

[^153]: **A direct exception to the sexual accusation.** The verse expressly calls the woman upright and not adulterous. Its attached chart compares the nayin of Dinghai Earth and Yimao Water; the basis of the interpretation is not the ordinary phase of the Ding stem. The husband’s alleged gambling and dissipation remain a reported example rather than a general conclusion about Salty Pool.

[^154]: **An incomplete example stays incomplete.** After the full Guiyou/Jiwei/Bingwu/Gengyin chart, S gives only 甲戌癸卯日者. The two pair names are retained as a fragment, not padded out with an invented month and hour. “He himself” in the first example follows 自, marking the switch to a male subject within this women’s verse collection.

[^155]: **Bei is retained as a name.** 孛 belongs to the text’s star vocabulary, not to the ten heavenly stems. The verse does not specify its location or motion, so no observed comet or modern planet is supplied. 同音 refers to the shared tonal/phase association in this context, not an instruction merely to compare pronunciation.

[^156]: **Shen and Shang.** 參商 evokes two stars that do not meet, hence estrangement. It is not the branch Shen 申 followed by a missing branch. “Bowing again before a mother-in-law” implies another marriage, rather than a ritual remedy performed for the first wife’s mother.

[^157]: **A renewed string means remarriage.** 朱絃再續 carries the domestic image of restoring a broken string. S’s 寶月修真 is less transparent; the translation retains the precious-moon and cultivating-the-true imagery instead of inventing a named ritual or a recoverable biographical event. The attached example is explicitly marked as remarriage.

[^158]: **An allusion to Consort Yang’s violent end.** Mawei Slope, the powder and paint, and the preceding Yang imagery belong to the poem’s courtly allusion, not a geographical prediction. 蕭牆 is the household’s screen-wall and evokes trouble arising from within. 羅紋 and 倒戈 retain their net-like pattern and reversed-spear images rather than being turned into a medical mechanism.

[^159]: **Ten lines, with a separate gloss.** S runs this long verse continuously; it has been divided at its seven-character lines. The closing commentary identifies the complete Zi–Wu–Mao–You group. Master Cui and the sleeping Consort Yang are literary comparisons; the source does not supply enough detail to identify the former securely, and no given name has been invented.

[^160]: **A proverb-like count, followed by examples.** The one/two/three Noble Persons sequence is preserved even though other passages qualify such judgments by strength, Void, and supporting influences. It is not converted into a quantitative probability. The two attached charts and the distinction between a courtesan and an unmarried childless woman remain separate.

[^161]: **A recognizable poetic rendezvous.** 期我桑中 echoes the invitation among the mulberries in [*Shijing*, “Sangzhong”](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/詩經/桑中). Dengming and Taiyi stand for Hai and Si. 服內 refers to the period of mourning; it is not merely an encounter while wearing ordinary clothes. The allegation in the example is the source’s, not independently established biography.

[^162]: **Four star names.** 紫木羅陽 is segmented as Purple, Wood, Luo, and Sun, consistent with the earlier 紫木太陽. Luo is left in transliteration because this passage supplies no calculation or full name; it is not made into a new heavenly stem. The claim of conferred titles remains part of the verse’s prognostic rhetoric.

[^163]: **Two literary allusions, not two stars.** 卷耳情懷 invokes the longing of [*Shijing*, “Juan’er”](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/詩經/卷耳); 柳絮才 invokes the celebrated comparison of falling snow with willow catkins in [*Shishuo xinyu*, “Speech”](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/世說新語/言語). “Gathering Cocklebur” is a conventional title-level rendering, not a botanical determination. 紫木 here has the same star-name segmentation as in the preceding verses.

[^164]: **Gifted offspring.** 鳳毛 is a phoenix feather, a metaphor for an exceptional descendant; 岐 and 嶷 praise early perceptiveness and stature. They are translated as qualities, not personal names. The poem praises the hour’s Noble Person, Lu, and Horse without adding a new formula for computing them.

[^165]: **The outstanding one among brothers.** 白眉 means the distinguished child, not a predicted congenital marking. S has 乾生, rendered yang Birth, but supplies no additional explanation; a conjecture that this names a specific trigram-based procedure would go beyond the text.

[^166]: **Plainness rather than clever calculation.** 嘍囉 evokes quick, bustling cleverness, while 頑福 is solid, unshowy good fortune. A thousand jin gives the image weight, not a measurable quantity of blessing. The stanza intentionally praises a slow and sincere nature rather than the ingenuity admired elsewhere.

[^167]: **The exception is part of the evidence offered.** Heavy Seal is not automatically childlessness: the commentary gives Wealth restraining Owl and preserving Food. The second example changes the hour to Renwu and reports five sons rather than seven, with a different marital outcome. It is not silently treated as the same complete chart.

[^168]: **The day lists and glosses are all retained.** Several stanzas enumerate days with a seated husband-star, then warn against mixing in another husband influence. The list uses its own broad 官/煞 vocabulary. 恭淑 is the honorific language of the verse; the translation does not identify a specific legal patent class beyond what the text provides.

[^169]: **An explicit rejection of indiscriminate judgment.** The gloss says not to call every Solitary-Luan chart childless and particularly favors an Officer-supported yin day. The ensuing warning concerns competing Companions in a strong-body course. Both the exception and the later condition are necessary to the argument.

[^170]: **“As at first” is an internal reference.** Hurting Officer appearing again restores the earlier adverse condition after Wealth had supplied support; it does not mean a literal return to a woman’s first pregnancy. This relative sequence is retained rather than recast as a universal chronology of childbirth.

[^171]: **The commentary limits its own generalization.** It distinguishes timely Hurting Officer from Food God, permits Seal in the latter case, but rejects excess. The chart Guiwei/Yimao/Gengzi/Gengchen specifically employs Wealth with Hurting Officer and reports a titled woman with one child. Neither the favorable outcome nor the qualification is omitted.

[^172]: **A favorable description of Hurting Officer.** The commentary praises alert judgment and calls its subject a “man among women.” That gendered compliment is the author’s wording. Its presence prevents the translation from making every occurrence of Hurting Officer uniformly foolish, vicious, or passive.

[^173]: **Purity of the relation is the stated distinction.** A single, unmixed Killings receives a favorable beauty judgment; mixture receives the reverse. These are correlative physiognomic assertions, not an objective relation between appearance and moral character. The Metal/Water language is not a statement about a person’s measured mineral or fluid composition.

[^174]: **Expand the verse through its own gloss.** The compressed 陽肩墓 is explained by the commentary as Yang Blade, Companions, and Hurting Officer entering the Tomb, alongside Death and Extinction of Wealth and Killings. The translation preserves the explanation instead of creating a new star called “Yang Shoulder Tomb.”

[^175]: **One sha, contrasting relations.** The commentary expressly says that Peach Blossom’s encounters with Officer and Killings differ greatly. It also distinguishes having no support from having a supported configuration. These qualifications are retained without accepting the accompanying sexual accusation as evidence about living people.

[^176]: **The reference shifts to the husband.** Ji takes Jia as husband; Jia then takes Bing as Food and offspring. The child is therefore derived through the husband in this example, not simply as Ji’s own output. Azure Dragon’s blessing position is named but not fully calculated here.

[^177]: **Yi need not be a mistaken character.** S’s 乙夫 can be read as a Yi husband: for a Wu woman, Geng Food combines with Yi Officer. G611 instead transmits 巳夫, possibly 己夫, “one’s own husband.” Since both can be made intelligible but the evidence is not decisive, the translation retains S’s Yi and records the alternative rather than silently normalizing it.

[^178]: **A day-pair in the verse, a stem in the gloss.** The verse has 丙子, Bingzi; its commentary explains Bing’s injury to Geng and Yi’s competing combination. Bingzi is retained instead of deleting Zi as redundant. The eastward Yi warning is likewise kept alongside the westward Metal preference.

[^179]: **Two appended charts are distinct.** Xinwei/Jiawu/Jiwei/Jiaxu is identified as a prince’s consort; Bingwu/Xinchou/Jiwei/Jiazi is followed by a son’s metropolitan success and a daughter’s princely marriage. The charts are not merged, and those statements are reported as the source’s examples rather than independently checked genealogies.

[^180]: **Natal presence and arrival through luck differ.** The gloss first discusses injury to an existing Xin husband, then allows a favorable Xin course when no Xin was originally present. Preserving this distinction matters more than reducing the passage to “Bing is always bad for a Jia woman.”

[^181]: **The reference is Gui’s support.** Xin Metal supports Gui Water; Wood and Fire drain or exhaust the Water configuration. The final 傷見則非 is compressed and has been rendered closely, not expanded into a missing fully specified Officer/Hurting-Officer rule.

[^182]: **Wu and Xu in the gloss.** S writes 戌 in two places where the poem’s 合戊 and the explanation of Gui’s husband require 戊, Wu Earth. The translation normalizes these contextually; it is a graphic repair, not a change to the chart. The passage’s qualification concerning travel south or to Shen is preserved without turning it into an unconditional favorable direction.

[^183]: **Repeated Food treated as Hurting Officer.** The gloss explicitly treats Yin and Jia together as excessive Food and hence as Hurting Officer, while allowing either alone. This is not a claim that Jia’s ordinary relationship to both Ren and Gui is identical. The four-son example and its patent are retained as transmitted.

[^184]: **Hidden and exposed Fire differ.** The stanza favors the seasonal presence of Fire without its excessive exposure in the stems. The commentary names both mixed Officer/Killings and two Bing competing for one Xin. These are different mechanisms within its argument, not interchangeable names for one condition.

[^185]: **Distinction does not guarantee longevity.** The first chart receives an explicitly short lifespan despite its husband, children, and patent. That qualification remains. The second is said to resemble it in investiture and children; the translation does not automatically transfer the first woman’s lifespan to the second.

[^186]: **The final couplet governs how the catalogue is read.** The song closes by subordinating minor spirits and sha to a sound Officer-and-Seal configuration. That limitation is part of the poem, not a modern editorial addition. Its favorable list also includes configurations rejected in another account earlier in this juan; both versions remain.

[^187]: **A second summary in five-character lines.** The whole song is retained, including the changing Noble-Person counts, Storehouse instructions, and objections to repeated stems and branches. The recommendation belongs to a historical text addressing a prospective husband, not present-day advice to judge a partner from a chart. “Worth a thousand gold” is rhetorical valuation, not a price.

[^188]: **The cultivation analogy is substantive.** Roots, shoots, flowers, and fruit correspond to year, month, day, and hour. Earth, Water, sunlight/Fire, and pruning/Metal all contribute; no one phase is universally preferred. The text’s 易養 and 難養 concern its prediction of survival and development, not a judgment that an infant’s temperament is easy or difficult for parents.

[^189]: **A Pass is a dangerous checkpoint.** 關 is explained through the need for an official travel document, with unharmed Seal serving as the corresponding protection. The passage calls Indirect Officer the Pass and Indirect Wealth the sha; it does not merely equate both with any evil star. 生成之數 are generating and completing numbers, not an infant’s measured developmental score.

[^190]: **The source acknowledges frequent failures.** Its caution that many Pass predictions do not correspond to events precedes the formulas and must not be omitted. Ziping’s Killings-based method is distinguished from the Three-Fates and Five-Planet traditions. 無財 begins the contrasting case without Wealth supporting Killings; it is not read as a description of the preceding Wu-Earth example, which does contain Wealth.

[^191]: **Inconsistent ages are not repaired by guesswork.** The first list gives Bing encountering Ren a one-year Pass; the explanation later gives one and a half years. Gui encountering Ji is assigned a half-year Pass. G612 preserves these difficulties. The rule also restricts this particular calculation to original natal placements, excluding later arrivals in luck or the annual influence.

[^192]: **Names are retained; absent procedures are not invented.** The author lists nine Pass or Arrow names and says their rules may be sought in the 百中經, but declines to reproduce them because they often fail. Completing that list with outside calculation tables would alter the scope and argumentative force of the source.

[^193]: **Unusual round names and an irregular age scheme.** The formula supplies groups named by Jiazi, Renzi, Wuzi, Gengzi, and Bingzi. These are not the usual six Jia-headed ten-day rounds; the transmitted names are retained rather than silently replaced. Its Xinwei illustration counts correctly from Shen to Mao in eight positions. The palace scheme then singles out the ninth with a special age limit. Life-chart palaces are not automatically the four natal pillars.

[^194]: **The Lesser Pass is incomplete as transmitted.** The birth-branch grouping omits an explicit Shen-year case, and the age clause 六九不過三百日三五週同十二宮 is obscure. The main text preserves its numbers in quotation marks rather than presenting a newly constructed table of ages. Comparing G612 does not yield a secure complete repair.

[^195]: **The transmitted Chou pairing remains.** The list gives Chou–Wei, whereas a familiar directional Three-Punishments sequence might lead a reader to expect another assignment. It also includes the four self-punishments. The translation does not replace this particular Pass list with a standardized general punishment table.

[^196]: **Do not lose the direction within paired months.** After listing two branches for each pair of months, the source specifies Si for the first month and Hai for the seventh. That clarification is retained; the paired entries should not automatically be treated as two equal alternatives for each month. Solitary Time/Widow’s Lodge and the subsequent Six-Clashes rule remain separate.

[^197]: **The limitations immediately follow the fatal prognosis.** A strong birth month, an extinguished ghost, or the specified parental correspondence can mitigate the Pass. The clause about parents’ Four Pillars is compressed; the translation makes its apparent overlap sense readable without supplying a detailed compatibility algorithm. Hour nayin, not the bare hour branch alone, controls the opening Ghost-Pass example.

[^198]: **An incomplete computational direction is acknowledged.** S says 卻向日上起子 after reaching the birth-month position; the English takes the position reached as the place to establish Zi, but the wording does not fully explain 日上. No missing day-count step has been invented. The compiler then says the foregoing rules do not all verify, qualifying the preceding assertion of certainty.

[^199]: **A separate annual progression.** This rule starts one group at Yin and the other at Shen, advancing or retreating one branch per year. It is not the ten-year major luck calculation. The named danger positions Chen, Si, Xu, and Hai are retained, not recalculated from a modern calendar or applied to any living child.

[^200]: **Five categories, not the preceding twelve-palace list.** Life, Wealth, Illness, Wife, and Blessing are the five positions named in this separate star-chart method, used through age fifteen. The translation does not silently substitute the standard twelve-house order or supply omitted details of how each year is located.

[^201]: **Weather is a separate historical technique.** All five natal phases and their cloud, sunshine, wind, rain, or snow conditions are retained in the following two paragraphs. These are not climate statistics or reliable lifespan estimates. 陰天, 昏暗, and 晴朗 distinguish overcast, dark, and clear conditions rather than being reduced to a single day/night distinction.

[^202]: **Symptoms and personality claims remain distinct.** The source calls repeated Wu/Wei or Xu obstinate, and separately predicts shan-qi, sores, speech timing, and head shape. These are not validated pediatric correlations. 金水火相剋制 combines three phases in the wording and should not be silently rewritten into just one pairwise relation.

[^203]: **Birth away from father is the stated mitigating condition.** 背父生 is not itself translated as father’s death. The paragraph separately predicts which parent may be harmed, gives a different Chen/Xu versus Chou/Wei rule, and adds conception/year conditions. The alternatives are preserved without deriving a single uniform parental-mortality table.

[^204]: **Several household relationships.** 過房 is transfer into another household or family branch; 寄生 evokes rearing away from the birth household; 螟蛉 is an adopted child. 嫡 and 庶 distinguish principal and secondary maternal status. These historical categories should not be collapsed into a modern claim of illegitimacy, and the chart is not evidence of parentage.

[^205]: **Reported surroundings, not chart construction data.** The five lists concern the supposed place, sounds, or circumstances of delivery. Weapons and martial sounds belong with bells and drums in the Metal list; they are not a prediction that a weapon caused birth. The source gives four Waters but three occurrences of each other phase, and those counts remain unchanged.

[^206]: **Crown-whorls and delayed effects.** 旋 refers to the hair-whorl at the crown, not an additional head. The comparison between girls and boys concerns the purported timing or severity of the result, not slower physical growth. The claim about later childbirth remains the source’s projection and is not a medical conclusion about a newborn girl.

[^207]: **A difficult symptom phrase.** S has 白懵尿草, retained in quotation marks as “white bewilderment and wetting the bedding.” Its precise historical sense is uncertain; neither a modern diagnosis nor a confident character repair has been imposed. The seven-year boundary and the contrast between frailty and later plumpness are retained as given.

[^208]: **The first pair uses nayin.** Wuchen Wood and Gengchen Metal are nayin identities, not their ordinary stem phases. The following example changes the explanation to Fire obstructing Metal’s movement. 勢不行 and 氣不轉 are distinct phrases, rendered force not advancing and qi not turning; they are not both translated simply as ill luck.

[^209]: **Ambiguous kinship and bodily expressions.** 不是雙生異母必是寄姪抱歸 is read with 雙生異母 together, “twins with different mothers,” but could instead enumerate twins and different maternal affiliation as separate possibilities. The source does not resolve it. 乳粗口有角 is likewise kept close rather than assigned a modern feeding disorder. 驚弔 is a historical childhood affliction term, not necessarily literal hanging.

[^210]: **A retrospective hour-divination rule.** The three postures are presented as signs from which an hour can be inferred. They are not instructions for positioning a child during delivery, and the text supplies no evidence that they determine birth time accurately. “Immortal method” preserves its claim to authority.

[^211]: **Shi, ke, and divisions.** 時 here is a traditional double-hour, not a modern sixty-minute hour. The source gives eight ke and twelve divisions without explaining how the two subdivisions relate. The translation retains the count instead of converting it to an exact number of modern minutes or silently changing it to fit a selected historical clock system.

[^212]: **Competing rules and reported cases.** The first explanation favors different twins by day/hour polarity; the Shenbai quotation gives a different rule about birth order and death; a third uses direction. All remain distinct. The Wang and Li brothers are examples reported by the compiler, not independently verified charts or an empirical validation of one method.

[^213]: **Six Relations is a conventional grouping.** The text names parents, siblings, wife, and children; these are kinship classes rather than a list of exactly six individuals. The explanation begins with hypothetical marriages among stems. It should not be read as a genealogy of real people or as a replacement for the differing household-pillar scheme used elsewhere.

[^214]: **Children are derived through a coupled pair.** Jia and Ji are said to produce Geng and Xin through Ji’s generation of Metal, which controls Jia. Thus a man’s heirs are what controls him, while a woman’s children are what she generates. This is the source’s correlative genealogy, not an account of biological inheritance. The subsequent change of reference must be followed at each generation.

[^215]: **A compressed mother/Wealth clause.** S and G612 pass from Gui as mother to “if Ji Earth, Direct Wealth, is encountered,” without explicitly stating its consequence before turning to Wu father. The translation does not invent the missing assertion of maternal injury. Gui, Wu, Geng, and Xin are then assigned differently as Direct or Indirect relations to Jia and Yi, while their family roles remain fixed in this account.

[^216]: **The disagreement is explicit.** The main account assigns male family roles to yang stems and female roles to yin stems, regardless of the usual Direct/Indirect distinction. The quoted alternative instead takes Direct Wealth as wife and Xin as a Jia woman’s proper husband. Both are translated; neither is silently made the exclusive modern rule.

[^217]: **Kinship terms change with the reference person.** 婆 and 公 first designate paternal grandmother and grandfather here, while 丈母 and 丈人 then denote the wife’s mother and father. 妻舅 is the wife’s brother, not the speaker’s maternal uncle. 妗 ordinarily denotes an uncle’s wife; here the text applies it to the wife of that explicitly defined brother-in-law.

[^218]: **The family model is repeated from a woman’s position.** Geng husband is followed by his generating Ji mother and her controlling Jia husband. The alternative son/daughter rule using Food God and Hurting Officer is reported separately. The original wording does not justify imposing one uniform sex-assignment rule on all the juan’s examples.

[^219]: **Strength and damage are evaluated independently.** The account allows longevity with poverty, or wealth and distinction with early death. These are not omitted to produce a simple equation of flourishing with every favorable outcome. The stigmatizing links among appearance, disability, sexuality, and fortune are the source’s historical assertions, not validated descriptions of relatives.

[^220]: **Care in old age versus conducting the funeral.** 送老 generally concerns remaining to support or accompany a parent’s final years; 送終 more specifically includes attendance at the end and funeral obligations. Neither is automatically translated as “a male heir survives” when the wording only says child or offspring. The note on a concubine’s position marks another shift of reference.

[^221]: **Absence of a written stem is not absence of a real parent.** The method seeks a missing influence through the one present, then infers an unusual parental union when the stems do not combine. Marriage during mourning, maternal seniority, and remarriage are its conjectured explanations. The translation preserves them as claims, not evidence of actual parentage or marital history.

[^222]: **Two principles are juxtaposed.** The earlier paragraph calls Jia elder and Yi younger; the question here receives the answer that stronger is elder and weaker younger irrespective of position. This tension is not removed. The subsequent insistence on yang males and yin females is the author’s preferred theory at this point, not a summary of every quoted authority.

[^223]: **The author accepts a conflicting account.** After strongly rejecting yang mothers, he says the Shenbai method taking Ren as Jia’s mother is also intelligible. The subsequent labels call that mother Direct Seal, despite Ren’s usual Indirect-Seal relation to Jia. S and G612 transmit the wording, which is retained. “Developing without father/mother” is ambiguous about loss or separation and is not made into a recommendation to sever family ties.

[^224]: **The qualifications must remain attached.** A season-ending hour is first called harmful, then explicitly exempted when malign sha is absent; another source says a harmful parent position need not be season-ending at all. Four corners and four cardinals are preserved as the source’s distinction, not a substitute modern pedigree chart. Brotherly blossoms translate 棣萼, the familial floral image.

[^225]: **One long song, not an invented new set of rules.** Its twelve seven-character lines are retained in order. The statement about bearing multiple surnames belongs to fostering, adoption, and household transfer in this discourse. The Four Corners and Corner Separation are technical names; “Hai and Zi” is not silently changed to a more familiar branch pair.

[^226]: **The clause 主不了剋妻 is difficult.** S and G612 both transmit it after the weak-Ji/strong-Jia example. “Failure to sustain the union and harm to the wife” renders the apparent sense, but the exact force of 不了 remains uncertain. No missing wife-star or chart pillar has been inserted to repair it.

[^227]: **Four and nine are Metal’s numbers.** 四九 is not forty-nine in this passage. The text links them to the phase’s numerical correspondences, without proving a literal number of children. Officer as child is the male-reference method just explained, distinct from the woman’s Food/output method.

[^228]: **Sex and maternal rank are separate distinctions.** 兩般子 may concern children of principal and secondary unions; the next sentences also distinguish sons from daughters through Officer and Killings. The translation preserves both axes rather than making “secondary child” a synonym for daughter. The compiler presents his own observations, not an independently sampled dataset.

[^229]: **The years are selected relationally.** Heavy Officer/Killings calls for restraint; weak Officer/Killings calls for support, with further qualifications for excess Wealth and Food. The text supplies no actual calendar year without a chart and luck sequence. “Living methods” translates 活法, adaptable interpretation rather than a single inflexible formula.

[^230]: **Parent-stars are not assigned uniformly across the book.** This subsection uses Indirect Wealth for father and Seal for mother, and its Wu example takes Ding as mother. Earlier alternatives used different polarity principles. Two Ren are interpreted as two husband-influences of the mother, not asserted to be two biological fathers. Direct and Indirect Seal distinguish principal and secondary mothers in this account.

[^231]: **The brother’s Officer can injure his sibling.** Ding, the Officer of Geng, controls Xin; Bing, the Officer of Xin, controls Geng. The source’s Geng/Ding/Xin/Bing string is a set of interacting stems, not a four-pillar chart. This explains the shift in reference without validating an inference of actual sibling hostility.

[^232]: **The beginning is compressed and sometimes obscure.** “My influence exposed, the other concealed” retains 我明他暗 without supplying missing stems. The Geng-to-Fire statement is likewise transmitted rather than normalized to Geng’s ordinary Metal nature. The song’s accusations of clandestine birth and absence at a father’s bier are the source’s judgments, not established facts.

[^233]: **A difficult death clause is retained.** S and G612 have 食明旺相慬然殂. 慬 can evoke sorrow or anxiety; “death in sorrow” stays close, but the clause’s relation to the preceding favorable Food terminology remains uncertain. It has not been changed into a statement of prosperity merely to remove that tension.

[^234]: **A variant in the last formula.** S gives 丙戌丁壬妻護靈, while G612 gives 丙戊丁壬妻護靈. The main text retains Bingxu, Ding, and Ren, with the wife guarding the departed’s spirit-tablet; no complete chart is reconstructed. 陽母 and the delayed-birth phrase likewise remain compressed technical expressions, not verified obstetric observations.

[^235]: **Parallel clauses determine the punctuation.** Transfer to another household goes with year–month clash; following mother goes with empty Wealth and flourishing Seal; father’s early death with weakened Indirect Wealth; mother’s departure with Seal encountering Wealth and Death. Some punctuation in G612 breaks these links. The translation follows S’s parallel syntax rather than reproducing those misleading sentence boundaries.

[^236]: **Borrowed palace is not fully defined here.** 借宮所生 is used both for oneself and for wife, with dependency or adoption as the interpreted consequence. It is retained without equating it to one specified modern chart technique. The immediately following phrases separately assess father, mother, wife, and children and should not be read with an unchanged reference throughout.

[^237]: **The in-law assignments differ from the earlier formula.** This rhapsody says mother-in-law is Indirect Wealth and father-in-law Companions/Rob Wealth; earlier prose derived them by generating and controlling the husband’s mother. The translation preserves the discrepancy. 食比, Food and Companions, likewise remains rather than being changed to the more familiar 食神.

[^238]: **Internal and external clash.** 外衝 and 內衝 are contrasted through kin outside the marriage and the spouses within it, but no full locating procedure is supplied. “Three generations complete” describes the rhapsody’s ideal of an intact family, not an extra number of natal pillars.

[^239]: **Principal and secondary births are historical ranks.** The triple Direct and triple Indirect categories are interpreted through 嫡/庶 household status. This neither proves parentage nor measures a child’s legitimacy in any modern sense. The source moves from generic ancestors to grandfather and father; those kinship references are not all translated as a single “parent.”

[^240]: **Taking the eldest’s place.** 奪長 can mean being thrust into the eldest’s position, not necessarily a literal act of theft by a sibling. Yin Error and Yang Dislocation refer to named configurations from earlier juan. Their alleged effects on in-laws and household affiliation remain separate from the moral and sexual claims in adjacent sentences.

[^241]: **The author warns against automatic childlessness.** Male Hurting Officer/Blade and female Hurting Officer/Owl can still give offspring under the stated Officer, Killings, Wealth, or luck conditions. The concluding stronger-daughters/weaker-sons rule again uses Officer and Killings differently from some earlier passages. No single preferred version has been imposed.

[^242]: **The closing geographic image is expansive, not a list of places.** Five Lakes and Four Seas means friendship widely extended. 不義之橫財 is wealth suddenly gained without righteousness, and is translated as such rather than euphemized into ordinary investment returns. These maxims remain attributed judgments rather than descriptions of identifiable families.

[^243]: **The matching rule is not fully coherent as transmitted.** S moves from the four cardinal birth groups to 申子辰生丑生甲巳生, retaining both branch and stem names. The translation gives the string rather than completing it into a modern compatibility table. The exemption after a first marriage’s loss is also part of the text, not a recommendation to remarry.

[^244]: **No Wife in the Configuration is a technical absence.** The source immediately allows a supportive birth-day position to indicate a wife despite that label. The clause adding Noble Person, Lu, Horse, and 年 is compressed; the English retains the year-reference without inventing a lost condition. Subsequent discussion expressly identifies nayin as an alternative reference for wife.

[^245]: **Sha need not mean malignant.** 名譽煞, Renown Sha, is given a favorable interpretation. The two example hours contain the day ruler’s Lu, but the text further attributes advancement to wife. 駙馬 and 郡馬 are consorts linked with imperial or princely women; they are not merely men who own horses.

[^246]: **Jiyou and the older references.** The transmitted 己酉 is retained as the valid pair Jiyou, after the stronger warning about Chou days. It has not been split into two branch names, Si and You. A day carrying the year’s Tomb or Direct Seal belongs to the older set of references used here; the paragraph must not be reduced throughout to the day stem’s Ten Gods.

[^247]: **A supported graph and a difficult verse.** S’s 日光華蓋 is clarified by G612’s 日坐華蓋, Canopy seated in the day. The next poem’s 日若值時 remains obscure in both witnesses; the translation preserves its day/hour wording without claiming a recovered text. G612’s last line names courtesan, nun, maid, and servant differently from S’s prostitute/nun contrast; S controls the translation.

[^248]: **A favorable-sounding name with an unfavorable gloss.** Phoenix Pool is the name attached here to year and day sharing a position. It is not substituted for an unrelated configuration of that name. 同官 in S is read as 同宮, Sharing a Palace, with G612; the surrounding discussion concerns position, not two people holding one official post.

[^249]: **Not the same formulation as the earlier Well-Railing Fork.** The four day/hour pairs are explicitly listed and should not be replaced by juan six’s three-Geng Water pattern. Beating the basin alludes to Zhuangzi’s response to his wife’s death in [*Zhuangzi*, “Perfect Enjoyment”](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/莊子/至樂); the allusion denotes bereavement here, not advice about mourning.

[^250]: **Nayin numbers, not observed frequencies.** The five numbers are used symbolically, with doubling allowed. The source also prefers clash for the supposed year of marriage, rather than the combination many readers might expect. That contrast is retained and is not presented as a reliable method for forecasting marriage or counting spouses.

[^251]: **The day acts in relation to the parental year.** Xin controlling Yi, Gui as Yi’s Inverted Food, and Wu as Yi’s Birth explain why the reference cannot remain the day stem alone. The final Ren intervention explicitly reverses the adverse chain through generation. “Older wife” follows 老婦 but does not establish a precise age difference.

[^252]: **The analogy is marital music.** 琴瑟不調, zither and lute out of tune, means disharmony between spouses. The text requires a natal Harm and a later recurrence for 有前見害. Its claim that a day branch ahead of Tai Sui gives fear of wife is preserved as a separate rule, not derived from that musical image.

[^253]: **Order changes the interpreted bereavement.** Hai encountering Zi and Zi encountering Hai are given opposite spouse-loss meanings; Yin encountering Chou and Chou encountering Yin are likewise distinguished. The entire explanatory gloss is retained. No universal symmetric compatibility rule has been substituted for these directional relations.

[^254]: **A prediction, not a reproductive prohibition to follow.** The passage links a daughter’s or son’s birth with parental death through the spouse-star’s Extinction. Its examples explicitly use Jiazi Metal nayin, not the Jia day stem. Nothing here establishes that having a child of either sex causes loss of a parent or should guide reproductive decisions.

[^255]: **Related names, different lists.** The Beating-the-Basin days, Looking-Homeward phase positions, and seasonal Disarray list are separate classifications. The author himself compares the first with Solitary Luan and the second with Established Lu. The month names in the *Calendar of a Hundred Prohibitions* are then another appended account, not a replacement for the seasonal list.

[^256]: **A second Household-Extinguishing rule.** This one is defined by birth-branch groups and months, not the earlier Well-Railing day/hour pairs. The same name does not warrant silently merging their calculations. Its quoted effects distinguish a man’s natal family from a woman’s husband and in-laws.

[^257]: **The child is derived through the wife for a man.** Wood man → Earth wife → Metal child; a Wood woman instead generates Fire child. The ensuing five character sketches follow this older phase-level kinship scheme. Their positive and negative descriptions are historical physiognomic assertions, not dependable assessments of children’s personalities.

[^258]: **The hour can supply what the configuration lacks.** “No Child” is immediately qualified by the hour’s position, just as “No Wife” was qualified by the day. The numeric rule gives Water 1, Fire 2, Wood 3, Metal 4, Earth 5 and allows increase or reduction. The author admits cases that do not fit, postponing judgment until after fifty; the translation retains that admission.

[^259]: **The compiler explicitly contrasts two methods.** Guiwei year is Wood nayin, whose male-reference child is Metal; the cited chart lacks that child and uses combination to explain surviving daughters. From Bing day, however, Gui child reaches Lu at Zi and would suggest sons. The text reports that this did not occur and favors the year-nayin interpretation in the example. The contradiction is evidence within the author’s argument, not removed by silently changing a pillar.

[^260]: **The full pairs are retained.** In the second example, Guisi hour draws the Metal child of Guiwei Wood into Si’s Birth, while Jiazi supplies Metal within the chart. The third records death at fifty-six and no children. These are the source’s reported cases, not independently corroborated family histories.

[^261]: **Several different references for an hour.** The hour may carry the year’s Void, a self-punishment, or the original nayin’s Tomb. These are not all a single relation to the day stem. The text distinguishes children from grandchildren when describing care in old age. No omitted age calculation is supplied for its “after fifty” qualification.

[^262]: **Reciprocal pairs and an adoption image.** 狡害煞 is attached to Shen/Hai and Si/Yin day–hour encounters in both directions. The reference to a different surname concerns adoption or an heir from outside the immediate line. It is not a claim that a particular modern family ought to give up on having children.

[^263]: **Extreme parity reverses its ordinary result.** More yang and more yin are distinguished from all yang and all yin. The Jiazi, Chen/Shen, and Xinsi illustration follows that symbolic counting rule. No biological mechanism or validated probability of fetal sex is established by it.

[^264]: **The example fixes the order of the lines.** Father’s age forms the upper line, mother’s the lower, conception month the middle. Even gives a broken line, odd an unbroken one. Thus even–odd–even forms Kan and odd–even–odd Li, as the prose states. These are trigram images, not hexagrams. The source does not specify modern birthday-based versus traditional reckoned age, nor handling of an intercalary month; none is imposed.

[^265]: **The worked arithmetic is sound; the prognosis is not thereby established.** The stated example is 49 + 1 − 31 = 19, an odd result. The source checks the final digit nine, which has the same parity as nineteen. Its assertion of early death when the predicted sex is wrong is retained but has no evidential basis supplied here. The closing subtract-one/two/three alternatives do not state a complete iterative stopping rule; the translation does not invent one or treat them as equivalent algorithms.
