# Sanming Tonghui · Juan Eleven
## A Comprehensive Gathering of the Three Fates

<!-- source: L14-L18 -->
**Attributed to Wan Minying of the Ming dynasty.** From the imperially commissioned *Siku Quanshu*.[^01]

*Reading conventions.* Capitalized names such as Wealth, Officer, Seal, Food God, Killings, Lu, Birth, and Extinction retain the technical meanings established in the preceding translations. “Body” commonly denotes the day stem and its capacity to sustain the configuration; “root” and “master” distinguish the year and day when the passage does so. **〈 〉** preserves the source’s small-character commentary, including its objections and cross-references. Square brackets identify supplied readings and explicit repairs. The numbered treatises and paragraph divisions are editorial. The collected authorities are allowed to disagree: ordinary phase relationships, transformed phases, *nayin*, and year-based methods are not silently made into one system. Predictions about illness, death, character, or relatives are historical assertions, not established judgments about living people.[^02]

## 1. On Qi and Its Images
### 氣象篇

〈Written by Zuixingzi.〉

Now, in setting out the Four Pillars and taking their Five Phases, one fixes a luck course governing ten years. Clear and turbid, pure and mixed—their countless forms are unequal; likes and dislikes, right and wrong, cannot be governed by one inflexible rule. The ancients who discussed fate investigated its finest subtleties, proceeding from substance to embrace function. Those who discuss it today cling to configurations, grasp the false, and lose the true. One must first observe the overall disposition of qi and its images: this is the governing outline of wealth and distinction, poverty and low station. Next discuss where the useful spirit comes forth, pursuing the subtleties of life and death, obstruction and attainment. The Eight Characters need not be splendid; the Five Phases need only harmonious qi. To point carelessly at the Three Origins and Six Jia—who could thereby understand their countless threads and beginnings? The student must draw out the mysterious and seek the hidden, bring what is concealed to light and return it to its root, seek the empty within the substantial, and take presence from absence. Although the principle of fate is subtle, this carries understanding more than halfway. Yet the great sea begins with a ladle of water, and young yin is born from old yang. Accomplishment contains the impulse toward defeat; alteration is the gradual approach to transformation. These too require close examination. Consider a single yang thawing the frost, or cold arising amid the Three Fu heats. 〈The commentary on this has already been quoted above, under “Heat encountered in winter; summer grass meeting frost.”〉[^03]

<!-- source: L24-L26 -->
**Yang firmness without the mean does harm through excess; firmness capable of yielding is the way to good fortune.**

〈This image has excessive yang without restraint, and contains no yin within it. If the courses also travel southeast, yang firmness loses the mean and must produce harm. A person employing this is solitary, poor, inauspicious, and violent, dying amid Water or Fire. But when one of the five yang stems is born in a yin month, the stems and branches enclose or combine with yielding yin influences, and the courses also travel through yielding yin country, this is called auspicious. Such a person, though born in poverty and humble station, must ultimately attain splendor.〉
<!-- source: L28-L30 -->
**Yielding weakness, lopsided and parched, is the petty person’s image; firm vigor, centered and upright, is the gentleman’s bearing.**

〈The former image departs from the mean. If the Four Pillars show only yielding yin without entering a configuration, and their stems and branches contain no yang, the person remains timid and yielding all day. One employing this image is calculating, covertly malicious, and capable of any poison. Firm vigor, by contrast, is the gentleman’s substance; centrality and rectitude are his virtues. Yang containing yin in the Four Pillars, with firmness and yielding properly governed and no breaking, control, punishment, or clash, gives conduct surpassing others and an uprightness outstanding in the age. Hence “the gentleman’s bearing.”〉[^04]
<!-- source: L32-L34 -->
**Excessive cold and thinness cannot readily rise even in warmth; excessive dryness and fierceness instead suffer calamity when Water strikes.**

〈All four pillars are yin, the birth is in the tenth month, the Five Phases’ roots are empty and extinguished, and the day stem is weak without vigorous qi: even a warm region cannot finally bring advancement. All four pillars are Fire and the birth precedes the summer solstice: Fire’s nature is dry and fierce. A sudden encounter with Water’s agitation in year or luck not only fails to restrain it but instead does harm. One employing this image suffers early death, solitude, poverty, and frequent penal punishment.〉[^05]
<!-- source: L36-L38 -->
**Excessive attachment to the substantial leaves affairs without openness; excessive clarity and coldness leave thought desolate.**

〈“Attachment to the substantial” means employing one thing without allowing passage beyond it. Employing Officer without Wealth, or Seal without Killings, with many combinations but little accomplishment, leaves a person unable to deal openly and comprehensively with affairs. If Metal and Water are excessively clear and cold and never meet warming luck—as when Geng or Xin is born in the tenth month, the pillars are pure Water, and the courses travel northwest—the person eats alone and sleeps alone throughout life. Such a lonely livelihood brings grief others could scarcely endure.〉
<!-- source: L40-L42 -->
**Excessive attachment leaves ambition unable to travel far.**

〈The influences within a configuration must not be excessively attached. Too much affection entangles and bewilders them, so that they cannot free themselves and see nothing beyond. Jia Wood takes Ji Earth as wife; affection is certainly fitting. But if Jia and Ji also ride Zi and Chou below, combination is added within and without. With no Wealth, Officer, or Seal-Ribbon elsewhere to move Jia’s heart, Jia remains always beneath Ji Earth. How could its ambition reach far?〉[^06]
<!-- source: L44-L46 -->
**Excessive exertion makes even accomplishment difficult.**

〈What the pillars obtain naturally is subtle and excellent. What needs laborious support is never wholly fine. Suppose Wealth is employed, but none appears in the configuration: one must seek what Hurting Officer or Food God generates. If Food and Injury have lost their season and lack qi, one must again seek a Companion to pass assistance onward, or an outward clash or remote combination. All this is called excessive exertion. Its accomplishment must be difficult.〉

<!-- source: L48-L50 -->
**With Noble Persons in abundance, calamity heals of itself; with evil sha in abundance, blessing is hard to enjoy.**

〈The Eight Characters originally contain many Noble Persons, with the Two Virtues supporting the employed Wealth and Officer and no punishment or breaking. Even amid adversity, there is no danger. But when evil sha, the Three Punishments, and Six Clashes abound and oppose Wealth and Officer, what foundation for enjoying blessing can there be, even upon reaching Wealth-and-Officer territory?〉
<!-- source: L52-L54 -->
**At the Five Phases’ Extinction, Lu and Horse may support the body; amid the Four Pillars’ wonders, Companions divide the blessing.**

〈Do not immediately call every encounter with Extinction ominous: even an inauspicious place may contain blessing spirits that give support. Wood is extinguished at Shen, for example, yet Shen contains Ren Water as Seal and Geng and Wu as Officer and Wealth. All are things I employ and can support the body and advance its blessing. What is feared is an influence controlling or injuring the employed palace: then what is useful is extinguished, and only then is it ominous. Officer supplies distinction, Wealth the wonder; encountering Wealth and Officer within the configuration is auspicious. Companions, however, have no scruple about contesting Officer and robbing Wealth, so complete excellence is lost.〉[^07]
<!-- source: L56-L58 -->
**Yin and yang certainly possess firmness and yielding; how could stems and branches lack reversals?**

〈Yang’s firmness and yin’s yielding are the Way of Heaven and Earth. Turning them upside down means their interchange and reversal. This introduces the passages that follow.〉
<!-- source: L60-L62 -->
**Though a wife is betrothed, she does not know her husband.**

〈Once husband and wife have entered their palace, how could they fail to know one another? Yet when their affinity is separated and cannot communicate, she does not see her husband. Yi Wood employs Geng Metal as husband. If Bing Fire intervenes and cuts them off, Geng is injured by Fire; or Geng sits upon the Defeat and Death grounds of Zi and Wu. Thus the wife can never see her husband.〉
<!-- source: L64-L66 -->
**A child is present from the beginning, yet does not care for its mother.**

〈A child’s care for its mother accords with both principle and affection. But one held by other bonds cannot support her to the end. Jia takes Bing as child, for example, but Xin Metal combines with Bing. Absorbed in affection for its wife, Bing exchanges its love for its mother for that attachment. Thus, though Bing Fire is present in the configuration, it cannot be employed. A discussion of fate that reaches this depth will come close to avoiding error.〉[^08]
<!-- source: L68-L70 -->
**A father without a child is not alone; a child with a father is nevertheless orphaned.**

〈Wood takes Fire as offspring. No Bing, Ding, Si, or Wu in the Four Pillars means no child. Yet Fire concealed in the earthly branches, or heavenly stems whose restraint and transformation make them useful, prevents the judgment of childlessness. Wood takes Water as parents. If Water is injured and controlled, Wood loses what generates it. Suppose Jia or Yi is born in a Hai or Zi year, but the month falls in one of the four seasonal endings: Water is injured by Earth. The one who gave birth is lost—how could the child not be orphaned?〉

<!-- source: L72-L74 -->
**What lives may yet live again; what has died cannot die a second time.**

〈An influence originally has Birth within the configuration but is first controlled and injured. If year and luck again reach Birth and flourishing and its bodily force recovers, this resembles a second birth. Death means an ending. When an influence in the Four Pillars originally occupies Death or Extinction, a later year or course reaching the same place is not judged more ominous: there is no second death.〉[^09]
<!-- source: L76-L78 -->
**Even dead, it need not become a ghost; meeting Birth, it need not become a living person.**

〈Wood born in spring obtains its season and therefore flourishes. Though the pillars contain Death or Extinction, courses through Birth and flourishing prevent it from being considered dead. Wood born in autumn loses its season and is therefore weak. Though the pillars contain Birth and flourishing, courses through decline and Extinction prevent it from being considered alive.〉
<!-- source: L80-L82 -->
**Many children sicken the mother: as though farming an immense field. Many mothers sicken the child: as though standing above a deep abyss.**

〈Children are what the mother generates; too many drain her qi. This is precisely how children can empty the mother. If decline and illness are added, her strength is inadequate and she certainly cannot nurture them. Is this not what “farming an immense field” means? A mother has no second equal in authority: only then is her affection whole. If mothers are numerous, yin gathers, jealousy arises, and malicious schemes begin. This is the Five Stars’ “Two Mothers Contesting Authority.” Excessive indulgence causes the mother to lose what she loves; on what can the child rely? If Illness or Death is further approached, Shensheng’s catastrophe must arise overnight.〉[^10]
<!-- source: L84-L86 -->
**Without direct opposition, no clash; without an oblique relation, no combination; without crossing, no punishment; without a direct encounter, no breaking. Clash opens the divergent gates of the six extremities; combination reveals the traces of the myriad things. Punishment changes and corrects; breaking opposes and wounds. Thus producing Metal in thorny ground is inferior to planting jade in Lantian.**

〈These four conditions are images of battle, control, striking, and stripping. Among them lie the operations of punishing the empty and drawing forth the distant. Yet taking a useful spirit as distinction or blessing amid inversion and disorder is inferior to the natural subtlety of Wealth generating Officer, or Seal obtaining Killings. Is this not why Ziping discusses Wealth, Seal, and Food in particular?〉[^11]
<!-- source: L88-L90 -->
**When an auspicious spirit assists my work, seek the spirit that assists that auspicious one.**

〈A weak fate, or one subject to punishment, injury, breaking, and harm and unable to become useful, requires an auspicious spirit to support it and complete its blessing. Then examine how heavy or light the assisting spirit’s force is. If it is rootless, out of season, or itself injured, one must first seek what can support it. Suppose Jia is born in summer and scorched by Fire. Ren, Gui, Hai, or Zi gives aid and rescue, but Water has already suffered Fire and Earth’s draining and control, and cannot bring me blessing. Metal must therefore be found to generate Water in turn and make it flourish, so that Water has the affinity to care for me. The accomplishment in such a case lies not in Water but in Metal. Again, Wu clashed by Zi relies on Wei combining with it and piercing Zi: Wei is the assisting spirit. If Wei is injured and cannot be used, seek an influence that generates and supports Wei Earth. Only when that influence has strength can Wei become useful.〉[^12]
<!-- source: L92-L94 -->
**When an inauspicious influence injures the body, resolve it by employing what injures that influence.**

〈When an inauspicious spirit controls the palace of my body, seek which thing in the pillars can restrain the spirit injuring me. It will have no leisure even to extricate itself—how could it reach me? Jia Wood originally injured by Metal cannot escape harm, but Fire’s control makes danger recede. Mao clashed by You with Wu appearing in the pillars is judged in the same way.〉

<!-- source: L96-L98 -->
**When each of the Five Phases obtains its place, their return and gathering make blessing.**

〈The Five Phases must not bear empty names while losing their positions. They must obtain the command and return to their own precincts before distinction is possible. If they return and gather into one configuration, their subtle excellence cannot be expressed.〉
<!-- source: L100-L102 -->
**When every influence in a configuration loses its precinct, there is wandering without support.**

〈Day Master and useful spirit alike need somewhere to settle. If neither can connect with roots or find support in the Four Pillars, and Void, Death, Extinction, Bath, punishment, and clash also occur, nothing can finally be established. Drifting and the loss of a place to belong are inevitable.〉
<!-- source: L104-L106 -->
**Major courses convert the counted interval into years; minor courses proceed forward or backward from the hour.**

〈The two commentaries have already been quoted in the opening volume’s discussions of major and minor courses.〉[^13]
<!-- source: L108-L110 -->
**Clash the Literary Storehouse and literary brilliance flourishes; close the Arsenal and weapons are laid to rest.**

〈Xu is the Literary Storehouse, because Fire signifies literary illumination. When the Eight Characters originally lack Wealth, Officer, Seal-Ribbon, Food God, and generating qi, there is no impulse toward writing or learning. Merely possessing Fire’s Storehouse, moreover locked shut, gives an unlettered person. Concealed Hurting Officer or an unmanifest Seal, however, also indicates intelligence. If Chen, Wei, or Chou in the pillars clashes with or punishes Xu’s Storehouse, and the course further enters southeast territory, Fire gives forth its light and writing must flourish. I have seen many thus rise high in the Hanlin. Chou is the Arsenal, because Metal signifies shields and spears. An autumn influence in the Eight Characters, with Shen, You, Geng, or Xin as Killings and Indirect Officer and Yang Blade in the same palace, gives a fearless, warlike person. But if Zi, Si, or You forms a combination in the pillars, and southeast Wood-and-Fire luck restrains stubborn Metal, martial force is shut away and weapons fall quiet. I have seen warriors thus put off their armor and retire.〉[^14]
<!-- source: L112-L114 -->
**The flying Dragon leaves Heaven and follows its Cloud into the abyss; the hidden Dragon in the abyss follows its Cloud up to Heaven.**

〈Dragon is Chen, Heaven Hai, Cloud Ren. A Dragon obtaining its Cloud flies. Hai in the year, Chen governing the month, and Ren upon the year or month stem place Dragon in Heaven. If day and hour have flourishing Water and join Dragon in a configuration, it must follow Cloud into the abyss: Water is Dragon’s home, so it leaves Heaven above and hides in Water below. One obtaining this image has literary gifts outstanding in the age, with obstruction and passage alternating through life. Though fame and achievement emerge in court and council, the life’s work ends among woods and springs. Si or Wu in the pillars instead gives poverty and humble station. Hai in the year and Chen in the hour, with day and month assembling Water, place Dragon hidden below in the abyss. Punishment, clash, control, and breaking among stems and branches then leave Dragon no peace. Ren must appear in day or hour, and Dragon will follow Cloud up to Heaven. Without Hai in the year, using Si to clash it forth in reverse is also auspicious; but the person begins in poverty, without ancestral support, and later rises through another’s assistance. Fame and achievement give distinction in close attendance. Si and You courses, the territories of Defeat and Extinction, bring household ruin and dismissal. This is the Ren Riding the Dragon’s Back configuration.〉[^15]
<!-- source: L116-L118 -->
**The Great-Forest Dragon emerges upon the Heavenly River; with all four Earth Storehouses complete, it occupies the Nine in the Fifth Place.**

〈The Great-Forest Dragon is Wuchen. The Four Pillars’ nayin must contain Heavenly-River Water: then Dragon flies in Heaven. If all four Storehouses are present as well, the Four Seas are complete and all beneath Heaven receives its nourishing rain. The person must be the great ruler of the Nine in the Fifth Place. Ming Taizu’s fate was **Wuchen / Renxu / Dingchou / Dingwei**. This formulation, too, was made with a particular example in view.〉[^16]
<!-- source: L120-L122 -->
**The Long-Flowing Dragon returns to the Great Sea; the waters of the Five Lakes gather, and it governs the multitude.**

〈The Long-Flowing Dragon is Renchen. Dragon amid a long current obtaining Hai among the branches is called Dragon Returning to the Great Sea, and also Dragon Leaping at Heaven’s Gate. Its subtlety lies in obtaining Great-Sea Water in the nayin. Water throughout the Four Pillars makes the Five Lakes’ waters both complete and deep, to Dragon’s increasing delight. Geng or Xin must generate it. Then its coming and going shake mountains—is this not a noble image? Wang Yangming, enfeoffed as Earl of Xinjian, had **Renchen / Xinhai / Guihai / Guihai**. This explanation likewise arose from Yangming’s fate.〉

<!-- source: L124-L126 -->
**Sixfold Combination accomplishing its work gives authority over the Six Ministries.**

〈Punishment, clash, control, harm, or a broken image in the Four Pillars is originally judged ominous. But an influence powerful enough to draw it back through combination turns it auspicious instead, giving lofty and far-reaching blessing. Becoming useful in year and month gives great distinction; becoming useful in day and hour is secondary.〉
<!-- source: L128-L130 -->
**The Three Punishments becoming useful give authority that commands the Three Frontiers.**

〈Punishment is not originally auspicious. Made useful, it gives wealth, distinction, and intelligence; useless, it gives solitude, poverty, misfortune, and early death. What makes it useful? The Three Punishments possess qi and the Day Master is firm and strong. The reverse makes them useless.〉
<!-- source: L132-L134 -->
**Zi and Wu, the Upright Gate, doubly arched: exceptional stature depends upon outward and direct arching.**

〈Zi and Wu are upright rather than oblique, hence “Upright Gate.” Enclosed or arched, without injury or breaking and with further strength, they must give intelligence and vigorous achievement. Direct arching is Hai and Chou arching Zi, or Si and Wei arching Wu. Outward arching is Shen and Chen arching Zi, or Xu and Yin arching Wu. Void, control, and breaking are feared as harmful.〉[^17]
<!-- source: L136-L138 -->
**Si and Yin, the grounds of Birth, combine with Qian and Kun in complete refinement.**

〈Si and Yin possess generating force and can combine with Hai and Shen. Hai is Qian, Shen Kun. Without clash or mixture, Shen and Hai ride noble qi and give gifts surpassing the multitude.〉
<!-- source: L140-L142 -->
**Heaven and Earth containing and concealing useful spirits make the mind open and clear.**

〈Hai is Heaven and Shen Earth, openly possessing force. If these two signs are absent from the Eight Characters but the influences on either side arch them into being, with noble qi and without Void, the mind must be open and clear. Another interpretation takes Shen and Hai as enclosing You and Xu: examine what these are relative to the heavenly stem, and take their usefulness as distinction.〉
<!-- source: L144-L146 -->
**Wind and Thunder fiercely aroused, with nobility unimpaired, carry the name aloft.**

〈Si is Wind Gate and Mao Thunder Gate. If the Eight Characters emptily arch these two positions and further contain Noble Person, a year or course clashing them into activity must bring advancement.〉[^18]
<!-- source: L148-L150 -->
**A household is founded on a thief’s ground; renewed disorder by the thief destroys house and life.**

〈This method concerns the five yin influences in the monthly branch. If a spirit in year or day contends to combine with it as wife, the monthly branch is trapped within that relation and cannot emerge: hence “thief’s ground.” If the year’s or day’s spirit is then occupied with self-punishment and has no leisure to combine with me, the hour’s branch can seize the opening and combine with the monthly branch. This is founding a household on a thief’s ground, giving substantial wealth and distinction. A major course that removes the thief brings peace; the thief returning to disorder brings misfortune. Another account says: appeasing the thief brings peace; attacking the thief brings misfortune.〉[^19]

<!-- source: L152-L154 -->
**Timber for a beam presents itself to the axe, but abundant Wood and deficient Metal make usefulness hard to achieve.**

〈Wood relies upon Metal’s cutting to become a useful vessel. If another spirit holds Metal in combination so that it cannot come to control Wood, Wood should be placed beside Metal and submit to its shaping there. Yet if Wood flourishes and Metal is weak, even approaching Metal cannot make it cut effectively. If Wood and Metal combine with both parties strong, the judgment is distinction.〉
<!-- source: L156-L158 -->
**Pure yang at Earth’s Door containing yin gives conspicuous military authority.**

〈Pure yang throughout the Eight Characters is originally a one-sided faction. Yet Zi, Yin, Chen, Wu, Shen, and Xu secretly arch the yin of Chou, Mao, Si, Hai, Wei, and You. The two images aid and respond to one another, instead completing Heaven and Earth’s proper qi. With no Void in the Four Pillars and living intent among the heavenly stems, this is especially subtle and excellent. Its authority reaches the frontier, its rank that of duke or marquis, and its blessing is no small thing.〉[^20]
<!-- source: L160-L162 -->
**A solitary Tiger with Wood at Heaven’s Gate gives pure distinction in the council halls.**

〈A single Yin in year or month requires Heaven’s Gate in the hour. Tiger must then face Heaven and roar toward the sun. Add Mao and Wei forming a combination in the pillars: flourishing Wood generates Wind, and Wind follows Tiger. How magnificent! But punishment, clash, control, and breaking, with neither Seal-Ribbon, Wealth, nor Officer available, leave the image useless.〉
<!-- source: L164-L166 -->
**Hall of Learning encountering Traveling Horse gives writing like Mount Tai and the Northern Dipper.**

〈The body seated upon Birth is Hall of Learning. Traveling Horse further galloping between positions, with one clash and one combination, and a lofty overall disposition, gives the image. Wealth, Killings, and Noble Person make it especially distinguished: the writing rises outstandingly above the dust of the world.〉[^21]
<!-- source: L168-L170 -->
**Day Master seated upon Salty Pool gives a life of flowers and wine upon the rivers and lakes.**

〈Salty Pool is also called Peach-Blossom Sha. Men or women encountering it must be sexually unrestrained, often drifting through the world because of flowers and wine. Yet Wealth, Officer, Noble Person, or Virtue in the same palace can instead produce a pure and exceptional bearing, with wealth and distinction enjoyed in peace. Punishment and combination are greatly feared; only Void is welcomed.〉[^22]
<!-- source: L172-L174 -->
**Fullness of blessing requires care against calamity; much misfortune need not exclude an auspicious sign.**

〈Employing Seal to generate the body is, broadly, my blessing. Suppose Officer and Killings in the pillars generate Seal in turn, so that it flourishes without Wealth injuring it, while Food God drains qi and gives distinction. A course entering flourishing Companion-and-Seal territory makes generating support excessive. How could calamity fail to arise at the very fullness of blessing? Hence the gentleman fears occupying the height of prosperity. Again, a configuration originally full of Officer and Killings suffers greater misfortune when their years and courses return. Yet after passing through these dangers, a course restraining them and strengthening the body must follow: Obstruction reaches its limit and Peace arrives. If Jia is oppressed by Officer and Killings, Shen and You courses make the trouble severe. Going forward, however, there are Hai and Zi Seal courses; going backward, Si and Wu restraining courses. These offer rescue—how could they not be welcome? The two lines concern yin and yang’s waxing and waning, calamity and blessing latent within one another, and the communication of Heaven’s Way with human affairs. Savor them closely.〉[^23]

<!-- source: L176-L178 -->
**Horse bearing an arrow at its head: born in Qin, dead in Chu. Horse whipped from behind: north in the morning, south by evening.**

〈Traveling Horse beneath day or hour must carry combination, called Joining the Reins. This gathers great wealth and blessing and gives exceptional ability in conducting affairs. Punishment or clash ahead of Horse is an image of bearing an arrow and breaking the reins. If what comes to clash is Metal and what is controlled is Wood, the calamity is especially severe: death in another country. In taking Traveling Horse for use, the forward method derives its day and hour positions from the year; the reverse method employs the day from the hour. With no embankment or restraint, Horse runs unchecked. Further punishment or clash behind it forces it into swift movement, and it never finds a place to rest. The person toils throughout life, racing about the four quarters. But if the punishing or clashing spirit encounters Triple or Sixfold Combination, it is not considered a whip applied from behind.〉[^24]
<!-- source: L180-L180 -->
**A discerning nature in an unlovely form often comes from clarity flowing through turbidity; a handsome face with an obscured mind comes from turbidity held within clarity.**

〈When the useful spirit is mixed, disordered, punished, or clashed, do not immediately pronounce it turbid and useless. Examine what lies concealed within. If a single point of clarity flows from the turbidity, the person may be plain and unattractive yet perceptive, quick to understand, and unusually resourceful. Conversely, a useful spirit standing clear and exceptional, without mixture, punishment, or injury, must not immediately be called pure. Something concealed within may injure what is employed, leaving a defect that cannot finally be removed. Thus the person, though handsome, fails in learning and accomplishment and loses himself in wine and sex.〉
<!-- source: L182-L184 -->
**One general holding the pass makes every hostile influence submit.**

〈General is a weighty noble spirit; pass is a critical place; the hostile influence is what begrudges me. Jia or Yi born amid flourishing Metal may be controlled by every influence in year and month. Bing emerging upon the month then restrains Killings as authority, and Killings submits of itself. Again, Ren or Gui meeting Wu, Ji, and earthly branches that chaotically control the body cannot withstand them. At the critical position, Geng or Xin is needed as Seal to transform Killings, so that it dares no longer make disorder.〉
<!-- source: L186-L188 -->
**Many inauspicious forces controlling the master are hard to withstand alone.**

〈This concerns heavy Killings and a light body, solitary and unsupported. Without a rescuing spirit holding the pass, the person cannot bear what controls him. Early death or illness must follow.〉
<!-- source: L190-L192 -->
**Having left this company, avoid meeting this company again; having transformed into this spirit, welcome meeting this spirit.**

〈The subtlety of following and transformation cannot be exhausted at a glance: apply the mind and examine closely. Jia and Ji transforming into Earth relinquish Wood’s qi and follow the wife’s house. If Jia, Yi, Yin, Mao, Wei, or Hai appear, all belong to my Companion party and provide support from my original flourishing. How could I be free of attachment? Companions and Blades can also contest combination with my Wealth, preventing Jia and Ji from completing one another and instead producing the bitterness of estrangement. Yi and Geng transforming into Metal welcome flourishing Metal, allowing wife to rely on husband. Ding and Ren transforming into Wood welcome flourishing Wood, allowing daughter to rely on mother. Bing and Xin transforming into Water welcome flourishing Water, allowing mother to rely on child. Wu and Gui transforming into Fire welcome flourishing Fire, allowing master to rely on Wealth. Void, Killings, and Companions’ contention and jealousy are greatly feared. One who fails to become a renowned minister or great lord instead becomes an orphan under another surname.〉[^25]
<!-- source: L194-L196 -->
**Traveling Horse without reins makes a guest of north, south, east, and west.**

〈Without reins means that Horse has no combination. North, south, east, or west—nowhere is beyond its wandering. A person whose fate meets this must drift.〉
<!-- source: L198-L200 -->
**Peach Blossom bearing sha gives the company of courtesans, entertainers, servants, and soldiers.**

〈This means Peach Blossom appearing in day or hour. Not only punishment and affectionate combination are feared; the Five Sha occupying the same place are especially feared. Those meeting this do not accept the teachings of ritual propriety, righteousness, integrity, and shame.〉[^26]

<!-- source: L202-L204 -->
**Mother and child have support from beginning to end; husband and wife rely upon one another through life and death.**

〈Mother and child, husband and wife, here refer specifically to substance and function, chiefly in month and day. Suppose Wu sits upon Chen and is born in Shen month. Earth takes Metal as child; Metal is Nurtured at Chen, relying on mother while young and becoming strong of itself. Earth is born at Shen, finding support from child in old age. This image is exceptional and greatly fears breaking by year or luck. Again, suppose Bing sits upon Zi and employs You Metal from the month. Fire takes Metal as wife. Xin Metal is born at Zi, entering the husband’s house to nourish itself; Fire dies at You, relying on the wife’s Wealth to sustain its life. This image makes noble use of Wealth and Officer and greatly fears punishment or clash dispersing the configuration.〉[^27]
<!-- source: L206-L208 -->
**Eyes without pupils: Fire and Earth have boiled Gui Water dry.**

## 3. Rhapsody on Aversion and Affection
### 憎愛賦

No wealth exceeds purity and wholeness; no poverty exceeds conflict. No distinction exceeds refinement brought to fruition; no low station exceeds injury turned back upon oneself. Writing gleams like brocade when Noble Horse assembles at the Hall of Learning. The spirit is broad and capacious when Water and Fire combine in temperament. Deep counsel and distant foresight arise when Virtue Star dwells in a quiet palace; arts become mysterious and subtle when the imperial seat guards the lodge of letters. Kuigang holds the impulse toward inspired adaptation; Li and Kan are the gateways of intelligence. Noble Person, Lu, and Horse should be encountered; Robbery, Blade, and Void should be kept far away. Birth invites Noble Person’s affection; decline and Defeat meet the petty person’s dislike.[^53]

The four palaces in disarray lack benevolence and righteousness; the Five Phases generating one another give filial devotion and loyalty. Seal and Lu in places of punishment and clash leave the mind confused and the body busy. Day and hour within Ghost’s Storehouse give more worry than joy. A flourishing day stem brings few calamities; declining Wealth and Fate bring much distress. Toil for food and clothing comes from control at a flourishing place; success and failure in name and profit come from injury in noble ground. Lifelong calamity and blessing depend upon day and hour; a year’s good and ill rest upon qi and its course. Blessing Star possessing qi brings change and promotion; the year controlling and the course inauspicious scatter people and wealth. A dangerous major course gives birth to a hundred troubles; an auspicious passing year removes a thousand calamities.

Passing from no Extinction into Extinction imperils Wealth and Fate; seeking Birth and obtaining Birth fulfills name and profit. Triple and Sixfold Combination make good weighty and ill light; Seven Killings and the Four Inauspicious make calamity deep and blessing shallow. Promotion in rank and office must come in a year of Lu’s assembly; acquired property and increased fields must arise where Wealth combines. The Year Ruler’s oppressive clash signifies calamity; an injured major course offers little good. The year should generate the course; the course welcomes generating the body. The three positions generating one another make the year satisfactory. Wealth and Officer both flourishing should bring distinction in official life; Wealth and Food thriving together—how could they linger in a humble house?

Lu entering a ground of gathered generation makes wealth and distinction evident; Horse running toward flourishing Lu permits a judgment of splendor. To gain profit in dealings, Sixfold Combination must be encountered. The hour stem bearing Lu and turning toward the Origin must bring blessing in peace. A declining month and flourishing hour give abundance in early years; a heavy root and light master give lifelong drifting. Habitual profit in the marketplace must come from Wealth encountered at flourishing. Sudden distinction and the founding of a house must arise from Noble found within punishment. Master and root in season obtain a worthy woman’s support; Noble and Lu possessing affinity gain auspicious accord through a gentleman. Southbound commerce and northbound travel reveal an open Horse-road; peddling east and racing west must come from a favorable carriage-course.[^54]

With the day stem afflicted and weak, would Boniu dare complain against Heaven? With Lu and Horse faint and declining, Master Yan cannot escape a short life. Nothing is more ominous than Blade in a branch, nothing more auspicious than strength in a stem. Poor Horse and slight Wealth send men fleeing and women wandering. Heaven’s Net and Earth’s Mesh bring disaster or sudden harm. Robbery upon an exhausted road makes self-punishment a source of danger and doubt. Wealth encountered at Extinction makes it hard for wife and children to grow old together. Great and Lesser Depletion often ruin the house through gaming; Official Talisman and Death Talisman must bring recurring prison and lawsuits. If the Four Pillars meet Extinction again and the Three Fates suffer punishment and injury, penal labor or strangulation is hard to escape, and the torment of a tattooed face must finally be endured. Five Ghosts encountered leave no doubt of lightning injury or a tiger’s bite. Many inauspicious influences added permit a firm judgment of evil calamity and violent death. Women are often sexually degraded, men wild and reckless.[^55]

Asked about a person’s disposition—worthy or foolish, good or evil—first infer the flourishing or decline of Noble and sha; only then investigate ingenuity and inspired adaptability. Kuigang causes harm in the proud-hearted; Sixfold Combination brings good to the compliant. The secluded and untrammeled encounter Canopy and the lodges of solitude and emptiness. Those who rely upon power and impose their will encounter Indirect Officer, Rob Wealth, and Blade’s authority. Robbery and Blade generate mean avarice and dangerous stratagems. Strategy often comes from Ren and Gui; formidable force must rest upon Bing and Ding. Jia and Yi in accord give benevolence and generosity; Geng and Xin impaired leave resolution without firmness. Solitude and Imprisonment leave no vitality; breaking and Defeat give carelessness and dissipation. Punishment and battle make a person dull and stubborn; stillness and peace make one worthy and gifted. Restless failure comes from flourishing Fire; concealed endurance from abundant Metal.[^56]

Metal and Water commanding the season and generating one another, Fire and Earth obtaining their time and assisting one another, give sufficient food and clothing without anxious thought and establish the household without strained effort. With Virtue spirits further supporting them, the person must be esteemed in the village. Noble and Lu arching their positions must bring fame in the ministries and censorial halls. What is feared is blessing that does not bless; what is troubling is accomplishment that does not accomplish. Blessing fails when good encounters evil; accomplishment fails when the configuration is broken. Injure its form and blessing is injured; break its arrangement and calamity is invited. It is like seedlings meeting autumn drought, leaving winter granaries empty, or flowers struck by spring frost, leaving summer fruit unformed. Though intelligence and strategy abound, their application achieves nothing. Even with the ability to turn Heaven and shift its axle, one finds no fulfillment in establishing achievement.[^57]

Have you not seen Master Li boiled in a cauldron, Fan Zeng’s abscess upon his back, Yuanming returning east, Zimei departing west, Meng Yu finding no opportunity, and Feng Yan returning empty-handed? Maichen carried firewood and sang as he walked; Jiang Ge sat reading in bitter cold. There are shoots that never flower, and flowers that never bear fruit. With excessive injury and defeat, even a blessing amounts to no more than cutting grass and gathering firewood. A hundred arts and many abilities cannot avert hunger, cold, illness, and suffering, or confinement to the ditches. Fate makes it so.[^58]

How are wealth and distinction obtained together in their fullness? Nothing is greater than establishing a sound foundation, nothing more exceptional than refinement brought to fruition. Those who attain sagehood or worthiness are found in every age; the supremely rich and distinguished have always arisen in this way. One may be born within a Killings configuration and become eminent in letters or arms; one may dwell beneath Cap and Belt and possess great undertakings and unusual gifts. How are such subtleties inferred? First examine the Three Wonders and Four Blessings within the Hall of Learning; then examine, beyond the configuration, the one auspicious and the two fitting influences. Jiwei meeting Jiazi is auspicious; Renchen meeting Dingsi is a propitious sign. Renzi with Bingwu gives a person of elegant bearing and literary cultivation; Xinyou with Bingshen gives refinement and splendor. Yin and yang depend wholly on purity and excellence; creation most welcomes mutual generation. Hard to distinguish are the Sun’s Essence and Moon’s Brilliance; hard to fathom are the Jade Hall and Golden Coffer. Obtaining them gives honor; encountering them gives distinction.[^59]

Worthiness and folly, prominence and obscurity, are all fashioned upon creation’s potter’s wheel. A phoenix may be born from a kite, a snake become a dragon. Fragrant orchids continue among tangled weeds; withered trees may yet put forth growth upon wild mountains. Distinction while young and low station when old, obstruction first and passage afterward, arise through the major courses’ decline and flourishing and the consequent changes in wealth and rank. A pure configuration turning mixed brings the melancholy of fading spring; a course obtaining its time in old age gives ease in the late years. Thus the seasons have spring and autumn, and the moon fullness and waning.

We have seen children sustained by wealth and inherited privilege lose their support when a parent dies; we have also seen ploughmen and fishermen rise to unusual distinction when their course opens. Rank and stipend held for many years may end in a single morning. When appointed luck arrives, one meets the occasion; merely encountering Birth and flourishing does not guarantee freedom from misfortune. Affinity gives passage, lack of affinity stagnation; possession of the command gives good, clash gives ill. A year bringing Officer and Seal promises promotion in official life; a course meeting Food and Wealth permits even a commoner to prosper. Some rely on ancestors’ splendor in youth and borrow descendants’ distinction in maturity. Others suffer from childhood and remain without support into old age. The cause lies in the Four Pillars’ flourishing and decline and the major courses’ passage or obstruction.[^60]

Have you not seen withered trees fail to flourish even in spring, and vigorous branches withstand frost without decay? Day and hour further depleting year and month leave no good ending; flourishing qi in the birth hour turning toward the Origin must give late blessing. In antiquity, polished jade was worth linked cities; in the world, solitary people establish their own livelihood. They resemble what remains unharmed after the furnace’s refining, or what does not wither after the year’s cold. The subtlety of waxing and waning lies in adaptation; calamity and blessing require examination of decline and flourishing. May gentlemen who understand fate weigh these matters together.[^61]

## 4. Rhapsody on Waxing and Waning
### 消息賦

〈Annotated by Luoluzi; explained by Yuwuzi.〉[^62]

**Primal, a single qi before Heaven; pure and turbid endowment arises of itself. The Three Powers become manifest as images; the four seasonal qi unfold into the year.**

〈This traces the beginning of creation and the origin of the Three Fates. The Three Fates take the stem as Lu, called Heavenly Origin; the branch as Fate, called Human Origin; and nayin as Body, called Earthly Origin. Thus the ancients glimpsed creation, modeled Heaven and Earth, embodied yin and yang, and paired the Four Pillars into Eight Characters. This is the meaning of Luoluzi’s opening.〉[^63]
<!-- source: L368-L368 -->
**Take the stem as Lu: approach and departure determine poverty and wealth. Take the branch as Fate: examine backward and forward movement through their cycles.**

〈Stem resembles the trunk of a tree, branch its limbs. Taken together, stems are yang and branches yin; taken separately, each class has yin and yang. The ten stems’ Lu lodges among the twelve branches. Yang’s way moves forward; yin’s turns backward. Both are counted from Birth and lodge where their own tone reaches Coming to Office. Yang born and yin dying, yin born and yang dying: this is natural principle. In deriving Lu from the stem, there is approach and departure. Jia’s Lu is at Yin: Chou is called approach, Mao departure. Thus one position ahead of Lu is Yang Blade; one behind is Lu Storehouse. The classic says, “Approaching Lu gives life; turning from Lu gives death.” Is this not the determination of poverty and wealth through approach and departure?[^64]

In examining Fate through the branch, there is backward and forward movement. Yang men and yin women proceed forward from the birth month; yin men and yang women backward. The yin–yang endowment and its forward or reverse qi lie within stems and branches, returning from end to beginning, coming and going in cycles like the endless passage of cold and heat through the seasons. Hence “Take the branch as Fate: examine backward and forward movement through their cycles.” Tanying says: “Deriving Lu from the stem gives approach and departure; investigating good and ill gives shallow and deep effects. Departure with reversal can determine poverty; approach with the forward order can reveal wealth. Yet one must not follow a single track: there is also turning from Lu without poverty. Accordingly the branch is Human Origin, moving merchants through gain and loss. Men welcome, women send away; Obstruction and Peace occupy one another’s place. Assemblies of good or ill determine their effect.”〉
<!-- source: L370-L372 -->
**In a course, one branch governs ten years; conversion makes three days into one year. Refine the study of Rest and flourishing to attain subtlety; exhaust adaptation and transformation to reach the mysterious.**

〈The preceding account of stems and branches fixes the Eight Characters. Courses are the most essential part of the Three Fates, so their method is presented first for instruction. One branch governing ten years, three days converted into one year: this is the ancients’ method of establishing the courses. The conversion requires a clear count of the actual calendar interval. Fates differ in the depth of qi within a solar term; courses differ in being reckoned toward birth or toward the term. Rest or flourishing along the way must accord with the Eight Characters. Some welcome Birth and flourishing and dislike Rest and Defeat; others suit Rest and Defeat and dislike Birth and flourishing. Among these countless changes, who could participate without penetrating mystery, understanding the hidden, and weighing waxing and waning to exhaust creation’s subtlety?〉[^65]
<!-- source: L374-L374 -->
**As qi, what is coming advances and what has completed its work retreats: like a snake in ashes, like an eel in dust.**

〈Qi means the Five Phases’ qi distributed through the four seasons. In spring, Wood flourishes, Fire receives assistance, Earth is at Death, Metal Imprisonment, and Water Rest. What approaches through Coming to Office and Imperial Prosperity is the advancing future; what turns away through Rest, decline, Death, and Extinction is the retiring accomplished. The Five Phases’ qi advances and retreats in cycles. Whenever a person’s course occupies a branch, the assisted phase advances and the flourishing one retreats. Employ what holds authority, and it brings blessing; employ what lacks authority, and it gives no benefit. When a phase’s qi has passed its time, it retreats. Snakes and eels both belong to Fire. Fire at Imprisonment and Death becomes Earth; at Rest and decline it becomes ash. Si’s three creatures are snake, eel, and earthworm: hence snake and eel are known to be Fire, and in Imprisonment, Death, Rest, or decline they lie in dust or ash. Earth advances while Fire retreats.[^66]

The monk Ying says: “Eels and earthworms belong to Water and Earth, and must suffer when lodged in dust. Soaring Snake is the spirit of ash and Fire and delights in ashes. Ways gather by kind and things divide into groups. Accord with what they desire and there is good; oppose where they tend and there is ill. Creation can be observed through things themselves. Though people travel a course in the same palace, qi advances in some and retreats in others. The place occupied is no different, yet one fate has life and another death. Thus Rest and flourishing must be studied precisely, adaptation and transformation pursued to their limit.” This explanation has grasped the matter.〉
<!-- source: L376-L380 -->
**As presence, it establishes being from nonbeing; as absence, Heaven hangs down images to reveal the pattern. As constancy, it establishes benevolence and righteousness; as affairs, it may be seen or heard.**

〈The first pair makes clear that the Five Phases’ qi establishes presence from absence, and therefore borrows the five planets’ heavenly images to explain it. At the first unfolding of things, who makes them present? After the Great Ultimate, what can be called absent? Presence emerges from absence, absence arises from presence. In qi they form images; on Earth, shapes: change and transformation become manifest.〉

〈The Five Phases are the five planets in Heaven, the Five Sacred Peaks on Earth, and the five viscera in the person. Carried into conduct, they become the Five Constants. Constancy is a way that can endure. The *Changes* says, “Establishing Heaven’s way is yin and yang; establishing Earth’s way is yielding and firm; establishing humanity’s way is benevolence and righteousness.” Without benevolence and righteousness, humanity’s way cannot stand. The *Documents* says: “Second, the Five Affairs: first demeanor, second speech, third seeing, fourth hearing, fifth thought.” The Five Constants and Five Affairs are both transformations of the Five Phases communicating with human affairs. Character, choice, seeing, hearing, movement, and stillness never escape these numbers. “Seen or heard”: Metal, Wood, Water, Fire, and Earth are seen, while gong, shang, jue, zhi, and yu are heard; demeanor, speech, seeing, hearing, and thought are seen, while gravity, order, discernment, counsel, and sagacity are heard. The Five Phases’ uses cannot be exhausted. Who but someone of supple understanding and clear wisdom could examine them precisely and know them without speech?〉[^67]

<!-- source: L382-L384 -->
**Elevation is treasure; the exceptional is noble. General Star supports Virtue and Tianyi approaches; with root and master at Rest or Imprisonment, public life and retirement sink into obscurity.**

〈Elevation is the opposite of lowliness; the exceptional is the counterpart of the paired. Things rise through accumulation: their height is elevation, represented among the Five Phases by generation from above downward. A thing unequaled is exceptional: among the phases, different things nevertheless gather into a company. General Star here is the monthly general; Virtue means Heavenly and Monthly Virtue; Tianyi is the noble spirit. Birth year is root, birthday master; Rest and Imprisonment stand against Birth and flourishing. If the four pillars of year, month, day, and hour contain generation from above downward and the Three Wonders Yi, Bing, and Ding, further bearing General, Virtue, and Noble, with master and root at Birth and flourishing in their proper ground, this is “auspicious generals approaching together, blessing arriving as celebration,” a fate of the highest distinction. The rhapsody first calls elevation and exceptionality treasure and nobility, then warns against Rest and Imprisonment in master and root. Elevation and exceptionality are rare, but master and root are essential; the several directional spirits and sha are discussed secondarily. Thus the Five Phases come first, Birth and flourishing are superior, and General, Virtue, and Noble are the most auspicious among the spirits and sha.[^68]

Xu says: “Elevation concerns master and root. The branches governing longevity, Wealth, calamity, and blessing in a fate are also called elevated. Exceptionality concerns Lu and Horse; Wealth, Officer, Seal, and Food are also called exceptional. Virtue is the day’s meeting with its virtuous branch, namely Sixfold Combination. In **Renyin year / Gengxu month / Guimao day / Yimao hour**, the ninth month’s general is at Mao and supports the birthday. The ninth month’s Five Phases are Metal and Earth; the Sixfold relations combine Mao with Xu, Yi with Geng, and Wu with Gui. With none of the Five Phases at Rest or Defeat, there is distinction.” This seems not to be the rhapsody’s meaning.〉
<!-- source: L386-L388 -->
**Hooked Array obtaining its position does not neglect small acts of trust, and thus completes benevolence; True Warrior holding authority reveals great ability and receives the auspicious token.**

〈Water and Earth illustrate the remaining phases. Hooked Array is Earth’s general and corresponds among the Constants to trustworthiness. True Warrior is Water’s spirit and corresponds to wisdom. Trustworthiness suffices to reach sagehood; wisdom suffices to comprehend its Way. Here the Five Phases’ use is especially fine. “Obtaining position” is Wu and Ji born in the seventh month: mother in the child’s country. “Holding authority” is Ren and Gui born in the seventh month: child in the mother’s house. The two share a source, both born at Shen. Xu takes Wu and Ji seated at Yin or Mao or the Hai–Mao–Wei combination, and Ren and Gui seated at Wu or Si or Chen, Xu, Chou, and Wei, with Officer, Seal, Lu, and Horse below in flourishing, assistance, Storehouse, or Tomb, as obtaining position and holding authority. This seems contrary to the rhapsody. Better simply to take Earth born in the four seasonal endings and Water meeting winter’s three months.〉[^69]
<!-- source: L390-L392 -->
**Without benevolence or righteousness: Geng and Xin crossing Jia and Yi. Right and wrong in contention: Ren and Gui feared by Bing and Ding.**

〈The preceding account of authority and position entails neither crossing nor mutual fear. Jia meeting Geng, Yi meeting Xin, Bing meeting Ren, and Ding meeting Gui resemble two women living together or two men dwelling side by side: yin and yang do not unite, and no auspicious result is formed. Geng and Xin govern righteousness, Jia and Yi benevolence; crossing makes them neither benevolent nor righteous. Bing and Ding govern ritual propriety, Ren and Gui wisdom; mutual fear makes right and wrong uncertain. But Geng combining with Yi and Xin joining Jia let firmness and yielding bear upon one another, with benevolence and righteousness mutually effective: this is not crossing. Bing meeting Gui as Officer, or Ding combining with Ren as Lu, pairs yin and yang and gives Water and Fire in Completion: this is not mutual fear. Another interpretation calls Jiashen and Yiyou unbenevolent, Gengyin and Xinmao unrighteous, because Yin, Shen, Geng, and Jia cross, while Mao, You, Yi, and Xin battle in secret. Bing with Ren makes Bing wrong and Ren right; Ding with Gui makes Gui right and Ding wrong. Zi and Wu are alike in this, Si and Hai follow the same rule. Only when a fate encounters one of these positions may the judgment be made.〉[^70]
<!-- source: L394-L396 -->
**Thus former worthies humbled themselves amid the world and sought immortality: honoring the Buddha, they cultivated stillness in Li’s palace; returning to the Way, they sought mystery in Water’s mansion.**

〈Benevolence and righteousness are often estranged through gain and loss; right and wrong continually entangled in honor and decline. People use these forces daily without knowing them and never find rest. Thus former worthies humbled themselves amid the world and sought immortality, cutting attachment, diminishing selfishness, and reducing desire. Some honored the Buddha to extinguish the heart’s Fire; some returned to the Way to enrich the kidneys’ essence. They guarded essence and spirit within and removed illusion and delusion without, understanding that things and self possess no independent being and discerning form and emptiness to their ultimate ground. This is the meaning.〉
<!-- source: L398-L400 -->
**Thus the Five Phases communicate with the Way, and their uses have many doors. In the worthy they are ordered, in the unworthy confused; subtle use accomplishes, incapacity defeats.**

〈Nowhere is the Way absent; nothing is outside it. The Five Phases’ transformations communicate with the Great Way—what do they not embrace? Their uses are not one, hence “many doors.” The discerning take them to cultivate stillness; immortals take them to seek mystery. Who but a person of sudden insight could participate? The worthy obtain them, exhaust principle and nature, and attain the Five Phases’ subtle use. The foolish lose them, remaining obscured and gaining nothing. The capable nourish them and obtain blessing; the incapable ruin them and incur calamity. The *Changes* says, “Without the fitting person, the Way does not act in vain.”〉[^71]

**Forms seen and unseen are present at every time; threads drawn and undrawn continue through all antiquity.**

〈Seen and unseen forms: the ten stems’ Lu lodges in the twelve branches, where both visible and invisible forms exist. Jia’s Lu at Yin is visible. If Yin is absent but Xu appears, the Five Rats’ Origin cycling reaches Jiaxu; Xu is Jia’s Hall of Lu. This is unseen Lu. Jia takes Xin as Officer, and Xin’s Lu is at You: Jia receiving the Golden Rooster has visible Officer. If You is absent but Wei appears, the Heavenly-Officer cycling, “Jia enters the flock of sheep,” places Xin above Wei. This is unseen Officer.[^72]

Drawn and undrawn threads: yang qi is born at Zi, flourishes at Mao, and ends at Wu; yin qi is born at Wu, flourishes at You, and ends at Zi. When yang is born, yin dies; when yin alters, yang transforms. Zi and Wu are the endless beginning and end of yin–yang transformation and generation. Yin at its extreme gives birth to yang, yang at its extreme to yin. Qi buds and pushes forth from Zi and Wu, emerging and entering without hindrance, coming and going without exhaustion, like silken threads continuous through all antiquity. The *Great Mystery* says: “Forms seen and unseen, threads drawn and undrawn: days shift, months change, heat departs and cold arrives. Decline and rise alternate, Rest and flourishing succeed one another; one becomes manifest while another darkens, one contracts while another extends. Continuous as though abiding, they are present at every time.” Yin, yang, and the Five Phases possess the seen and unseen, the drawn and undrawn. Their principle is mysterious, their impulse to manifestation subtle. Nothing lacks it, no time is without it. Since Heaven, Earth, and humanity came into being, it has been so. What matters is how sudden understanding is attained.〉
<!-- source: L406-L408 -->
**Thus Hegong feared his Seven Killings, and Father Xuan dreaded his Original Spirit. Emei expounded the Three Lives, leaving neither scholar nor commoner wholly secure; Guigu spread his Nine Fates, condensing them through the stars’ observation. Now I gather the essentials of the several schools and set aside the capacities of their partial views. What has not been understood must be penetrated in its intricacies; subtlety requires inspired insight.**

〈Original Spirit and Seven Killings are the fiercest sha. Since fate receives the Five Phases, who can escape such dangers? Ancient sages and worthies, such as Heshang Gong among immortals and King Wenxuan at the summit of sagehood, still feared both: how much more everyone else? Thus books were written to aid the world, announcing good and ill, calamity and blessing before they appeared. Emei’s immortal expounding the Three Lives was certainly precise; Guiguzi spreading the Nine Fates was certainly penetrating. Yet their mysterious statements are obscure and hard to fathom, hence “condensed through the stars’ observation” and “neither scholar nor commoner wholly secure.” The Three Lives are Lu, Fate, and Body. The Nine Fates are the two palaces of Body and Fate, the two positions of Lu and Horse, and birth year, conception, month, day, and hour. Luoluzi gathered the schools’ essential meaning, set aside their partial capacities, and expressed an understanding obtained in his own heart. His writing greatly elucidates the mysterious meaning and penetrates the intricate workings. The student’s inspired understanding and adaptability make it good.〉[^73]

**Your servant comes from Lanye and from youth admired the authentic way. Entering the market, I had not the wonder of the suspended gourd; traveling the streets, I had not the power of the transformed staff. I still the single qi to concentrate spirit, and bring the Five Phases to rest to communicate with the Way.**

〈“Your servant” addresses a ruler. Lanye is a place-name: the speaker recounts his origin. Admiring the authentic way from youth shows the greatness of his aspiration. The suspended gourd and transformed staff are the stories of Hugong and Fei Changfang. Praising the predecessors’ supreme subtlety, he regrets his own incapacity. Cutting off desires without and holding no thoughts within, he stills the single qi to concentrate spirit and brings the Five Phases to rest to communicate with the Way. He therefore calls this rhapsody *Waxing and Waning*: creation has diminution and increase.〉[^74]

**Qian and Kun establish male and female; Metal and Wood determine firm and yielding. Day and night alternate as ruler and minister; blue-green and red in their season are father and son.**

〈This is the great design of creation’s waxing and waning. Qian belongs to yang: Heaven’s way, the ruler’s way, the husband’s way. Kun belongs to yin: Earth’s way, the minister’s way, the wife’s way. Movement is Qian’s substance, called opening the door; stillness is Kun’s substance, called closing it. Qian and Kun establish the union of yin and yang, female and male. Through their communication the Five Phases transform. This is precisely why the *Changes* begins with Qian and Kun. Benevolence yields and righteousness is firm: these are the natures of Wood and Metal. One yin and one yang, firmness and yielding, impel one another. Firmness without yielding cannot engender alteration; yielding without firmness cannot engender transformation. Day is firm, producing alteration and advance; night is yielding, producing transformation and retreat. Firmness and yielding accumulated make change and transformation; days and nights accumulated make advance and retreat. Day is yang, imaging the ruler; night yin, imaging the minister. Subtly, their way has waxing and waning; manifestly, fullness and emptiness; in its divisions, darkness and light; in its numbers, life and death. Peace and Obstruction, decrease and increase, beginning and end following one another, old and new succeeding one another, honor and disgrace arriving, blessing and Lu arising—all have their root here. The spirits of the Five Phases are called emperors. The eastern Blue-Green Emperor as father generates the southern Red Emperor as son: blue-green and red convey the father’s way to the child. Within yin, yang, and the Five Phases lie the relations of ruler and minister, father and son, husband and wife. Is this not creation’s great meaning communicating with human relations?〉[^75]

**Do not take one road as the sole track, nor infer everything from one principle. There are times when winter meets blazing heat or summer grass encounters frost; there are creatures such as the yin-rat dwelling in ice and the numinous tortoise lodging in fire.**

〈The way of yin, yang, and the Five Phases is subtle and hard to penetrate, concealed and difficult to measure. It cannot be followed by one track or inferred from one principle alone. Winter cold and summer heat are the regular principle and the season’s proper condition. Winter encountering heat or summer grass meeting frost is out of season. If a command operates outside its season, can ordinary principle confine it? Rat with Fire and tortoise with ice are the established principle and their kinds’ fitting association. But yin-rat in ice and numinous tortoise in fire depart from their kinds. If a creature occupies a place contrary to its kind, can it be discussed along one road? The regular is readily investigated, the exceptional hard to exhaust. Is creation easily spoken of? Master Zou blew the pitch-pipes and a cold valley returned to spring; a filial woman suffered injustice and frost flew in the sixth month. Ancient and recent records contain many such anomalies. They cannot be denied as transformations of yin, yang, and the Five Phases. Fire-rat hair spun into cloth and ice-silkworm fat served upon a platter are known in the world. The *Classic of Divine Marvels* says that in the north ice extends ten thousand li, a hundred zhang thick, with a rat weighing ten thousand jin and hair more than a chi long concealed within. This is yin-rat dwelling in ice. The *Erya* says, “First, the numinous tortoise ... tenth, the fire tortoise.” Guo Pu’s encomium says, “Heaven produces numinous creatures, the tortoises of ten kinds; some wander in fire.” This is the numinous tortoise lodging in fire.[^76]

Xu Ziping identifies one yang born at the winter solstice and one yin born at the summer solstice with winter heat and summer frost. He takes Gui’s Lu at Zi as Human Origin and Gui as Bing’s Officer-Seal; Wu’s Lu at Si as Human Origin and Wu as Gui’s Officer-Seal, explaining these as yin-rat in ice and numinous tortoise in fire. I fear this is not the rhapsody’s meaning.〉

〈Winter heat, summer frost, ice-rat, and fire-tortoise depart from ordinary yin–yang principle and the usual response of kinds; hence they are seldom fathomed and hard to exhaust. The present lines turn back to clarify the preceding ones. Though winter sometimes meets heat, its three months must mostly have little heat; though summer grass encounters frost, its ninety days must mostly have abundant yang. Since cold and heat have their regular course, the mystery of yin and yang can be glimpsed. Calamity and blessing should be inferred through principle; portents become manifest through corresponding kinds. Specialists in the Three Fates, Five Phases, passing years, and courses judge Peace when a course meets flourishing, assistance, and proper position, and Obstruction when it meets Rest, Imprisonment, and loss of position. By discussing the regular alone, they may hope for eight or nine correct judgments in ten, and that suffices. Alternatively: the responses of calamity and blessing in people’s years and courses are as variable as portents, and the record of things as hard to exhaust. Even practitioners can hardly hope to grasp eight or nine in ten of this principle. Heaven and Earth themselves do not accomplish everything—how much less a person! This reading is also tenable.〉[^77]

**Birth at Rest and Defeat gives solitude and poverty in youth; flourishing country encountered in age gives hardship in the declining years. Ill first and good afterward: a turbid source, a clear stream. Good first and ill afterward: a sweet root, bitter fruit.**

〈Though the body follows the course, it must borrow the course’s support; though force must obtain its season, it must borrow the season to ride that force. Birth and the vigorous years suit courses in flourishing country; late age and declining years suit constrained ground. This is adapting waxing and waning to what is fitting, at ease with Rest and flourishing. Initial exhaustion followed by prosperity in later years is a turbid source and clear stream; flourishing youth followed by [loneliness] in old age is bitter fruit from a sweet root. Weigh the courses’ qi and pursue their source: first examine the foundation’s thickness or thinness, then clarify the courses’ beginning and end. Though not every one of a hundred judgments may strike home, eight or nine in ten may be hoped for.[^78]

In a person’s fate, establish the year as the honored ruler; conception, month, day, and hour support it in descending order. Establish the year as the Four Pillars’ ruler and father, presiding over good and ill. Take the month to clarify the basis of the courses’ qi and infer fullness and emptiness. Take the day to observe signs of security or danger and examine the source of suffering and joy. Take the hour to determine high or low standing and decide the timing of life and death. Take conception to distinguish the beginnings of childhood protection and investigate what precedes establishment. The month governs the beginning, the day the middle, the hour the end; the year commands all. Beginning and end must aid one another, earlier and later respond: then wealth and distinction are complete and Wealth and Lu both manifest, without the differences of good first/ill last or ill first/good last. This is not easily obtained. Even prosperity in the middle and lower positions alone may make a fate of real accomplishment.〉

〈In discussing fate, conception is root, month shoot, day flower, hour fruit. Investigating roots reveals the shoots; seeing flowers permits knowledge of fruit. Thus the sage observes signs before emergence and examines the source to understand its later growth. Xu says: “To know a course’s good and ill, first examine victory and defeat in its original root. Noble in the root must give distinction when the course reaches Noble; Wealth in the root develops Wealth in a Wealth course; calamity in the root produces calamity when that course arrives.” This explanation is also sound.〉

**Combination without apparent combination is hard for later students to understand; obtaining one and dividing it into three was not recorded by former worthies.**

〈The Way is established in two, accomplished in three, and altered in five; Heaven and Earth’s numbers complete their ten through pairing. Combination without apparent combination: Jia combines with Ji; Ji is absent but Wu appears, for Wu contains Ji’s Lu. Yin combines with Hai; Hai is absent but Ren appears, for Hai holds Ren’s Lu. Or Yin, Wu, and Xu combine: Yin is absent but Jia appears, for Yin holds Jia’s Lu. Obtaining one and dividing it into three: Jia obtaining Ji is one combination, obtaining Wu a second, obtaining Hai a third. This is obtaining one Lu and distinguishing three Lu, continuous in principle with the earlier visible/unseen forms and drawn/undrawn threads.[^81]

Li Xuzhong discusses a configuration of complete stem-and-branch combination. Year, month, day, hour, and conception—the five positions—can combine their stems and branches completely: where Zi is named, Chou is present; where Yin, Hai; where Jia, Ji; where Yi, Geng. If the five Lu-stem positions contain Jia, Yi, Bing, Ding, and Wu, they naturally combine up Ji, Geng, Xin, Ren, and Gui. If the twelve branches contain Yin, Mao, Chen, Si, and Wu, they naturally combine up Wei, Shen, You, Xu, and Hai. Add Lu and Horse at Zi and Chou, and the ten stems and twelve branches are completely combined. Xu says: “Combination without apparent combination means configurations such as Punishing Combination or Chou/Zi Remotely Combining Si. Obtaining one means Yin already punishing Si or Chou breaking Si. ## 6. Rhapsody on Penetrating Clarity
### 明通賦

〈Written by Xu Ziping of Donghai; explained by Wan Yuwu of Yishui.〉

The Great Ultimate divides into Heaven and Earth; the single qi differentiates into yin and yang. The Five Phases flow forth, transforming and generating the myriad things. Human endowment in fate—poverty and wealth, high and low station—arises from this. The practitioner who knows its workings can determine good and ill, calamity and blessing. 〈This traces creation’s beginning.〉[^152]
<!-- source: L698-L700 -->
**In reading a fate, take the day stem as master, commanding the Three Origins and pairing the Eight Characters’ stems and branches.**

〈Heaven has four seasons to create things; a house has four pillars to establish its form; a fate has Four Pillars to inscribe honor and decline. The interpreter takes the birthday’s heavenly stem exclusively as Fate-Origin’s master. The branch is Earthly-Origin Lu; what it conceals is Human-Origin longevity. Eight Characters means the Four Pillars’ heavenly stems and earthly branches. The *Chapter on Continuing Goodness* says: “Set out the Four Pillars, then distinguish the Three Powers; take the day stem’s Heavenly Origin to pair the Eight Characters’ stems and branches.” This is the meaning.〉[^153]
<!-- source: L702-L704 -->
**In discussing courses, begin with the monthly branch, distinguishing the four seasons to raise the Five Phases’ waxing and waning.**

〈Major courses begin with the monthly branch, hence month is the governing thread. Examine the depth of qi within its solar term and which seasonal division is obtained: spring Wood, summer Fire, autumn Metal, winter Water, and seasonal-ending Earth. Initial and middle qi differ in waxing and waning. Whether the course moves forward or backward, flourishes or declines, assists or drains the Eight Characters, controls or generates them, all begins here. Naming day stem first and monthly branch next presents what matters most.〉
<!-- source: L706-L708 -->
**Approach Coming to Office and flourishing to accomplish; enter a configuration to attain distinction. Officer, Seal, Wealth, and Food are auspicious: settled balance fulfills the good. Killings, Injury, Owl, and Defeat are inauspicious: redirected use makes blessing.**

〈Coming to Office and Imperial Prosperity in the Four Pillars are the original palace’s grounds of completion. Entering a configuration gives distinction; breaking it low station. Officer, Seal, Wealth, and Food are originally auspicious, but must be without injury, control, clash, punishment, breaking, or Defeat to form settled balance and complete goodness: that is entering the configuration. Killings, Injury, Owl, and Defeat are originally harmful, but restraint, removal or retention, combination and transformation can redirect their use to blessing: this too enters a configuration. The following configurations reveal their preferences and aversions. The Four Auspicious and Four Inauspicious are the most important categories, so they come first. The rhapsody says, “Day Master should be vigorous and flourishing; useful spirit must not be injured.”〉[^154]
<!-- source: L710-L712 -->
**Complete stores lie within Chen, Xu, Chou, and Wei; Birth dwells securely at Si, Hai, Yin, and Shen. Zi and Wu reverse accomplishment and defeat; Mao and You exchange entrance and departure.**

〈The twelve branches contain the ten stems, with life and death, accomplishment and defeat, entrance and departure exchanged. *Walking Alone* says Chen, Xu, Chou, and Wei are the Four Storehouse configuration; Yin, Shen, Si, and Hai Four Births; Zi, Wu, Mao, and You Four Defeats. The *Chapter on Preferences and Aversions* says Wealth, Officer, and Seal-Ribbon are stored complete in the four seasonal endings, and Officer Star and Wealth qi are born and dwell at Yin, Shen, Si, and Hai. Calling Zi/Wu Heaven and Earth’s foundational pillars and Mao/You the Sun and Moon’s gateways merely identifies their ground while neglecting their principle.〉
<!-- source: L714-L716 -->
**Stems and branches possess unseen forms: take presence from absence. Solar-term qi retains residual numbers: seek distinction within mixture.**

〈This sums up creation’s subtlety. Unseen stem-and-branch forms and residual solar-term numbers are the Human Origin concealed within the twelve branches: taking presence from absence and distinguishing mixture. Remotely Combining Si and arching or enclosing configurations use heavenly stems to obtain things within earthly branches—what is that but presence from absence? The first three *ke* of Zi belong to Ren Water; the first three of Chou to Gui Water; the first three of Yin to Gen Earth. Solar-term qi concealed in one sign has several rulers—what is that but distinguishing mixture? The rhapsody says, “Combination without apparent combination is hard for later students to know; obtaining one and dividing it into three was not recorded by former worthies.” *Continuing Goodness* says, “Seen and unseen forms are present at every time.”〉[^155]

<!-- source: L718-L718 -->
**When good and evil cross, welcome the transformation of evil and elevation of good; when good and ill are mixed, fear injury to good and additions to ill.**

〈This turns to the method of reading fate from taking presence and distinguishing mixture. A Jia day seeing Bing/Ding turning from Lu encounters evil and ill; seeing Wu/Ji Wealth encounters good. Bing and Ding transforming Wood and assisting Wealth is transforming evil and elevating good. Yi Wood controlling and injuring Wealth is injuring good and adding ill. The following examples explain it: Wealth and Seal crossing welcome Officer/Killings transforming them; mixed Officer/Killings welcome Seal-Ribbon. Without Seal, Wealth Horse assists transformation; without Wealth or Seal, Yang Blade combines them, or Food God/Hurting Officer restrains them. Conversely, a restrainer of Killings damaged so that Killings cannot be held, or Seal’s transformation broken by Wealth, turns to ill.〉[^156]
<!-- source: L720-L722 -->
**Thus a configuration obtained and the Origin attended give wealth or distinction; an offended precinct and broken configuration give early death or poverty.**

〈Configuration means the proper palaces of Triple Combination and the four mixed positions, such as Hai–Mao–Wei Wood or Chen–Xu–Chou–Wei Earth. Hai–Mao–Wei is the original configuration for Jia/Yi, Seal for Bing/Ding, Officer for Wu/Ji, Wealth for Geng/Xin, Injury for Ren/Gui. Origin is the original precinct or temple: Gui attending the Origin at Zi, Ji Earth at Chou/Wei, and the like. Obtained as Fate-Origin, it gives longevity; as Officer or Seal, distinction; as Wealth, riches; as turning from Lu while generating Wealth, greater riches. Any one obtained without clash, punishment, control, or breaking gives achievement, wealth, and distinction. The contrary denies them. Offending the precinct: nobility at Zi fears control and breaking from Chou/Wei, clash from Wu, punishment from Mao. One Zi with two Wu loses its whole integrity; half obtained gives half blessing. Breaking the configuration: Shen–Zi–Chen fearing Yin–Wu–Xu’s clash is profoundly ominous. Yet if the employed star is empty and forms Flying Lu and Horse, distinction instead follows. Infer the rest.〉[^157]
<!-- source: L724-L726 -->
**Gain and loss are held in balance; advance and retreat recur.**

〈Obtaining and attending, offending and breaking, are creation’s gain and loss, advance and retreat. One gains as another loses; strength advances while weakness retreats. Their balance and recurrence change beyond easy measurement and require precise observation. Jia attending its precinct at Yin fears Shen’s clash. Yet two Yin against one Shen, or two Jia against one Geng, are harmless: Jia has force and advances. Yin clashed by Shen may obtain Hai for rescue. Hai firm and abundant gives complete blessing; Hai controlled by Wu or clashed by Si fails. Numerous Hai need not fear these: strength advances. Advance and retreat succeed, accomplishment and defeat alternate. Observe which side year and luck assist to know blessing or calamity.〉

〈These configurations are also explained in *Preferences and Aversions*. **Dingsi / Bingwu / Jiayin / Jiazi** arches Chou Noble. **Renzi / Dingwei / Dingsi / Dingwei** has Zi in the year clashing up Wu, hence great distinction. **Guimao / Gengshen / Wuchen / Wuwu** arches Si Lu. **Xinchou / Xinchou / Jiayin / Jiazi** fills Chou; it is instead discussed through Xin Direct Officer. **Jiwei / Wuchen / Wuyin / Wuwu** has many Officer/Killings influences; Yin clashing Shen opens through clash, so arching cannot form. **Renchen / Wushen / Jiwei / Jisi** fits this configuration and is greatly noble.〉[^194]
<!-- source: L1004-L1004 -->
**Officer and Seal secretly combining Heaven and Earth give evident distinction; blessing and virtue hidden in branches are more concentrated still.**

〈Food God secretly combining Direct Officer and Indirect Wealth secretly combining Direct Seal are meant. Jia employs Xin Officer: abundant Bing can secretly combine Xinwei as Officer. Jia employs Gui Seal: flourishing Wu can secretly combine Guichou as Seal. Zi in the branches combining Chou, or Wu combining Wei, completes concealed blessing and virtue. Blessing means Blessing-Star Noble, virtue Heavenly/Monthly Virtue; alternatively Blessing-Virtue Refined Qi or Tianyi Noble. Concealment within branches is subtle. Jia, Wu, or Geng not seeing Chou/Wei but obtaining Ji already qualifies; Ji in Chou/Wei’s palace is finer still. No Mao/Yi breaking it, with birth in a seasonal-ending month and Ji Earth in command, makes Noble Person pleased, the future exalted, the wife attractive, and her Wealth obtained.[^195]

Another account says Jia seeing Chou welcomes Yang Blade seated there as Tianyi on duty; Wei in a night birth obtains force and complete blessing. The reverse halves it. Jia, Wu, and Geng complete in the Four Pillars obtaining Yichou are Gathered Noble, increasing blessing. One Geng and one Jia seeing three or four Chou/Wei branches are Assembled Noble, also increasing it. Root/master and Noble both born and flourishing need only one sign for complete blessing. Triple and Sixfold Combination are welcome; Rest, Imprisonment, Void, and breaking are feared. The poem says:〉

> Noble Person upon the imperial seat meets generation;  
> In flourishing Officer’s country, the name forms early.  
> But Rest, Imprisonment, and broken restraint  
> Leave empty fame far from the Three Excellencies.

<!-- source: L1006-L1008 -->
**The Five Phases’ proper Noble fears punishment, clash, control, and harm; auspicious spirits in the Four Pillars welcome Coming to Office, flourishing, generation, and combination.**

〈Proper Noble means proper-qi Officer Star, as Jia born in You month. You’s self-punishment, Mao’s clash, Ding’s control, and Xu’s harm injure its noble qi. Alternatively Noble means the noble spirit: Jia seeing Chou, which contains Xin Direct Officer. Mao or Yi in the pillars controls and damages Chou Noble. Si/You’s Triple Combination is welcome, preventing Yi Wood’s control and clash from entering Chou. Zi combining Chou likewise separates Wood’s control. With Yimao, Zi and Mao punish one another and cannot control Chou/Wei Tianyi. Auspicious spirits are Officer, Seal, Wealth, Food, Wonders, Noble, Blessing, and Virtue. Any one obtaining Birth, Imperial Prosperity, Coming to Office, proper Storehouse, or Triple/Sixfold Combination gives wealth and distinction. The poem says:〉[^196]

> One strong influence obtained at birth,  
> Or day and hour approaching Lu and Horse’s country:  
> Examine support and combination before and behind;  
> Surely brocade robes will enter the central hall.

〈*Preferences and Aversions* says proper Noble avoids punishment, clash, breaking, and harm, while Four-Pillar stems and branches welcome Triple and Sixfold Combination. Yet it also says many combinations are Coveting Combination and Forgetting Officer. Both meanings must be considered.〉
<!-- source: L1010-L1012 -->
**Bath encountering sha sends the earthly soul to Fengdu; an original offense injured again sends the heavenly soul to the mountain court.**

〈Continuing the warning about punishment, clash, control, and harm, Naked Form meeting sha is feared because generation is slight and control heavy. An original natal offense is already unfavorable; repetition in year and luck must cause death. Officer/Killings originally impure after removal, with no Food God to resolve them, kills when repeated in year and luck. Original damage to Seal kills when repeated in a passing year. All injured useful spirits are included. These four clauses come from Luoluzi’s original text.〉
<!-- source: L1014-L1016 -->
**Fearing Killings and meeting it brings an early end; fearing a barrier and falling into it brings death.**

〈This further explains the preceding. A natal barrier or sha—Jia seeing Gengshen as Killings, for example—repeated in year and luck without rescue brings early death. Yearly Killings is heavier. Seal transforms it, Food restrains it, Blade combines it, bodily strength opposes it. A flourishing-Killings course can also give an early end. Jia seeing Chen reaches the utmost yang number and the Iron-Snake Barrier. Ren seeing Chou, Geng Xu, Bing Wei or Shen are likewise yang barriers, called heavy. Yi seeing Chen, Gui Chou, Xin Xu, Ding Wei or Shen are yin barriers, slightly lighter. An offense in the Four Pillars repeated in a passing year, with a resting or imprisoned course, causes death or harm by spirits, sha, ghosts, or thieves. Another account says barrier/sha names are numerous, not exclusively Seven Killings and yang barriers; their detail appears earlier under longevity and children’s barriers and sha.〉[^197]
<!-- source: L1018-L1020 -->
**Drawing a barrier-sha into combination wrongly injures the body; middle and lower extinguished bring violent shortening of life.**

〈Weak Bing Fire seeing Xinwei combines with Xin and approaches Xin’s palace. Wei is Bing’s extreme yang barrier; Ren further controlling it brings death. Xinhai as Killings’ ground is more urgent still and brings an unexpected offense. **Bingxu / Dingyou / Xinyou / Yiwei** died in Renshen year. Middle and lower extinguished: Renxu day sits upon Wealth and Killings; their presence within the day branch permits longevity. But Renchen luck gathers Ren/Gui Water in Chen, controlling and breaking Fire/Earth in Xu. Without other rescue, life ends early. This is Reversely Clashing Fate-Origin. When Earth flourishes, Water flourishes; Earth’s body itself collapses—how could it rescue? When Water flourishes, Fire goes out: hence middle and lower extinguished, the middle/lower division of Earthly and Human Origins. Infer other cases.〉[^198]
<!-- source: L1022-L1024 -->
**Hurting Officer seeing Officer brings a hundred troubles; driving Horse away and meeting Horse brings a thousand labors.**

〈Each specifically meets what it fears. Only Wealth Star can resolve Hurting Officer’s poison and soothe Officer’s anger, turning calamity auspicious. Only Officer/Killings can restrain Blade’s robbery when Horse is driven away. Though laborious, Wealth is obtained. With many Companions, others strong and self weak, some Wealth remains despite depletion, but the share is unequal: only a third or fourth.〉

<!-- source: L1026-L1028 -->
**Wealth meeting Yang Blade is much injured; Seal seeing wife-Wealth is not broken.**

〈This shares the foregoing meaning. Yang Blade is most feared: it breaks Wealth configurations, seizes Seal configurations, and angrily clashes Officer configurations. Only Seven Killings’ restraint is welcome. Yin Blade has slight force and does no harm. Seal seeing wife-Wealth concerns Coveting Wealth and Damaging Seal. Repetition in year and luck gives lost wealth, injury to wife, or litigation through her. If Wealth was originally absent or weak, its appearance in year and luck is lighter. Only Officer/Killings advancing generation can resolve and reconcile it, instead bringing recommendation and fame.〉[^199]
<!-- source: L1030-L1032 -->
**Food God meeting Owl without Wealth gives an early end; a weak body with Wealth repeatedly meeting Direct Seal is also ominous. Restraining Killings and meeting Seal, with clash, brings execution; a strong Fate without Officer, meeting Seven Killings alone, is better still.**

〈*Preferences and Aversions* says Seven Killings fully manifest in the pillars with a weak body gives extreme poverty and no ground to stand upon. This is the meaning.〉[^200]
<!-- source: L1034-L1036 -->
**Three Punishments in opposing clash produce violent calamity; Yang Blade in opposing combination brings extraordinary harm. Bath following generation makes a homeless guest; Rest and Imprisonment seeing Killings leave a person unburied.**

〈Bath, Rest, and Imprisonment all mean bodily weakness. Following generation makes drifting; meeting Killings makes injury.〉[^201]
<!-- source: L1038-L1040 -->
**Rob Wealth beneath the month gives no Wealth: welcome Killings without Seal, and gain becomes possible.**

〈Killings restrains Blade; Seal transforms Killings, which can then no longer restrain Blade. Hence welcome Killings and remove Seal.〉
<!-- source: L1042-L1044 -->
**Seal broken in secret, or injured close at hand: welcome Officer without Food, and enfeoffment increases.**

〈Wealth breaks Seal, Officer generates it. Food injures Officer and generates Wealth, so Seal is hurt still more. Hence welcome Officer and remove Food.〉
<!-- source: L1046-L1048 -->
**Mixed Officer and Killings bring low station and affliction; too many brothers scatter and divide. Seal without restraint favors letters; restraint without Seal favors arms. Both together leave toil without accomplishment.**

〈Seal transforms Killings, Food restrains it. With transformation, do not restrain; with restraint, do not transform. Too much of both removes Killings’ qi and becomes unfavorable. Yang Blade relies wholly on Killings’ restraint or transformation into Seal, as with Wu day in Wu month. Employing only one of restraint and transformation allows accomplishment.〉
<!-- source: L1050-L1052 -->
**Lu turned away and Horse driven off bring hunger and cold; Wealth and Seal breaking one another tie shut the purse. Welcome Officer bearing Killings as authority, love Killings bearing Officer as nobility. Either alone leaves petty frustration.**

〈These cases require repeated Officer/Killings to restrain Companions and Rob Wealth and generate Wealth, or generate Seal-Ribbon and transform Wealth. A single one is too weak: how could it fulfill the intention?〉[^202]

<!-- source: L1054-L1056 -->
**Owl and Seal mixed bring favor and disgrace; excessive Wealth and Horse steal qi. Welcome a flourishing body as blessing; fear weak courses that produce calamity.**

〈Indirect and Direct Seals mixed, or both kinds of Wealth present, cannot have their generation fully received or their Wealth jointly borne by a weak body. Distinguish direct/indirect and strong/weak. Powerful Indirect Seal and Wealth, with flourishing body and strong luck, bring sudden blessing; Direct Seal and Wealth are alike.〉
<!-- source: L1058-L1060 -->
**Officer and Lu controlled and broken give early death; Storehouses and Tombs clashed apart leave no food. Repeated breaking without support is feared; Companions able to rescue are welcome.**

〈Officer means Officer Star, Lu proper Lu. Jia seeing Xin and Yin, then also Gengshen and Si or Wu, dies early when year and luck repeat them. A flourishing body with Companions can nevertheless be judged through strength. Jia takes Chou as Officer Storehouse and needs Wei to open it. Using Wei, do not see two Chou; having Chou, do not see two Wei. Dingchou and Dingwei are also feared, since Ding injures Xin Officer. Guiwei or Guichou has Gui Water restraining Ding; Jichou or Jiwei has Ji Earth generating Xin Officer. Ji is Jia’s Wealth, Gui its Seal, Bing its Food. Stems and branches born, flourishing, and unbroken give wealth and distinction. Slight clash/control reduces the share; excess instead gives poverty. All the foregoing interweave Wealth, Officer, Seal, Food, Injury, Killings, Blade, Companions, and Owl in mutual avoidance, need, restraint, and combination. Examine the useful spirit and the day stem’s strength. “Day Master should be vigorous; useful spirit must not be injured”: brief words that encompass it all.〉
<!-- source: L1062-L1062 -->
**Rob Wealth and Yang Blade are especially feared in the hour: year and luck arriving together make calamity immediate.**
<!-- source: L1064-L1066 -->
**The year clashing the course brings collapse; the course clashing the year brings obscurity.**

〈This begins the specific discussion of years and courses. Year is what Heaven covers, course what Earth carries. They must not clash and agitate one another: severe conflict collapses, lighter conflict obscures. Harmony is essential, giving Heaven/Earth free passage and blessing arriving of itself. Tai Sui clashing the course gives heavier calamity, the course controlling Tai Sui lighter. Yet the *Deep Source* and *Deep Sea* accounts all call the course controlling the year heavier and the year controlling the course lighter, the principle of the day offending the Year Ruler. In my own fate, Dingsi luck met Guihai year: Gui injured Ding and Hai clashed Si, the year clashing the course. That year I was dismissed from office and lost my mother, suffering the deepest calamity. Consider the example.〉[^203]
<!-- source: L1068-L1070 -->
**Yin qi exhausted and yang qi severed: lamentable even before death. Yang number at its limit and yin Fate pursuing: what remains before the end?**

〈Jia seeing Chen, Bing Wei, Wu Chou, Geng Xu, and Ren Chou are yang qi’s limits. Yi seeing Xu, Ding Chou, Ji and Gui Wei, Xin Chen are yin number’s endings. Years and courses encountering them are especially ominous. Another account calls Yi–Chen, Ding–Wei, Ji–Chou, and Xin–Xu yin tokens pursuing yang numbers already severed. Present only at birth in the Four Pillars, they do no harm; what is feared is a natal offense repeated in year and luck, bringing immediate death. Another account calls a yin stem meeting yang’s limit Yin Encountering a Yang Barrier, and a yang stem meeting yin’s end Yang Encountering a Yin Barrier. Weak and alone, each dies early; a flourishing body with an allied Companion party suffers no harm.〉[^204]
<!-- source: L1072-L1074 -->
**The Five Phases providing rescue remove anxiety where it was due; the four seasons encountering Void leave good news without joy.**

〈Ominous years or courses involving the clashes, controls, exhausted qi, and terminal numbers above cause no anxiety when the Five Phases rescue. Seasonal Void is passing-year Tai Sui meeting an auspicious spirit that falls in Void: hearing joy without joy. Another interpretation: Jia fearing Geng can be rescued by Yi; spring without Earth does not fear Earth as calamity or welcome Earth as blessing. The classic says Geng/Xin injuring Jia/Yi is harmless if Bing/Ding appear first, and spring lacks Earth, summer Metal, autumn Wood, winter Fire. Following this are twenty lines concerning women, already quoted in the dedicated discussion of women; they are not repeated here.〉[^205]
<!-- source: L1076-L1078 -->
Thus yin and yang are seldom fathomed and cannot be inferred along one road. High and low station are hard to distinguish: grasp both ends before judging. Briefly investigate the ancient sages’ surviving writings and bring them together with the present worthies’ detailed research. Following this method in thoughtful comparison, one may read fate with scarcely an error.

〈This concludes the preceding configurations. All have already been discussed under their corresponding configurations in earlier juan, so their annotations here are not exhaustive. *Preferences and Aversions* and *Continuing Goodness* were transformed out of this rhapsody. People now know those two chapters but not this rhapsody, so it is recorded here.〉[^206]

## 7. Chapter on Preferences and Aversions
### 喜忌篇

Set out the Four Pillars, then distinguish the Three Powers. Take the day’s Heavenly Origin as principal and pair the Eight Characters’ stems and branches. Seen and unseen forms are present at every time.[^207]
<!-- source: L1084-L1086 -->
Spirits and sha entangle one another: weigh their force. Seven Killings encountered in the hour need not be ominous; with monthly restraint and a strong stem, Killings instead becomes a Seal of authority.
<!-- source: L1088-L1090 -->
Wealth, Officer, and Seal-Ribbon are stored complete within the four seasonal endings. Officer Star and Wealth qi are born and dwell securely at Yin, Shen, Si, and Hai.
<!-- source: L1092-L1094 -->
Gengshen hour on a Wu day is the territory of a flourishing Food-God stem. Jia, Bing, Mao, or Yin in year and month makes encountering it as though not encountering it. The month generating the day stem without Heavenly Wealth is called Seal-Ribbon.
<!-- source: L1096-L1100 -->
Day Lu returning to the hour without Officer Star is called Finding the Road to Azure Clouds. Yang Water meeting repeated Chen is Ren Riding the Dragon’s Back. Yin Wood meeting Zi hour alone is the Six Yi Rat-Noble ground.
<!-- source: L1102-L1104 -->
A Geng day with complete Moistening Downward avoids Ren, Gui, Si, and Wu; Zi or Shen in the hour halves its blessing. A Hurting-Officer monthly establishment, even in ominous [ground], need not be ominous: within it may be direct or reverse Flying Lu, which fears Officer Star and entanglement.[^208]
<!-- source: L1106-L1110 -->
The six Gui days meeting Yin hour fear Wu and Ji in year and month. Jiazi day meeting Zi hour again fears Geng, Xin, Shen, You, Chou, and Wu. Xin or Gui days with many Chou dislike Officer Star; Zi or Si in year and hour gives empty name and empty profit.
<!-- source: L1112-L1116 -->
Arching Lu or Noble becomes ominous when the empty place is filled. Indirect Wealth in the hour must not appear in another palace. The six Xin days at Wuzi hour dislike Wu and welcome western luck.
<!-- source: L1118-L1120 -->
The Five Phases encountering Indirect Officer in the monthly branch also require restraint in year and hour. Some configurations remove Officer and retain Killings; others remove Killings and retain Officer. Pure Killings in the Four Pillars with restraint determines the dignity of first rank. Even one Direct Officer mixing with Killings instead makes low station.
<!-- source: L1122-L1126 -->
A Wu day in Wu month need not be judged as Blade: abundant Fire in hour and year instead makes Seal-Ribbon. Even monthly Established Lu greatly fears assembling Killings as calamity. Yet when Officer Star and Seven Killings cross, combining Killings can give distinction.
<!-- source: L1128-L1130 -->
Excessively flourishing Officer Star in the pillars is called weak Heavenly Origin. A day stem excessively flourishing without support becomes monk or Daoist.
<!-- source: L1132-L1134 -->
Seal-Ribbon in the birth month fears Wealth Star in year and hour; a course entering Wealth country calls for retirement from office. Rob Wealth and Yang Blade are especially feared in the hour: year and luck arriving together make calamity immediate. The ten stems turning from Lu welcome Wealth Star in year and hour. A course reaching Companions is called Turning from Lu and Driving Horse Away. Proper Noble among the Five Phases avoids palaces of clash, punishment, control, and breaking.

<!-- source: L1136-L1138 -->
The Four Pillars’ stems and branches welcome Triple and Sixfold Combination. A day stem without qi meeting Yang Blade in the hour is not ominous.
<!-- source: L1140-L1144 -->
Officer and Killings evenly poised: retain what is loved and abandon what is hated. Numerous combinations among branches and stems are also called Coveting Combination and Forgetting Officer. Flourishing Killings in the pillars with a course devoted to strengthening the body gives pure distinction in office.
<!-- source: L1146-L1148 -->
Great weakness in Heavenly Origin may contain renewed generation within the weak place. Seven Killings fully manifest in the pillars, with the body [weak], gives extreme poverty and no longevity. 〈Another account says: no rescue.〉[^209]
<!-- source: L1150-L1152 -->
A woman’s fate without Killings, with one Noble, can make a good wife. Numerous Nobles and many combinations determine a religious teacher, nun, courtesan, or maid.[^210]
<!-- source: L1154-L1158 -->
Indirect Officer encountered in the hour and restrained excessively makes a poor scholar. Hurting Officer in the Four Pillars entering Officer country must suffer breaking. The Five Phases’ Extinction is precisely Embryo-Origin; the birthday encountering it is called Receiving Qi.
<!-- source: L1160-L1160 -->
Thus yin and yang are seldom fathomed and cannot be inferred by a single example. Seek an endowment of balanced qi and discern high and low station through the spirits. Briefly unfold the ancient sages’ surviving writings and bring them together with the present worthies’ broad learning. Following this method in detailed comparison, one may read fate without error.

## 8. Chapter on Continuing Goodness
### 繼善篇

People receive their endowment from Heaven and Earth; fate belongs to yin and yang. Living between what covers and what carries, all remain within the Five Phases. To know high and low station, first observe the monthly command as governing thread.[^211]
<!-- source: L1166-L1170 -->
Next judge good and ill, taking the day stem alone as master and root. The Three Origins must form a configuration. The Four Pillars welcome Wealth and Officer.
<!-- source: L1172-L1178 -->
The useful spirit must not be injured; the Day Master should above all be vigorous and flourishing. The year injuring the day stem is called Discord between Root and Master. Mixed Officer and Killings in year, month, and hour are greatly feared.
<!-- source: L1180-L1186 -->
Selection of use depends upon the birth month: investigate the depth of its qi. Manifestation lies in day and hour: examine their strength and weakness. Officer Star’s proper qi avoids punishment and clash. Indirect Wealth in the hour fears brothers.
<!-- source: L1188-L1194 -->
Generating qi as Seal-Ribbon benefits from Officer luck and fears entering Wealth country. Seven Killings as Indirect Officer welcomes restraint, but not excess. Hurting Officer proceeding again through Officer luck brings unforeseen calamity. Yang Blade clashing or combining with the Year Ruler brings disaster in a sudden surge.

<!-- source: L1196-L1202 -->
Wealth and distinction together must come from flourishing Wealth generating Officer. Early death or poverty must come from a declining body meeting ghost. The six Ren days approaching Wu are called Lu and Horse in One Country. A Gui day seated toward Si’s palace is Wealth and Officer in Double Beauty.
<!-- source: L1204-L1210 -->
Abundant Wealth and a weak body make a poor person in a rich house. Transforming Killings into authority surely reveals a noble guest from humble origins. Examination success comes when Officer Star approaches an unbroken palace. Presenting grain and submitting one’s name comes when Wealth Storehouse occupies Birth and flourishing.[^212]
<!-- source: L1212-L1218 -->
Excessive noble Officer topples as soon as it reaches flourishing. Injured Seal-Ribbon allows splendor, perhaps, but not for long. Officer and Seal unbroken make material for the councils of state. Without Seal or Officer, a formed configuration can still serve the court.
<!-- source: L1220-L1226 -->
A name upon the golden list requires a flourishing body meeting Officer. Assisting a sage ruler depends upon clashing up Officer and encountering combination. Without configuration or pattern, how could the encounter be called exceptional? A weak body obtaining Officer afterward merely spends its strength in vain.
<!-- source: L1228-L1234 -->
Even the petty person’s fate may contain Direct Seal and Officer Star; even the gentleman’s configuration may offend Seven Killings and Yang Blade. Few illnesses throughout life come from a strong, elevated Day Master. A whole life of peace comes from Wealth and Fate possessing qi.[^213]
<!-- source: L1236-L1242 -->
Official punishment is not offended when Seal-Ribbon shares a palace with Heavenly Virtue. Little joy and much worry arise from the Day Master’s own weakness. With a strong body and slight Killings, borrow Killings as authority. Heavy Killings and a light body bring lifelong injury.
<!-- source: L1244-L1250 -->
Decline changes Officer into ghost; flourishing transforms ghost into Officer. When the month generates the day stem, courses do not welcome Wealth country. A Day Master without support instead welcomes courses through Wealth ground.[^214]
<!-- source: L1252-L1256 -->
Lu returning to the hour gives a lifelong dislike of Officer Star. Yin Turning toward Yang greatly fears Bing, Ding, and Li’s position. Tai Sui is master of all sha: entering the fate need not bring calamity. But meeting a country of battle must bring punishment to natal Fate.
<!-- source: L1258-L1264 -->
The year injuring the day stem brings lighter calamity; the day offending the Year Ruler brings heavier disaster. If the Five Phases provide rescue, that year instead must bring Wealth. Four Pillars without affinity are therefore discussed as Controlling the Year.[^215]
<!-- source: L1266-L1272 -->
Geng and Xin coming to injure Jia and Yi cause no danger if Bing and Ding appear first. Bing and Ding turning to control Geng and Xin need not be feared if Ren and Gui are met. Wu and Ji anxious at meeting Jia and Yi need Geng and Xin upon the stems. Ren and Gui fearing Wu and Ji are rescued by Jia and Yi’s arrival.
<!-- source: L1274-L1280 -->
Ren coming to control Bing needs Wu standing at the head. Gui going to injure Ding welcomes Ji lending assistance. Geng obtaining Ren as son to restrain Bing turns early death into long years. Jia marrying younger sister Yi to Geng turns misfortune into an auspicious sign.[^216]
<!-- source: L1282-L1288 -->
Heavenly Origin, though flourishing, without support makes an ordinary person. An excessively yielding Day Master, even meeting Wealth and Officer, remains an impoverished scholar. A woman without Killings and bearing the Two Virtues receives enfeoffment in two states. A man with a strong body and the Three Wonders attains the dignity of first rank.[^217]

<!-- source: L1290-L1292 -->
Jia meeting Ji in Birth and flourishing must harbor a centered and upright heart. Ding encountering Ren excessively must fall into sexual disorder and deceit.
<!-- source: L1294-L1296 -->
Bing approaching Shen and meeting yang Water can scarcely extend its years. 〈Seal-Ribbon in the month instead gives peace, riches, honor, and splendor.〉 Ji entering Hai’s palace and seeing yin Wood ultimately suffers shortened life. 〈Bingyin encountered in the hour instead gives the cap, belt, pin, and tassel of office.〉[^218]
<!-- source: L1298-L1304 -->
Geng at Yin meeting Bing is without danger when the master flourishes. Yi at Si seeing Xin suffers calamity when the body declines. Yi encountering flourishing Geng constantly holds benevolence and righteousness in the heart. Bing combining with Xin at Birth holds a post of commanding authority.
<!-- source: L1306-L1312 -->
One Wood repeatedly meeting Fire positions is called the Pattern of Dispersed Qi. A solitary Water meeting Geng and Xin three times is called the Image of Complete Substance. Water returning to winter flourishing gives lifelong joy without worry. Wood born in spring passes peacefully through the world and must be long-lived.[^219]
<!-- source: L1314-L1320 -->
Weak Metal entering blazing Fire territory gives blood disorders beyond doubt. Hollow Earth meeting flourishing Wood country certainly injures the spleen. Aching sinews and painful bones all come from Wood injured by Metal. Darkened eyes and dim sight must come from Fire controlled by Water.[^220]
<!-- source: L1322-L1328 -->
Metal encountering Gen and meeting Earth is called Returning Soul. Water entering Xun and seeing Metal is called Unextinguished. Earth approaching Mao begins to lose heart before middle age. Metal meeting Fire country must suffer frustrated ambition even while young and strong.[^221]
<!-- source: L1330-L1336 -->
Metal and Wood contending in punishment and battle lose both benevolence and righteousness. Water and Fire alternately injuring one another produce disputes every day. Wood is nurtured by Water, but abundant Water sends Wood drifting. Metal relies on Earth for birth, but thick Earth buries Metal.
<!-- source: L1338-L1338 -->
Thus the Five Phases must not become lopsided and parched; seek an endowment of balanced qi. If, further, anxious calculation is stilled and distracting thought forgotten, fate may be read without error.

The final comment refers forward in this juan’s present sequence to the *Rhapsody on Waxing and Waning*, where the winter/summer analogy is explained. “Above” is retained as a transmitted cross-reference, not evidence for moving the passages. Three Fu denotes the traditional midsummer heat periods; no conversion to a fixed modern date is implied. Young yin/old yang are stages of alternation, not ages of people.

[^04]: **Moral cosmology, not a personality test.** The source assigns gentlemanly and petty conduct to patterns of firmness, yielding, and balance. Those valuations are preserved, including their uncomfortable language, rather than converted into a modern psychological typology. Its exception for pure yin that enters a configuration matters: the sentence does not simply condemn every yin-dominant chart.

[^05]: **A small graphical repair; the exception retained.** S’s 和煖之卿 is read 鄉, “warming region,” as in G622. The warning that sudden Water worsens excessive Fire is deliberately retained; it qualifies rather than repeats the elementary control cycle. The tenth month and summer solstice are the text’s seasonal references, not interchangeable calendar labels.

[^06]: **Qing as attachment.** 情 can denote responsive affinity among influences and human affection at once. The Jia–Ji example deliberately makes the technical combination into a marriage image, and Zi–Chou adds a second combination below. The translation varies “affinity,” “affection,” and “attachment” with that double sense rather than treating them as different calculations.

[^07]: **Collective naming does not reverse the relations.** S gives 庚戊為財官, naming Geng/Wu and Wealth/Officer collectively. For Wood, Geng controls it and is Officer/Killings, whereas Wu is the controlled Wealth. The translation orders the English pair correspondingly. No new stem is supplied. The passage insists that a phase’s Extinction position may contain usable hidden stems; it does not equate the technical stage with literal death.

[^08]: **Allegory and prognosis overlap.** Mother, child, husband, and wife first dramatize relations of generation, control, and combination; the commentary also draws literal familial judgments from them. Those levels remain distinct where the wording allows it. Bing combining with Xin is not omitted simply because Bing was just called Jia’s child. The argument turns precisely on a useful generating chain being diverted by combination.

[^09]: **Two different uses of life and death.** The technical stages and the renewed or exhausted capacity of an influence are not identical. The two maxims exploit that distinction. S’s 死絶之官 is read 宮, “palace,” as in G622 and the surrounding usage. “There is no second death” is the commentator’s reasoning, not a biomedical assertion.

[^10]: **The great field and Shensheng.** The field image recalls [*Odes*, “Futian” of the Airs of Qi](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/詩經/甫田_(齊風)), with its warning against cultivating an overlarge field and exhausting the heart. Shensheng’s fate in family and succession intrigue is recounted in the [*Zuo Tradition*, Duke Xi, fourth year](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/春秋左氏傳/僖公#僖公四年). Here the reference illustrates jealousy among rival maternal influences. 五星, Five Stars, is retained rather than corrected to Five Phases: the commentary explicitly borrows that terminology.

[^11]: **Natural completion versus contrived extraction.** “Planting jade in Lantian” retains the literary image of producing something precious in fitting ground. The commentary itself explains the contrast: ordinary Wealth→Officer or Killings→Seal relations versus extracting a useful spirit through complex remote operations. It need not be reduced to the marriage meaning the same expression carries elsewhere. “Direct,” “oblique,” “crossing,” and “straight” are the maxim’s relational images, not a newly supplied geometrical diagram.

[^12]: **Supporting the supporter.** The source makes Metal, rather than the immediately rescuing Water, the decisive influence when Water itself is depleted. This is more specific than advising the same favorable phase in every chart. S’s 午破子衝 is interpreted as Wu being clashed by Zi, supported by G622’s 午被子衝. 穿 is kept as “piercing”; here it describes the Zi–Wei harmful relation.

[^13]: **A reference, not missing commentary supplied.** S says the two explanations were given in the opening volume. The repository places the discussions of major and minor courses in [juan two](juan-02.md), not juan one. The reference is translated as transmitted, with its imprecision explained here. Nothing from the other juan is silently imported into this one.

[^14]: **The commentator’s “I.”** S twice prints 子 where G622 has 予 in the sentences about people personally observed entering the Hanlin or retiring from military life. “I” follows that supported reading. The Wood/Fire directions are luck-course phase regions in this explanation; they are not instructions to relocate. Literary Storehouse and Arsenal name Xu and Chou through Fire and Metal respectively, not buildings actually present in the chart.

[^15]: **The image defines its own symbols.** Dragon, Heaven, and Cloud are explicitly Chen, Hai, and Ren. The sequence mixes branch configuration, stem relations, and figurative movement. Its designation “Ren Riding the Dragon’s Back” is retained without forcing this explanation to coincide in every detail with the configuration in juan six. Forests and springs mean retirement from public office, not necessarily a literal change of profession.

[^16]: **Nayin and retrospective illustration.** Wuchen is Great-Forest Wood, Dingwei Heavenly-River Water, Renchen Long-Flowing Water, and Guihai Great-Sea Water in the nayin series translated in juan one. These are not their ordinary stem phases. Nine in the Fifth Place, 九五, is the ruler’s place in the Changes’ Qian imagery. Both commentaries acknowledge that their rules are being stated with particular named charts in view. The charts are preserved as transmitted examples, not certified birth records or independent proof of the predictions.

[^17]: **Two repairs and two arching methods.** S’s 岐㠜 is interpreted through G622’s 岐嶷, exceptional or precocious stature. The following 己寅 is Si and Yin, 巳寅, not a Jiyin pair; both the Hai–Shen combinations and G622 support that reading. Direct arching encloses the missing branch between its neighbors; outward arching supplies the missing member of a Triple Combination. Qian/Hai and Kun/Shen are the text’s directional correspondences.

[^18]: **A numerical variant retained.** S has the Eight Characters arching “two positions,” whereas G622 has “one position.” S’s two is retained. Wind/Si and Thunder/Mao are the image’s named correspondences, and their invisible arching must not be mistaken for two additional natal pillars.

[^19]: **A genuine disagreement within S.** The first account favors removing the thief; the appended account favors appeasement and condemns suppression. Both remain. G622’s consulted text lacks that final alternative and gives year/month where S gives year/day in the self-punishment clause. S governs both the included alternative and its year/day reading. “The five yin influences in the monthly branch” is compressed and not securely unpacked by the commentary; no complete new five-item method is manufactured here.

[^20]: **A correspondence list, not six natal branches.** The six yang and six yin signs are the two complete branch classes. The paragraph explains concealed arching by class; it does not claim that a four-pillar chart literally contains six displayed branches. Its advocacy of a suitably completed pure-yang image is not harmonized away against the earlier warning about one-sidedness.

[^21]: **Literary stature and a contrasting rule.** 山斗 compresses the images of a great mountain and the Dipper as standards of eminence. The maxim praises Hall of Learning with Horse under its stated conditions. Juan ten’s Jade Well commentary instead warns that Horse in a learning position without sufficient noble qi gives empty learning and travel. Both qualified judgments are preserved rather than turned into a universal “Horse improves scholarship” rule.

[^22]: **The exception is part of the text.** Its condemnation of sexual conduct is followed by an explicit reversal when Wealth, Officer, Noble, or Virtue shares the palace. The translation retains both, including the preference for Void here, without applying these historical judgments to a living person’s fidelity or worth.

[^23]: **A small but material variant.** S reads 比印旺地, flourishing Companion-and-Seal ground; G622 reads 此印旺地, this flourishing Seal ground. S is retained. The sequence of future rescuing courses and its certainty are the source’s reasoning, not a guarantee supplied by the translator. “Obstruction” and “Peace” preserve the Changes resonance of 否 and 泰.

[^24]: **Metaphor is not an added travel forecast.** Qin and Chu dramatize death away from one’s birthplace; morning north/evening south dramatizes restless movement. The branch relation that produces an “arrow,” “reins,” or “whip” is specified in the commentary. The forward and reverse derivations are retained without adding an unprovided full calculation table.

[^25]: **The kinship assignments change with the transformation.** Wife→husband, daughter→mother, mother→child, and master→Wealth are the source’s several analogies. They do not constitute one fixed kinship table. Jia’s former Wood allies obstruct an Earth transformation precisely because they offer a return to its original identity. No preference for lifelong estrangement from actual relatives is implied by that technical reading.

[^26]: **An unenumerated collective.** The commentary says “Five Sha” but supplies no five-item list here. The translation retains the count without borrowing a list from another tradition. The occupation and sexual-morality labels are the author’s social hierarchy, not the translator’s assessment of those occupations.

[^27]: **Substance and use are expressly primary.** The commentary explicitly says the parent/child and husband/wife pairs concern 體用, substance and function. It also takes Earth’s Birth at Shen and Fire’s Death at You in these examples. Those stage assignments are retained instead of silently adjusted to a single contemporary table.

[^28]: **Water restored; historical physiology retained.** S’s 癸木, Gui Wood, is G622’s 癸水, Gui Water, agreeing with the heading and entire explanation. “Water” follows that supported repair. Kidneys, pupils, lower origin, large intestine, and spleen belong to the text’s correlative physiology. None supplies a modern diagnosis, causal proof, or treatment instruction.

[^29]: **Two Analects exempla.** [*Analects*, “Yongye,” 6.10–11](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/論語/雍也第六) juxtaposes Confucius’s grief at Boniu’s illness with Yan Hui’s joy despite poverty. This commentary converts them into phase images; it does not establish either man’s chart. The 九夏 of the prose means summer’s full span, not a ninth summer month. The physiological claims remain those of the historical text.

[^30]: **The wife-Wealth equivalence is local.** This section explicitly takes Wealth as wife, unlike the Jade Well passage in juan ten that distinguishes the combining wife from controlled Wealth. Both traditions are preserved. “Rouge and powder” retains the verse’s gendered livelihood image; the commentary says the person profits through his wife, without specifying a profession that the translator should invent.

[^31]: **A difficult closing phrase retained.** S and G622 both have 巨微徵矣. “The great and the subtle will become evident” interprets 巨 and 微 as contrasted scales and 徵 as manifestation or verification. It is not presented as a securely recovered emendation to another phrase. S’s 神自心生 differs from G622’s 神入心生; S’s “arises from the heart” governs.

[^32]: **The Six Spirits and the conditional maxim.** The chapter concerns the principal relational influences rather than six newly introduced deities. Horse in the opening maxim means Wealth. The commentary immediately limits “Seal is inferior to Wealth” to a strong body with slight Officer and excessive Seal; it is not a universal ranking of Wealth above Seal. Huzhongzi’s dictum is retained as the source quotes it.

[^33]: **Lu can disrupt following.** Return to the day stem’s own Lu restores an independent claim to strength, which this explanation considers harmful to an already submissive relation. The metaphor is political: a weak party reopens a battle it cannot win. It is not corrected merely because other configurations welcome Lu or bodily support.

[^34]: **Two opposite judgments, each with conditions.** The first case uses Seal as its indispensable support; the second needs Seal to help a strong body bear further Wealth. S and G623 both specify 水火, Water and Fire, in the latter case. The phrase is retained rather than generalized to every day stem.

[^35]: **Supported repairs in ordinary words.** S’s 無偏財遇敵 is read 禦敵, “resist the enemy,” with G623. Indirect Wealth’s role is to restrain Owl, thereby preserving Food’s restraint of Killings. In the next commentary S’s 波局 is read 破局, “break the configuration,” supported by the maxim and G623.

[^36]: **The target of Injury changes the judgment.** The two consecutive maxims deliberately reverse common prescriptions. Removing contested Officer can stop Companions’ quarrel; restraining the sole Killings that governs Companions can unleash it. Neither sentence is omitted as inconsistent with the more familiar advice to protect Officer or restrain Killings.

[^37]: **Moral roles are personified.** The commentator casts Officer as cultivated and Killings as violent, then reasons about the dominant company each inhabits. Elsewhere in this volume Killings can be transformed into useful authority. The apparently absolute language here remains, without claiming that all collected authors follow one uniform rule of transformation.

[^38]: **A Companion may help rather than rob.** The assistance is valuable because it enables the Day Master to withstand Killings and employ Wealth. 萬貫, ten thousand strings of cash, is an image of great wealth, not a modern monetary amount or a statistical forecast.

[^39]: **“Fills the gate” restored.** S’s 門克駟馬 is G623’s 門充駟馬, supporting “fill the gate with four-horse teams.” These are marks of elite attendance, not four additional Horse stars. S has 劫害 where G623 has 剋害 in the commentary; the translation retains robbery/harm rather than silently substituting control/harm. The sequence after 不遇 is construed as a series of avoided conditions.

[^40]: **Authentic Birth and nayin Birth.** The commentary explicitly includes the year’s branch and nayin in evaluating Birth, Coming to Office, and Imperial Prosperity. It is not confined to the ordinary day stem’s twelve-stage cycle. Cauldron-lifting and mountain-uprooting are heroic images attached to the source’s military judgment.

[^41]: **The objection belongs to the commentary.** The source first explains the two worthies as two Killings, then records another account as Officer and Killings, pointing out that the first explanation overlaps the next maxim. That critical observation is not a translator’s note inserted into the main text. “Six States” dramatizes political competition over a coveted object; no six-pillar calculation is supplied.

[^42]: **Transformation is distinguished from suppression.** Seal receives what Killings generates and transmits it to the body; Food restrains Killings by control. The commentary explicitly prefers the former when force cannot subdue numerous Killings. 疾之已甚亂也 echoes the Analects’ warning that excessive hatred provokes disorder; here the phrase functions as the commentator’s political analogy, not a separate astrological rule.

[^43]: **A yin-stem stage is retained.** The Xin-at-Zi example treats Zi as a generating or rooting position for Xin while also being Water Seal for Yi. That is compatible with the passage’s reverse yin-stage account, though not with every generalized five-phase Birth table. The prescription to clash an otherwise useful Seal palace follows this particular configuration, not a blanket preference for destroying Seal.

[^44]: **The two successive clashes are not silently harmonized.** S and G623 both move from many You clashing Mao to many Mao breaking Wu. The translation keeps that sequence rather than replacing it with a newly calculated remote-Wealth explanation. The title’s “brothers” means Companions in the ensuing commentary.

[^45]: **Exposed Wealth is the stabled Horse in both witnesses.** It may seem counterintuitive that open, unshielded Wealth has settled ownership while hidden Wealth attracts theft. S and G623 agree, however. The positions of stable and open country are not reversed to make the metaphor conform to another school’s account.

[^46]: **Adoption and maternal affiliation are distinct alternatives.** The commentary gives both transfer as another lineage’s adopted heir and leaving the father to follow the mother. 宗嗣 concerns ancestral succession, not simply owning two houses. The preference for beginning with Wealth/father is this passage’s method and does not supersede every other parent-assignment method in the book.

[^47]: **The apparent contradiction is qualified by footing.** Wealth and Seal in one palace are first condemned through their controlling relation, then permitted when both are rooted and Birth/Lu support them. Their co-presence alone is not the whole judgment. “Birth and Lu share their country” is retained as the compressed expression 生祿同鄉, without an invented example.

[^48]: **Three family exempla.** Bodao is Deng You, whose childlessness is lamented in [*Jin History*, juan 90](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/晉書/卷090#鄧攸). Meng Guang exemplifies devoted conjugal conduct in [*Later Han History*, juan 83, Liang Hong’s biography](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/後漢書/卷83). Daoyun is Xie Daoyun, whose eloquence and literary judgment are recorded in [*Jin History*, juan 96](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/晉書/卷096#王凝之妻謝道韞). These references explain the rhetorical comparisons; they do not verify natal charts for the three people. The first maxim’s “child filling the child’s position” is not changed into a general favorable rule for the hour.

[^49]: **Yin/yang distinction not replaced by a single table.** The text says the five yin Hurting Officers return to life at the Storehouse that proves fatal in the contrasting case. It does not give a five-entry table here, and the English does not supply one from a modern school. The following Blade rule also distinguishes combination with Killings from battle with Officer.

[^50]: **Two levels of personification.** The text moves from Killings/Blade and Wealth/Officer as chart influences to weapons, drugs, salary, and corrupt officeholding as outcomes. The transition is preserved, not treated as evidence that one occurrence of the influence establishes an actual death or misconduct. Number 數 is the source’s determinative numerical order, not a measured statistical probability.

[^51]: **The weak body’s new ambition is the stated mechanism.** The commentary does not merely say that Birth is bad. It says regained strength tempts a body unable to sustain Wealth into a damaging contest. This sits beside passages where support to a weak body is welcome; no universal rule is imposed on the collected explanations.

[^52]: **A deliberately permitted Officer/Killings mixture.** Killings restrains or combines Blade, enabling Wealth to recover and generate Officer. The text expressly forbids dismissing this example merely because Officer and Killings are both present. That exception is preserved with the rule it qualifies.

[^53]: **Title order and literary form.** S heads this 憎愛賦, Aversion and Affection; G623 heads the parallel 愛憎賦, Affection and Aversion. S’s order governs. A fu is rendered “rhapsody”: its balanced, accumulating clauses are kept in rhythmic prose, not forced into an invented English rhyme scheme. Its several occupational and moral judgments belong to the historical voice.

[^54]: **“Early years” is not silently changed to “late.”** 月衰時旺早歲豐肥 appears in both S and G623, although a flourishing hour often indicates later rather than earlier life elsewhere in the text. The transmitted early-years statement remains. S’s 女士 gives “worthy woman,” where G623 has 女人, woman. The carriage/Horse language combines travel imagery with technical names; it is not expanded into an unprovided travel algorithm.

[^55]: **Penal language and transmitted fragments.** S’s 黔面, darkened face, is G623’s 黥面, tattooed face, supporting the penal sense. 徒 and 絞 are penal labor and strangulation, not one combined sentence. 時有或再 is awkwardly transmitted around the transition; the translation conveys recurring lawsuits and renewed Extinction without asserting a uniquely recoverable punctuation. The deterministic violent-death claims are retained as historical claims only.

[^56]: **A difficult Metal clause retained.** 庚辛虧而果斷無剛 gives resolution or decisiveness without firmness when Geng/Xin are impaired. G623 agrees. This is not silently repaired to make every moral pairing neatly symmetrical. 疎脫 is read as carelessness or dissipation in S; G623’s 疏跣 differs and is not imposed on S.

[^57]: **A seasonal difference.** S has 夏果, summer fruit; G623 has 百果, all fruits. S is retained. The agricultural images explain failure to realize potential, not missing portions of an actual chart. 傷格/破局 remains a paired distinction in the rhapsody even though English “configuration” can translate either term in other contexts.

[^58]: **A gallery of frustrated lives, with one doubtful name.** Li’s boiling is the death of Li Yiji in [*Records of the Historian*, juan 97](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/史記/卷097); Fan Zeng’s back abscess is recorded in [juan 7](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/史記/卷007). Yuanming and Zimei are the literary appellations of Tao Qian and Du Fu. S has 孟輿, Meng Yu, where G623 has 孟軻, Mencius: S is retained because no independent recovery of its intended name has been established. Jiang Ge’s cold-weather study fits the Liang scholar’s account in [*Liang History*, juan 36](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/梁書/卷36), rather than requiring identification with the Han filial exemplar of the same name. The allusions illustrate unrealized capacity, not verified natal charts.

[^59]: **Names without a new calculation table.** Three Wonders, Four Blessings, One Auspicious, Two Fitting, Sun’s Essence, Moon’s Brilliance, Jade Hall, and Golden Coffer are named without full definitions here. Their appearances elsewhere do not justify manufacturing a single system for this rhapsody. The adjacent two-pair examples are not complete four-pillar charts; no missing year or hour has been supplied.

[^60]: **A material variant in the rule.** S’s 有令者吉, “possession of the command gives good,” is G623’s 有合者吉, “combination gives good.” S governs. 貨廕 is rendered wealth and inherited privilege; G’s 貲廕 supports the sense. The ensuing examples of childhood comfort and later poverty qualify any attempt to read only one favorable sign in isolation.

[^61]: **Vitality and seasonal encounter are not interchangeable.** The living/withered branch comparison explains why the same season does not benefit all equally. It is the rhapsody’s analogy, not a quantitative account of survival. Its final invitation to common judgment, 共評, is retained rather than replaced by an assertion that the rules have been empirically certified.

[^62]: **The source’s attribution is preserved.** S labels this “annotated by Luoluzi; explained by Yuwuzi.” Later commentary treats Luoluzi as the rhapsody’s voice and disputes his identification. The translation does not silently repair the heading into a modern authorship claim. 消息 means alternation of diminution and growth, hence Waxing and Waning; it does not mean a message or news report here.

[^63]: **An older mapping of the Three Origins.** Here stem/Lu is Heavenly Origin, branch/Fate Human Origin, and nayin/Body Earthly Origin. This differs from the later familiar mapping of stems, branches, and hidden stems as Heaven, Earth, and humanity. The difference is explicit in S and G623 and is essential to the subsequent arguments; it is not standardized away.

[^64]: **Approaching and turning away.** Chou is immediately before Jia’s Lu at Yin and Mao immediately after it; the text calls these approach and departure. “Lu Storehouse” in this sentence means the position behind Lu, not necessarily one of the four Earth Storehouses. Tanying’s ensuing exposition is a separately quoted interpretation. The text itself warns that turning from Lu need not always give poverty.

[^65]: **A conversion ratio, not an independently reconstructed calendar.** Three days per year and ten years per branch are the stated method. The commentary stresses solar-term depth and the direction of counting, but gives no fully dated birth from which a modern starting age can be certified. No timezone, civil-calendar conversion, or additional rounding rule has been invented.

[^66]: **Competing classifications remain visible.** The first explanation assigns snake, eel, and earthworm to Fire through Si; the monk Ying assigns eel and earthworm to Water/Earth and contrasts them with Soaring Snake. The commentator approves the second. These zoological correspondences belong to the historical symbolic system, and their disagreement must not be erased by translating the animals as one undifferentiated “snake.”

[^67]: **The Documents’ Five Affairs.** The underlying passage is [*Documents*, “Hongfan”](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/尚書/洪範): demeanor, speech, seeing, hearing, and thought, with their qualities of gravity, order, discernment, counsel, and sagacity. S’s 肅又 is read 肅乂 in accordance with that primary parallel. The commentator’s own visible/audible arrangement is nevertheless retained; it is not the Documents’ exact classification. Gong, shang, jue, zhi, and yu are the five pitch categories, not five additional elements.

[^68]: **Root restored; General redefined by the commentary.** S’s 木主休囚 is read 本主, root and master, as in G623 and the explanation below. 將星 is explicitly the monthly general here, not automatically the separately calculated General Star of every other chapter. The rhapsody’s annotation then rejects Xu’s worked explanation as missing its meaning. That objection is the source’s, not a translator’s judgment added to the text.

[^69]: **Three accounts, not one reconciled scheme.** The first uses Earth and Water both born at Shen; Xu instead gives Officer/Seal/Lu/Horse positions; the commentator finally prefers Earth in the four seasonal endings and Water in winter. All three remain. “True Warrior” renders 真武 as printed, while related passages use Dark Warrior/玄武; the translation does not infer a different calculated star from the name alone.

[^70]: **A shared unresolved combination.** S and G623 both write 辛合於甲, Xin combining with Jia, although the usual five stem combinations pair Xin with Bing and Jia with Ji. The main text keeps the transmitted relation, using “joining” rather than silently substituting either stem. This is not evidence for adding a sixth standard combination. The rest of the paragraph distinguishes same-polarity conflict from differently polarized relations.

[^71]: **Religious and practical readings coexist.** The commentary turns from chart technique to cultivation, quieting desire, and seeking the mysterious. S’s 識者 is rendered “the discerning”; G623 instead has the obscure 莫者. Neither is silently emended to 釋者, Buddhists, merely to make a Buddhist/Daoist pair. “Without the fitting person, the Way does not act in vain” is quoted from the Changes tradition as the author’s conclusion.

[^72]: **Two distinct cycling procedures.** Five Rats’ Origin produces Jiaxu in the first illustration; Heavenly-Officer cycling places Xin above Wei in the second. Neither example licenses treating every invisible branch as a displayed pillar. The following Zi/Mao/Wu/You account concerns yin–yang’s growth and decline collectively, not the same twelve-stage table for each separate stem.

[^73]: **Attributions and the nine-part count.** Hegong is explained as Heshang Gong and Father Xuan as Confucius under his Wenxuan honorific. The Emei immortal is not securely identified here. The Nine Fates count is explicit: Body and Fate, Lu and Horse, then year, conception, month, day, and hour. Its five final positions include conception, so “nine” is not corrected to eight. The references to supernatural attainment are the historical commentator’s claims.

[^74]: **An uncertain origin and a literary allusion.** Lanye is retained as a place-name, not silently identified with the Lanling proposed at the end of the rhapsody. “Your servant” preserves the address to a ruler without identifying that ruler. The suspended gourd and transformed staff refer to the transmitted Hugong/Fei Changfang stories; they mark the speaker’s self-deprecation and spiritual aspirations, not independently verified events.

[^75]: **The text’s social correspondences remain its own.** Heaven/ruler/husband and Earth/minister/wife express the commentator’s hierarchical cosmology. They are not substituted with contemporary social categories or adopted as advice. The Blue-Green and Red Emperors personify the eastern Wood and southern Fire phases; their father/son relation is generation within that symbolic order.

[^76]: **An argument from exceptions, not natural history.** The ice-rat’s enormous measurements, fire-resistant creatures, pitch-pipes warming a valley, and summer frost are retained as marvels used to challenge a single rigid interpretive rule. Traditional units remain untranslated into spurious precision. The commentary then explicitly rejects Xu’s technical explanation of the same images. The two readings are not combined into one literal zoological account.

[^77]: **A proposed accuracy and its reversal.** One explanation says ordinary regularities may yield eight or nine correct judgments in ten; the other says even that is difficult because transformations are inexhaustible. Both are admitted by the commentator. Neither is a measured success rate established for this translation or for contemporary practice.

[^78]: **A damaged word repaired from the parallel.** S’s old-age phrase 老入蛉 is corrupt; G623 reads 臨老伶仃, loneliness or isolation in old age. The bracketed “loneliness” follows that supported sense. The subsequent ranking of year, conception, month, day, and hour is the annotation’s own older framework, not silently converted into an exclusively day-centered method.

[^79]: **Thirty-six symbolic creatures, with variant birds and reptiles.** The table preserves S’s twelve groups of three, including Zi’s oriole and Chou’s softshell turtle. G623 instead prints 鸞, luan bird, and 鼉, alligator-like tuo, in those places. The *yu* 㺄 creature is left provisionally named rather than assigned an unsupported modern species. Likewise 猱 is rendered descriptively as a climbing monkey. The alternative explanation of “three creatures” as year, month, and conception is separately retained.

[^80]: **Wu–Gui restored; two pipe families.** S writes 壬癸徵火, Ren–Gui with Fire’s zhi; G623 has 戊癸, Wu–Gui, agreeing with the five transformation pairs. “[Wu]” follows that attested correction. 律 and 呂 are the complementary six yang and six yin pitch-pipe families; the shared unaccented romanization does not make them one word. Yin’s being called first of the twice-six hours is retained as transmitted, not silently changed to Zi.

[^81]: **Wu is the branch 午 here.** In the Jia/Ji example, an absent Ji is represented by Wu, the Horse branch containing Ji’s Lu, not by Wu Earth 戊. The three related presences and the later complete-combination method are distinct accounts. The list of branches and their implied partners is kept in S’s order rather than repaired into an invented five-pair chart. Xu’s Punishing-Combination explanation is another account, not the uniquely established reading.

[^82]: **Residual effects across a boundary.** Cap and Belt carries something of the preceding Bath/Defeat, while Decline carries something of preceding flourishing. The comparison explains overlapping effects rather than a mechanically instantaneous change. The first/last five-year division is asserted by the commentary but not developed into a new calculation here.

[^83]: **Middle and lower change with the interpreter.** The first explanation explicitly uses earthly branch and nayin; Xu gives Human and Branch Origins. Their terminology must not be homogenized. The Nine-Palace and Eight-Trigram alternatives are likewise separate interpretive frames, not a newly supplied overlay on the Four Pillars.

[^84]: **“Honored” is disputed within the commentary.** The first account ranks year and major course above their subordinate positions; Xu calls the strongest useful influence honored. The commentator finds Xu reasonable but insufficient to explain the actual wording. Lu’s three assemblies are also expressly said not to mean technical stem-Lu. The translation retains this dispute rather than adjudicating it without evidence.

[^85]: **Two quoted hexagram lines.** The references are Great Exceeding, first six, and Fellowship, nine in the fifth place, in the [*Changes*](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/周易). The commentator uses them to counsel care and self-mastery, not to give a procedure for casting those hexagrams from a birth chart. White cogon is a protective mat placed beneath an offering; the image is deliberate precaution.

[^86]: **Qualified examples, not interchangeable phase tables.** The paragraph moves among ordinary stem relations, nayin, and a phase’s exhausted state turning toward its offspring. Thus the Dingyou Fire-to-Earth explanation is retained without recasting it solely as the ordinary Ding stem. “Loss and fullness” remains a flexible description of increase and diminution, as the commentator himself insists.

[^87]: **Eight, six, and five are names with differing explanations.** One account derives Six Empty by opposition to Void; another calls the eight branches outside the four Earth branches Solitary. Five Tombs occupy four Earth branches because the five phases share those sites. No fifth Earth branch or missing natal pillar is invented. The reversed directional language remains the transmitted explanation, not a modern reconstructed diagram.

[^88]: **The author explicitly rejects “all one kind.”** “Heavenly Origin’s single qi” is explained as its unified celestial function, not four identical stems. The contrary contemporary interpretation is mentioned only to reject it. “Determine” and “turn” deliberately distinguish a settled judgment of rank from circulation of commercial gain and loss.

[^89]: **Wealth and Fate can use different reference levels.** Gengyin is Wood by nayin although Geng is ordinary Metal. The Jiachen/Bingyin example similarly refers to Chen Earth, Fire, and Metal in different relational roles. These shifts are not silently flattened into one Day Master calculation. Xu’s alternative then supplies his own day-centered reading.

[^90]: **Difficult examples are not corrected merely for doctrine.** Metal returning to Hai and meeting Fire, and Fire at Shen meeting Metal, do not transparently match a single ordinary five-phase growth table. They remain as transmitted, while the commentator expressly says nayin is involved. S’s 受制則天 is read 夭, shortened life, from the opposition to longevity and the paragraph’s whole argument; it is a contextual repair.

[^91]: **Driving away is expressly distinguished from pursuing.** The commentary explains 逐 as expulsion and dispersal, not chasing after Horse. Its Guihai/Yin example dramatizes Lu behind and Horse ahead. The Five Rats procedure and Xu’s Wealth/Officer interpretation are distinct explanations, not two parts of a single newly assembled algorithm.

[^92]: **A supported day-pillar correction.** S gives Jiyou in the Jiashen/Dingchou example but immediately discusses Jihai among its three self-born nayin. G623 gives Jihai as the day. The bracketed “[Jihai]” restores the internally supported parallel reading; S’s Jiyou is recorded here rather than erased from the textual record. The longevity and rank remain the source’s claims about the example.

[^93]: **Two senses of robbery are juxtaposed.** The first explanation concerns Robbery Sha at a branch position, while Xu discusses the polarity distinction between Rob Wealth and Defeated Wealth. They must not be treated as one calculation merely because 劫 appears in both. Arching a missing Lu also differs from having Lu actually displayed in a pillar.

[^94]: **Several competing methods, with substantial witness differences.** Wang Tingguang, the monk Ying, Xu Ziping, and the commentator each give a monthly-Lu explanation. G623 materially abbreviates or changes Ying’s calculation and Xu’s birth-group examples; S’s fuller transmitted account governs. Wang’s Three Wonders and Six Instruments conceal Jia, whereas Ying uses Jia–Wu–Geng and Yi–Bing–Ding. The commentator expressly prefers Wang. These are not silently reconciled into a single contemporary list.

[^95]: **A historical convention, not a judgment of personal identity.** The forward/reverse course distinction uses the text’s yin/yang and male/female categories. Its normative language is preserved as part of that cosmology. Xu’s Original Spirit here is explained through injury to Officer or Seal, not assumed identical with every earlier Yuanchen calculation.

[^96]: **The source rejects one of the interpretations it preserves.** Wang’s father retiring before the child, Ying’s flourishing ghost at Birth, and Xu’s Officer/Wealth declining when the body reaches its own ground are different accounts. The last is explicitly criticized. The translation therefore does not present the paragraph as one agreed prescription that every Birth course requires resignation.

[^97]: **Older examples broaden familiar labels.** Jiazi with Gengzi is treated as Repeated Recitation despite different stems; Jiazi with Gengwu or Wuwu as Reversed Recitation. The branch and nayin relation, not a modern insistence on identical full pillars, governs these examples. Good and ill versions are both given. “Facing one another” translates 相對 without silently changing the source’s definition.

[^98]: **Names and counts vary by the cited account.** The passage gives more than one definition of Four Sha and Five Ghosts, then a numbered nine-category list. S has 二宜, Two Fitting, where G624 has 二凶, Two Inauspicious; S is retained. The later Xu-based definitions and rankings are preserved separately, not used to overwrite the first list.

[^99]: **The commentary reverses its first simple reading.** Its first sentence makes assistance to sha swift and restraint of blessing slow. The commentator then offers four distinctions: supporting calamity swift, blessing slow; restraining blessing swift, calamity slow. The difference remains visible rather than being silently turned into one uniformly consistent speed rule.

[^100]: **Mencius’s agricultural comparison.** The quoted line is from [*Mencius*, “Gongsun Chou I,” section 1](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/孟子/公孫丑上): even a hoe is less useful than the right season. 鎡基 is an agricultural implement here, not simply a sound foundation. Its use elsewhere in this book can be metaphorical. The two subsequent readings of weary person/poor Horse use different reference points and are retained as separate accounts.

[^101]: **Repeated and Reversed Recitation need not be fatal.** 覆 and 衝 are explained as these two encounters, with an example of flourishing Wealth protecting the person. The Renwu/Gengwu/Wuwu example and next Xinwei course are transmitted illustrations, not newly calculated dates. “Abundant years” is a favorable phase condition, not a promised modern investment return.

[^102]: **Great and Lesser Auspicious are branch names.** Great Auspicious is Chou, Lesser Auspicious Wei; Heavenly Gang is Chen, Heavenly Kui Xu. Their opposite positions are not automatically judged as harmful Reversed Recitation. The explanations depend on received qi, hidden stems, and True Seals rather than one ordinary growth-stage table.

[^103]: **Five seals in four Storehouses.** Yichou Metal, Guiwei Wood, Renchen Water, Jiaxu Fire, and Wuchen Earth are the specified True Seals. Their ordinary stems and their nayin should not be confused. The Wuchen example’s stated starting age and later fifty-six-year point are retained; an exact birth day and solar-term interval are not supplied, so the dates cannot be independently reconstructed here.

[^104]: **Lunar lodges are not branch names.** Mao 昴 and Wei 胃 are the lunar lodges situated in You’s region, not Mao 卯 and Wei 未 of the twelve branches. The first explanation identifies Azure Dragon with Mao branch, Xu with Chen. S’s assertion that You Metal controls “Mao Metal” is rendered Mao “[Wood]” as an internal correction supported by its immediately following Yi Wood explanation. The two competing Azure-Dragon assignments remain distinct.

[^105]: **Collective phase and reverse yin-stage accounts meet.** Geng’s Birth/Death/Extinction and Xin’s are separately specified. The note about Yi–Geng combination supplies another explanation of relative severity, not proof that all preceding stage statements have one origin. Neither the yin/yang distinction nor the alternative is omitted.

[^106]: **The tuning analogy.** The blind musician is a conventional learned performer, not someone assumed incapable of music because of blindness. The point is lack of skill in tuning: interpretation likewise requires more than reciting an outline. “Plain explanation” therefore does not promise an uncomplicated or invariably certain rule.

[^107]: **The commentator explicitly distinguishes branch from stem.** Northern people here are Hai/Zi, not simply Ren/Gui. The rejected interpretation would conflate branch-Fate with stem-Lu. This distinction is essential to the trading example even though other passages use “north” collectively for Water. The text’s contrasting gentleman/petty-person outcomes are its social valuations.

[^108]: **Ethical and technical senses coexist.** Metal/Wood, grief/joy, and righteousness/benevolence are paired. The first account invokes mastery of emotion; the alternative explains refinement through a controlling influence. Their different uses of Water/Fire and one-sided yin/yang remain rather than being reduced to a single elemental recommendation.

[^109]: **Casting the Seal is a named image, not ordinary metallurgy alone.** The first account values balanced Metal and Fire; the alternative warns that their abundance need not produce distinction. Both are accepted as tenable. Carriage and cap signify official status, and yin/yang being “unsettled” concerns an unstable relation, not an uncertainty in which stem was calculated.

[^110]: **The text preserves and then rejects Xu’s explanation.** Smoke preceding flame is read as obstruction before development; Dragon and Tiger illustrate responsive forces. Xu’s Wuchen/Jiayin interpretation is mentioned but not supplied in full, so no missing detailed chart has been invented. The closing rejection is the commentator’s voice.

[^111]: **A relational example, not a contradiction silently repaired.** You’s Xin obtains Chen’s Yi as Wealth, while Chen’s Water may impair Xin’s Officer. A single encounter therefore contains gain and loss under different headings. The second interpretation additionally distinguishes ordinary patterns from exceptional moral reversals. The book’s own discussion, not an English claim of universal causality, supplies these judgments.

[^112]: **Overlapping course effects and multiple sex-based rules.** The first five/last five-year explanation and the entrance/exit-year alternative are retained separately. The final Tanying account distinguishes minor year-qi and major month-qi. S’s malformed 三人一歲 is rendered three “[days]” to a year by the repeatedly stated conversion elsewhere in the same rhapsody; this is an internal repair, not a new calendar rule.

[^113]: **Self-Flourishing refers to nayin here.** Xinmao Wood, Wuwu Fire, Guiyou Metal, and Bingzi Water belong to the nayin framework rather than the ordinary phases of their stems. The subsequent Yihai/Guisi and Renyin/Gengshen examples likewise depend on their transmitted phases. Their reciprocal rescue must not be rejected merely by changing the reference system midway through the paragraph.

[^114]: **Two readings of the same numbers.** The main account takes three as the Three Origins’ generating qi and five as Five Ghosts; the alternative explicitly lists three favorable and five unfavorable influences. All eight names are translated. Neither list is silently equated with the five ordinary phases or with five extra natal pillars.

[^115]: **A pronunciation gloss and a distinct explanatory layer.** Xu directs that 占 be read in the departing tone, in the sense of occupying a position. The gloss is preserved without supplying a speculative historical phonetic reconstruction. His distinction of “mark” and “root,” directions, and natal versus course positions is his own account, not silently substituted for Wang’s foregoing interpretation.

[^116]: **Wood restored after Ren generates Wood.** S has 水歸於寅卯春旺, Water returning to Yin/Mao spring flourishing. The preceding clause says Ren generates Wood, and the parallel series requires Wood here. “[Wood]” is an explicit internal repair. The paragraph treats the phase classes collectively, whereas the earlier Metal/Wood discussion separated yin and yang stem stages; those frameworks remain distinguishable.

[^117]: **Nayin names and directional qi overlap.** Furnace Fire and Great-Forest Wood are the named nayin of the four pairs. Hai is both Wood’s Birth and Qian’s directional palace, allowing the commentator to introduce Qian Metal as a qualification. This is not silently rewritten into one ordinary branch-hidden-stem account.

[^118]: **The named pairs carry nayin.** Xinsi Metal, Jiashen Water, Gengyin Wood, Yihai Fire, Guihai Water, Renshen Metal, and Dingsi Earth must be read in that frame. The commentary later turns from technical stages to kinship and literal prognoses. Their juxtaposition does not make a technical Death position a modern diagnosis.

[^119]: **Five Tomb encounters, not a sixth hidden phase.** The examples pair a phase’s Tomb with a controlling nayin. Mourning Gate is separately derived by branch position. Thus a case can simultaneously enter Tomb and encounter Mourning. “Beneath the feet” emphasizes imminence in the image; it does not identify a physical symptom.

[^120]: **The annual labels are disputed.** The first account likens Heavenly Year to the minor course and Year Star to Tai Sui. Xu treats both as Tai Sui. Both derivations and the associated Hook, Entanglement, Holding, Inviting, Peering, and Pressing names remain. No one standardized star list is imposed on these alternative explanations.

[^121]: **Parent/child and husband/wife first name phase relations.** The initial Metal/Water/Fire example explains rescue through a generated child; Xu gives a different growth-stage interpretation. The paragraph finally extends the analogy to the actual relatives’ fates. All levels are preserved without presenting a relative’s chart as a reliable medical explanation for another person’s illness.

[^122]: **Two definitions are supplied.** The first means the Birth palaces of Lu, Fate, and Body; Xu instead emphasizes originally usable noble influences across the inner and outer Three Origins. These are alternative accounts, not three newly calculated life palaces added to the Four Pillars.

[^123]: **A funeral song and four household sounds.** 薤露, “Dew upon the Shallot,” is the named funeral lament contrasted with harmonious music. The four sounds are then explicitly enumerated: groaning, pain, separation, and weeping. The suggestion that moving house may avoid harm belongs to the historical commentary and is not offered as practical risk-reduction advice.

[^124]: **Weight is evaluated differently above and below.** Repeated control of the stem is called heavy; branch punishment not reaching natal Fate is called lighter. The examples are preserved with exactly the pillars supplied. The lower origin is then explicitly nayin. “Head” and “limbs” are the commentator’s corresponding prognostic regions, not diagnostic findings established here.

[^125]: **A different course-counting scheme.** The quoted method gives Jia/Ji five years, Yi/Geng four, Bing/Xin three, Ding/Ren two, and Wu/Gui one. It must not be substituted unnoticed for the ten-year major-course scheme. The worked Guiyou example totals its successive spans explicitly and makes ages twelve through eighteen the seven Metal-stem/Wood-nayin years. Tao the Recluse is left as the transmitted attribution; no otherwise unprovided personal identity is assigned.

[^126]: **Ghost names a controlling relation.** The Xinmao/Dingyou example evaluates both stem-Lu and branch-Fate, then the alternative extends “ghosts” to conduct, food, rest, and attendants. These are the source’s analogical layers, not a claim that a reader has supernatural enemies. Wu’s Food Geng and combining Ji are different rescue mechanisms.

[^127]: **Naked Form means Bath.** The text explicitly defines it through the phase’s second stage and gives Water/Earth at You, Wood at Zi, Fire at Mao, and Metal at Wu. Fengdu and Mount Dai are destinations in the historical afterlife imagery. The alternatives explain flanking sha through both Original/Seven Killings and an arched missing position. They are not merged into one unqualified mortality rule.

[^128]: **Day-selection language enters a fate text.** Yellow and Black Roads here belong to auspicious/inauspicious calendrical and directional systems, not simply the Western astronomical ecliptic. The alternative applies the monthly Establishment, Removing, and Fullness sequence to courses. Its analogy with marriage and building is translated in full; the compiler later acknowledges that some of this material is not strictly Three-Fates doctrine.

[^129]: **The alternate “Three Lives” are named spirits.** In the second explanation, Three Lives are Generating Qi, Heavenly Physician, and Blessing-Virtue; Five Spirits are five unfavorable influences. They are not the Lu/Fate/Body triad or the five ordinary phases. The commentator explicitly allows the interpretation to concern travel, marriage, and construction rather than birth-fate analysis.

[^130]: **Three Returns has more than one definition.** The first assigns the name to four Earth branches, also calling them Three Mounds and Five Tombs. The alternative instead uses the distinct Birth places of Lu, Fate, and Body: Jiazi’s Wood Lu at Hai, Water Fate at Shen, Metal Body at Si. “Three” is therefore not repaired to four, nor are the two explanations collapsed.

[^131]: **The complete Twelve-Branch Five-Ghost list is retained.** Its reciprocal Zi/Chen, Chou/Mao, and other pairs are a separate method from earlier Five-Ghost definitions. The annotation then offers an account through self-born nayin and parent/child relations. Its expression “a Five-Ghost son” is the historical text’s prognosis, not a legitimate label for a living child.

[^132]: **Two usages of Separated Angles remain.** The paragraph first names Yin, Shen, Si, and Hai, then illustrates separation with Chou/Mao day-hour pairs belonging to different directional qi. It does not explain this transition fully. The translation preserves both rather than silently replacing the example with a modern standardized definition.

[^133]: **An explicit boundary that later authors cross.** This commentary insists that Lu, Horse, Wealth, Officer, Seal, and Food are ordinary phase relations, not spirits and sha. The later *Penetrating Clarity* commentary explicitly uses “spirits and sha” for those very relational influences and contrasts its method with Luoluzi’s. The disagreement is substantive and has not been edited away.

[^134]: **River/Luo and Changes imagery.** The reference is to the traditional River Chart and Luo Writing, with Great Ultimate, sixty-four hexagrams, and 384 lines as its explanatory frame. The passage gives an analogy of one source unfolding into multiplicity, not a complete new procedure for deriving trigrams from the natal pillars.

[^135]: **Numbers, phases, and a second definition.** The two complete charts are retained with their nayin evaluations and claimed outcomes. The alternative defines Saddle as the branch ahead of Horse and Whip/Spur as the branch behind it. It is not substituted for the preceding account of numerical completion. The text’s prophecy of long-distance wandering remains its own claim.

[^136]: **A stated age, not a freshly verified biography.** Four times eight is thirty-two. Both examples provide a minor-course pair and annual pair but omit enough birth information to prevent independent reconstruction of their complete chronologies. They are translated as given. The following note expressly admits that some examples concern geography, Nine Palaces, and yearly divination rather than the Three Fates alone.

[^137]: **A Confucian criterion and an awkward physiognomic quotation.** The conduct test recalls *Analects* 2.10: observe what a person does, follows, and rests in. The Hemp-Clad saying in S makes the heart follow appearance into extinction in its second clause; G624 likewise has 心隨相滅. Although a more familiar formulation reverses that relation, it is not silently imported. The main text preserves the transmitted wording and its moral argument, not a scientifically validated physiognomy.

[^138]: **The moral conclusion is not erased by fatalism elsewhere.** The annotation makes virtue capable of changing the practical meaning of a threatening sign, while earlier examples sound predetermined. Both positions belong to the collection. Its attribution of calamity to misconduct is translated as a historical ethical argument, not an accusation that people suffering adversity caused it through moral failure.

[^139]: **A hypothesis within the source, not established authorship.** The commentator infers a Six-Dynasties setting from the mention of Guo Jingchun and tentatively proposes Liang’s Zhaoming through Lanye/Lanling. He rejects identification with the Zhou prince Zijin. The translation preserves the tentative character of the first proposal and the source’s certainty about its rejection without endorsing either as independently proven.

[^140]: **The mirror is the text’s own image.** The conclusion links a heart-mirror, the Clear Terrace, and one gain in a thousand to continued interpretation. The praise of Luoluzi is the commentator’s. The final “hundred generations” claim is literary aspiration, not a translator’s certification of the work’s reliability.

[^141]: **The first verse has a difficult Fire/Earth sign.** S prints 丙戊巳午, Bing, Wu, Si, Wu; G625 also does not securely supply the expected Ding in place of Wu. The main text preserves the signs rather than silently perfecting a Fire-only list. The commentary clearly makes Fire Officer and Wood Wealth for Metal. The twelve named Metal day-pairs are all retained.

[^142]: **Seasonal subdivisions are retained.** The first, middle, and last ten-day portions of named months are the commentary’s qi distinctions, not a modern civil-date table. “Clinging to Dragon’s scales” is an image of advancement through an exalted patron; it is not an instruction to add a Dragon branch absent from the example.

[^143]: **Six Harms is used loosely here.** The commentary calls Earth’s appearance Six Harms in its discussion of Water as Fire’s Officer. It supplies no standard six-pair branch calculation, so the name is retained without inventing one. “Ding’s Pig, Bing’s Rat” preserves the paired Hai/Zi image of Officer ground.

[^144]: **The missing stanza remains missing.** S expressly says the Wood section is absent and supplies a prose inference. That prose is translated, but no new Wood verse is composed. The Earth commentary’s 辛戌, Xinxu, is not a valid sexagenary pair; G625 preserves it too. It remains visibly named rather than silently becoming Xinyou or Gengxu. 丁未 can be read as a pillar or as Ding and Wei in the compressed list; the slash in the translation preserves that uncertainty.

[^145]: **Palaces restored; the framework changes again.** S’s 九官 is read 九宮, Nine Palaces, as in G625. The passage explicitly takes stems, then branches and nayin, before naming the Three Origins. This is not silently converted into the hidden-stem definition given later in *Penetrating Clarity*.

[^146]: **Two difficult readings retained.** Both S and G625 call the verse’s subject 丙子, Bingzi, rather than a collective Bing/Ding. Both also give 木火既濟, Wood and Fire in Completion, although Water and Fire are the familiar Completion pair and the commentary goes on to discuss Water’s growing strength after the summer solstice. Wood is retained in the main text because the consulted witness does not establish a correction. The juxtaposition remains an unresolved feature of the transmitted explanation.

[^147]: **“Employs Xin” restored.** S’s 月辛為官星為子 is G625’s 用辛, employing Xin as Officer Star and child. The English follows that reading rather than inventing an additional month-pillar condition. The sweeping judgment on Jia born in Yin is the text’s particular account; later chapters permit Established Lu with appropriate Wealth or Officer, and that disagreement remains.

[^148]: **Kui Star is expressly defined as a ten-day group.** Here it is Jiachen through Guichou, not simply the familiar literary deity or a differently calculated modern star. The Guichou/Wushen example belongs to that group. The child-prodigy examination is an institutional image of early literary success, not proof of a child’s actual aptitude from a chart.

[^149]: **An unresolved compressed Wealth clause.** The Jiawu example says 金旺土為財 and concludes that Lu, Human Origin, and Wealth lack qi. G625 preserves the same wording. The English does not rewrite it into a newly consistent table of every relation. The following Bingwu example explicitly distinguishes weak Officer from flourishing Wealth and Horse; both accounts remain complete.

[^150]: **Birth can refer to one relation while another declines.** The commentary explains a body’s Birth position through its Officer’s illness or extinction and consequent loss of office. That is the particular reading earlier commentary criticized. Its appearance here is preserved as evidence of different schools, not harmonized into one universally accepted rule.

[^151]: **A critical conclusion, not a translator’s disclaimer.** The final statement says Tongxuanzi’s account is his own compilation, not Luoluzi’s authentic original, and compares it with Xu Ziping’s. This is part of S. G625 writes 通元子 where S has 通玄子; the translation follows S’s name without inferring a second person solely from the graph variant.

[^152]: **Attribution follows the transmitted heading.** S attributes the rhapsody to Xu Ziping of Donghai and the explanation to Wan Yuwu of Yishui. These are reproduced as source attributions, not independently verified authorship. “Penetrating Clarity” preserves 明通 as a paired quality rather than treating it as the title of a new divination method.

[^153]: **This chapter’s Three Origins differ from Luoluzi’s.** Day-stem Heavenly Origin is called Fate-Origin, branch Earthly Origin Lu, and its concealed contents Human-Origin longevity. The distinctive assignments are retained. The opening quotation attributed to *Continuing Goodness* closely resembles the actual opening of *Preferences and Aversions* later in this juan; the source’s attribution has not been silently changed.

[^154]: **Four auspicious and four inauspicious relations.** The eight named categories are Officer, Seal, Wealth, Food, Killings, Injury, Owl, and Defeat. Their labels do not make every occurrence invariably good or bad: the commentary immediately explains redirected use, combination, and restraint. “Seal of authority” elsewhere likewise need not mean that Killings has literally become the ordinary Seal relation.

[^155]: **The text mixes solar-term depth and subdivisions of a time position.** Its three *ke* at the beginning of Zi, Chou, or Yin are preserved without a modern minute conversion. The residual Ren/Gui/Gen-Earth sequence is the stated example; no full hidden-stem timing table has been supplied from another school. Seen/unseen forms and residual qi are the two distinct subjects of the maxim.

[^156]: **Transforming Wood is not a Bing–Ding stem combination.** 丙丁化木 describes Fire receiving or transforming Wood’s output in this explanation, then assisting Earth Wealth. It does not assert that Bing combines with Ding to become Wood. The ensuing Wealth/Seal, Officer/Killings, and restraint examples are alternatives with different reference points.

[^157]: **Half blessing remains a compressed qualification.** S’s sequence about one Zi and two Wu, complete damage, and half blessing has uncertain punctuation. The translation preserves both complete breaking and a lesser, half-obtained condition rather than supplying a numerical scoring system. The paragraph also explicitly allows remote Flying Lu/Horse to change the interpretation of an otherwise broken configuration.

[^158]: **The terminology changes by school.** This commentary calls Wealth, Officer, Seal, Food, Injury, Killings, Blade, Defeat, and related influences spirits and sha, then contrasts that with Luoluzi’s usage. The disagreement is explicit. The five examples retain their complete transmitted pillars and the final dated death account; those dates are catalogue claims, not independently verified biographies.

[^159]: **A difficult ten-line mnemonic preserved.** The song uses branch combinations rather than the ordinary Three-Wonders stem triad. Several compressed lines, especially 水鼠未人火龜池, do not yield a secure single procedure. They are translated without inventing missing conditions. Lu and Horse sharing a palace are also called Three Wonders in the commentary; the terminology is local, not forcibly standardized.

[^160]: **Shi need not mean the birth hour here.** 真官時遇 can mean True Officer met at its fitting time. The commentary expressly calls this the monthly-command Direct Officer configuration. “Met in its season” follows that contextual reading, avoiding an artificial contradiction created by translating every 時 as the hour pillar. The Weng Zhongyi example is retained as given.

[^161]: **Direct and Indirect are not silently merged.** The Jia-in-Hai example distinguishes hidden Ren Indirect Seal from exposed Gui Direct Seal. Its subsequent social judgments about fostering, secondary birth, office, and rank are the commentator’s historical categories. They are not a dependable modern test of family legitimacy or ancestry.

[^162]: **The hour is essential to this configuration.** Repetition in the monthly branch is separately discussed as Established Lu and requires its own useful Wealth, Food, or Officer. The text does not simply say that every additional Lu improves the same pattern. The favorable and unfavorable courses are retained in their stated order.

[^163]: **The commentary explicitly corrects the maxim’s incompleteness.** It cites *Preferences and Aversions* to require restraint as well as strength, calling the rhapsody one-sided. That criticism and all three example charts are part of the source. The translation does not replace the maxim with a reconstructed rule and thereby erase the disagreement.

[^164]: **A single hourly position is qualified by the examples.** Ren Indirect Wealth can coexist with Gui Direct Wealth; the second chart also has Ding and Bing. The examples therefore should not be rewritten to enforce an indiscriminate ban on every other Wealth-related stem. The commentator’s discussion of combination and one Fire taking another’s authority remains as transmitted.

[^165]: **Glory Spirit is explicitly Seal-Ribbon.** 榮神 is not introduced as an independently calculated star. This Blade configuration welcomes Killings governing Blade and can be harmed if Food removes that governing force. The apparent reversal of a general “restrain Killings” instruction is intentional in this context.

[^166]: **Three Wealths are defined through Lu, Fate, and Body.** They are not three copies of ordinary Wealth or a new addition to Direct and Indirect Wealth. The commentary reads the maxim through Food/Injury generating Wealth, then records a differently worded alternative. Both are kept.

[^167]: **The transmitted day lists govern.** S’s 真宫 is read 真官, True Officer, from its following list and explanation. The True Noble list is retained as Dingyou, Guisi, Guimao, and Dinghai; it is not replaced with another school’s preferred Day-Noble enumeration. “True” here contrasts with borrowed or mixed conditions in the annotation.

[^168]: **A specific configuration, not a general dismissal of Food.** The text welcomes Seal and fears Direct Officer and Food in this case. Other passages welcome Food restraining Killings. Their different contexts remain, including the unusual condition that absence of Earth makes the body clear. No universal rule is manufactured from either example alone.

[^169]: **Two charts with contrasting outcomes.** The first uses strong Food without obstruction; the second is said to escape or fall out of the configuration, 脫局, while Ding impedes the Xin Officer that should fly in. The term’s exact scope is not fully explained. Both charts and the remote-configuration language are retained rather than replaced with a modern purely visible-stem analysis.

[^170]: **Brotherhood is a stem-polarity analogy.** Ding and Gui are called the younger brothers of Bing and Ren, inheriting the elders’ Lu positions at Si and Hai. The commentary extends the analogy to rank and exclusion of rival Companions. It is not a prediction about an actual elder sibling’s office. Its mixed and pure conditions are preserved in full.

[^171]: **Embryo describes receiving qi at Extinction.** The four listed days are interpreted through that technical relation, with Seal nourishing slight life and Wealth threatening it. “Infant’s favor” is the maxim’s courtly image of dependence and grace. It does not introduce a medical account of pregnancy or embryonic development.

[^172]: **The second example is not a complete chart in S.** Vice Minister Zhang is discussed through a Yi day and monthly Officer/Killings conditions, without all four pillars. No missing year or hour has been invented. The two surrounding complete examples remain. The distinction between a primarily Officer configuration and a primarily Killings configuration is expressly drawn by the commentary.

[^173]: **Renewed generation does not use one fixed referent.** The discussion moves from a weak day stem receiving monthly Seal to hidden branch qi generating Wood and to day/hour support. The commentator finally defines the older middle/lower usage as day/hour branches. These changing reference levels remain visible rather than being silently recast as the same modern Three-Origins table throughout.

[^174]: **Original Spirit and Solitary Branch are alternative labels here.** They name annual Indirect Officer in this account and are not automatically the independently calculated Yuanchen and Solitary stars elsewhere. The two complete examples illustrate restraint or transformation of repeated Killings; the text nevertheless says injury cannot wholly be avoided. Both the favorable judgment and that qualification remain.

[^175]: **The three hours are presupposed, not missing material supplied.** The Metal-Spirit account in [juan six](juan-06.md) identifies the traditional hour group Jisi, Guiyou, and Yichou. Here the source only refers back to “the three hours,” then distinguishes Jia from Ji days. The main text does not silently expand the cross-reference into a new stanza.

[^176]: **Storehouse is named through its use.** Chou, Chen, Wei, and Xu are assigned different offices relative to Jia; Ding’s Chen Officer Storehouse is then treated separately. Clash as a key can release use, but domination or injury by an incompatible stem can shut it away. The metaphor therefore does not say every clash of every Earth branch is beneficial.

[^177]: **The stated count is problematic.** S says the Officer-Seal relation has “two controls and one generation.” The Jia/Xin/Gui example more readily gives two generations and one control under ordinary phase relations. The count is retained as transmitted rather than silently reversed; the remainder of the annotation clearly requires proportionate strength among body, Officer, and Seal.

[^178]: **The poem remains a compressed example.** Jia/Ji with a Metal configuration injures the support of a Bing day; flourishing Fire in the course restores bodily capacity. S’s wording is not expanded into a complete invented Four-Pillar chart. The commentary distinguishes rescued but impure development from an originally pure configuration.

[^179]: **A consequential supported correction.** S reads 敗財破印尤吉, “Defeated Wealth breaking Seal is especially auspicious.” G625 reads 不吉, “inauspicious,” and S’s own commentary immediately explains the loss of useful form, wealth, and refinement. The main text adopts bracketed “[inauspicious]” on both external and internal evidence. The original positive reading is preserved here for comparison.

[^180]: **Seal is used broadly in this warning.** The maxim names Direct Seal as well as Owl, and the commentary treats Officer/Killings generating Seal as strengthening the threat to Food. It is not silently narrowed to one modern definition of Indirect Seal. The contrasting usefulness of Seal to Hurting Officer is part of the passage’s explicit explanation.

[^181]: **One branch can sustain different interpretations.** Wu Earth in Wu Fire month is first considered Blade and then, with abundant Fire, Seal-Ribbon. Ji emerging restores the Blade reading. The example and the requirement of appropriate restraint remain; the translation does not reduce the discussion to “Fire always means Seal” without its stated conditions.

[^182]: **The commentator explains shi as season, then allows hour.** Chou first means the monthly command, and the final sentence separately admits a Chou-hour case. The main text’s “Chou time” preserves that breadth. Metal emerging or forming the Si–You–Chou configuration is what redirects the initial Hurting-Officer judgment toward Wealth.

[^183]: **An unresolved hidden-Ding assertion.** The Xinmao illustration says Ding Fire within Mao injures Xin. This is not the ordinary hidden-stem assignment for Mao. The source does not securely explain the reference level, so Ding is retained and the difficulty recorded rather than inserted into a modern branch table as established fact.

[^184]: **The kinship assignments are not uniform across the collection.** This maxim links removal of Officer/Killings to loss of parents, while earlier passages often use them as children and Wealth/Seal as parents. Its only commentary says that both clauses concern relatives. No missing explanatory genealogy or swapped couplet is invented to resolve the difference.

[^185]: **Dehumanizing comparisons belong to the historical text.** The maxim’s cattle/sheep and pig/dog language is preserved as its moral and social hierarchy, not adopted as the translator’s description of people. The two complete examples and their condemnatory commentary likewise remain source claims, not a valid basis for judging anyone’s character.

[^186]: **Purity means the selected relation without its competitor.** The examples use ordinary Officer/Killings and Direct/Indirect Wealth distinctions, not necessarily one repeated sign in every pillar. The commentary gives exceptions for Blade month and warns about longevity. Those qualifications prevent treating “pure” alone as an unconditional favorable verdict.

[^187]: **Four different forms of completeness.** Seal, Clash, Restraint, and Food are separately defined. S’s 全制 example gives Ding or Wu 戊 where G625 has Ding or Wu 午; the main text retains S’s Wu without silently substituting the branch. The following generating-Wealth explanation makes the intended relation uncertain. The complete example charts are retained as S prints them.

[^188]: **A split sign restored to the heading.** S places 申 in small-character commentary after 庚日, but the accompanying *Preferences and Aversions* quotation clearly specifies 庚申時逢戊日, Gengshen hour on a Wu day. The translation rejoins Gengshen and preserves the actual day/hour relation. It does not introduce a new Geng-day configuration.

[^189]: **Direct and reverse labels vary in the text.** The Bing/Ding and Ren/Gui examples move among direct clash, reverse clash, and remote combination; the concluding Si-heavy examples are called direct clash. These terms are retained in their immediate contexts rather than forced into one revised classification. Filling, holding, and interfering with the empty position remain separately identified prohibitions.

[^190]: **Three commentaries restored from an interleaved transcription.** S’s source lines 966–998 contain a substantial displacement, not merely an isolated wrong character. The Ren/Four Uprights maxim at L966 is followed at L968 by the tail of the Rat-Noble example. The Ren commentary appears instead at the end of L978, interrupting the Jia/Ji transformation explanation. That transformation’s continuation, including its quoted classic and two complete charts, appears inside the Rat-Noble material at L998. G625 preserves coherent versions of all three; the corresponding facsimile page 82 also confirms their coherent sequence. The translation therefore places Ren’s explanation after its own maxim, joins the interrupted transformation commentary across L978/L998, and returns the Rat-Noble tail to its own example. The maxims remain in S’s order and all S’s substantive material is retained. Hidden source-line comments document the moved segments. This is a supported restoration of reading order, not a change to the repository’s Chinese source. In the last transformation example, S’s “Officer and body both flourishing” is retained where G625 has “Officer and Seal.”

[^191]: **True and false transformation are distinguished, not equated with any combination.** The commentary demands ground, seasonal support, and appropriate strength, and retains the contrasting examples of failed or excessive combination. S’s 合得申 is interpreted through G625’s 合得中 as a combination attaining the proper balance. The family and political comparisons are the text’s imagery, not independent biographical evidence.

[^192]: **These are output and remote-configuration applications of familiar names.** Wu with a Metal configuration is called Reforming, and Geng with a Water configuration Moistening Downward, rather than being reassigned to the ordinary five exclusive-phase patterns by day stem. The annotation explains their loss of qi and remote extraction of Officer. Its claims of rank combined with deficient wealth or offspring are retained together.

[^193]: **The broken third chart is recoverable.** S’s Rat-Noble paragraph ends its third example’s beginning with 辛亥甲; the displaced segment at L968 begins 午乙亥丙子. Joined, they give Xinhai/Jiawu/Yihai/Bingzi, exactly the coherent chart in G625 and its facsimile page 82. Its Wu interference and two Hai self-punishment explain the ensuing judgment. The full third example and its commentary are restored in the main text, not silently replaced by a different chart. The other two Rat-Noble charts remain unchanged.

[^194]: **The examples retain incomplete explanations.** In the Wuyin/Wuwu example the commentary invokes Yin clashing Shen although no Shen is displayed in the four supplied pillars. It may concern a remotely aroused position, but that is not made explicit. No extra Shen pillar is supplied. The existing chart and the statement that arching fails remain as transmitted.

[^195]: **Hidden representation is not an extra displayed pillar.** Ji can stand for its Chou/Wei palace, and Food or Wealth can draw a hidden combining Officer or Seal. The passage then distinguishes Gathered Noble, Assembled Noble, and a single effective Noble. Its alternative day/night and Blade qualifications are retained rather than collapsed into a single count of auspicious stars.

[^196]: **The commentator gives two meanings of Proper Noble.** One is ordinary Officer in its proper monthly qi; the other is the noble spirit at a branch containing useful Officer. The later warning that many combinations may cause Forgetting Officer qualifies the earlier welcome for combinations. Both statements are source doctrine, not a mistranslation to be removed.

[^197]: **Barrier names are not reduced to Seven Killings.** The commentary first supplies a particular yin/yang barrier list, then says many other named barriers exist and refers back to the longevity and childhood discussions. The English retains that limitation. These historical danger signs are not medical screening criteria or reliable forecasts of a child’s safety.

[^198]: **The difficult Water/Earth sentence remains.** S says “when Earth flourishes, Water flourishes,” then describes Earth collapsing; it is not silently changed merely to produce an elementary control-cycle rule. The Renxu/Renchen example concerns the day branch’s concealed Wealth/Killings, while the preceding chart and death year are a separate illustration. Their reference points must not be merged.

[^199]: **A shared unresolved negative.** S and G625 both say 印見妻財而不破, Seal seeing wife-Wealth and not being broken, while the commentary discusses Wealth damaging Seal unless rescued. Unlike the earlier Defeated-Wealth maxim, the consulted parallel supplies no secure repair here. The negative is retained and the contradiction identified instead of silently deleting 不.

[^200]: **The maxim and its citation are not a full explanation of one another.** A weak body with Wealth meeting repeated Direct Seal is called ominous, although other passages welcome Seal as support. The appended citation concerns abundant Killings and a weak body. It does not resolve every clause of the maxim; no missing causal account has been invented.

[^201]: **Fei means an untoward calamity here.** 非殃 is read as extraordinary or unforeseen harm, in parallel with the preceding violent calamity, not as the reassuring “no calamity.” The commentary only explains Bath and Rest/Imprisonment, leaving the first pair without a separate expanded rule.

[^202]: **The text expressly permits combined Officer and Killings here.** These difficult cases require enough governing force to restrain Companions, transform Wealth, or support Seal. A single influence is called insufficient. That exception does not erase the earlier preference for purity; the commentary itself makes the context decisive.

[^203]: **A first-person counterexample to another textual tradition.** The commentator reports dismissal and his mother’s death in Guihai year during Dingsi luck, treating the year’s clash with the course as heavier. He contrasts this with the *Deep Source* and *Deep Sea* accounts, which reverse the ranking. The report is translated as his testimony, not an independently verified dated biography or proof that one rule is universally correct.

[^204]: **Several barrier lists coexist.** The passage gives one list of yang limits and yin endings, then alternatives using yin tokens and reciprocal yin/yang barriers. Their differences are retained rather than reconciled into a newly standardized table. It also says a natal occurrence alone is not necessarily harmful: repetition in year/course and lack of strength or support matter.

[^205]: **The omission belongs to S.** The source says twenty lines about women were already quoted in the dedicated discussion and are not repeated here. The translation retains that notice and does not import a fresh twenty-line passage into this juan. The complete women’s material is in [juan seven](juan-07.md). This is not an unfinished translation or the separate editorial preface.

[^206]: **A textual-history claim by the compiler.** The statement that *Preferences and Aversions* and *Continuing Goodness* were transformed from this rhapsody is the source’s assertion. It has not been presented as an independently established genealogy of the texts. The notice also explains why the local annotations are selective while earlier configuration chapters are fuller.

[^207]: **A concise collected text, not an invitation to invent commentary.** S prints this chapter largely without explanatory notes. Its maxims are translated continuously, preserving their sequence and the few actual annotations. The definitions and exceptions developed in the preceding rhapsodies are not silently inserted as though original to this chapter.

[^208]: **A supported small repair.** S’s 如凶慶 is read 如凶處, “in ominous ground,” as quoted in the *Penetrating Clarity* commentary and G625. The bracketed word records the correction. The statement refers to the possible direct/reverse Flying-Lu use of Hurting Officer, not a general declaration that inauspicious configurations are harmless.

[^209]: **Weak, not flourishing, is supported by the internal quotation.** S here has 身旺, body flourishing, but its own *Penetrating Clarity* commentary quotes the same maxim with 身弱, weak; G625 likewise quotes 身弱. The main text therefore adopts bracketed “[weak].” S’s final 無壽, no longevity, is retained, along with its explicit alternative 無救, no rescue. G625’s quotation instead ends 無地, no ground: that further variant is not silently substituted.

[^210]: **Historical valuation retained without endorsement.** The two lines classify women through absence of Killings, one Noble, or many Nobles and combinations. They use the period’s hierarchy of wives, religious women, courtesans, and servants. They are not trustworthy grounds for inferring a living woman’s conduct, occupation, or worth.

[^211]: **Continuing goodness and the two cosmic supports.** “What covers and what carries” means Heaven and Earth. The title’s continuation of goodness resonates with the Changes’ 繼之者善也, but the text does not explain its title in a separate preface. The translation preserves the chapter as a compact series of maxims rather than adding new prose doctrine between them.

[^212]: **Two routes to public recognition.** The golden list is examination success; presenting grain refers to contribution-based recognition or advancement. They are not both translated as passing an examination. S’s 無破之官 is read 宮, an unbroken palace, from the parallel wording and the spatial structure of the maxim; the Office/Palace graphical distinction is recorded rather than concealed.

[^213]: **The chapter itself denies a simple moral reading from one star.** It expressly allows Officer/Seal in a petty person’s fate and Killings/Blade in a gentleman’s configuration. This qualification matters when reading its later moral and medical assertions. The purported good and bad labels alone are not sufficient even within the text’s own argument.

[^214]: **An apparent tension remains in the compact maxim.** A Day Master without support is said to welcome Wealth luck, whereas other passages warn that weak bodies cannot sustain Wealth. The verse supplies no full qualifying example here. It is not silently expanded into either a Following-Wealth configuration or an unconditional support rule; the preceding commentaries must be consulted for the competing contexts.

[^215]: **The stated result is Wealth.** S says that with Five-Phase rescue the year instead becomes 財, Wealth, not simply “auspicious.” The translation preserves that word. The adjacent statements about year injuring day versus day offending year likewise retain their transmitted hierarchy, despite the preceding commentary’s debate about year/course clashes.

[^216]: **Kinship dramatizes two rescue relations.** Geng generates Ren as son, and Ren restrains the Bing that threatens Geng. Jia’s younger sister Yi combines with Geng, drawing the threatening controller into another relation. The text is not advising marriages between actual people on that basis; the family language is its technical allegory.

[^217]: **Courtly rank, not two modern countries.** Enfeoffment in two states is a historical honorific image for a distinguished woman. First rank in the following line is official grade. The precise institutional award or holder is not named, so none is invented. These promises remain the source’s assertions, not certified outcomes.

[^218]: **The exceptions are original commentary.** The two small-character notes reverse the threatening Bing/Shen and Ji/Hai judgments when monthly Seal or Bingyin hour supplies the stated help. They are retained in the main text, not mistaken for translator additions. Cap, belt, pin, and tassel are insignia of official distinction.

[^219]: **The two examples contrast drainage with support.** Repeated Fire drains the one Wood; repeated Metal supports the one Water. The exact phrasing about “three” and the two named Metal stems is kept rather than completed into an invented four-pillar chart. The ensuing seasonal assurances should be read alongside the chapter’s explicit warnings against excess and imbalance.

[^220]: **Correlative medicine, not diagnoses.** Blood disorders, spleen injury, sinew/bone pain, and dim sight are assigned to phase conflict in these historical maxims. The translation preserves them without converting them into modern diagnostic categories or recommending treatment. The broader book sometimes assigns the same symptom through different phase relations.

[^221]: **Directional palaces carry phase implications.** Gen and Xun refer to the northeastern and southeastern trigram regions used in the text’s phase-stage reasoning. Returning Soul and Unextinguished name rescue where a phase seems exhausted; they do not establish literal resurrection or survival predictions. The chapter closes by requiring balanced qi, which qualifies any isolated reading of its earlier maxims.
