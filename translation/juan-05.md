# Sanming Tonghui · Juan Five
## A Comprehensive Gathering of the Three Fates

**Compiled by Wan Minying of the Ming dynasty.** From the imperially commissioned *Siku Quanshu*.[^01]

*Reading conventions.* Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, and Water are the Five Phases. *Qi*, *yin*, *yang*, *nayin*, and **Lu** retain their technical meanings; stem–branch names use unaccented pinyin. Capitalized expressions such as Direct Officer, Seven Killings, Seal, Food God, Birth, and Extinction are technical names. **〈 〉** marks the source’s small-character commentary. Square brackets mark supplied wording or an explicitly noted repair. The source’s predictions and social judgments are translated as historical assertions, not presented as established facts about living people. The Chinese source is unchanged.[^02]

## 1. On the Meanings of the Names Seal, Food, Officer, and Wealth Established by the Ancients
### 論古人立印食官財名義

<!-- source: L14-L22 -->
In discussing configurations, Xu Ziping takes just four things—Seal, Food, Officer, and Wealth—as his guiding framework. What is the meaning behind these names? Creative transformation flowing between Heaven and Earth is nothing beyond yin and yang and the Five Phases. Their mutual workings are nothing beyond generation, control, restraint, and transformation. Take Jia and Yi as an example. Considered as day stems, they belong to Wood among the Five Phases: Jia is yang, Yi yin. When someone is born on a Jia or Yi day, this is called the Day Master and represents “me.” Ren and Gui Water generate me; I generate Bing and Ding Fire. Geng and Xin Metal control me; I control Wu and Ji Earth. Thus all ten stems are accounted for.

What generates me has the meaning of parents; hence the name **Seal-Ribbon**. “Seal” suggests sheltering protection; “ribbon” suggests receiving. Parents possess kindness and virtue, sheltering their descendants, and the descendants receive their blessing. When the court establishes offices and assigns duties, it gives the officers seals and ribbons so that they may administer their charge. An officer without a seal—what warrant has he? A person without parents—upon whom can he rely? The principle is one and the same; hence “Seal-Ribbon.”[^03]

What I generate has the meaning of children and descendants; hence the name **Food God**. Eating is like an insect eating something: it injures what is eaten. The insect is filled by its food; a person benefits from eating; what is eaten suffers loss. In creative transformation, the child comes to maturity and provides nourishment—just as a son supports his parents. Hence “Food God.” What controls me has the meaning of my being subject to someone else; hence **Officer and Killings**. “Officer” means “[coffin]”; “Killings” means harm. When the court grants someone an office, his person belongs to the public service. He submits to its commands, going through boiling water and treading fire without daring to disobey, and is released only when the coffin is closed over him. In this sense, office harms him. That dreaming of a coffin portends obtaining office carries the same meaning; hence “Officer and Killings.”[^04]

What I control has the meaning of others being subject to me; hence **Wife and Wealth**. To establish a household and acquire property, a man needs his wife’s assistance within the home; hence this name. These are the principal meanings by which practitioners established the four terms. Generation lies close to oneself, whereas control operates across an intervening position. Creative transformation favors generation and dislikes killing: such is its natural principle. Within this, yin and yang following their own kinds, and yin and yang pairing with one another, each embody a consummate principle.[^05]
For “what generates me” and “what I generate,” consider Ren generating Jia, Gui generating Yi, Jia taking Bing as Food, and Yi taking Ding as Food. Yin generates yin and yang generates yang; yin takes yin as Food and yang takes yang as Food. Thus yin and yang each follow their own kind. Jia therefore welcomes generation by Ren: dead timber immersed in still, lifeless water can endure for many years without decaying. It does not welcome generation by Gui: dead timber drenched by rain rots before a year is out. Jia welcomes Bing as Food, because Bing can restrain Geng Killings and so let Jia rest secure. It does not welcome Ding as Food, because Ding can injure Officer and prevent Jia from being fashioned into useful timber. This is the meaning.[^06]

For “what controls me” and “what I control,” consider Xin controlling Jia, Geng controlling Yi, Jia controlling Ji, and Yi controlling Wu. Yin controls yang and yang controls yin; yin mates with yang and yang mates with yin. This is the principle of their pairing. Jia therefore takes Xin as **Direct Officer** and Geng as **Indirect Officer**. Officer favors the direct rather than the indirect: the official who holds the seal and the official who serves as deputy perform different duties. Jia takes Ji as the principal wife and Wu as a secondary wife. The principal is honored above the secondary: the one who stands as her husband’s counterpart and the one who stands in attendance occupy different stations. Such is the principle.[^05]

Officer fears injury: injured, it brings calamity. Wealth fears robbery: robbed, it is divided. Seal fears Wealth: greed for Wealth destroys it. Food fears Owl: meeting Owl, it is seized. The principles are no different from those of human affairs. Once a student understands human affairs, he can speak of creative transformation.

The Five Phases’ successive generation and control all carry the meaning of children avenging their parents. Jia and Yi generate Bing and Ding as children. Jia and Yi fear Geng and Xin and depend on Bing and Ding to restrain them. Bing and Ding generate Wu and Ji as children. They fear Ren and Gui and depend on Wu and Ji to restrain them. Wu and Ji generate Geng and Xin as children. They fear Jia and Yi and depend on Geng and Xin to restrain them. Geng and Xin generate Ren and Gui as children. They fear Bing and Ding and depend on Ren and Gui to restrain them. Ren and Gui generate Jia and Yi as children. They fear Wu and Ji and depend on Jia and Yi to restrain them.

The same principle holds for the twelve earthly branches. Though movement and stillness differ, and the square and round are distinct, generation and control remain one. Let us explain. Northern Hai and Zi Water generate eastern Yin and Mao Wood; eastern Yin and Mao Wood generate southern Si and Wu Fire. Earth borrows its flourishing from Fire and generates western Shen and You Metal; western Shen and You Metal generate northern Hai and Zi Water. Yet Chou intervenes between Hai–Zi and Yin–Mao; Chen between Yin–Mao and Si–Wu; Wei between Si–Wu and Shen–You; and Xu between Shen–You and Hai–Zi. Earth stands at the four corners, and the Five Phases all depend upon it. Thus Si and You combine with Chou to form the Metal configuration; Shen and Zi with Chen to form Water; Hai and Mao with Wei to form Wood; and Yin and Wu with Xu to form Fire. Metal generates Water, Water Wood, Wood Fire, Fire Earth, and Earth Metal: generation proceeds without interruption.

Chou, Metal’s Storehouse, generates Hai and Zi and controls Yin and Mao. Chen, Water’s Storehouse, generates Yin and Mao and controls Si and Wu. Wei, Wood’s Storehouse, generates Si and Wu and receives Metal’s control. Xu, Fire’s Storehouse, controls Shen and You and receives Water’s restraint. The southeast governs generation, the northwest killing: this is the great mechanism of Heaven and Earth. Chen, Xu, Chou, and Wei settle the four corners securely; Metal, Wood, Water, and Fire all rely upon them for birth and storage. The *Changes* says, “Completion is spoken of in Gen; ending is spoken of in Kun.” Earth’s work is therefore especially great among the Five Phases.[^07]

Taking stems and branches together: Jia is born at Hai and dies at Wu; Yi is born at Wu and dies at Hai. They reach Lu at Yin and Mao. In this respect Jia, Yi, Yin, and Mao are the same. Bing is born at Yin and dies at You; Ding is born at You and dies at Yin. They reach Lu at Si and Wu: Bing, Ding, Si, and Wu are the same. Geng is born at Si and dies at Zi; Xin is born at Zi and dies at Si. They reach Lu at Shen and You: Geng, Xin, Shen, and You are the same. Ren is born at Shen and dies at Mao; Gui is born at Mao and dies at Shen. They reach Lu at Hai and Zi: Ren, Gui, Hai, and Zi are the same. Wu is born at Yin and dies at You; Ji is born at You and dies at Yin. They reach Lu at Si and Wu, sharing Fire’s positions: the child flourishes along with its mother. Chen, Xu, Chou, and Wei, however, are Earth’s own proper positions.[^08]

Thus heavenly stems and earthly branches combine and pair, generate and control, restrain and transform, flourish and assist, rest and become confined. Their names are Seal, Owl, Food, Hurting Officer, Officer, Killings, Wealth, and Rob Wealth. Through punishments, clashes, breaking, harms, invitations from absent positions, and hidden combinations, their transformations are inexhaustible. Xu Ziping penetrated this principle. He therefore discusses only Wealth, Officer, Seal, and Food, dividing them into six configurations. Wealth and distinction, poverty and low station, long life and early death, obstruction and success—none of human destiny falls outside them. All other configurations are merely extensions of these.[^09]

## 2. On Direct Officer
### 論正官

<!-- source: L24-L30 -->
〈Favored: a strong body; Seal-Ribbon; Food God; Wealth as the leading influence. When Officer appears, examine Wealth. Avoided: a weak body; Indirect Officer; Hurting Officer; punishment and clash; drainage of qi; greedy attachment to combination; entry into the Tomb.〉

〈Also called **Direct Officer** and **Lu Spirit**.〉

**Direct Officer** is exemplified by Jia meeting Xin or Yi meeting Geng: yin and yang pair, their mutual restraint serves a purpose, and their proper way is fulfilled. Hence Direct Officer stands first among the six configurations. Only one occurrence is allowed; multiplicity is unsuitable. First examine the monthly command, and only then the other positions. Of the Five Phases’ qi, that which holds the monthly command has the greatest seasonal authority. Moreover, the Four Pillars each govern a span of life: the year governs the first fifteen years—too early if that is all one has; the hour governs after fifty—too late if that is all one has. Thus the monthly command is taken as the proper basis. The other configurations follow this rule.[^10]

Jia born in You month; Yi in Shen or Si; Bing in Zi; Ding in Hai; Wu in Mao; Ji in Yin; Geng in Wu; Xin in Yin or Si; Ren in Wu, Wei, or Chou; and Gui in Chen, Si, or Xu: these all have Officer Star in its proper seasonal qi. If it also emerges in a heavenly stem—as Jia meeting Xinyou or Yi meeting Gengshen—this is called “concealed in the branch, exposed in the stem.” It should not appear again elsewhere. The Day Master must be vigorous, with both Wealth and Seal supporting it, and without Hurting Officer or Killings in the pillars. A luck course that draws it into Officer’s territory then makes a fate of great wealth and distinction.

Punishment, clash, breaking, harm, Hurting Officer, Seven Killings, forgetting Officer through greedy attachment to combination, and Rob Wealth dividing the blessing are all greatly feared: they break the configuration. For Jia born in You month, Mao clashes with You, You punishes it, Wu breaks it, Xu harms it; Bing combines, Yi robs, Ding injures and controls, and Geng brings mixture. Officer must be single and pure, the Five Phases harmonious and unalloyed, before this is judged as Direct Officer. Even if something in the pillars removes one of these faults, the arrangement is no longer entirely pure.
If Officer Star forms a complete configuration and Wealth also supports it, development waits until the luck course reaches ground that strengthens the body. If there are only one or two Officers, with Seal but no Wealth, a weak body does no harm. If all Four Pillars turn their backs on Lu, examine whether the years and luck courses approach or depart from the flourishing grounds of Wealth and Officer. If Wealth and Officer fill the entire chart while the Day Master is too weak to bear them, the effort is fruitless. A course into flourishing Wealth and Killings often brings wasting illness. With Seven Killings already present, meeting it again in the luck course makes a fate of penal servitude and exile.

Another account says: Jia born in You month has Xin Metal as proper Lu. Suppose Ding appears to injure it, but the branches form no supporting configuration for Ding, while the hour draws Ding into Decline, Defeat, Death, or Extinction; or suppose restraint or combination removes it. How can Fire in Decline and Extinction injure flourishing Lu? But if the hour draws Officer into Decline, Defeat, Death, or Extinction, while drawing Ding Fire into Birth and flourishing, or into Killings’ ground, demotion, loss of office, and calamity follow without doubt. The hour is where things finally come to rest: their good and ill depend entirely on its waxing and waning. When the Day Master or the useful influence is excessive, the hour should regulate it; when it gradually declines, the hour should replenish it. Even an ominous influence in the pillars cannot work harm if the hour restrains it. This is an essential method of reading a fate.[^11]

Another account says: Jia born in Chou month already has Xin Metal within Chou. Meeting a You hour repeats it. If many Xin also emerge in the stems and the course proceeds west, the body cannot sustain their force: Officer becomes a ghost, and at the point of flourishing collapse is inevitable. Calamity and early death often follow; combination or restraint is needed for good fortune. If the body itself rides flourishing qi—as on Jiayin or Yimao days—and Seal further generates and supports it, even many Officers cause no harm. Jia born in Xu month sits at Fire’s Storehouse, but without a completed configuration or allies Fire cannot injure it. Xin’s Blade is at Xu, and flourishing Wu within Xu generates Xin. Even if Geng emerges and creates mixture, it has no qi in Xu month; a little restraint or combination removes the concern.[^12]

It is also said: when Officer is employed and the day stem sits upon Wealth or Seal, eventual distinction follows, as on Jiazi or Jiachen days. Sitting upon Hurting Officer or Killings eventually leaves some impediment or defect, as on Jiawu, Jiaxu, or Jiashen days. These matters require weighing.

Another account says: in selecting Officer Star, do not insist exclusively upon the branch of the monthly command. It may be in the month stem or in the stems or branches of year, day, or hour. If it appears in only one place and has suffered no damage, it can be used. Hence the classic says: “If the visible stem has qi, take the visible stem; if it lacks qi, take what is hidden.” Even a visible stem without qi may be led back to a branch or receive support; if its luck course reaches the right ground, its blessing is no less than Officer within the month.

Another account says: in discussing Officer Star, even one firmly seated Food God can damage the configuration. Only when Lu is concealed within the monthly command does meeting Food instead confer the nobility of the Three Wonders. The essential question is whether Officer and Food are substantial or merely apparent. If Officer is firmly seated and the combining influence is somewhat insubstantial, it follows Officer and assists distinction. If the combining influence is firmly seated and Officer gradually weakens, Officer follows it: this is “forgetting Officer through greedy attachment to combination.”[^13]

Another account says: the Direct-Officer configuration should travel through Seal’s territory. This is the rule “When Officer appears, examine Seal.” When Seal is originally present, judge the luck course by the relative weights of Officer and Seal and the strength or weakness of the day stem. If the body is weak and Seal light, replenish Seal; if the body is strong and Officer light, replenish Officer. Traveling a Hurting-Officer course is “turning one’s back on Lu”; traveling a body-strengthening course is “driving away Horse.” Luoluzi says: “Turning one’s back on Lu and driving away Horse, one remains distressed upon the road of poverty.” Traveling a Killings course brings Killings to mingle with Officer; traveling a Tomb course brings Officer into the Tomb. The classic says: “Officer and Killings mixed: poverty or early death. Flourishing Killings entering the Tomb: life is difficult to prolong.” Thus Direct Officer favors approaching Lu and coming to Wealth. Examine it in this way, and not one judgment in ten thousand will miss.[^14]

The *Seal of the Three Fates* says: “The three kinds of Officer belonging to Lu, Fate, and Body each receive the Five Phases; judge them according to the nature of their phase.” If Metal is Officer, the post is distinguished and exacting, often administering penal justice, money, or grain; the person judges clearly and promptly. When the annual Tai Sui reaches Chou, it reaches Officer’s Storehouse, a welcome event. Yet one must still distinguish flourishing, assistance, rest, and confinement, and possession or absence of qi. If Wood is Officer, rank is pure and lofty, the manner harmonious with others and cautious in conduct; Wei is its annual Storehouse. If Fire is Officer, rank is dazzling, the nature fierce, punishments harsh, and advancement and withdrawal irregular; Xu is its annual Storehouse. If Water is Officer, one begins in a low office and rises step by step, modest and agreeable, winning people’s support, compassionate toward orphans and widows, with an inclination toward the Way; Chen is its annual Storehouse. If Earth is Officer, office is secure and difficult to assail; the person is substantial, straightforward, and clear in law and command; Chen is its annual Storehouse.

Each phase’s Officer is auspicious when it follows its own nature; departing from that nature means office will not last. A Guichou person with a Wood fate, for example, takes Earth as Lu’s Officer, Wood as Fate’s Officer, and Metal as Body’s Officer. These are judged by the respective standing of the three fates and the Five Phases’ strength or rest. For the Storehouses of these three Officers, take Jiazi Metal: Jia is Lu and belongs to Wood. Metal controls Wood, so Metal is Lu’s Officer. Metal’s Tomb is Chou, making Chou the Storehouse of Lu’s Officer. Apply the same reasoning to Fate’s and Body’s Officer Storehouses.[^15]

A reader of fate from western Shu says: “When the four stems of year, month, day, and hour provide Officer and Seal to one another in full, they indicate distinction.” Examining great fates, one often finds two Seals. Academician Lü Jifu’s chart is **Renshen / Jiyou / Dingsi / Gengzi**: Ren with Ji and Geng with Ding form Officer relationships; Ren with Geng and Ji with Ding form Seal relationships. Chancellor Zhang Zihou’s is **Dinghai / Wuzi / Dingwei / Renyin**, with **Jimao** as conception: Wu with Yi, Ren with Ji, and Ding with Ren form Officer relationships; Yi with Ren and Wu with Ding form Seal relationships. Two Officers are less often encountered. It is further required that Direct Officer meet the Four Noble positions or Canopy for great distinction. This too is a one-sided opinion.[^16]

It is also said: in examining Officer Star, there is **Heavenly-Officer Territory**, where the monthly stem controls the year stem. In a Jia year, for instance, the first month is Bingyin, the fifth Gengwu, the sixth Xinwei. Geng and Xin Metal can control Jia Wood; Wu and Gui accompany one another in this reckoning. Thus Jia and Gui people take Wu and Wei; Yi and Bing take Chen and Si; Ding and Wu take Yin and Mao; Ji and Geng take Xu and Hai; Xin and Ren take Shen and You. Whoever meets this shall receive Heaven’s emolument and hold honored place at court.[^17]

There is **Earthly-Officer Territory**, where the monthly branch controls the year stem. Jia and Yi year stems belong to Wood; the seventh and eighth months bring Metal, which can control Wood. Thus Jia and Yi take Shen and You; Bing and Ding take Hai and Zi; Wu and Ji take Yin and Mao; Geng and Xin take Si and Wu; Ren and Gui take Chen, Xu, Chou, and Wei. Meeting this indicates early receipt of inherited favor and easy promotion. There is also **True-Officer Territory**: take the third branch ahead of the natal position, where the month stem combines with the natal stem. A Bingzi person born in the second month meets Xinmao, three positions ahead; Bing combines with Xin. This is birth within True Officer. A gentleman obtains a post close to the ruler; an ordinary person also becomes wealthy. Censor Liu Xingsu’s **Bingzi / Xinmao / Bingzi / Xinmao** exactly fits this account.
Another account says: practitioners call Jia meeting Xin Officer, but do not know that a **True Officer** arises when the True Five Phases control the *nayin*. This is a blessing influence of the greatest importance. The finest arrangement has Officer directly in the stems and obtains combination; it is best of all when the other influences come to pay court. Someone born in Jiayin has Water *nayin*. Obtaining Jihai brings Jia–Ji as True Earth: the *nayin* encounters the control of a True Phase. If control does not involve Tai Sui’s own stem, Yi and Geng appearing in day, month, or hour may also qualify. Geng alone is called Indirect Officer and has less force than the complete pair. If neither Yi nor Geng appears, but only Yisi, Yichou, or Yiyou, Shen should occur repeatedly elsewhere. Without Shen, only three to five tenths of the power remain. Infer the other cases by analogy.[^18]

But Jiyou or Jimao meeting Ding–Ren, and Renzi or Renwu meeting Yi–Geng, are different. Although the True Phase controls the *nayin* and would thereby be Officer, the original stem also receives control. Judge this not as True Officer but as a **True Ghost**. Encountering it in a major course, minor course, or flowing year makes the calamity especially severe. Anyone examining Officer Star must also understand these preferences and aversions.

The *Chapter on Preferences and Aversions* says: “Direct Officer in the Five Phases avoids palaces of clash, punishment, control, and breaking.” The *Chapter on Continuing Goodness* says: “Passing the examinations requires Officer Star to occupy an unbroken palace.” It also says: “Officer and Seal, without breaking, make a talent for the council halls.” And: “To have one’s name on the golden list, the body must flourish when Officer is met. A weak body meeting Officer merely wastes its strength after obtaining the post.” The *Five-Word Verses* say: “With Officer there must be Seal; without punishment, the arrangement deserves praise. If not a guest of the golden palace, one becomes the head of a wealthy house.” The *Rhapsody on Penetrating Clarity* says: “True Officer met in the hour, with the fate strong: early investiture in gold and purple.” The classic says: “When noble Officer is excessive, the moment it reaches flourishing, it must fall.”[^19]

The *Three Fates* says: “With a shallow root and source, one meets noble Officer without attaining splendor. A weak body welcomes Seal-Ribbon generating it, and Companions and Yang Blade sustaining it.” *Establishing the True* says: “Most noble is a fate grounded in Officer Star, with Direct or Indirect Wealth in day and hour making its blessing.” It also says: “Officer Star meeting Rob Wealth: though there is office, there is no distinction.” The *Secret Instructions* say: “Dismissal and retirement from office arise because Officer meets combination in the luck course.” And: “Repeated Officer Star is judged as Killings. Returning to Officer’s territory, calamity and trouble are hard to avoid. If it is excessive, restraint makes blessing.”

The *Rhapsody on Pointing Out the Maze* says: “The day meets noble True Officer, and the hour adds Lu and Fate. Officer and Seal encountered at birth in day and hour must rest upon ground possessing qi. Clash, breaking, and Void make an encounter no encounter at all. Officer and Seal meeting a ghost are as though absent. Examine whether ghost is flourishing or declining, and which side prevails. Officer’s control of Fate is welcome; do not let Fate turn back to control Officer. Officer and Seal subjected to punishment make a soldier or a clerk. Officer and Seal absent in the visible but present in the hidden are like jade belts laid one upon another. Meeting in day and hour, their blessing exceeds mere occupation of a seat tenfold. Officer without Seal makes a reputation for pure distinction difficult; Seal without Officer does not develop swiftly.”[^20]

The *Rhapsody on the Source* says: “Direct Officer names loyalty, trustworthiness, dignity, and gravity; it signifies governing the state and ordering the household. With Heaven’s timing and Earth’s advantage in the Four Pillars, one passes the examinations early, wins rank for the wife, and patronage for the sons. Years and courses through Officer and Seal bring ten thousand cauldrons, a thousand measures of grain, paired banners and five horses. For a common person, however, it often brings harassment by official affairs; for a woman, it may bring the investiture of a titled lady. A woman’s fate may hold distinction within humble station; a man who does not obtain office may prosper through reliance on the powerful.”[^21]

The *Rhapsody on Examining the Heart* says: “Officer Star is kindly and gracious; its noble qi rises high. It embraces abundance with benevolence, compassion, and generosity; its heart is open, its voice harmonious and clear. Its bearing is handsome and refined; its nature keen and intelligent.” The *Essential Instructions* say: “Direct Officer makes a person miserly.” The *Rhapsody of Myriad Auspices* says: “Officer Star is the ruler that brings honor to the person, the source administering Lu. Meeting Wealth, it advances with ease; meeting Blade, it is obstructed and alone. It welcomes Seal-Ribbon to fulfill its intentions and avoids partisan influences that damage its spirit. Thus exceptional achievement belongs to a strong body and flourishing Officer; depleted income and standing belong to a weak body and declining Officer. A strong body with a slight Officer has little accord with wealth and fame. When Hurting Officer is heavy, Seal’s nourishment is welcome; when the body is light and the adverse luminary strong, Seal-Ribbon’s generation and restraint are especially desired.”[^22]

### Heavenly-Blessing Noble Person — 天福貴人
<!-- source: L32-L34 -->
This means that Officer Star occupies its Lu: to hold office and receive emolument is surely Heaven’s blessing. A Jia person takes Xin as Officer, and Xin’s Lu is at You; thus Jia meeting You, Yi meeting Shen, and analogous cases qualify. For Jia using Xin Officer, Xinyou in the pillars is finest when a blessing influence further supports it and it possesses Birth, flourishing, and qi. It is also called **Stem-of-Lu Blessing Spirit**. Meeting it indicates lofty examination success and honored office, often with the distinction of drafting imperial pronouncements and literary documents.

### Heavenly-Officer Noble Person — 天官貴人
<!-- source: L36-L38 -->
This is the place occupied by Officer Star, obtained by tracing the stem–branch sequence of one’s own ten-pair round. A Jia person, for whom Xin is Officer, traces to Xinwei; Yi traces to Gengchen; Bing to Guisi, and so forth. When the ten stems’ Officer Stars sit upon Tianyi Noble Person—as Jia meeting Xinwei or Bing meeting Guisi—it is called “Noble Person bearing Officer Star upon its head.” Seal-Ribbon, Lu, and Noble Person all appearing, the Heavenly Origin clear and refined, and no reverse injury to the *nayin* make it especially auspicious. Reverse injury, clashes, adverse control, or absence of qi turn Heavenly Officer hostile and drive good fortune away. Another name is **Officer-and-Noble-Person Hall Configuration**.[^24]

### Heavenly Origin Seated upon Lu — 天元坐禄
<!-- source: L40-L42 -->
The classic says: “Metal meeting Fire possesses weighty authority, a military commissioner or prefect.” 〈Such days include Gengwu, Gengyin, Gengxu, Xinsi, and Xinwei.〉 “Water meeting Earth enters the Officer configuration and may receive a vice-minister’s emolument.” 〈Such days include Renwu, Renxu, Guisi, Guichou, and Guiwei.〉 “Wood meeting Metal suffers injury and decline; transforming Killings into authority, its power is like thunder.” 〈Such days include Jiashen, Jiaxu, Yisi, Yiyou, and Yichou.〉 “Fire meeting Water commands the troops, a general guarding the three frontiers.” 〈Such days include Bingshen, Bingzi, Bingchen, Dinghai, and Dingchou.〉 “Earth meeting Wood obtains proper Lu, the blessing of the Eight Seats and Three Terraces.” 〈Such days include Wuyin, Wuchen, Jimao, Jiwei, and Jihai.〉 These are configurations such as **White Tiger Holding the World**. The Day Master and noble Officer must balance each other; one-sided weakness prevents creative transformation from being fulfilled. Punishment, clash, breaking, and harm that damage the noble qi are greatly feared, for they destroy the configuration.[^25]

A Gengwu day sits upon Ding Officer. It welcomes Jia and Yi Wealth generating Officer and Wu and Ji Seal generating the body. It avoids Bing Killings mingling with Officer, Gui Water injuring Officer, and Zi clashing and breaking Wu. Infer the other stems from this. ## 3. On Indirect Officer
### 論偏官

<!-- source: L136-L144 -->
〈Favored: a strong body; Seal-Ribbon; combining with Killings; Food restraining it; Yang Blade; Companions. When Killings appears, examine Seal and Blade. Use Food as the leading influence. Avoided: a weak body; Wealth Star; Direct Officer; punishment and clash; entry into the Tomb.〉

〈Its names are **Indirect Officer**, **Seven Killings**, **Five Ghosts**, **General Star**, and **Star of Utmost Solitude**.〉

**Indirect Officer** is exemplified by Jia meeting Geng or Yi meeting Xin. It is likened to two men not sharing one dwelling or two women not forming a household: they do not make the required pair, hence “indirect.” The controlling opponent is seventh in the sequence, hence “Seven Killings.” It resembles a petty man, violent and unrestrained. Without rites and laws to govern him, without punishment or warning, he must injure his master. Thus, with restraint, it is called Indirect Officer; without restraint, Seven Killings.[^43]

If the Day Master is vigorous and Seal-Ribbon assists the transformation, this is the classic’s “Killings meets Seal and becomes distinguished”: Killings helps to generate Seal. If Wealth Star gives generation and support, this is “When Killings appears, examine Wealth.” A strong body and weak Killings welcome Wealth; a weak body and strong Killings with Wealth drawing in the ghost and draining qi bring poverty or early death. If Food God emerges to restrain it, this is “The moment restraint appears, distinction follows.” If Yang Blade is originally present to pair with it, this is “Without Blade, Killings does not shine,” and “When Killings appears, examine Blade.”

All these forms of restraint, combination, generation, and transformation must avoid both excess and deficiency. They borrow the petty man’s power to guard the gentleman, thereby establishing authority. Such a fate possesses great power and distinction, together with an intelligent disposition. A declining Day Master, repeated Seven Killings, the Three Punishments and Six Harms, Robbery and Loss together, and clashing Kui and Gang are feared; their calamities cannot all be described.

If Seven Killings occurs only once and restraint occurs in two or three places, a course into flourishing Killings is welcome. If luck instead brings still more restraint, it is “all law and no people”: even the fierceness of tiger and wolf has no room to exercise its skill. One must not speak only of restraint; relative weight must be appropriate. Hence the classic says: “With restraint already present, Killings emerging brings blessing; with no restraint present, Killings emerging brings calamity.” This is the meaning.[^44]

Suppose Jia meets Geng and Shen, or Yi meets Xin and You, so that Killings flourishes and possesses qi in the pillars. A southeastern course that restrains Geng and Xin and deprives them of qi allows advancement. Alternatively, birth in Yin or Mao month, or sitting at Birth, Arrival at Office, or Imperial Prosperity, with many Companions and kindred influences providing support, can transform ghost into Officer and Killings into authority. A course drawn into Seal’s territory must then bring wealth and distinction. But if the year and course return to Killings’ ground, calamity follows before one can turn on one’s heel.

For example, Jiayin birth makes the body strong, while Gengshen in year or month makes Killings strong. No Fire emerges in the pillars to restrain it, but Zi and Chen in the branches assemble Seal’s configuration: Killings generates Seal, Seal generates the body. Judge this as authority and distinction. Exposed Killings in the year stem differs from Killings in the monthly command or hour branch. Tai Sui is the ruler of the whole life and carries the greatest weight. A Jia day meeting a Geng year, or a Yi day meeting a Xin year, born in Shen, You, or Chou month with much Metal in the pillars, is particularly ominous if the major course travels Metal and the flowing year brings it too. Birth in Yin, Wu, or Xu, or a month of flourishing Wood, gives Fire’s restraint and bodily strength. Metal at Extinction cannot injure, and the arrangement is auspicious.[^45]
The classic says: “Jia defeated by Geng: leaves wither, branches fall, and the roots dry up. Yi injured by Xin: the root is depleted and the shoots damaged. Blazing Bing Fire meets Ren: its darkened flame gives no light. Brilliant Ding’s red meets Gui: its radiance is extinguished. Wu facing Jia must guard against blessing turned into calamity. Ji seated in Yi’s country suffers injury to the source of Lu. Geng encountering Bing in battle stands in peril of collapse. Xin harmed by Ding is injured by control and cutting. Ren meeting Wu Earth is blocked and difficult to pass. Gui coming to Ji’s country runs to and fro without security. Stem-Lu in Birth and flourishing can provide support; Blade arriving is especially welcome, for it can combine and restrain of itself.”

It is also said: when the Five Phases encounter Indirect Officer in the monthly branch, allow only one occurrence in the earthly branches; more is unsuitable. Four Pillars with pure Killings, provided there is restraint, assuredly hold the honor of the first rank. Even one Direct Officer creates mixture and instead makes low station. If Killings flourishes in the Four Pillars and the luck courses consistently strengthen the body, office is pure and distinguished. Another account says: “Body and Killings both flourishing, without restraint, followed by flourishing Killings in luck: distinction will not last.” Another: “Seven Killings fully displayed in the pillars, the body weak: extreme poverty and no longevity.”[^46]

### Further Discussion of Removing and Retaining Officer and Killings, and Their Transformation into One Another
#### 官煞去㽞並官鬼互變雜論

The *Chapter on Preferences and Aversions* says: “Among mixed Officer and Killings, some arrangements remove Officer and retain Killings; others remove Killings and retain Officer.” This means that Officer Star and Seven Killings cross within the pillars: Officer in the month and Killings in the hour, Killings in the month and Officer in the hour, or repeated appearances throughout the Four Pillars. If something removes Officer and retains Killings, judge Indirect Officer. If something removes Killings and retains Officer, judge Direct Officer. In deciding what is removed and retained, examine which is heavy and which light. An influence exposed in the stems is easy to remove; one concealed in the monthly branch is difficult. Hurting Officer and Food God—the influences that remove Officer or Killings—must be numerous and powerful before removal can be accomplished.

For the five yang days, Food God can remove Killings and also retain Officer. Hurting Officer can remove Officer but cannot retain Killings: Yang Blade must combine before “removing Officer and retaining Killings” is achieved. Take a Jia day, which has Xin as Officer and Geng as Killings. If Officer is heavy and Killings light, one Bing Food God controls and removes Geng while combining with Xin. This is removing Killings and retaining Officer, a harmonious arrangement that makes distinction. If Killings is heavy and Officer light, Ding Hurting Officer controls and removes Xin, while Yi Wood Yang Blade combines with Geng. This is removing Officer and retaining Killings, likewise harmonious and noble.[^63]

For the five yin days, Food God can remove Killings but cannot itself retain Officer: the Day Master retains it. Hurting Officer can both remove Officer and retain Killings. Take a Yi day, which has Geng as Officer and Xin as Killings. If Officer is heavy and Killings light, one Ding Food God removes Xin Killings, and Yi combines with Geng. If Killings is heavy and Officer light, Bing Hurting Officer removes Geng and then combines with Xin. Both make the appropriate removal and retention, with harmonious distinction. The *Rhapsody on the Original Principle* says: “Removing Killings and retaining Officer should be judged noble; removing Officer and retaining Killings governs commanding authority.” It also says: “Officer Star and Seven Killings cross, yet combination with Killings brings distinction.” This is the meaning.

Combining with Killings has two senses: combining it away and combining it in. Combining it in removes Officer and retains Killings; combining it away removes Killings and retains Officer. A Jia person exposes both Xin Direct Officer and Geng Seven Killings. Yi in the pillars combines with Geng Killings, while Ding controls Xin Officer: this removes Officer and retains Killings. A Ji person exposes Jia Direct Officer and Yi Seven Killings. Geng in the pillars controls Jia Officer and comes to combine with Yi Killings: this too removes Officer and retains Killings. In the first case, Yang Blade combines with Killings; in the second, Hurting Officer does so.[^64]

Ren in the pillars controls Bing and combines with Ding: Killings is removed and Officer retained.

The rhapsody says: “Combining Officer Star is not noble; combining Seven Killings is not ominous.” Here combining Officer means that an uncommitted influence in the pillars combines Officer away, so distinction is lost. Combining Killings means that an uncommitted influence combines with Seven Killings. Thus combination with Officer forgets nobility, combination with Killings forgets low station. If the Day Master’s own stem or branch combines with Officer or Killings, however, combining Officer makes distinction and combining Killings makes low station. The book says: “Displayed Killings combined away: the Five Phases harmonize like a spring breeze. Hidden Killings combined in: punishment and injury in the Four Pillars harm oneself.” Without distinguishing the Day Master from uncommitted influences, how could one distinguish combining in from combining away?[^65]

A combination between day and year cannot be discarded, for day and year are essential. The remaining positions are treated as uncommitted influences. The classic says: “Officer and Killings equally balanced: preserve what is welcome and remove what is disliked.” When Direct Officer and Seven Killings are evenly matched, an influence receiving generation, support, gathering, and harmony concentrates its force and should be preserved. If their weight is equal, employ the one receiving generation and support and approaching its season; abandon the one out of season and unsupported. If removal and retention remain unclear, this is mixture. Jia born in the first portion of the seventh month has Killings holding command; even Bing Fire cannot remove it.[^66]

## 4. On Direct Wealth
### 論正財

<!-- source: L190-L196 -->
〈Favored: a strong body; Seal-Ribbon; Food God. When Wealth appears, examine Officer; use Food as the leading influence. Avoided: a weak body; Companions; Yang Blade; Void and Extinction; clash and combination.〉

〈Its names are **Wealth Star**, **Heavenly-Horse Star**, **Officer-Urging Star**, and **Spirit of High Aspiration**.〉

**Direct Wealth** is exemplified by Jia meeting Ji or Yi meeting Wu. It receives my control and represents my wife. It is like marrying a woman who brings wealth with her: only if my vitality is sound and strong can I enjoy it. If I am weak and cannot rise, however abundant the wife’s property, I can only look at it and never use it. Wealth therefore needs to obtain its season and ride flourishing qi, without Direct and Indirect becoming confused, and without excessive repetition. With one’s own Day Master strong, blessing can unfold. Much Wealth with a weak body and no Seal to assist it, or little Wealth with a strong body and Companions and Rob Wealth in the pillars, are both excess or deficiency and cannot make blessing.[^74]

The classic says: “Repeated injury to the wife: Wealth slight, body flourishing.” The *Chapter on Continuing Goodness* also says: “Much Wealth and a weak body make a poor man in a rich house.” Luoluzi says: “When the Heavenly Origin is gravely weak, auspicious Officer is insufficient to confer honor. Even Deng Tong casting money remains unwealthy and dies of hunger.” This is the meaning. If the monthly command supplies a Wealth configuration but the body declines, Seal’s support warrants a judgment of wealth. If Seal is seen first, however, subsequent Wealth is feared. *Walking Alone* says: “Wealth first, Seal afterward, instead completes the blessing; Seal first, Wealth afterward, instead completes the disgrace.”[^75]

Wealth in use should not appear openly. If Companions and Rob Wealth appear in the pillars, however, it should emerge so that everyone can see it and cannot seize it. The rhapsody says: “Wealth should be hidden: hidden, it is substantial; exposed, it drifts and scatters.” Every Wealth configuration welcomes Officer Star appearing openly without other injury. With Food generating it, Seal assisting, and the Day Master vigorous, wealth and distinction are complete. Killings appearing in stems or branches can also be put to use: this is “When Wealth appears, examine Killings.” Owl seizing Food is greatly feared, for then generation fails; Blade robbing Wealth prevents enjoyment; the Storehouse meeting Void prevents accumulation.[^76]

For Jia born in Wu month, Ren injures Bing, Mao breaks, and Yi robs. For Yi born in Si month, Gui injures Ding, Hai breaks, and Jia robs. For Ren born in Xu month within the Jiazi round, Xu falls into Void. Infer the other cases by analogy.[^77]

## 5. On Indirect Wealth
### 論偏財

<!-- source: L218-L220 -->
What is **Indirect Wealth**? It is exemplified by Jia meeting Wu or Yi meeting Ji. It is not wealth brought by the wife, but the wealth of the multitude. Brothers and sisters dividing and seizing it must be avoided. Without Officer Star in the pillars, a hundred calamities arise. The classic says: “Indirect Wealth welcomes emergence and does not fear concealment. It fears only division and robbery, and falling into Void. If either occurs, office cannot be established and wealth cannot remain.” Weak Wealth must await a flourishing course before it prospers; abundant Wealth finds no direction unfavorable—unless the body is too powerless to bear it.[^91]

The Indirect-Wealth configuration makes a generous person, not excessively attached to money, affectionate toward others yet often deceptive. If it obtains its proper ground, not only is Wealth plentiful; Officer can also flourish, for abundant Wealth generates Officer of itself. A flourishing and assisting luck course brings both blessing and emolument. The moment Officer’s territory is reached, blessing can develop. Officer Star originally present makes a good fate; if many brothers appear, however, even entry into Officer’s country yields little blessing.

Indirect Wealth in the monthly command is weightiest and should not be repeated often in the pillars. Indirect Wealth in the year, flourishing in the monthly command and communicating with the pillars’ qi, indicates a rich inheritance from paternal uncles and forebears, or from the maternal grandfather’s estate and nurturing favor. The Day Master must prosper, Wealth Star must be in Birth and flourishing, and the course must approach flourishing Wealth before blessing unfolds. Punishment, clash, breaking, harm, Companions and Rob Wealth dividing it, excessive decline of Wealth, extreme weakness of the Day Master, or much Wealth generating Killings all make a fate that dissipates the ancestral estate through toil.
Wealth in the monthly command generally indicates riches and distinction in youth. If the birth hour lacks proper ground or brings robbery and defeat, and luck further enters ominous territory, the ancestral fortune is exhausted in old age and poverty follows: first wealth, then poverty. If year and month originally lack Wealth but day and hour carry it, with no robbery, defeat, clash, or control, the person establishes his own household and prospers greatly in middle or later life. If the pillars contain much Wealth but the body is weak, and youth passes through rest and defeat, troubles crowd together and nothing answers one’s wishes. Then, in middle or later years, sudden entry into the parents’ territory, or a triple combination capable of assisting oneself, brings vigorous success. If youth rides flourishing qi but old age departs from the configuration, one not only remains distressed on poverty’s road: disputes swarm like bees. Wealth can benefit oneself, but it can also invite slander.[^92]

If the Four Pillars generate one another, carry another noble configuration, avoid Void, travel flourishing luck, and form a triple-combination Wealth Star, they make a noble fate. The depth of blessing and emolument is judged by the configuration’s strength. Another account says: “Two Wealth positions in a fate need not harm a weak body. If Direct Wealth was originally employed, bodily strength develops wealth; if Indirect Wealth was originally employed, bodily strength releases wealth.” Another says: “Direct and Indirect Wealth have largely the same preferences and aversions, differing slightly in whether Officer Star is welcome. Direct Wealth is not so good as Indirect Wealth: Indirect Wealth substantial and weighty gives thick blessing.”[^93]

Most feared are Robbery-Defeat and Companions, weightiest in the year and somewhat lighter in the month. This influence is also called **Lonely Star**, and **Driving Away Horse**. It indicates harm to wife and children, loss of wealth, and poverty. One must also guard against covertly hostile petty people and injury from one’s own kind. If the natal pillars offend this prohibition, a flourishing-Wealth course can nevertheless unfold blessing. A further Companion or Rob-Wealth course may bring decline and death, or official trouble and ruin.

The *Rhapsody on Penetrating Clarity* says: “Indirect Wealth upon the month, without robbery or defeat: wealth foremost under Heaven.” The *Rhapsody on Examining the Heart* says: “Indirect Wealth displayed values money lightly and righteousness highly, loves people’s company and attention, enjoys discussing right and wrong, delights in wine, and craves flowers.” The *Rhapsody on Stirring the Spirit* says: “Indirect Wealth and a flourishing body: one who seeks gain in trade.” *Profound Meaning* says: “Indirect Wealth can add to the allotted count and lengthen life.” The *Thousand-Li Horse* says: “Indirect Wealth appearing: less love for the principal wife, more for the concubine.”[^94]

## 6. Direct and Indirect Wealth Considered Together
### 偏正財合論

<!-- source: L238-L244 -->
The *Detailed Records* has **Generated-and-Completed Wealth**: Jia and Yi see Wu and Ji Earth as Wealth, seated at Birth, flourishing, and Storehouse upon Shen, Zi, and Chen. Wushen, Wuchen, and Wuzi form the completed stem-and-branch arrangement. If the fate enters a noble configuration, it must add great wealth to distinction, and often administers money and grain. Even without a noble configuration or a blessing spirit’s assistance, it makes a wealthy commoner. Its own Birth and flourishing—as Jia meeting Wuwu or Jihai—also indicate wealth. Infer the other cases from these.[^101]

There is **Wealth through Generation and Combination**: Jia meeting Wu–Gui; Ji meeting Gui–Wu; Geng meeting Jia–Ji, and the like. It indicates the establishment of wealth and distinction. There is **Mother-and-Child Wealth**: a Wood fate meets a Fire month and Earth day or hour. This brings many joyful occasions throughout life.

There is **Kindred-Wealth Sha**: Yin, Wu, or Xu people meet Yi–Geng; Si, You, or Chou meet Ding–Ren; Shen, Zi, or Chen meet Wu–Gui; Hai, Mao, or Wei meet Jia–Ji. It is also called **Hidden-and-Subtle Sha** and indicates reputation and profit advancing together. There is **Wealth Meeting Sha**: Yin, Wu, or Xu meet Xinchou; Si, You, or Chou meet Yiwei; Shen, Zi, or Chen meet Bingxu; Hai, Mao, or Wei meet Wuchen. This spirit gathers Wife and Wealth. Meeting it indicates abundance, a beautiful wife, and unexpected wealth; yet it also harms the wife, and another person may threaten life with poison.[^102]

There is **Wealth of Name and Position**, where a Storehouse appears within Food God. A Wuzi Fire person meets Gengxu: Wu takes Geng as Food, Fire’s Storehouse is at Xu, and Fire controls Metal as Wealth. Meeting this gives the receipt of emolument throughout life.

There is **Wealth at Birth**: Jia uses Wu and Ji as Wealth and sees Wushen; Gui uses Bing and Ding as Wealth and sees Bingyin. This kind often acquires wealth from outside. Another account says: practitioners commonly take Jia meeting Wu or Yi meeting Ji as Wealth, but do not know that Jia–Ji meeting Bing–Xin, or Bing–Xin meeting Wu–Gui, makes **True Wealth**. Birth in a position possessing qi, flourishing, and assistance indicates great riches.[^103]

The original stem of a *nayin* can also encounter the True Five Phases directly. A Yihai person sees Geng in month, day, or hour. Yihai has Fire *nayin*, and Yi–Geng is True Metal: the stem itself supplies Wealth. This is **Heavenly Wealth**, indicating affluence and ease. If *nayin* instead controls the phase at the stem—as Dingmao Fire controlling Yi–Geng Metal—it is called **Ghost Wealth**. It indicates acquiring worldly wealth throughout life, or rising as a powerful, crafty clerk. Wuyin or Wushen obtaining Bing–Xin, and Yiyou or Yimao obtaining Wu–Gui, follow this rule. Chen, Xu, Chou, or Wei accompanying it indicates a practitioner of the arts who acquires great worldly wealth.[^104]

Earth’s Tomb is Chen, making Chen the Storehouse of Lu’s Wealth. Zi is Fate and belongs to Water; Water controls Fire, so Fire is Fate’s Wealth. Fire’s Tomb is Xu, making Xu the Storehouse of Fate’s Wealth. Metal is Body; Metal controls Wood, so Wood is Body’s Wealth. Wood’s Tomb is Wei, making Wei the Storehouse of Body’s Wealth. This indicates abundant property, hardship in youth, and gradual fulfillment with age.

There is **The Year Controlling the Monthly Branch**: Jia or Yi people in Chen, Xu, Chou, or Wei; Bing or Ding in Shen or You, and the like. Meeting it indicates exalted office and distinguished seals and ribbons. But a declining body cannot control Wealth: for example, Renyin Metal meeting Wuchen Wood, or Dingmao Fire meeting Guiyou Metal. Unable to exercise control, it instead loses qi. Infer the remaining cases in this way.[^105]
## 7. On Seal-Ribbon
### 論印綬

<!-- source: L260-L266 -->
〈Favored: Food God; Heavenly and Monthly Virtue; Seven Killings. When Seal appears, examine Killings; use Officer as the leading influence. Avoided: punishment and clash; Hurting Officer; Death and Tomb. Seal at Chen or Xu fears Wood; Seal at Chou or Wei does not fear Wood.〉

〈Its names are **Direct Seal**, **Chief Star**, and **Star of Utmost Solitude**.〉

**Seal-Ribbon** is the name for the Five Phases’ generation of me: Jia or Yi in Hai or Zi month, Bing or Ding in Yin or Mao month, and the like. It is the source of my qi, the qi of life, my parents. It can protect my Officer Star from injury and control. It is like a person receiving something that assists and nourishes him, enjoying a blessing already prepared. Is this not excellent? This configuration indicates intelligence and wisdom, a kind and generous nature, benevolent speech that is slow and hesitant, a full and substantial appearance, and a good appetite. Throughout life there is little illness and no violent calamity; only attachment to money is a fault. In office the person commonly holds the principal appointment and receives imperial commands. Civil or military, he holds the official seal. Officer Star is welcome because Officer generates Seal. The classic says: “Seal depends upon Officer’s generation.” It also says: “Officer without Seal is not True Officer; Seal with Officer completes substantial blessing.”[^106]

Wealth Star is avoided because Wealth breaks Seal. The classic says: “The month generates the day stem, with no Heavenly Wealth: this is named Seal-Ribbon.” It also says: “Seal-Ribbon injured: whatever glory there is will not endure.” And: “Seal-Ribbon in the birth month avoids Wealth Star in year and hour. When the course enters Wealth’s country, withdraw oneself and relinquish the post.” Years and courses are judged alike. Undamaged Seal-Ribbon often receives parental shelter and resources already established, peacefully enjoying wealth and distinction. Comparing fates, those with abundant Seal should be placed highest. The month is most important, day and hour next. Though the year stem is weighty, Seal must return to Lu in month, day, or hour before it can be used. Seal exposed in the year but absent from month, day, and hour is ineffective.

Officer Star originally present in the Four Pillars is excellent. If Seal is slight and Officers or ghosts abundant, or another configuration is entered, one must not speak solely of Seal-Ribbon. Seal joined to Arching Lu, Exclusive Lu, Returning Lu, Rat Noble, Flanking Noble, or Hour Noble is especially exceptional. Yet it indicates few sons or none. Abundant Seal makes purity and solitude; the old collection says: “Much Seal cannot avoid a solitary purity.”[^107]

Seal-Ribbon generally welcomes Seven Killings, but Killings must not be excessive, lest it injure the body. If Seven Killings was originally absent, meeting it in the course brings advancement. If it was originally present, a Wealth course, Seal’s Death or Extinction, or its approach to Tomb ground is ominous. The classic says: “Killings can generate Seal, but Wealth’s country is feared: it breaks Seal and assists the ghost, surely bringing misfortune.” It also says: “Seal in the Tomb: early death is difficult to escape.” Every configuration favors a flourishing body; Seal-Ribbon alone favors bodily weakness. If the natal configuration carries Wealth injuring Seal, a Companion or Rob-Wealth course that strengthens the body can also bring blessing. Without that condition it is unsuitable. No Officer, Killings, or Wealth Spirit, followed by a body-strengthening course, makes an ordinary fate.[^108]

*The Source* discusses Seal-Ribbon as follows. A **Jia day** meeting Zi month has Direct Seal, and meeting Hai month has Indirect Seal. Heavenly and Monthly Virtue are especially welcome: in Zi, Heavenly Virtue is at Xun and Monthly Virtue at Ren; in Hai, Heavenly Virtue is at Yi and Monthly Virtue at Jia. You or Xin Direct Officer in the hour is excellent. If Shen or Geng Seven Killings appears instead, Companions and Rob Wealth should assist the body and combine with Killings to make distinction. Wu and Ji Wealth injuring Seal are feared; Bing and Ding Food and Hurting Officer generating Wealth to break Seal are avoided. **Yi** in Hai or Zi month has the same preferences and aversions as Jia.

A **Bing day** in Mao month has Direct Seal: Heavenly Virtue is at Kun, Monthly Virtue at Jia. In Yin month it has Indirect Seal: Heavenly Virtue is Ding, Monthly Virtue Bing. Zi or Gui Direct Officer is welcome. Ren or Hai Seven Killings requires Companions and Rob Wealth to assist the body and combine with Killings. Shen, Geng, You, and Xin Wealth injuring Seal are feared; Wu and Ji Food and Hurting Officer generating Wealth to break Seal are avoided. **Ding** in Yin or Mao month has the same preferences and aversions as Bing.

A **Wu day** in Wu [午] month has Direct Seal: Heavenly Virtue is at Hai, Monthly Virtue at Bing. In Si month it has Indirect Seal: Heavenly Virtue is at Xin, Monthly Virtue at Geng. Mao or Yi Direct Officer and Yin or Jia Seven Killings are welcome. Killings should meet Companions and Rob Wealth supporting the body and combining with it. Ren, Gui, and [Zi] Wealth breaking [Seal], and Geng and Xin Food and Hurting Officer generating Wealth to break Seal, are avoided. **Ji** born in Si or Wu month has the same preferences and aversions as Wu.[^109]

A **Geng day** born in Wu [午] month has Direct Seal: Heavenly Virtue is at Qian, Monthly Virtue at Bing. In Si month it has Indirect Seal: Heavenly Virtue is at Xin, Monthly Virtue at Geng. Wu or Ding Officer Star and Si or Bing Seven Killings are welcome. Killings should meet Companions and Rob Wealth supporting the body and combining with it. Flourishing Jia, Yi, Yin, or Mao Wealth breaking Seal, and Ren or Gui Hurting Officer and Food generating Wealth to break Seal, are avoided. **Xin** in Si or Wu month has the same preferences and aversions as Geng.[^110]

A **Ren day** born in You month has Direct Seal: Heavenly Virtue is at Yin, Monthly Virtue at Geng. In Shen month it has Indirect Seal: Heavenly Virtue is at Gui, Monthly Virtue at Ren. Ji or Wu [午] Direct Officer in the hour is welcome, or Si or Wu [戊] Seven Killings; in the latter case Companions and Rob Wealth should assist the body and combine with Killings. Flourishing Bing or Ding Wealth breaking Seal, and Jia or Yi Food and Hurting Officer generating Wealth to break Seal, are avoided. **Gui** in Shen or You month has the same preferences and aversions as Ren.[^111]
The classic says: “Official punishment does not assail Seal-Ribbon sharing a palace with Heavenly Virtue.” It also says: “A vegetarian diet and a compassionate heart: Seal-Ribbon welcomes Heavenly Virtue.” Chief Censor Meng Zhong’s **Yihai / Dinghai / Yichou / Bingxu** has Seal and Heavenly Virtue in one palace. In **Jiayin / Bingyin / Bingyin / Dingyou**, Heavenly Virtue is Ding, Monthly Virtue Bing, and Seal lies at Yin. In **Gengshen / Gengchen / Gengzi / Renwu**, Heavenly and Monthly Virtue are both Ren, while Seal lies at Chen.[^112]

This configuration above all needs Birth and flourishing and most avoids Death and Extinction. Jia and Yi meeting Hai and Zi have Seal. Metal completes benevolence and righteousness by generating and nourishing Seal’s source; Earth confuses the configuration. Northwestern Officer-and-Seal courses make blessing. If the hour draws them toward Mao, Chen, or Si, or luck travels there, Wealth and Seal return to Death and Extinction. A flowing year that again brings Wealth to control Seal must then lead to the Yellow Springs. When Seal-Ribbon fulfills its configuration, the major course most fears a change through combination: a branch triple combination may turn it into a Hurting-Officer, Wealth, or Killings configuration. This is especially inauspicious.[^113]

The rhapsody says: “Metal depends upon Earth for generation, but thick Earth buries Metal. Wood depends upon Water for nourishment, but abundant Water sets Wood adrift. Blazing Fire and scorched Earth cannot generate things.” 〈As a Ji day obtaining Wu [午].〉 “Rigid Metal cannot generate Water.” 〈As a Geng day obtaining Xu.〉 Flourishing Earth meeting flourishing Fire must indicate eye disorders, abscesses, and fever. Filthy Earth cannot generate Metal; parched Metal cannot generate Water; extinguished Water cannot generate Wood. Examine such encounters carefully: do not indiscriminately call them Seal-Ribbon.[^114]

Water generated by Metal in autumn is clear Water and white Metal, of admirable refinement. One Water with three Metals is called the image of **The Substance Complete**. Fire generated by Wood in spring is refined Wood and bright Fire, its radiance red and green. Metal injures its Wood and extinguishes Fire’s flame. Thus abundant Metal Seal and Wood Seal are both judged auspicious. But abundant Fire Seal scorches Fire and intensifies Earth’s dryness; abundant Water Seal floods and floats Wood; abundant Earth Seal buries Metal beneath its weight. None of these is auspicious.

If these influences offend, and the course again reaches the declining grounds of body and Seal and the flourishing country of Wealth, greed for Wealth must break Seal, stripping away office and rank. The classic says: “Seal-Ribbon and Wealth Star seen repeatedly: nothing proceeds smoothly.” It also says: “The month’s Seal pure, without Wealth Star: literary accomplishment wins a place on the yellow list.”
Another account says: “Body flourishing, Seal abundant: a Wealth course does no harm. Body weak but possessing Seal: what harm is there in a Killings course?” Another says: “Rooted Seal-Ribbon welcomes Wealth Star; rootless Seal-Ribbon avoids the Wealth luminary.” Officer Star is Seal’s root. With Officer and Wealth, Wealth generates Officer, Officer generates Seal, Seal generates the body, and the body controls Wealth: thus honor and distinction arise, and Wealth is not feared. It is also said: “Rooted Seal meeting Wealth develops; meeting Officer becomes distinguished; meeting combination grows obscure; meeting clash suffers calamity.”[^116]

Taken together, these accounts reveal Seal-Ribbon’s preferences and aversions.
### Ghost Transformed into Seal — 鬼化為印
<!-- source: L268-L270 -->
The classic says: “In Completion, ghost transforms into Seal-Ribbon: first under Heaven in the examinations.” Take **Yichou / Guiwei / Bingzi / Yiwei**. Bing rests at Zi, seated upon Officer. Bing is Fire’s spirit and Zi Water’s ghost; this is called **Achieving Completion**. Year, month, and hour contain one Chou and two Wei, all Ji Earth, which is Hurting Officer and a ghost capable of dissipating Seal. The pillars have two Yi and two Wei Wood Storehouses assembling a configuration. Luck travels backward into Seal-Ribbon; the ghost is transformed or controlled, hence great distinction.[^125]

### Yang Blade Transformed into Seal — 陽刃化印
<!-- source: L272-L274 -->
The classic says: “A Wu day in Wu [午] month should not be judged Blade; much Fire in hour and year instead makes Seal-Ribbon.” Wu takes Ji Earth within Wu as Blade. Ding Fire’s generation and support, together with the Fire in year and hour, transforms it into Seal-Ribbon; it is not judged as Blade. Water Wealth controlling Fire is greatly feared: it turns back into Day Blade and brings especially grave [calamity]. Day Blade means that one’s own seat encounters robbery. Killings restraining it makes **Combining Killings for Distinction**. Without Killings’ restraint, Wealth must provoke contention. It is like a gentleman encountering robbers: without valuables he can preserve his life; with valuables he must suffer their harm.[^126]

Take **Wuyin / Wuwu / Wuwu / Wuwu**. Wu is the Day Master, seated on Wu as Blade. Day and hour are both Wu Fire and should be judged ominous. Yet Jia Wood within the year’s Yin branch restrains Blade and generates Fire. Yin and Wu also assemble Fire’s configuration, transforming into Seal-Ribbon. There is no Ren–Gui Water configuration in the pillars to injure Seal. Numerous Wu Companions are welcome too: even when year and course meet Wealth, they divide and draw it off so it cannot break Seal. Hence the great distinction.

### Generating Seal Met in the Hour — 時逢生印
<!-- source: L276-L278 -->
For a Jia day with Zi hour, take Gui Water within Zi as Seal supporting the Day Master. The person is resourceful and full of plans, enjoying emolument peacefully. Xin Officer in year or month should generate Seal. A northwestern Officer-and-Seal course then makes a noble fate. But if the pillars have heavy Wu and Ji Earth, together with Wu [午] to clash and break, and luck runs southeast where Officer and Seal decline and become extinct, nothing is accomplished. The person is a public clerk or a tradesman in the markets.

### Womb and Embryo Meeting Seal-Ribbon — 胞胎逢印綬
<!-- source: L280-L282 -->
The classic says: “Womb and Embryo meeting Seal-Ribbon enjoy a thousand measures of emolument.” Gengyin, Xinmao, Bingshen, or Yiyou in day or hour, with the monthly command at Seal-Ribbon’s ground, indicate distinction. The classic says: “The hour and day form the Womb-and-Embryo configuration; the month communicates with Seal-Ribbon. Killings, Officer, and Seal courses assist: rank stands among the Three Dukes.”[^127]

### Abandoning Seal and Turning to Wealth — 棄印就財
<!-- source: L284-L286 -->
The classic says: “In abandoning Seal and turning to Wealth, distinguish Direct and Indirect.” Seal-Ribbon avoids Wealth: this principle is clear. Direct Seal occupying the monthly command must never meet Wealth. If it occupies year or hour, however, and the monthly command has Wealth, employ the Wealth configuration alone. Seal generating the body so it can match Wealth is welcome and makes blessing. Indirect Seal in the monthly command can meet Wealth in year or hour without harm. This is **Abandoning Seal and Turning to Wealth**: relinquish what is light and employ what is heavy. Ren born in Shen month or Bing in Yin month sits at Birth. Wealth obtained in year or hour finds the body flourishing and therefore welcomes Wealth territory. Such a configuration must indicate leaving the ancestral foundation and establishing an independent livelihood.[^128]
## 8. On Inverted Food
### 論倒食

<!-- source: L288-L290 -->
〈Also called **Devouring Sha** and **Retreating Spirit**.〉

**Inverted Food** means Indirect Seal; another name is Devouring Sha. Food God most fears meeting it. Jia, for example, generates Bing Fire as Food. Fire generates Earth, Jia’s Wealth; flourishing Wealth generates Metal, Jia’s Officer. Food God in Birth and flourishing therefore supplies Wealth and Officer in full. Jia’s meeting Ren is called Inverted Food because flourishing Ren controls Bing Fire. Bing removed, it cannot generate Earth: Jia has no Wealth. Ren combines with Ding to injure Jia’s Xin: Jia has no Officer. Ren controlling Bing also leaves Geng Killings secure, free to injure Jia Wood: calamity arises for Jia. This is why Inverted Food is avoided when Food is employed.[^129]

A fate carrying Inverted Food generally has slight blessing and short life. But there may be restraint or combination. A Jia day meeting Renchen or Renxu has Earth within Chen and Xu to restrain, and Ding to combine. A Yi day meeting Guiwei or Guichou has Ji within Chou and Wei to restrain Gui. A Bing day meeting Jiashen; Ding meeting Yisi or Yiyou; Wu meeting Bingzi, Bingshen, or Bingchen; Ji meeting Dinghai; Geng meeting Wuyin or Wuchen; Xin meeting Jimao or Jihai; Ren meeting Gengwu or Gengxu; Gui meeting Xinsi or Xinwei—these Indirect Seals cannot harm Food because restraint is present. With the body flourishing in the pillars and Wealth and Officer both generated, Indirect Seal may be taken as a blessing that assists the body. On yang days it can secretly combine with Hurting Officer and generate Wealth; on yin days it can secretly combine with Wealth Star. If there is no Food in the pillars, discuss it simply as Indirect Seal.[^130]

Another account says: Food encountering Owl is like an elder controlling me, leaving no freedom. Undertakings advance and retreat amid regret and indolence, begun but not completed. Sources of wealth repeatedly form and fail. The appearance is crooked, the person small, the courage timid, the heart insecure. Nothing succeeds; the six relations suffer injury. In childhood it harms the mother; in adulthood, wife and children. The rhapsody says: “Inverted Food is named Indirect Seal and called Owl Spirit. With a flourishing body and abundant Wealth, blessing is substantial; meeting punishment and Killings, life is short and the person poor. Wealth Star appearing: under stars and moon, one never stops. Killings Star arising: there is no appointed day to put down the burden and rest the shoulders. A weak body meeting Indirect Seal repeatedly must fear Yan Zi’s injury. Proper Food encountering Owl cannot avoid Han Xin’s calamity. Its first encounter brings a sluggish spirit; repeated offense, a crooked appearance.”[^131]

The *Rhapsody of Myriad Auspices* says: “Owl Spirit meeting Officer and Killings brings many successes and many failures. Indirect Seal encountering Wealth’s luminary turns disgrace into honor. A flourishing body makes distinction; a weak body, an ordinary fate. ## 9. On Mixed Qi

## 11. On Food God
### 論食神

<!-- source: L312-L316 -->
〈Favored: a flourishing body; travel through Wealth’s country. When Food appears, examine Wealth. Avoided: a weak body; Companions.〉

〈Its names are **Advancing Spirit**, **Rank Star**, and **Longevity Star**.〉[^164]

**Food God** is what the day stem generates, the third position when counting forward: Jia takes Bing as Food, Yi takes Ding, and so forth. Jia generating Bing originally drains its qi, but Bing generates Wu as Jia’s Indirect Wealth. Indirect Wealth is Heaven’s Lu, naturally available wealth. Without exhausting one’s own thought and effort, one enjoys blessing and emolument already prepared. Jia and Bing follow the way of father and son. If the child flourishes and generates Wealth and Lu to offer the parents, how could they not enjoy ease? Jia also takes Geng as Killings and Wu as Wealth. Its Bing Fire Food God can restrain Geng Killings from injuring Jia Wood and generate Wu Wealth for Jia’s use.

Whenever a fate encounters Wealth and Killings, a flourishing Food God restrains Killings so it dares not cause harm, and generates Wealth so it is abundant and inexhaustible. Hence the names Longevity Star and Rank Star are well founded. This configuration requires both Day Master and Food God to be in Birth and flourishing, without clash or breaking. It indicates substantial wealth, abundant food, a generous capacity for blessing, a large and full body, leisure and contentment, offspring, and long life. Wealth and Food in year and month give abundant ancestral resources; in day and hour they bless wife and sons. Mother and child both declining or extinguished are feared, for neither then achieves anything. The classic says: “Food God should feed at Birth and flourishing, not at Decline and Extinction.” It also says: “Food God in Birth and flourishing surpasses Wealth and Officer.”[^165]

Another account says: Food God greatly fears Indirect Seal, which is Inverted Food. It makes a person begin without finishing, crooked in appearance, small in stature, narrow in disposition, full of desires but accomplishing nothing. Suppose Jia sees Bing as Food, but Ren in the pillars acts as Jia Wood’s Indirect Seal. It restrains Bing Fire so Bing cannot generate Wu Earth or control Geng Metal. Jia Wood is then controlled and Wealth retreats: is this not distress? The *Rhapsody on the Original Principle* says: “Food God restraining Killings meets Owl: poverty or early death.” Yixing says: “The Five Phases in rest and exhaustion encounter exceptional rescue: calamity must be light. The Four Pillars’ waxing and waning attain balance: blessing and virtue grow weighty. But encountering Inverted Food surely makes wealth dissipate.”

Another account says: Food God on a yang day secretly combines with Officer Star; on a yin day it secretly combines with Direct Seal. Officer and Seal should not be visibly displayed: Food God pure and unmixed makes distinction with emolument and wealth with longevity. Only one Food God is suitable, not many, lest it steal the original qi. The classic says: “One Wood repeatedly meeting Fire positions is called the pattern of Scattered Qi.” Much Food God should travel Seal’s course; little Food should not, for that is Owl seizing Food. Food therefore welcomes flourishing Lu’s assistance. Established Lu in the monthly command is best, hourly Lu next. Noble Person appearing as well, and luck traveling Food God’s Birth and flourishing, greatly develop blessing and emolument. Bodily decline and flourishing Owl are avoided.

Although Wealth in the pillars is welcome, it should not be excessive. Excess is impure and makes no more than a rich commoner. Repeated Food God turns into Hurting Officer, giving few sons; even if sons exist, they may be impaired or obstinate. It must not enter the Tomb: this is Hurting Officer entering the Tomb, where life is difficult to prolong. Void is greatly feared. Officer and Killings also emerging make a court physician, spirit-medium, or practitioner of calculation among the nine vocations. Food God controlled and also meeting Void is not noble. A further Death-and-Extinction or Owl course brings calamity through eating or qi: stomach rebellion, food obstructed, shortage of clothing and food, hunger and cold.[^166]

Another account says: a Jia day taking Bing as Food is fine only when Ren, Gui, Hai, and Zi are absent. If Water qi is present, Bing itself receives restraint and bows under another’s power, unable to stand upright on its own. How can it generate things to nourish its father? Without such restraint, Birth, flourishing, and approach to Lu are still needed. If Bing is born in summer and luck runs southeast, Fire and Earth both flourish and Jia’s usable Wealth must be substantial. If born in spring’s three months, Jia flourishes and Bing Fire receives generation, yet Wu and Ji’s qi is slight. Only when luck reaches the south and both Fire and Earth flourish may blessing be promised.
Again, Geng takes Ren as Food. Luck traveling north through flourishing Water must bring abundant wealth; traveling east through flourishing Wood must bring swift blessing. Examine it closely. Ren as Geng’s Food is born at Shen, so Shen ground should be judged substantially fortunate. At You it suffers Sudden Defeat: Ren Water reaching You is no longer fine. Ren generates Jia Wood as Geng’s Wealth—wealth generated and distributed from oneself, not Wealth brought by marriage to the principal wife. Jia reaching You is Wood trapped in Metal’s country. Ren Water itself is defeated; how can Wood nourish its father? Judge this course ordinary.

In Xu luck, Ren or Jia in the stem gives only half good and half ill; Wu indicates calamity, Geng a slight blessing. Hai luck should be called greatly auspicious. In Zi luck, Gui Water is Hurting Officer: heavy injury drains the body’s qi. Geng also dies at Zi and Jia is defeated there, so misfortune and bodily calamity are indicated. Chou luck is Geng Metal’s Storehouse and flourishing Water’s country, with Jichou assisting Geng. Jia reaches Cap and Belt, the ground of coming to maturity. Judge these ten years prosperous. Yin luck is also auspicious; Mao luck has calamity. Infer the other cases accordingly.[^167]

Bingwu does not fear Jia Inverted Food: Jia combines with Ji and transforms into Earth, while Ji’s Lu is at Wu. ### Reversed Clash of Lu — 倒衝禄
<!-- source: L322-L324 -->
Among Hurting-Officer monthly configurations is **Lu and Horse by Reversed Clash**. Its preferences and aversions are the same as Flying into Heaven, except that the hour is not considered here. This configuration has only two days, Bingwu and Dingsi, in the wholly yang summer months. Bing takes Gui Water as Officer: several strong Wu 午 in the pillars must clash out Gui Water from Zi, and Bing thereby obtains Officer Star. Ding takes Ren Water as Officer: several strong Si must clash out Ren Water from Hai, and Ding thereby obtains Officer Star. Chou or Yin, or Shen, Chen, Mao, or Wei, is further required to combine with and hold the Lu and Horse called forth. One such character is excellent; more is unsuitable. Bingwu fears Wei, and Dingsi fears Shen, Chen, and similar binding characters. Bound in this way, Si or Wu becomes intent upon combination and cannot clash out the Lu within Zi or Hai. Hai, Ren, Zi, or Gui in the pillars visibly exposes Killings or Officer and diminishes the configuration. The same applies to years and courses.[^189]

Another account says: for Bingwu and Dingsi, do not insist upon a combination with Lu; only entanglement is unwelcome. Year and month clashing together is best. If the relevant branch occurs only in the [day] and not in the month, it cannot clash out Lu and obtain distinction. If absent beneath the day but present in month and hour, it may still be employed. Bingwu, however, is Blade: even with distinction, it ends ominously. A combination binding Blade makes the fate ominous. Yet when the monthly command is Hai or Zi and Officer–Killings forms a configuration, or Officer–Killings emerges with vigorous, flourishing qi, combining with Killings instead makes a greatly noble configuration. One must not judge solely by Day Blade, nor cling entirely to the prohibition on filling the absent position. A Dingsi day may meet Xinhai hour; Jihai in the pillars need not spoil the configuration. Ding born in the fourth month has flourishing Si and powerless Hai; the third month may also qualify. Water’s country is welcome in the course. Fire encountered brings blessing, but only Si Fire is suitable, not other Fire.[^190]

Another account gives six days: Bingwu, Bingyin, Bingxu, Dingsi, Dingwei, and Dingmao. Yang days make Reversed Clash, yin days Direct Clash. A Bing day has only Wu 午 itself, but may employ the complete Yin–Wu–Xu triple combination, or three Bing characters. Generation and assistance by Seal are welcome; mixed Killings is avoided.

An old song says:

On examining these poems, they speak only of Bingwu and Dingsi, not the other four days. Thus these two days are the proper basis of Reversed Clash.[^191]

### Heaven’s Kitchen: Food and Lu — 天衝食禄〔天廚食祿〕
<!-- source: L326-L328 -->
Jia takes Bing as Food, and Bing’s Lu is at Si. Finding Lu at Food God’s own position is called **Heaven’s Kitchen Lu**. The stem and branch must both appear: Bing without Si, or Si without Bing, does not qualify. It may occur in year, month, or hour and is not confined to Wu 戊 days; in this it differs from Combining Lu. When the Five Phases of a fate possess this Kitchen, the monthly command is pure, and Food proceeds harmoniously through the Four Pillars, blessing, wisdom, and ease follow. Meeting Wealth’s Storehouse as well indicates enjoyment of parental property and abundant emolument in office.[^192]

### Blessing-Star Noble Person — 福星貴人
<!-- source: L330-L332 -->
A Jia person meets Bingyin or Bingzi; a Yi person meets Dinghai or Dingchou. Derivation within the appropriate round gives the true Food God and indicates enjoyment of what comes naturally. Whoever encounters it, though not noble, is at least wealthy. Infer the others accordingly. The earlier song beginning “Jia and Bing invite one another into the Tiger’s country” proceeds from the year; hence Bingyin and Bingzi. It does not work as a derivation from the day.[^193]

### Food God Sharing the Nest — 食神同窠
<!-- source: L334-L336 -->
Jia takes Bing as Food: a Jiazi person meeting Bingzi is an example. The thirteenth position returns to the same place and belongs to one’s own household. Whoever obtains this, though not noble, is at least wealthy. The month is less effective than the day, the day less than the hour. Reciprocal exchange, Birth and flourishing, and Lu and Noble Person together make great distinction. Examples are Han, Duke of Wei: **Wushen / Gengshen / Gengchen / Gengchen**; Qin Hui of the Song: **Gengwu / Jichou / Yimao / Renwu**; and Wang Chonggu, Junior Guardian of the Ming: **Yihai / Xinsi / Wushen / Gengshen**. Inverted Food in the same household—a Jiazi year meeting Renzi hour, or Gengzi year meeting Wuzi hour—can also make distinction, but harms the children.[^194]

### Food God Carrying Combination — 食神帶合
<!-- source: L338-L340 -->
A Jia person sees Bing with Xin to combine with it; a Ji person sees Xin with Bing to combine with it. Likewise, Yi sees Ding and Ren; Geng sees Ren and Ding; Bing sees Wu and Gui; Xin sees Gui and Wu. These indicate an officer possessing authority and the seal of command.

### Exclusive Food Combining Lu — 専食合禄
<!-- source: L342-L344 -->
The *Chapter on Preferences and Aversions* says: “A Wu day meeting Gengshen hour is called the place where Food God’s stem flourishes. Jia, Bing, Mao, or Yin offended in year or month makes this an encounter that does not truly encounter.” Wu takes Geng as Food God, and Geng’s Lu is at Shen: Food God is vigorous and flourishing. Wu takes Yi as Officer, and Geng can combine with the absent Yi Wood within Mao to obtain noble qi. The two characters Gengshen combine with the two characters Yimao. There must be no Jia Wood injuring Wu Earth, no Mao filling the absent position, no Yin clashing with the framework, and no Bing injuring Geng. Only then can Gengshen effect the indirect combination. Jia, Bing, Mao, and Yin in year or month damage the noble qi and are therefore all unwelcome.[^195]

Autumn or winter birth and a flourishing day are favored, along with Wealth Star and Seal-Ribbon. Punishment, clash, breaking, and harm are feared, as in the Food configuration. Purity makes nobility; filling-in cuts its worth in half. Wuwu and Wuyin days are difficult to treat as this configuration. If the monthly command supplies Wealth or Officer, judge it through Wealth or Officer. The classic says: “With tablet at the sash and ceremonial belt hanging, he serves the sagely king: Exclusive Food Combining Lu.” The *Bright Mirror* says: “For Combining Lu, a Wu day and Shen hour fear Yin and Mao, which lose the noble qi; meeting You or Wei gives no slight blessing and emolument. Filled in, office is obscure; pure, rank reaches duke or minister. It delights in Wealth Star and Seal-Ribbon, but fears punishment, clash, Seven Killings, and Officer Star.”[^196]

An old song says:

> Gengshen’s combining influence meets a strong Wu day;  
> Food God’s flourishing stem gives extraordinary distinction.  
> Yi Officer Star delights Wu’s Heavenly Origin;  
> Six Harms, punishment, and clash must bring injury.  
> Yin and Mao defeat it; Jia and Bing destroy it.  
> Without clash or breaking, one sits in the court.  
> A course through Bing or Wu brings calamity:  
> Loss of children and injury to the wife are hard to bear.

A poem says:

> A Wu day meets Gengshen in the hour;  
> Officer and Seal must be absent; autumn and winter are welcome.  
> Jia, Bing, Mao, and Yin—these four characters—  
> Are feared together in the four positions, years, and courses.[^196]

On examination, this configuration is simply Heaven’s Kitchen Food God; there is no need to regard it as Combining Lu. Minister Wang Chonggu, whose chart was recorded above, has no Jia, Bing, Yin, or Mao. Minister Gao Yao has **Jiaxu / Gengwu / Wuchen / Gengshen**: although Jia appears in the year, the month’s Geng restrains it, and Yin, Mao, and Bing are absent. Both charts are pure. Comparing them, Gao has the Three Wonders Jia–Wu–Geng, while [Wang has] Lu and Horse Sharing a Home, together with Heaven’s Pass and Earth’s Axis, Heaven and Earth clear and peaceful. Thus both reached office of the first rank, while Wang had achievements upon the frontier and enjoyed abundant ease.[^197]

Zhang Guangyuan, Chief Administrator, differs from Lord Wang only in having Guiwei as the month. This is a Food configuration: Established Lu and a flourishing body confer the greatest distinction, not autumn or winter birth alone. Lord Wang possesses these, and thus his achievement and renown reach much further. Yet Zhang is learned and an accomplished poet, also a fine gentleman.

### Red Luan and Heavenly Seal — 紅鸞天印
<!-- source: L346-L348 -->
Bing takes Wu as Food and obtains Wuxu; Xin takes Gui as Food and obtains Guichou; Ren obtains Jiachen, and Yi obtains Dingwei. Meeting these in day or hour indicates wealth and distinction.[^198]

### Ink Pool and Gushing Spring — 墨池涌泉
<!-- source: L350-L352 -->
Xinsi obtains Guisi and Guihai. Councillor Chen’s chart, **Xinsi / Renchen / Guisi / Guihai**, is an example. By the same principle, Bingyin welcomes Wuyin; Wushen welcomes Gengshen; Jisi welcomes Xinhai; Gengwu welcomes Renshen; Jiaxu welcomes Bingyin; and Renchen welcomes Jiashen. All belong to this configuration and indicate literary distinction.[^199]

### Exclusive Seal Combining Lu — 専印合禄
<!-- source: L354-L356 -->
This is a Gui day with Shen hour. The classic says:

> Secret combination fears punishment and assault;  
> An open encounter fears control and clash.  
> Straight toward the vermilion palace one hastens:  
> The palace of Exclusive Seal Combining Lu.

A Gui day meeting Gengshen hour has Geng and Wu in the same palace. Through an absent combination with Wu Earth within Si, Gui obtains Officer Star. Geng secretly combines with Yi Wood, Gui’s Rank Star; Bing and Wu share a palace and supply Gui’s Wealth. Thus the three exceptional influences are all complete. Wu 戊, Ji, Si, and Wu 午 are avoided, as are Bing controlling Geng and Yin clashing with Shen: all diminish the configuration. The same applies to years and courses. Courses strengthening the body and Seal are welcome; Metal-and-Water luck brings great advancement, while Fire’s country is avoided. A pure, unbroken configuration makes a court officer or chancellor. Deputy General Hu Maogong’s **Dingyou / Jiachen / Guiyou / Gengshen** is an example.[^200]

A song says:

> Exclusive Seal occurs upon a Gui day;  
> Gengshen secretly combines with the Snake’s palace.  
> Wealth and Officer lie hidden within:  
> Their Lu’s course arrives and sets them in motion.

> Gengshen in the hour, birth upon a Gui day:  
> This is Exclusive Seal Combining Officer Star.  
> No Officer or Killings appears, and Bing Fire is absent;  
> Seal, body, and Food courses reveal achievement and renown.

> Gui Water as day stem, Gengshen as hour:  
> Born in autumn or winter, a person rich and noble.  
> Yin coming to clash with noble qi is greatly feared;  
> Birth in spring or summer invites calamity and obstruction.

On examination, Exclusive Seal Combining Lu is not itself a subdivision of Food or Hurting Officer, but shares a configuration with Food; hence they are discussed together.

## 12. On Yang Blade
### 論陽刃
<!-- source: L358-L360 -->
〈Read this alongside the earlier discussion of Sheep-Blade Sha.〉

**Yang** is the yang of yin and yang; **Blade** is a knife’s cutting edge. It lies one position ahead of Lu: flourishing has exceeded its proper share, and therefore becomes dangerous. Consider a Jia person meeting Mao. Mao contains Yi Wood, Jia’s younger sibling, able to rob the elder’s Wealth, clash away Xin Officer within You, and combine in marriage with Geng. Geng is Jia’s Seven Killings. Robbing Wealth, clashing away Officer, and combining with Killings make this extremely ominous. Only the five yang stems, Jia, Bing, Wu, Geng, and Ren, have Blade; the five yin stems, Yi, Ding, Ji, Xin, and Gui, do not. Hence the name **Yang Blade**. Yet Hurting Officer brings them the same calamity. Thus Yi meeting Bing is also called Blade: Bing injures Yi’s Geng Officer and combines with Xin Killings, which controls Yi Wood. Yin Metal controlling yin Wood is extremely harmful; its ill effect is therefore the same as Yang Blade’s.[^201]

There are three kinds of Yang Blade. **Rob-Wealth Blade**, as Jia meeting Yi, harms the Wealth-and-Officer configuration. **Lu-Protecting Blade**, as Jia meeting Mao, greatly benefits the Returning Lu configuration. **Lu-Abandoning Blade**, as Yi meeting Bing, greatly benefits a configuration that removes Officer and retains Killings.

The *Chapter on Preferences and Aversions* says: “Rob Wealth and Yang Blade are especially to be avoided in the hour. When year and course bring them together, calamity arrives at once.” Yang Blade is singled out by its occurrence in the hour, where it is graver than in year, month, or day. Suppose someone born on a Jia day sees Yimao in the hour: this is True Blade. Once Yang Blade is present in the fate, injury to the wife and loss of wealth already carry calamity in embryo. Its reappearance in the flowing year and luck course is called **Arriving Together**. Jiyou brings a clash with the annual ruler; Hai, Wei, or Xu brings combination with it. Yang Blade is an ominous sha, and Tai Sui an ominous spirit. Tai Sui assisted and joined by auspicious spirits is fortunate; but Yang Blade’s ominous sha clashing or combining with it is **Gathering Misfortune and Assembling Sha**, and the calamity is hard to escape. The classic says: “Yang Blade clashes or combines with the annual ruler: calamity suddenly arrives.” This is its meaning.[^202]

Distinctions must nevertheless be examined carefully. For a shallow and meager natal foundation, encountering this indeed has that effect. But if the fate flourishes and the endowed qi is deep and substantial, or Heavenly Virtue, Monthly Virtue, and Pardon’s Writ provide rescue, the calamity is only passing and no great harm follows. Another account says: Blade originally in the pillars, with clash or combination, becomes greatly ominous when year and course bring clash or combination again. If the year clashes or combines but the course does not, or the course does but the year does not, judge the calamity as halved.[^203]

Another account says: “When the day stem has no qi, Yang Blade in the hour is not judged ominous.” The birth day’s Heavenly Origin may lie in Death, Extinction, Decline, Illness, or Sudden Defeat, without communication with the month’s qi and unable to bear Wealth and Officer. Yang Blade can then divide Wealth and transform Killings. It is like a weak elder brother burdened with much property: the younger brother shares the responsibility, making that property manageable and useful to him. Hence it is not judged ominous. A weak body seeing Wealth and Officer welcomes Yang Blade’s sharing of Wealth and combination with Killings. If Food or Hurting Officer appears and the weak body’s qi is drained, Blade’s support is also welcome. Seal-Ribbon present, however, means the day stem is no longer without qi. The earlier warning against Blade concerns a strong body able to bear Wealth and therefore unwilling to suffer robbery; the later welcome concerns a weak body unable to bear Wealth and therefore unafraid of its division. Each judgment has its own basis.

This configuration resembles Hurting Officer. A fate meeting it indicates large eyes, a yellowish beard, a hard disposition and a proud heart, without compassion or generosity, inclined to harsh exaction without concern for others. Chronic illness often accompanies it; greed and violence know no satisfaction, and advance and retreat are full of hesitation. Birth to a secondary wife, departure from the ancestral home, adoption into another family branch, harm to the father, and injury to the wife are indicated. With the Three Punishments or self-punishment and Kui-Gang complete, advancement comes upon the frontier. Further incompatible influences, or flourishing Wealth encountered, make it ominous. Yet when punishment and harm are complete, the influences all obtain their ground, and rescuing spirits appear, the distinction is beyond telling.[^204]

Another account says: a Yang-Blade configuration generally does not suit Wealth’s country, for a clash may rouse it. A Wu 戊 day has Blade at Wu 午 and avoids Zi Direct-Wealth luck; a Ren day has Blade at Zi and avoids Wu 午 Direct-Wealth luck; a Geng day has Blade at You and avoids Mao Direct-Wealth luck. Jia alone, with Blade at Mao, can travel Si, Wu 午, Chen, Xu, Chou, and Wei Wealth courses without harm, but avoids You. Bing, with Blade at Wu 午, can travel Shen, You, Geng, Xin, and Chou Wealth courses without harm, but avoids Zi. Thus the Wealth feared by Yang Blade is clashing Wealth: Zi Wealth clashes with Wu’s Blade at Wu 午; Wu 午 Wealth with Ren’s Blade at Zi; Mao Wealth with Geng’s Blade at You. That is why they are avoided. Jia’s Blade at Mao does not avoid Wu 戊, Ji, Si, and Wu 午 Wealth, yet avoids You Officer; Bing’s Blade at Wu 午 does not avoid Geng, Xin, Shen, and You Wealth, yet avoids Zi Officer. How can one say indiscriminately that Wealth is forbidden? If Wealth in the stems generates Officer and is precisely the useful influence, it is welcomed—how could it be avoided? The *Mirror of the Heart* says: “Repeated Yang Blade encountering Wealth brings wealth and distinction, abundant gold and silk.” This is its meaning.[^205]

One account says: Jia, Wu, and Geng meeting Blade and a clash frequently prove unfortunate, whereas Ren and Bing meeting Blade and a Zi–Wu clash often suffer no calamity. Bing seeing Zi and Ren seeing Wu both obtain Direct Officer, which is instead judged noble qi.

Another account says: Jia takes Ji as Wife-Wealth. Mao and Yi in the Four Pillars injure Ji Earth so that it cannot support Jia, indicating loss and injury to wife and children. When years and courses again bring flourishing Rob Wealth and Blade, the outcome is indeed difficult to avoid. But if Geng, Xin, You, or Shen occurs elsewhere, Geng can invite Yi as wife: a marriage is formed, and Geng no longer acts as Jia’s Seven Killings. Xin assists Jia toward distinction and restrains the harmful Yi influence, turning misfortune into good. The classic says: “Jia gives his younger sister Yi in marriage to Geng: ill becomes a fortunate sign.” Infer the other stems accordingly.

Another account says: the six Jia days meeting Yimao are ominous, but Xinmao is auspicious. Jiashen meeting Dingmao does not count as Blade: Geng within Shen combines with Yi Wood within Mao as Wealth. Yet Wealth exposed also makes it ominous: Ding Fire injures Officer and Yi Wood robs Wealth. When years and courses bring these together, calamity is inescapable. A Yiyou day with Gengchen hour does not count as Blade: Yi sits beneath Geng, and Xin Metal within You restrains Yi Wood within Chen. A Bingzi day with Jiawu hour does not count as Blade: Gui Water within Zi controls Ding Fire within Wu. A Dinghai day with Dingwei hour does not count as Blade: [Ren] Water within Hai combines with Ding. A Gengwu day with Yiyou hour does not count as Blade: Ding Fire within Wu restrains Xin. A Renwu day with Gengzi hour does not count as Blade: Ji Earth within Wu restrains Gui. A Xinsi day with Wuxu hour does not count as Blade: Bing Fire within Si combines with Xin. A Guisi day with Guichou hour does not count as Blade: [Wu] Earth within Si combines with Gui. These days should not encounter punishment, clash, breaking, or harm; without them, judge the fates good.[^206]

Another account says: Yang Blade is an ominous star in Heaven and an evil sha among people. It welcomes Indirect Officer and Seal-Ribbon and avoids Reversed Chant, Repeated Chant, Kui-Gang, and triple combinations. In general it resembles Seven Killings. Thus Yang Blade welcomes Seven Killings, and Seven Killings welcomes Yang Blade: the two ominous influences restrain each other, just as Direct Officer welcomes Direct Seal, good joining good to make blessing. The classic says: “Killings without Blade does not become illustrious; Blade without Killings does not command awe.” The ordinary person does not possess both Killings and Blade complete. A flourishing body and absence of Hurting Officer improve the arrangement further.

If the natal foundation contains Killings and Blade and years and courses bring them again, or contains Blade without Killings and years and courses reach Killings’ territory, great calamity results. Yet a fate with Blade and Seal but no Killings turns instead to abundant blessing when years and courses encounter Killings. If the pillars lack Blade and Killings and the fate accords with Wealth and Officer, their arrival in years and courses brings a year of obstruction: disputes over wealth, brothers separating households, and separation from wife or concubine. Blade luck arriving where Blade was originally absent may do no greater harm, yet still indicates injury to the wife. If Blade was originally present, years and courses must not repeat it or reach Hurting-Officer and Wealth ground. With Hurting Officer and Wealth Star originally present, their recurrence in years and courses makes calamity extremely grave, especially for a weak body.[^207]

Another account says: there are only three **Day Blades**—Wuwu, Bingwu, and Renzi—and their method is the same as Yang Blade’s. The classic says: “The red and yellow Horses lie alone; the black Rat keeps an empty room. In a man, harm to the wife; in a woman, harm to the husband.” These three days are meant. They dislike punishment, clash, breaking, harm, threefold assemblies, and Six Combination. Seven Killings must restrain them; further travel through Officer-and-Seal country then makes a good fate. A rhapsody says: “Day Blade greatly avoids clash and combination, and welcomes Officer and Killings restraining it. Combination and punishment are ominous; Seal encountered is auspicious. Killings without Blade has courage in action but no authority; Blade without Killings is turbid in action and wins no distinction. With no Killings, meeting Killings raises fear of calamity’s invasion; with Blade, meeting Blade requires guarding against peril. Blade at Birth and the body at Death make that year difficult to judge auspicious. Wealth flourishing and Officer injured need not make the year ominous.”[^208]

Another account says: a Wuwu day with much Fire in year and month is judged as Seal-Ribbon. A Renzi day with many Zi in month and hour is judged as Lu and Horse Flying into Heaven. A Bingwu day with many Wu 午 is likewise judged as Lu and Horse by Reversed Clash. Wealth and Officer are thereby brought into a configuration, so Day Blade is not greatly ominous. Examine the monthly framework: if its command forms a Wealth, Officer, Seal-Ribbon, or other configuration, or follows a transformation, judge through that configuration rather than fastening upon Yang Blade alone.

There are also three **Self Blades**—Guichou, Dingwei, and Jiwei—because Companions and Blade lie beneath them; and four **Flying Blades**—Bingzi, Dingchou, Wuzi, and Jichou—because their seated branches clash forth Yang Blade. Their preferences and aversions are broadly the same as Day Blade’s.[^209]

Yang Blade in the year and hour is gravest. In the year it indicates ruin of the ancestral foundation, failure to receive the parents’ estate, and kindness repaid with resentment throughout life. In the hour it harms wife and children and leaves no satisfactory outcome in old age. Repetition throughout the Four Pillars brings calamity and illness to the hands and feet. In the month it is somewhat lighter, and in the day lighter still. When the monthly and daily stems and branches carry much Wealth and the day stem is weak, Yang Blade in the hour does no harm. Seven Killings in the month, Yang Blade in the hour, and a Day Master possessing qi make great distinction. But Blade in the month with only a faint Officer Star in the hour, too weak to restrain it, remains ominous.

In general, Blade does the greatest damage to creative transformation. Yet if a good fate carries Blade and Rob Wealth and they are restrained according to the proper method, one must still allow it its development of blessing. Later, when years and courses bring them together, or reach punishment or combination, calamity remains. In a Blade configuration, blessing is blessing and calamity calamity: neither cancels the other.

Another account says: the five yin stems meeting the five yang stems make **Defeating Wealth**. Even when this does not injure the wife, it indicates dissipation of property, clandestine disputes, or intrusion by petty people. Officer and Killings in year or hour, whether seated or exposed, are auspicious. A weak body encountering Defeating Wealth also benefits when that influence is punished. A weak body traveling Officer, Killings, or Wealth luck still suffers harm. If Officer and Killings were originally absent and years and courses again reach Defeat’s ground, disputes over money and brothers dividing households follow; encountering this is “greatly auspicious.” Otherwise judge it as Blade. This configuration acts boldly, without benevolence or righteousness. An excessively strong body encountering it does not become noble: it is a Buddhist or Daoist fate.[^210]

Another account says: a man’s fate meeting Defeating Wealth or Rob Wealth together with Hurting Officer must harm wife and children; a woman’s harms her husband. A rhapsody says: “Defeating Wealth is a luminary of the Companion class, a spirit of seizure and robbery. Much Wealth and a weak body find it exceptional; weak Wealth and a flourishing body find it calamitous. Wealth encountering Rob Wealth, followed by Wealth luck, may establish a household. No Wealth encountering Rob Wealth must suffer loss even outside a Wealth year. Original Rob Wealth meeting a Rob-Wealth course again keeps one distressed upon poverty’s road. A flourishing body with Seal’s assistance must enjoy splendor and develop blessing.”[^211]

Another account says: yang meeting yang and yin meeting yin are Companions; yang meeting yin and yin meeting yang are Rob Wealth and Defeating Wealth. The two kinds bring the same sorts of misfortune. Repeated Companions in a fate weaken Horse and diminish the person: this is called **Wealth-Breaking Sha**. If the Day Master is vigorous while Companions sit weakly, I flourish and my brothers decline: I obtain the ancestral home, while they live elsewhere. If Companions sit in flourishing positions while I sit in Decline or Extinction, their assistance is welcome, yet the brothers prosper while I remain in difficulty. Wife-Wealth is meager and weak; repetition in Tai Sui can strip away office as well. Original Blade followed by Companions makes calamity heavier. Even where Companions were originally absent, meeting them in the major course indicates loss of wealth and injury to the wife.

Another account says: Companion, this sha, brings calamity when encountered in a rooted configuration possessing Wealth or Seal-Ribbon; in a rootless one possessing Hurting Officer or Seven Killings, it brings blessing. *Walking Alone* says: “Hurting Officer does not avoid Companions; Seven Killings needs to meet them. Rootless, one welcomes their help; flourishing, one dislikes their repetition.” It also says: “Jia and Yi in Yin and Mao months instead prosper with much Metal. Repeated Water is unsuitable; Fire and Earth provide clothing and grain.”[^212]

The *Rhapsody on Examining the Heart* says: “Rob Wealth and Yang Blade leave the ancestors to establish a home. Outwardly modest, harmonious, and honoring righteousness, inwardly cruel and uncomprehending: inclined to harsh exaction, without generosity.” The *Mirror of the Heart* says: “Repeated Yang Blade meeting Killings brings great distinction and success in the highest examinations.” The *Mysterious Mechanism* says: “Yang Blade especially welcomes Heavenly Officer, which levels disorder and calamity.” An instruction says: “Killings joining Blade holds military command.” Another: “Yang Blade sharing the qi of generation holds authority beyond the commander’s gate.” 〈The qi of generation means Seal-Ribbon.〉 The *Rhapsody on Body and Fate* says: “Yang Blade holding authority must make a frontier general.” The *Thousand-Li Horse* says: “Yang Blade and Indirect Officer under restraint receive military office.” It also says: “A man encountering Yang Blade finds it exceptional when his body is weak”; “Sheep Blade and Seven Killings enter office and win renown”; and “Sheep Blade entering Officer–Killings commands awe upon the frontier.”[^213]

The *Precious Mirror Rhapsody* says: “Repeated Yang Blade occupying a yang month achieves fame and profit.” The *Secret Instructions* say: “Repeated Yang Blade under restraint enjoys lifelong wealth and distinction and ends well”; “Yang Blade again meeting Seal-Ribbon has Lian Po’s hundredfold resourcefulness”; and “Blade in the branch, Officer in the stem, repeated in hour and month: office must become illustrious.” 〈As a Jia person meeting Xinmao and Xinmao.〉 The *Rhapsody on Penetrating Clarity* says: “Blade in month, day, and hour, together with Noble Person and Killings, gives wealth and distinction to the person.” The *Rhapsody on Waxing and Waning* says: “A little gain and great loss: perhaps this is Rob-Wealth ground.”

The *Jade Casket Rhapsody* says: “Fire, Metal, and Yang Blade: Lüzhu fell to her death from a high tower.” It also says: “Yang Blade repeated three or four times must bring illness, blindness, or deafness.” The *Three Carts* says: “Yang Blade holding Needle makes a thief with a tattooed face.” The *Rhapsody on Startling the Spirits* says: “A chart filled with Yang Blade surely means dismemberment.” The *Rhapsody on Penetrating Clarity* says: “Seal generating two Blades ends in punishment.” The *Chapter on Establishing the True* says: “Yang Blade meeting Seal-Ribbon may be wealthy, yet carries disability in the body.” The *Discourse on Creating Subtlety* says: “Yang Blade meeting the Five Ghosts must repeatedly incur penal servitude and exile.” In my observation, many who offend Sheep-Blade Sha are blind. ## 13. On Established Lu
### 論建禄
<!-- source: L362-L364 -->
〈Read this alongside the earlier discussion of Lu.〉

**Established Lu** means a Jia day in Yin month, a Yi day in Mao month, and the corresponding Arrival-at-Office positions of the Five Phases. Jia employs Metal as Officer, but Metal is extinct at Yin; it employs Earth as Wealth, but Earth is ill at Yin. With the body flourishing excessively, neither Wealth nor Officer is obtained. If no Wealth or Officer can be employed elsewhere and further robbery appears, Horse offers no support and Lu no nourishment: poverty and low station generally follow. Indirect Officer, Indirect Wealth, or Food God in the hour is especially suitable. Examine what emerges most abundantly in year and hour and take it for use. A little Wealth or Officer, however, provokes competition and seizure and is inauspicious.[^219]

A fate with Established Lu in the monthly command has difficulty obtaining the ancestral estate and generally fails to gather the wealth it encounters, but suffers little illness and enjoys long life. Companions appearing again in luck injure the wife, harm the father, and damage the children, or bring official disputes and loss of property, or contention over wife, children, and wealth. Yet if the Eight Characters already contain Wealth and Officer, visible or concealed, drawn into flourishing and obtaining their ground, assistance to Officer Star and a course reaching a place where it has qi also make distinction. Assistance to Wealth Star and a course reaching flourishing Wealth also make riches. Both Wealth and Officer flourishing make a fate of wealth and distinction. Wealth’s Storehouse in the hour, followed by a Wealth course, must bring great riches in old age. Wealth and Officer in the year with assistance must allow enjoyment of ancestral protection.

If Wealth and Officer were originally absent from the Four Pillars, even a course through their ground gives only empty blossoms. A fate without them, with years and courses again traveling through Companions, remains poor and obstructed throughout life. A rhapsody says: “Roots precede the shoots; fruit follows the flowers.” There must first be roots before shoots can grow, flowers before fruit can form. If Wealth and Officer were absent at birth, even their auspicious courses do not develop great blessing.

**Jia day, Yin month.** Many Yi, Mao, or Wei in the pillars indicate no ancestral wealth, injury to the wife, lifelong solitude and poverty, deceitful undertakings, and a grand outward manner.

**Yi day, Mao month.** Geng, Xin, Si, You, Chou, and Shen, together with Wu 戊, Ji, Si, Wu 午, Chen, and Xu, supply abundant Wealth and Officer and thus distinction. Ren, Gui, Shen, Zi, Chen, and Hai forming a Water-Seal configuration are also excellent; meeting them again in luck improves matters further. If Wealth, Officer, Seal, and Food are all absent, judge as above.

**Bing day, Si month.** Water and Metal forming configurations among the stems and branches of year and hour, with the course passing through flourishing Wealth and Officer, likewise indicate wealth and distinction.

**Ding day, Wu month.** Metal is defeated and Water extinct: Wealth and Officer both turn away. A forward course harms the wife; a backward course harms three wives. But Si, You, Chou, Geng, Xin, Ren, Gui, Hai, Shen, Zi, and Chen in the pillars, and a course reaching flourishing Wealth and Officer, also bring advancement. If Killings or Seal is employed, abundance makes distinction. Established Lu alone is judged as above.

**Wu 戊 day, Si month.** No Water in year, day, or hour indicates injury to the wife, no ancestral property, and many unworthy children. Much Officer in the pillars is auspicious; Indirect Officer indicates high distinction. Much Fire in year and month, or a Seal-Ribbon arrangement, is auspicious even without Wealth and Officer. Ren, Gui, Hai, Shen, Zi, and Chen making a Water configuration, concealed or visible in the pillars, indicate one or two children late in life. Jia, Yin, Yi, Mao, Hai, and Wei making a Wood configuration, with a course reaching flourishing Wealth and Officer, also bring advancement.

**Ji day, Wu month.** Ren Water is Wealth, but Water is confined in the fifth month: no ancestral estate, injury to the wife, and few children. Yin or Jia emerging in year or hour is Direct Officer, yet Jia dies in the fifth month, so office must be humble. Hai, Mao, Wei, and Yi are welcome; a flourishing body encountering Officer and Killings is excellent. Indirect Wealth is also fine.

**Geng day, Shen month.** Birth in the first ten days remains close to Wood’s residual qi, yet scarcely provides ancestral wealth. Although the solar-term qi approaches [Wood’s] Extinction, three or four tenths of Storehouse Wealth still supply blessing. By the Bingxu course, Wealth is exhausted. Much Wealth in year, day, and hour makes a good fate. Bing, Ding, Si, Wu, Yin, and Xu forming a Fire configuration supply Officer—Killings is transformed into Officer. Office is modest and not conspicuously eminent. Ren, Gui, Hai, and Zi controlling Officer are feared, for they prevent accomplishment.[^220]

**Xin day, You month.** There is no ancestral wealth. Much division and seizure in the pillars brings solitude and poverty without a wife, or injury to the wife and absence of wealth. Yet Wood and Fire in Birth and flourishing can make wealth and distinction. Without original Wealth or Officer, further travel to Birth ground makes the calamity of robbery particularly heavy. A Xinyou day is Exclusive Lu; with Wealth, Officer, Seal, or Food in addition, their reappearance in years and courses is especially good. A backward course south is auspicious; a forward course north accomplishes nothing. Xinmao or Xinwei sits upon Wealth and may be promised clothing and emolument. Xinsi has noble Officer, though its Lu is slight.

**Ren day, Hai month; Gui day, Zi month.** Neither obtains the ancestral estate. Much Fire and Earth in the pillars indicate an independent establishment and office. Much Water instead floods without accomplishment, harms the wife, and leaves poverty and meagerness.

This is obtaining distinction through Killings. Yet it must not be excessive: years and courses again meeting Killings’ ground indicate early death. One fate is **Bingxu / Dingyou / Xinyou / Yiwei**. The monthly command is Established Lu, and the day itself Exclusive Lu; life ended at forty-eight. Death came in the Renyin course and Renshen year. Year and course clashed and also assembled to injure Officer and Killings. Established Lu employs Wealth and Officer; injury to Bing Fire therefore cut life short.[^222]

The *Rhapsody on Clear Comprehension* says: “Established Lu, Seated Lu, or Dwelling at Lu, meeting Wealth, Officer, or Seal-Ribbon on its own, gives wealth, distinction, and long life.” Taking these accounts together, Ziping’s employment of Established Lu differs greatly from the ancients’ employment of Lu.

In my judgment, Yang Blade, Companions, Defeating Wealth, and Established Lu, though differently named, are influences of one household and one qi. In the earthly branches they are called Blade and Lu; in the heavenly stems, Companion and Rob Wealth. Their employment is broadly similar. For this reason, Established Lu follows Yang Blade. Formerly Established Lu was not a configuration in its own right; recently it too has been taken as one. The monthly branch offers no configuration to employ, but the heavenly stems may contain Wealth, Officer, and noble qi, so Established Lu is recognized. As for Companions and Rob Wealth, their meanings alone have been elucidated here.[^223]

## Translator’s Endnotes

[^01]: **Controlling text and witnesses.** **S** denotes the repository’s [Chinese juan five](../source/juan-05.md), exported from [revision 2082207](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=2082207); the file header records its SHA-256 checksum. **G604**, **G605**, and **G606** denote parallel extracts from *Sanming tonghui* in the *Gujin Tushu Jicheng*, Arts volumes [604, revision 1942567](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=1942567), [605, revision 1942568](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=1942568), and [606, revision 1942569](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=1942569). **U** denotes the shorter, unlabelled [Wikisource juan five, revision 2706483](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=2706483). These are selectively consulted transcriptions, not independently collated facsimiles. The G volumes use page transclusion: a cited volume revision identifies the containing page, not an immutable revision of every included leaf. The notes record the readings encountered in the consulted text. Agreement between online witnesses does not prove independent descent. S governs coverage and sequence. The separately transmitted Siku editorial preface remains untranslated; English section numbering is editorial.

[^02]: **Technical usage.** 身, “body,” ordinarily means the Day Master and its capacity to sustain the configuration, not physical strength alone. 用神 is the influence employed to make a configuration work; it is not invariably a single lifelong “lucky element.” 旺, “flourishing,” can mean seasonal strength rather than the specifically named stage Imperial Prosperity. 財, 官, 印, and 食 sometimes denote broad relational classes and sometimes their narrower configurations; the context decides. 本命, *nayin*, and the older “three fates” occasionally replace the day stem as the reference. These changes are retained, not silently recast in modern Day-Master terminology. The transmitted text often writes 巳 or 已 where the stem 己 is required, and sometimes the reverse. Unambiguous cases are rendered Ji or Si as the syntax requires; consequential uncertainties are noted individually.

[^03]: **Seal and ribbon.** 印綬, *yinshou*, is an official’s seal with its attaching ribbon, used as an image of parental support and received authority. The explanation pairs 印 with 蔭, “shelter,” and 綬 with 受, “receive,” through sound and association; it is not an account of the characters’ historical formation. “Seal-Ribbon” preserves the concrete image where that image matters; “Seal” elsewhere is its shorter technical name.

[^04]: **Officer and coffin: supported repair.** S reads 官者官也, literally “Officer means officer.” G604 has 官者棺也, “Officer means coffin,” which restores the pun *guan/guan* and agrees with the next sentences about service ending at the coffin. “[Coffin]” follows G604. In the preceding explanation, 食 concerns the generating parent’s qi being consumed by its offspring: “Jia takes Bing as Food” must not be read as Bing generating Jia.

[^05]: **Social hierarchy within the terminology.** The distinctions between principal and secondary wives, seal-holding chiefs and subordinate officials are the source’s historical analogies. Translating them does not endorse their hierarchy or turn a natal chart into evidence about someone’s character, marriage, or status. G604 contains an additional explanation of a wife bringing dowry and land; S lacks it. That expansion is not imported into the main translation.

[^06]: **Ren, Gui, and “dead timber.”** The contrast between dead timber preserved in still water and timber ruined by rain is the author’s explanatory analogy, not a claim verified here by material science. Nor is the preference for Ren over Gui a universal rule: later passages explicitly qualify how Direct and Indirect Seal function. 死水 is translated “still, lifeless water” rather than modern polluted “dead water.”

[^07]: **The quotation from the Changes.** S and G604 give 成言乎艮終言乎坤. The received [*Explaining the Trigrams*, chapter 5](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?title=易傳/說卦&oldid=2614055) has 致役乎坤 and 成言乎艮; its explanation assigns both ending and renewed beginning to Gen. The present quotation may combine or misremember formulations. It is retained rather than silently replaced by the received *Changes*. Gen and Kun are respectively the northeast and southwest Earth trigrams in the arrangement invoked here.

[^08]: **Birth and Death of the yin stems.** The paragraph deliberately assigns opposite directions of progression to paired yang and yin stems: for example, Jia is born at Hai and Yi at Wu. This is not the single forward Five-Phase cycle employed in every earlier passage. The text also follows Fire for Wu and Ji while recognizing Earth’s four proper branch seats. These are coexisting explanatory systems, not typographical errors to be harmonized.

[^09]: **Four headings, six configurations.** “Wealth, Officer, Seal, Food” are broad organizing categories. The sixfold discussion that follows differentiates Direct Officer, Indirect Officer, Wealth, Seal, Hurting Officer, and Food God; subdivisions and exceptional configurations multiply within them. The number should not be read as a claim that only six named patterns occur in the book. “Invitations from absent positions” renders 虛邀: an unoccupied position may be inferred by clash, combination, or flanking in the special configurations described later.

[^10]: **Pillars and life spans.** 年管十五 and 時管五十後 give the year’s first-fifteen-year and the hour’s post-fifty scopes. The text does not supply a complete age schedule for the intermediate pillars here, and none has been invented. Its argument is that the month gives the central seasonal basis, not that a year or hour Officer can never be used: later in this very section it explicitly permits taking Officer outside the monthly command.

[^11]: **The hour as final resting place.** 歸息 is the place in which a qi finally settles or finds its outcome. “Draws into” does not necessarily mean that a stem physically changes branch: the hour supplies the branch against which its stage and strength are assessed. The text’s own example is the contrast between Xin flourishing and Ding extinguished, or Xin declining and Ding flourishing. The principle is relational, not a rule that the hour overrides every other pillar without qualification.

[^12]: **Xin’s Blade at Xu.** This uses the earlier definition locating 羊刃 one branch ahead of Lu for yin as well as yang stems: Xin’s Lu at You gives its Blade at Xu. It is not the alternative rule in which yin stems have no Yang Blade. The assertion is left intact; compare the distinct Blade accounts in [juan three](juan-03.md).

[^13]: **Food, combination, and Officer.** For Jia, Bing is Food God and combines with Xin Officer; that is why the analysis passes from Food’s solidity to the strength of a “combining influence.” Food can support one arrangement and divert another. 虛 and 實 mean effective absence or presence, weakly supported or firmly established, according to context; they do not always mean that a written stem is literally missing or present.

[^14]: **Turning away from Lu and driving away Horse.** 背祿逐馬 is explained here through damage to Officer and competition for Wealth. A “body-strengthening course” can therefore be harmful where the body is already sufficiently strong and new Companions rob Wealth; the phrase does not cancel the preceding rule that an actually weak body needs help. S and G604 both transmit the categorical language and the maxim about flourishing Killings entering the Tomb. The translation retains these tensions rather than converting them into a uniformly qualified modern manual.

[^15]: **The three Officers.** In this older threefold reckoning Lu is the stem, Fate the branch, and Body the *nayin*. Thus in Guichou the stem Gui Water is controlled by Earth, the branch Chou Earth by Wood, and its Wood *nayin* by Metal. The initial expression “Wood fate” is an ordinary description of the natal *nayin*, not the technical second member of the subsequent triad. Without this distinction the example appears to contradict itself. S titles the cited work 三命鈴; G604 has 三命鈐. “Seal of the Three Fates” follows the latter title reading, not a supposed modern source.

[^16]: **Officer and Seal between several stems.** The examples test reciprocal relations, not only relations to the day stem. In Zhang Zihou’s example Yi does not appear as an exposed stem, but is available within Mao of the Jimao conception pillar. The source nonetheless introduces the argument as one about the four stems; that looseness is retained. The author’s closing “one-sided opinion” is important: the quotation is reported and then qualified, not simply endorsed.

[^17]: **Heavenly-Officer month list.** The list is copied as transmitted in S and G604. Its attached sentence 戊癸即相帶而行 is elliptical: “Wu and Gui accompany one another in this reckoning” is deliberately close. It should not be silently converted into a statement that all month calculations here follow one demonstrated formula. “Third branch ahead” in the following True-Officer example means Zi to Mao, counting three advances, not Zi itself as the first.

[^18]: **True Phases versus ordinary stem phases.** Jia–Ji combines into Earth, Yi–Geng into Metal, Bing–Xin into Water, Ding–Ren into Wood, and Wu–Gui into Fire in the transformed-phase scheme. Jiayin Water meeting Jia–Ji Earth is the explicit first example. The next sentence’s Yi–Geng belongs to a generalized alternative case, not a claim that Metal controls Jiayin Water; the underlying natal *nayin* is not restated. A “True Ghost” additionally injures the natal stem: Ding–Ren Wood controls the Earth *nayin* and the Ji stem of Jiyou/Jimao; Yi–Geng Metal controls the Wood *nayin* and, as a transformed phase, the true Wood attribution of Ren in the older scheme. The passage moves among these layers without spelling each out. Its shift of reference should not be erased by forcing every occurrence of 克 into ordinary stem relations.

[^19]: **Quoted source titles and ranks.** The names of cited treatises are translated as titles, not treated as statements by a single narrator. “Gold and purple,” “Jade Hall,” and “golden list” retain the courtly imagery of investiture, prestigious literary service, and examination success. These are not interchangeable modern job titles. In the two opening quotations S ends with 官, “Officer,” where G604 has 宮, “palace”; “palace” follows G604 because the phrases specify a location free from damage, not a second Officer.

[^20]: **The older Officer vocabulary within a later discussion.** 官刑命喜 is rendered as Officer’s controlling Fate being welcome. 刑 here participates in the broad language of subjection and injury and need not signify one of the specifically enumerated branch Punishments. “Tenfold” is the text’s rhetorical valuation, not a computed score. 不戎即吏 distinguishes military service from clerical service, not the complete absence of public employment.

[^21]: **Honor extending to the family.** 封妻蔭子 means rank granted to a wife and inherited privilege or appointment benefiting sons. “Cauldrons,” grain measures, banners, and five horses are images of ample emolument and high office. The final contrast 女命如平賤中却貴男命不遇靠貴而發 is compressed; the translation separates the female and male cases without inventing a missing condition. The source’s promised outcomes are historical divinatory claims.

[^22]: **Contradictory character portraits.** The kindly, generous Direct Officer and the miserly Direct Officer are both in the transmitted compilation. They are preserved as statements of different cited authorities. Their coexistence is not corrected by silently replacing one character trait with its opposite.

[^23]: **“Many Officers … transform into Wealth.”** S and G604 transmit 官多身旺化為財. The line is not changed to “transform into authority,” a tempting doctrinal smoothing. Here 財 may describe wealth as the outcome rather than a formal conversion of an Officer stem into the Wealth relation. The verse itself does not explain the mechanism; its wording is retained.

[^24]: **Tracing to Heavenly Officer.** 遁 is the calendrical operation of extending a stem–branch sequence, not physical concealment. Xinwei, Gengchen, and Guisi are the text’s examples; it does not provide a complete table or establish that every birth under a given stem uses the same natal ten-pair round. The final test also explicitly includes *nayin*. The passage is preserved as an older formula, not expanded into a software-ready algorithm with invented inputs.

[^25]: **Seated Officer and the two clash rules.** The first account expressly forbids clashes; the next relaxes that prohibition because Officer is “in the hand,” beneath the day. Both are retained. 官 here often includes the broad controlling class: the accompanying day lists contain both Direct and Indirect Officer. The “Eight Seats” and “Three Terraces” are images of high governmental authority; the translation does not pretend that each named pattern determines one exact historical office.

[^26]: **The youthful success example.** The age is transmitted as 十七八, “seventeen or eighteen,” not an exact calculated starting age. “The two Bing strengthen the body” translates the author’s judgment, not an independent recalculation of the chart. The claimed resistance of Zi to Wu’s clash depends here on the Shen–Zi configuration; later passages may judge superficially similar clashes differently.

[^27]: **Lu as office, not only stem-Lu.** Yi’s own stem-Lu is Mao, but westward movement here approaches its Metal Officer. Thus 向祿 is rendered “approaching Lu,” with Lu carrying its office/emolument sense, not misidentified as movement toward Mao. The sixth-month 中氣 is the central seasonal term of that month; it is not the midpoint of an arbitrarily chosen modern calendar month.

[^28]: **Different kinds of flourishing.** The source says Gui seated at Chou is 自旺 and says Xin Seal’s 臨官 lies at Shen. These should not be silently converted to a uniform yin-stem twelve-stage table: in the general forward Metal cycle Arrival at Office is Shen, while Xin’s individual stem-Lu is You. The text juxtaposes bodily vigor, branch rooting, ordinary phase cycles, and individual stem stages. This note identifies the discrepancy without claiming a fully proven harmonization of every phrase.

[^29]: **Compressed True-Officer/True-Horse formula.** S and G604 have 甲見辛巳乙戊庚丙辛癸. The translation parses three cases: Jia meets Xin and Ji, Yi meets Wu and Geng, Bing meets Xin and Gui. Reading 巳 as 己 gives Jia its Direct Wealth beside Xin Officer and parallels the following cases; reading 辛巳 as Xinsi would produce a structurally different first item. This is an interpretive normalization, not a newly attested character reading. “The third position” is left as transmitted: the short passage does not unambiguously define the counting frame.

[^30]: **Storehouse examples.** The list includes Gengwu for Bing/Ding and Yiwei for Geng/Xin. Their rationale is not reducible to “the Officer’s phase sits in its Tomb” in every case. The translation retains the list without replacing entries to make them conform to a single modern chart rule. The longer Dingchou example explicitly draws upon several different Storehouses.

[^31]: **“Noble” in the punishment verses.** 貴人 may designate an auspicious position or noble outcome, not always the single Tianyi lookup. The formula mentioning both Geng and Xin at Yin must therefore not be silently recast as an ordinary Tianyi table. The minister’s career is reported as the source’s example; no independent biographical verification is claimed here.

[^32]: **A triple combination without You.** Both S and G604 give 乙巳、乙丑、乙巳、辛巳, then explain it through 巳酉丑. You is not written in the example. This may invoke an absent member, or reflect a defective chart, but the available parallel does not establish which. Neither a You pillar nor an omitted character is invented. The main translation preserves both the chart and the author’s explanation.

[^33]: **Five Officers and Gathered Ghosts.** “Five Officers” is a transmitted configuration name, not a requirement that precisely five controlling stems be visible: its example enumerates stems and branches together. The different outcomes assigned to a gentleman and a common person reflect the source’s status-based interpretive assumptions. They should not be read as empirical facts about social groups.

[^34]: **“Unmixed” does not mean a one-phase chart.** The example contains Metal, Water, Fire, and Earth, but their roles are described as mutually ordered and noninjurious. 五行不雜 here praises a coherent arrangement, not literal absence of all but one phase.

[^35]: **Two elliptical maxims.** 木官不重 and 金鬼無偏 are closely followed by explanations of proportionate Metal/Wood and Fire/Metal. The added “[necessarily]” marks the interpretive qualification in English. It does not alter the Chinese or erase the repeated claim that what would otherwise control or injure can complete a properly balanced arrangement. The Yiyou example’s use of both transformed Metal and generating Wood is retained as the source gives it.

[^36]: **Completion through transformed phases.** 既濟 alludes to the *Changes*’ “Already Across/After Completion” and here names Water and Fire balancing and assisting one another. The actual explanation invokes Bing–Xin transforming to Water and Wu–Gui transforming to Fire, rather than merely counting ordinary Water and Fire stems. 奇儀 is kept as “wondrous and ceremonial influences”; no unmentioned Qimen calculation has been added.

[^37]: **Generated-and-Completed Officer.** Both S and G604 read 陽干配陰支陰支合陽干, repeating “yin branch” rather than giving the expected symmetric “yin stem.” This wording is retained. The sample pairs, including Jihai for Water people, combine stem relationship with the life-stage and *nayin* ideas inherited from earlier sections. Their name “True Officer” is not a guarantee that the preceding, differently defined True-Officer formula is being repeated unchanged.

[^38]: **Reciprocal Officer: variant retained in the note.** S reads 庚午見壬子, Gengwu meeting Renzi; G604 reads 庚午見壬午, Gengwu meeting Renwu. The translation retains S. The list’s reciprocities involve more than an ordinary day-stem Direct-Officer lookup, and the parallel alone does not establish that one pair is the uniquely correct archetype. The Fan Wenzheng example is reproduced as transmitted, without supplying an unmentioned pair to make it fit.

[^39]: **Flanking an absent seat.** Renshen–Guiyou–Jiaxu and Guiwei–Jiashen–Yiyou are consecutive sexagenary positions. The two outer positions imply the middle one. Guiyou is not called Jia’s Direct Officer because Gui controls Jia; its You branch is Xin’s Lu. This is a spatial/sequence construction, distinct from reading the middle stem as Officer.

[^40]: **Officer beneath Officer is year-based.** The “Jia person” in the example cannot be a Jia day, because the passage explicitly gives a Bing day. Here the original stem is a year-based reference. 貼職 is an added or attached appointment, not necessarily promotion to a higher rank. The source marks 官下食合 as an interposed small-character heading; that distinction is retained.

[^41]: **True Officer and Urging Officer Forward.** The latter examples also involve combining stems (Geng–Yi, Wu–Gui), so their “Officer” is not simply the ordinary controlling stem. The phrase 自下而上 is preserved as “from below upward”; the brief text does not provide an unambiguous universal algorithm for it.

[^42]: **Fire or Water in the Three Terraces example.** S begins 火人, “Fire person”; G604 begins 水人, “Water person.” S’s reading can describe a chain in which Water controls Fire, transformed Earth controls Water, and Wood controls Earth; G604 aligns the opening person directly with Jiayin Water. The translation retains S rather than declaring one version decisively wrong. 甲貴在丑己貴在子 names one Noble-Person position for each stem, and Zi–Chou supplies the stated Six Combination.

[^43]: **Why “seven,” and the historical pairing analogy.** Counting the originating stem as first places its same-polarity controller seventh: Jia, Yi, Bing, Ding, Wu, Ji, Geng. The two-men/two-women comparison belongs to the text’s historical yin–yang marriage model; it is not a judgment about modern same-sex relationships. “With restraint, Indirect Officer; without restraint, Seven Killings” is an evaluative distinction used here, but elsewhere the compilation also uses both names more loosely for the same stem relation.

[^44]: **“All law and no people.”** 盡法無民 pictures restraint carried so far that there is no subject left for authority to govern. It is the author’s explicit warning against treating more control as invariably better. Food, Seal, combination, and Blade do not restrain by identical mechanisms: Seal can redirect Killings through generation, while Food directly controls it.

[^45]: **Changing the reference in the Jiayin example.** The opening Jiayin describes the strong natal/day ruler; the subsequent “Tai Sui” discussion shifts attention to year-stem Killings. These paragraphs are not instructions to make every example’s first named pair the year pillar. 金絕, “Metal at Extinction,” participates in the broader seasonal argument; it is not asserted to be the literal stage at every one of Yin, Wu, and Xu.

[^46]: **Pure Killings and mixed Officer.** “Pure” describes consistency of the effective controlling relation, not moral purity or a literal chart containing no other phases. The succeeding text repeatedly admits exceptions when Seal, restraint, or bodily strength changes the result. Its maxims have therefore not been promoted into unconditional rules beyond their stated contexts.

[^47]: **Civil and military images.** The Raven Terrace is the censorial institution; “black-haired” signifies youthful appointment. The source alternately emphasizes Killings alone, Seal’s mediation, or their joint completeness. These are quotations from different authorities. “Killings does not part from Seal” is their compressed maxim, not an instruction to manufacture a missing Seal in every example.

[^48]: **Historical exempla and filial predictions.** The line about Yang Guifei is an astrological attribution in the cited *Jade Casket*, not historical proof of a celestial cause. Statements about a noble husband, rebellious sons, or many children are preserved as predictions of the original text. The Two Virtues are Heavenly and Monthly Virtue, discussed in [juan three](juan-03.md).

[^49]: **The restraining spirit being injured.** 煞神元有制神傷 is parsed as the killing spirit originally receiving injury from a restraining influence. The last couplet then warns that damage to that restraint reverses fortune. Translating the first line as “the restraining spirit was originally damaged” would blur the contrast. This is a syntactic interpretation, not an emendation of the Chinese.

[^50]: **Compressed directions in the Killings verses.** The Jia/Yi stanza’s 水運行來怕火金 is retained closely; the transmitted verse does not explain a complete set of seasonal conditions for it. Kun is the southwest Earth trigram and Kan the northern Water trigram. The preceding Earth/Mao verse thus contrasts a favorable Fire-and-Wood course with returning to Earth/Water territory; it should not be conflated with every other stanza’s directional advice.

[^51]: **Variant advice across juan.** This juan’s “six Yi” stanza warns against going into 金鄉, Metal country. The related quotation in juan four warns against 南方, the south. Both readings are transmitted in their respective Siku passages; neither has been changed to match the other. The final five-character stanza contrasts Kan–Li with Kun–Dui. G604 has additional obvious garbling there, including 乙未 where S has 乙木; S’s clearer reading is retained.

[^52]: **Water or Wood restraining Earth: supported repair.** S has 煞星須要水來降, “Killings must have Water come to subdue it.” G604 has 木來降, “Wood come to subdue it.” The latter restores the direct control of Ji Earth by Wood and is adopted in brackets in the translation; S’s Water reading is recorded here. This is an attested variant, not a silent appeal to a contemporary rule.

[^53]: **Two kinds of “rootless.”** The stanza juxtaposes 無根有土 and 主旺無根. It does not define whether “roots” refers to the day ruler, the controlling influence, or the configuration’s effective support in each occurrence. The translation retains the repeated word and the contrast between welcoming and fearing Fire, rather than inventing a precise missing condition. G604 preserves substantially the same wording.

[^54]: **Disability and moral character.** The source groups predicted bodily conditions with malicious behavior. This is a historical prejudice, not a connection endorsed by the translator. S and G604 both transmit 跛鱉; the translation gives the clear semantic core 跛, “lameness,” without turning 鱉, literally “soft-shelled turtle,” into an invented medical diagnosis. 駢指 can mean joined or supernumerary fingers; “fused fingers” conveys the transmitted bodily irregularity without claiming diagnostic precision. 癆 and related wasting terms elsewhere are likewise not automatically equated with a single modern disease.

[^55]: **Exposed in the stem, concealed in the branch.** The prose asks for the hour’s Indirect Officer to emerge in the stem; the subsequent *Walking Alone* quotation locates the single noble influence in the branch. Both formulations are preserved. Their coexistence warns against assuming that the configuration’s name always identifies one rigidly identical layout.

[^56]: **Roots defined as Wealth.** Here the source explicitly glosses 財 as Killings’ root. “Rootless Killings” therefore cannot everywhere mean absence of its own branch root in the modern technical sense. The gloss’s Gengshen/Yimao examples are retained from S; G604 corrupts Gengshen to 庚甲. Li and Du are the paired poetic exemplars invoked by the phrase 文過李杜; the point is that even extraordinary literary ability cannot overcome the stated structural deficiency.

[^57]: **Late sons and the obscure first graph.** S’s final stanza begins its third line 底月時中如有制. The clear clause is “if there is restraint within month or hour”; 底 has no securely established independent function here and has not been inflated into an additional chart rule. The verse itself qualifies the opening categorical prediction of childlessness.

[^58]: **Following, yielding, and Yingzhou.** “Abandoning the fate” is a technical yielding of the day ruler to an overwhelmingly dominant arrangement, not an instruction about conduct or self-harm. The prose allows some yang stems to follow but excludes yang Wood; the verse warns more generally about the five yang stems. That difference of scope is retained. Yingzhou is used as an elevated literary/courtly destination, continuing the book’s imagery of ascent, not a literal instruction to travel to an island.

[^59]: **Storehouse Killings and the transmitted hours.** S and G604 both give a Yi day with Xinchou hour and a Xin day with Wuxu hour. The latter’s Wu stem is not Xin’s Killings, but Ding Fire can be understood as stored within Xu. The former does not match the ordinary day-to-hour stem sequence used elsewhere: a Yi day’s Chou hour would be Dingchou. These transmitted examples are not silently changed into recalculated charts; no secure alternative witness was found in the comparison used here.

[^60]: **Three counterexamples to blanket rejection of mixture.** The text explicitly concludes that 官煞混雜 cannot automatically mean low station. The first example refers to Gui Officer’s combination with Wu; the second to Ding–Ren transformation and a Wood branch configuration; the third to a strong Xin ruler able to bear the controlling influences. These are distinct explanations, not one generalized claim that Officer and Killings should always be combined together.

[^61]: **Assembling Killings into Seal.** Shen–Zi–Chen is a Water configuration, not a Metal-Killings configuration for Jia in the narrow ordinary-phase sense. The explanation starts with Geng inside Shen as Killings and redirects it through the Water Seal configuration. The fatal example is not a complete four-pillar chart: the year pillar is unstated and has not been supplied. The Xu and Xinhai events are presented in the text’s order.

[^62]: **A substantive verse variant.** S’s final couplet says that after the body-strengthening course, punishment, clash, breaking, and harm can still give safety when Killings flourishes. G604 instead has 日主殺年傷不足，藏官露殺起災迍, a different and itself difficult couplet about the day ruler, Killings/year, injury or insufficiency, and concealed Officer with displayed Killings causing trouble. S is retained. The preceding prose distinguishes restraining encounters from an unqualified flourishing-Killings course, so the verse must not be read without that distinction.

[^63]: **Remove and retain.** 去 and 留 concern which influence remains effective in the configuration; they do not require erasing a character from the natal chart. The Jia example is explicit: Bing controls Geng and combines with Xin; Ding controls Xin but needs Yi’s combination with Geng to retain Killings. The Yi example changes the combining agent: Yi itself retains Geng after Ding removes Xin. This is why identical English verbs must not conceal different polarity mechanisms.

[^64]: **Combining in and combining away.** 合來 and 合去 are relational judgments about whether a combination brings an influence into effective service or removes it from the operative arrangement. The passage does not equate every combination with a full Five-Phase transformation. The Ji example’s Geng is Hurting Officer, controlling Jia and combining with Yi.

[^65]: **Day/year versus “uncommitted” influences.** 閒神 is translated “uncommitted influence,” meaning one not presently functioning as the principal ruler or useful influence, not an inherently useless star. The author explicitly prioritizes day and year in the later counting rule. His maxim “the Day Master combining Killings makes low station” sits beside favorable examples of combining Killings elsewhere; the distinction is preserved without falsely reconciling all transmitted authorities.

[^66]: **Substantial seasonal authority.** The first part of Shen month gives the controlling influence seasonal force that a merely present Bing cannot necessarily remove. The text’s stress is on effective strength, not a Boolean rule that one restraining stem cancels one controlled stem. S’s 官星有尘扶 is rendered “Officer Star receiving generation and support”; 尘 in that expression is an evident transcription defect for the sense supplied by the repeated 生扶 in the immediately surrounding argument.

[^67]: **What lies below supports what is above.** 丙下抱上 is compressed and not a separately explained configuration name. The translation renders the visible direction of support rather than inventing a lost branch or a technical label. The nearby 不受福德, describing the person after removing Officer and retaining Killings, is also ambiguous: “does not accept ease and blessing” is a close contextual reading of an unsettled, self-laboring disposition, not a claim that the example cannot ever be fortunate.

[^68]: **Food and Seal on yin days.** The quoted distinction relies on the author’s polarity conventions. It is not a universal prohibition on Food whenever any Seal exists: subsequent Food and Seal chapters supply their own qualifications. The text is an anthology of formulas with differing scope, not a deductively uniform treatise.

[^69]: **Adjacent and separately established.** 官煞相連 and 官煞各分 distinguish a joined controlling force from separately competing Officer and Killings. The source does not specify a simple distance in pillar-counts. Its following lines also broaden categories according to excess: repeated Food is treated as Hurting Officer, repeated Officer as Killings. These are interpretive reclassifications, not changes to the stems’ ordinary yin–yang identities.

[^70]: **Ding as True Wood.** This is the older transformed-phase attribution, not ordinary Ding Fire. Yi–Geng supplies True Metal. The argument then evaluates it against the branch Wood configuration of Hai–Mao–Wei or the Metal configuration of Si–You–Chou. Translating Ding as Fire here, merely for consistency with a modern introductory table, would reverse the source’s argument.

[^71]: **Peers through nayin.** Renyin and Renshen have Metal *nayin*, hence affinity with Yi–Geng True Metal despite Ren’s True-Wood attribution. The subsequent Xin, Ji, and Yi examples repeat the pattern of a potentially controlling True Phase becoming acceptable through *nayin* affinity. Noble-Person positions provide a separate qualification in the Ding/You/Hai example. These are not all the same mechanism.

[^72]: **The two ghosts.** In Dingwei / Jiachen / Guichou / Guihai, the two Gui are True Fire that might control Jia’s True Earth, but their ordinary Water can nourish Jia Wood. The *nayin* sequence is Guihai Water → Guichou Wood → Jiachen Fire → Dingwei Water, with the text’s explanatory emphasis on generation up to Fire and its Wealth relation to Water. The paragraph itself shifts reference; the translation does not pretend every relation is simultaneously valid in one phase system.

[^73]: **A fashioned vessel and Fire.** Renshen/Guiyou are Sword-Edge Metal in *nayin*. The passage combines the metaphor of a completed vessel harmed by renewed Fire with Ren’s True-Wood attribution at Extinction. In Guiyou, Gui’s ordinary Direct Officer is Wu, and Wu–Gui combination supplies the contrasting interpretation. Its statement that the original stem “carries Gui’s Officer” is elliptical and is retained rather than replaced by an invented visible Wu pillar. 右職 is rendered military appointment, preserving its contrast with clerical service.

[^74]: **Direct Wealth and mixture.** The opening prohibition against confusing Direct and Indirect Wealth is preserved even though later authorities distinguish the Wealth categories less strictly than Officer/Killings. 妻, “wife,” and 財, “wealth,” are linked by the author’s household analogy; they are not presented here as an acceptable modern equation of a person with property.

[^75]: **Wealth first, Seal afterward.** 先 and 後 may refer to order of appearance or operative precedence; the maxim does not specify a universal physical ordering of pillars. The Deng Tong example is quoted as the source’s exemplum of wealth one cannot enjoy. It is not independent evidence for the validity of the natal interpretation.

[^76]: **The surprising exposure rule.** Both S and G605 say that when Companions and Rob Wealth are present, Wealth should emerge so everyone sees it and cannot seize it. This contrasts with the immediately repeated preference for concealment. No negative has been inserted to make the passage more conventional; the tension is part of the transmitted text.

[^77]: **What is damaged in the three examples.** Ren injures Bing Food, Gui injures Ding Food, and the robbing Wood competes for Earth Wealth. Mao–Wu is the branch-breaking relation invoked for Jia; Hai disrupts Si for Yi. The final example concerns the empty Xu branch in the Jiazi ten-pair round. These are different routes to failure of Wealth, not one universal method of locating its Storehouse.

[^78]: **“Wealth need not fear multiplicity.”** The compilation gives this after warning that Wealth must not be excessive. Here the explanation immediately requires the body to be strong enough to bear it, while Wealth secretly generates an absent Officer. The statements are retained in their own contexts; no single numerical rule for “too much Wealth” has been invented.

[^79]: **Season, position, completion.** 得時, 得位, and 有成 refer respectively to month, day, and hour in this paragraph. Their ranking is the author’s, and the year’s ancestral significance is explicitly limited after middle age here. Other passages assign different weights; this is not a complete standardized age table.

[^80]: **The wife managing the household.** 幹蠱 is used for taking charge of and setting affairs in order. The source presents the capable wife and helpful son as the way an otherwise weak ruler may enjoy ease. Its status and gender language is retained without implying that a chart proves which partner should have authority.

[^81]: **Lu, Wen, old houses, and Red Luan.** 路温 is retained as the two abbreviated names “Lu and Wen”; the passage does not identify them securely enough to supply biographies. 喬木 evokes an old-established household, not literal tall trees as an astrological sign. Red Luan is the auspicious marriage indicator named by the source; the sentence is not converted into an exact promised wedding date.

[^82]: **Nine out of ten and ten thousand qing.** These are the source’s emphatic expressions, not statistical findings. A *qing* is retained as the historical field-area unit rather than converted into hectares across potentially differing systems. The source’s own small-character note expressly limits the maxim about Wealth and Killings to the weak-body case.

[^83]: **Nine Terraces and exemplary names.** 下九臺 is the source’s death/underworld image; it is retained rather than silently normalized to the more familiar “Nine Springs.” Fan Dan and Lin Gao are given as cited exempla, not newly verified biographies or reconstructed birth charts. S’s 范單 is rendered with the conventional pronunciation represented by “Fan Dan.”

[^84]: **A dissenting Wealth portrait.** The *Myriad Auspices* extract warns against Officer appearing, although other portions welcome Officer generating or protecting the Wealth arrangement. The translation preserves the attributed statement; it does not suppress a difficult witness merely because it diverges from an earlier maxim.

[^85]: **A forceful Blade.** This stanza specifically adds 刃剛強 to much Wealth and bodily weakness, then warns against strengthening territory. Its outcome is not silently reversed to align with neighboring verses that recommend support to a weak ruler. The widowhood imagery and repeated weeping are retained as the source’s rhetoric, not literal counts of future bereavements.

[^86]: **Body Palace, not body-and-Officer: supported repair.** S reads 全憑時印助身官. G605 reads 全憑時印助身宮, “all depends on the hour’s Seal assisting the Body Palace.” 宮 is adopted in brackets; it preserves the seven-character line and fits the preceding rootless-body condition. The S reading 官 is recorded here rather than silently ignored.

[^87]: **A monthly phrase restored from G605.** S has 或夏令月申巳土旺相, which could misleadingly suggest additional Shen and Si months. G605 reads 或夏令月中己土旺相: “or in summer’s command, Ji Earth within the month flourishing and assisted.” The translation adopts the clearer monthly reading in brackets. The opening 甲日午年巳 refers to Ji Earth associated with Wu 午, not an impossible “Jiwu” sexagenary pair; the analogous hour wording is treated the same way.

[^88]: **Secretly generating Officer versus Wealth-and-Officer.** This named configuration requires Officer to be absent from the visible chart: effective Wealth is said to produce its office-giving result indirectly. That is why the author distinguishes it from an ordinary chart with both Wealth and Officer. 納粟 is a grain contribution connected with obtaining status or office; 儀賓 is marriage into the imperial family. Neither is translated as a modern investment strategy. The prohibition on Food/Hurting Officer belongs to this specifically named pattern, not every example of Food generating Wealth.

[^89]: **Wet Wood.** The passage treats Water as preventing the Wood Wealth from generating Fire Officer. This is an image of the configuration’s operation, not the claim that all real wood exposed to water permanently loses combustibility. Geng–Yi and Xin–Jia use their ordinary Wealth relations here, unlike the transformed-phase True Wealth formulas later.

[^90]: **“Can bear it”: a conjectural repair.** S reads 吾未能任為福, literally “I cannot yet bear it as blessing”; G605 has the still defective 吾木能任為福. The argument has just changed weakness into strength through Earth Seal and asks why flourishing Metal and Water should be feared. The translation’s “[can]” is therefore a contextual repair, not an attested reading in the consulted witnesses. A fully diplomatic rendering would retain the negation and leave the contradiction. The seasonal births in the opening list belong to the Wealth phase—Water for Earth, Metal for Fire, Wood for Metal, Fire for Water—not to the natal ruler’s own phase.

[^91]: **“The wealth of the multitude.”** 衆人之財 distinguishes Indirect Wealth from the wife’s specifically assigned property in the analogy. It suggests wealth circulating beyond the intimate household rather than defining a modern category such as investment gains, communal ownership, or inheritance alone. The text itself includes trade, patronage, and several kinds of inherited property.

[^92]: **Parents’ territory.** 父母之地 means the generating/Seal territory in the relational scheme, not necessarily the geographical place where one’s parents live. The paragraph’s “elsewhere” and “other country” language, by contrast, can refer to leaving the ancestral home. Keeping those uses distinct avoids turning technical directions into unsupported travel advice.

[^93]: **“Releases wealth.”** S and G605 both transmit 元用偏財身旺脫財, contrasting Direct Wealth’s 發財 with Indirect Wealth’s 脫財. 脫財 can signify releasing, spending, or losing wealth; the sentence gives no secure further condition. It is retained as “releases wealth,” rather than silently emended into another promise of increasing riches. The subsequent claim that Indirect Wealth surpasses Direct Wealth is an attributed opinion within the compilation.

[^94]: **Generosity, deception, and desire.** The text combines an affectionate and generous portrait with allegations of deceit, gossip, and sexual appetite. These are historical character judgments associated with the pattern, not facts about people who have it. “Flowers” retains the erotic metaphor rather than replacing it with an invented modern clinical category.

[^95]: **Jiaxu hour is not a mistaken Wuxu.** The example gives Wuchen or Jiaxu. Wuchen exposes Wu Wealth; Jiaxu can draw upon Wu concealed in Xu. The following sentence explicitly ranks exposed Wealth above concealed Wealth, so there is no need to replace Jiaxu merely because its stem is Jia. S and G605 agree on these examples.

[^96]: **“Lu difficult to develop.”** The hour-wealth verse reads 運入財鄉發祿難 in both S and G605. It is retained, notwithstanding the favorable Wealth-course judgments elsewhere. The line may distinguish acquiring wealth from obtaining official Lu, but the source does not explicitly resolve it; “difficult” has not been changed to “easy.”

[^97]: **Stranded in Chen.** 困在陳 is an allusive image of deprivation and lack of provisions. It is not the Chen 辰 branch: the source writes 陳, the place name. “Food unable to supply” preserves the interplay between ordinary sustenance and the technical Food influence.

[^98]: **Exclusive Wealth and an implied Si.** The explanation draws Si, Bing’s Lu, into relation with Shen through punishment and combination. Si need not be an explicitly written pillar in each given chart. Ivory tablets and golden-fish insignia retain the imagery of official distinction; they are not literal objects that a practitioner is told to acquire.

[^99]: **Following Wealth reverses ordinary support preferences.** The named configuration requires yielding completely to Wealth. A root or Seal that would help an ordinary weak-body chart can therefore break the following arrangement. The text’s northern and southern directions belong to the Ding/You example; they are not supplied as universal directions for every Follow-Wealth fate.

[^100]: **Day in the heading, year in the verse.** The heading is 日坐天財, but the verse explicitly begins 年干剋下, “the year stem controls what is below.” Bi’s chart has Ji Earth as year stem and Chen, Water’s Storehouse, beneath the day, matching that year-based explanation. It should not be recalculated solely from Geng as the day stem. Both S and G605 preserve this shift of reference.

[^101]: **Generated Wealth’s layered examples.** The opening Wu examples use Earth’s forward Birth/flourishing/Storehouse positions at Shen, Zi, and Chen. The added Wuwu and Jihai examples introduce other self-Birth/self-flourishing associations. The text does not set out one uniform yin-stem stage table for all of them; they are retained rather than replaced by examples chosen for modern consistency.

[^102]: **Kindred Wealth and Wealth Meeting Sha.** The first list employs transformed phases: Yi–Geng Metal for the Fire group, Ding–Ren Wood for Metal, Wu–Gui Fire for Water, and Jia–Ji Earth for Wood. The second list uses the corresponding Wealth *nayin* at its Storehouse. The appended warning 卻妨妻人毒藥害命 is syntactically rough; it gives harm involving the wife and poisoning, but does not securely identify who poisons whom. The translation does not turn it into an accusation that a wife necessarily poisons her husband.

[^103]: **True Wealth.** Jia–Ji True Earth controlling Bing–Xin True Water, and Bing–Xin True Water controlling Wu–Gui True Fire, are the examples. Their “Wealth” relation is between transformed phases, not merely the visible ordinary stems. The preceding Wealth-at-Birth examples instead locate the Wealth phase’s Birth in an ordinary branch.

[^104]: **Heavenly Wealth and Ghost Wealth.** Yihai’s Fire *nayin* is correctly transmitted; Yi–Geng provides True Metal as its Wealth. The ensuing Ghost-Wealth examples shift to the relation between a *nayin* and a controlling/controlled True Phase. These examples are preserved with their own names; “ghost” here must not automatically be equated with the day stem’s ordinary Seven Killings. 世財 is rendered worldly wealth, without inventing a narrower distinction between lawful and unlawful income.

[^105]: **The three Wealth Storehouses and the last two examples.** The text expressly defines Lu as stem, Fate as branch, and Body as *nayin*. The final unpunctuated series is read as two weak-ruler/Wealth pairs: Renyin Metal meeting Wuchen Wood, and Dingmao Fire meeting Guiyou Metal. This respects their controlling relationships; it is not a single four-pillar chart. The translation adds no missing birth date or location.

[^106]: **Seal’s opening summary is not its final rule.** The header welcomes Food God, while later quotations warn against Food harming a Seal configuration. The chapter itself repeatedly supplies exceptions to broad slogans. The Chen/Xu versus Chou/Wei distinction in the header is retained without an invented explanation absent from that sentence. 正官 in the occupational portrait means the principal officeholder, not only the technical Direct-Officer star.

[^107]: **Abundant Seal and inherited resources.** 見成, also written 現成, means ready-made or already established. The passage’s preference for abundant Seal is followed by warnings about solitude, few children, and excessive support. These are preserved as the compiler’s different evaluative contexts. Returning Seal to Lu means giving its qi effective branch support, not necessarily introducing the day stem’s own Lu.

[^108]: **Seal and a weak body.** The categorical sentence “Seal alone favors bodily weakness” is the source’s contrast with other configurations. Later passages qualify it by the strength of Seal itself and by competing Wealth, Officer, and Killings. The translation does not turn it into an absolute rule that physical weakness or illness is desirable.

[^109]: **The Wu-day Wealth warning: supported repair.** S reads 壬癸丁財壊申庚辛食傷生財破印. G605 reads 壬癸子財壞印，庚辛食傷生財破印. The latter supplies Zi with Ren and Gui as Water Wealth and identifies Seal as what is damaged. The translation adopts [Zi] and [Seal]; S’s 丁 and 申 are not silently repurposed as Ding Fire Wealth or an additional Shen branch. S’s 午 in the Geng and Ren generating-Wealth phrases below is also rendered 生, “generating,” on G605’s clear parallel and the repeated formula.

[^110]: **Metal days’ Seal in Fire months.** Both S and G605 call Wu 午 month Direct Seal and Si month Indirect Seal for Geng. These months contain Earth as well as Fire: Ji in Wu can supply Geng’s Direct Seal and Wu 戊 in Si its Indirect Seal. Thus the wording need not be replaced by an Earth-month list. The same paragraph separately names Ding and Bing as Officer and Killings. The Heavenly-Virtue names Qian and Hai are retained as transmitted; the chapter uses both trigram directions and branch positions in these formulas.

[^111]: **Grouped stems do not preserve every polarity label.** “Yi has the same preferences as Jia,” and corresponding statements for the other pairs, give shared practical treatment; they do not make both stems’ Direct and Indirect Seals identical. The Ren-hour formula mixes stem and branch alternatives. Ji or Wu 午 refers to ordinary Direct Officer; Si or Wu 戊 invokes Wu Earth Killings at Si. It must not be read as an impossible single “Jiwu” pair.

[^112]: **Virtue examples.** The three charts are reproduced as the source’s illustrations. Meng Zhong’s name is preserved as 孟重, without an independently supplied identity or biography. The vegetarian/compassionate characterization is an attributed historical prediction, not evidence that a person’s diet or ethics can be inferred from a chart.

[^113]: **Wealth and Seal at Death or Extinction.** S writes 財印歸死絶; G605 has 則印歸死絕, “then Seal returns to Death and Extinction.” The translation retains S’s “Wealth and Seal.” The surrounding reasoning chiefly concerns Seal losing support, so this difference should be kept in view rather than forced into a single stage table for both phases.

[^114]: **Generation is not indiscriminate.** The material analogies explicitly warn that a nominal generating relation may fail under excess, dryness, flooding, or exhaustion. The gloss “Geng day obtaining Xu” after rigid Metal’s inability to generate Water is retained from both witnesses; its compressed connection is not expanded into an unprovided chart. Medical terms are translated historically: 癰疽 as abscesses, 熱病 as fever, not as a specific modern diagnosis.

[^115]: **The avoided-hour table.** This is a tabulation of the transmitted list, not a recalculated chart table. S’s 巳巳 is normalized to Jisi, supported by G605. Both retain problematic hours: for example, Jimao is not an ordinary Geng-day hour in the standard stem-hour sequence. The final You/Dingsi entry does not restate a day stem; Dingsi would fit a Gui day, but neither witness explicitly supplies Gui, so the table leaves that subject unstated. Clashes are separated into their own column to avoid confusing a branch with a sexagenary hour.

[^116]: **Officer as Seal’s root.** This passage expressly defines 根 through Officer’s generation of Seal, rather than only a branch root beneath the Seal stem. The complete cycle Wealth → Officer → Seal → body → Wealth explains why some Wealth need not destroy Seal. The word “root” elsewhere may have a different immediate reference; that difference is preserved.

[^117]: **Middle and lower positions.** 中下 contrasts with 天元, the Heavenly Origin. It refers to support in the other levels of the chart, rather than unambiguously giving a middle-age/old-age timetable. S cites 天玄, rendered *Heavenly Mystery*; G605 cites 天元. No new attribution is asserted beyond the consulted witnesses.

[^118]: **Mending the six-dragon robe.** 吉甫補六龍之袞 uses the courtly image of assisting the sovereign, whose ceremonial robe bears dragons. Jifu remains the source’s abbreviated name; no specific unverified biography is supplied. “Yellow list” and “yellow proclamation” preserve the examination and official-selection imagery.

[^119]: **Longevity and late recognition as exempla.** Old Peng and Taigong meeting King Wen at eighty are the source’s traditional comparisons. The translation does not authenticate their ages, derive the charts that the source omits, or treat them as statistical support for Seal. The apparently unfavorable combination of Seal, Wealth, Companions, and Rob Wealth is retained even though neighboring formulas may make Companions protective.

[^120]: **Death imagery in the Marrow Song.** The violent-death images are translated as part of the historical quotation, not personal predictions or practical instructions. The immediately categorical promise of survival without Wealth belongs to the same verse and is not endorsed as a medical or safety claim. “Seven in ten” is rhetorical authority in the source, not a measured probability.

[^121]: **Source titles and Three Origins.** S’s 淵鑑 is rendered *Mirror of the Source*; G605 has 淵海. The short citation is not enough to establish a modern edition or unique bibliographical identity. “Three Origins” retains the text’s chart-level term; the passage gives no new complete algorithm for them. The number of quoted authorities does not imply independent corroboration of their predictions.

[^122]: **Food’s hidden injury.** The poem’s 食神暗相刑 is broader than the named branch Punishments: it warns that Food can damage the configuration indirectly, including by generating Wealth that controls Seal. It is not changed to Owl because Food is normally regarded as auspicious in other contexts.

[^123]: **The author’s own rejection of rigid slogans.** The two small-character comments explicitly reject indiscriminate application of “Seal benefits from Officer luck” and “Seal fears Wealth luck.” Both are translated in full. The second concerns living Wood set adrift, not the preserved dead timber of the opening chapter. These distinctions matter more than forcing every occurrence of Water into one fixed meaning.

[^124]: **The two Ren/Gui–Shen poems and their qualification.** The first poem recommends northern Water generally; the following commentary limits this to sufficiently heavy Fire because Metal dies at Zi in the forward phase cycle. The later verse likewise says Water travel is fitting while Imperial Prosperity and Arrival at Office are not. The reference of those last stage names is not explicitly restated; no missing “of Water” or “of Metal” is supplied. The source’s qualifications are preserved rather than collapsed into one unconditional directional rule.

[^125]: **Ghost transformed into Seal: a compressed explanation.** The chart and statement 鬼能銷印 are retained from S. The account moves from Zi Water as Fire’s ghost to Earth Hurting Officer and then to the two Yi/Wood Storehouses; ordinary Water can generate Wood Seal, while Wood controls the Earth. The intervening assertion about dissipating Seal is difficult to reconcile word for word with that cycle. The translation follows the transmitted sequence without pretending that a definitive repair has been established.

[^126]: **Blessing or calamity: supported repair.** S reads 變為日刃發福尤重, “turning into Day Blade, blessing is especially heavy.” G605 reads 發禍尤重, “calamity is especially heavy.” The surrounding warning against Water destroying Fire Seal and the robber analogy support G605; “[calamity]” is adopted. The positive four-Wu chart later has no Water to break the Seal arrangement and is a distinct case.

[^127]: **Womb and Embryo.** 胞胎 names the configuration built from positions of exhaustion and renewed gestation; the listed Gengyin, Xinmao, Bingshen, and Yiyou cannot all be labeled the same single stage under every version of the twelve-stage cycle. Their transmitted name is retained instead of replacing the list with examples recalculated from one modern convention.

[^128]: **Abandoning Seal.** The chapter distinguishes Direct Seal in the month from Indirect Seal there, and from Seal in year or hour when the month supplies Wealth. The instruction “discard the light, use the heavy” makes this a matter of selecting the operative configuration, not literal deletion of a natal stem. “Establishing an independent livelihood” translates the contrast with inherited ancestral foundations.

[^129]: **Inverted Food as a relation, not a separate substance.** 倒食 describes Indirect Seal acting against Food God. The Jia example spells out three consequences of Ren controlling Bing: Earth Wealth loses its source, Ding can injure Xin Officer through its combination with Ren, and Geng Killings is no longer checked by Bing. The conclusion that no Food means “simply Indirect Seal” is essential and is translated explicitly.

[^130]: **Restraint of Owl.** The ten-stem examples include branch qi, combinations, and *nayin*-based qualifications inherited from earlier passages; they should not all be forced into a single exposed-stem control. 丁合 in the Jia sentence can indicate a Ding partner combining with Ren, not a claim that Ding lies hidden in both Chen and Xu. The translation retains the list and does not supply missing pillars.

[^131]: **The elder analogy and bodily descriptions.** The source compares Owl’s restriction with subjection to elders and associates it with bodily irregularity and family harm. These are historical judgments, not evidence of moral qualities in disabled people or grounds for judging relatives. “Under stars and moon” evokes incessant labor, not a prescribed time for undertaking an activity. Yan Zi and Han Xin are cited exempla; no reconstructed birth data are added.

[^132]: **Ding–Mao–Ji and Longevity Star.** S’s compressed 丁逢卯日遇巳土 is understood as Ding meeting Mao and then Ji Earth, the Food God endangered by Mao’s Yi Owl. It is not a claim that the branch Si belongs to Earth. “Longevity Star” here names Food God, so its arrival can activate the Owl–Food conflict rather than necessarily prolong life.

[^133]: **The three contrasting charts.** All three are retained, including the unfavorable first example with Ren restraining the two Bing that drain Jia. The source stresses that this nevertheless frees Killings to act. The two favorable examples have additional generation, combination, and returning Lu. Their presence prevents a blanket equation of Indirect Seal with misfortune.

[^134]: **Mixed Qi and the four seasonal endings.** 不正之氣 means qi not confined to a single principal, proper seasonal influence; it does not call these months morally improper. The four branches each contain three stated stems. 財官印 as the three “uses” below refers to their selected relations to the day ruler, not three new heavenly stems.

[^135]: **The Hour-Tomb illustration.** The complete Dinghai chart has Jichou as its hour. The following Renchen example is a further Officer-Storehouse case, not a fifth natal pillar to be appended to that chart. The text warns against Jichou pressing upon a Storehouse even after presenting it in the first example; no unmentioned intervening condition is invented to make every statement mechanically consistent.

[^136]: **Storehouse Wealth and Storehouse ghost use nayin.** Xinwei is Earth for the Wood person, Gengxu Metal for Fire, Renchen Water for Earth, Jiachen Fire for Water, Guichou Wood for Metal. The contrasting list gives the phase that controls the person: Yiwei Metal, Renxu Water, Wuchen Wood, Jichou Fire, Bingchen Earth. These are not errors in the ordinary phase identities of their individual stems.

[^137]: **Ghost above the Storehouse.** The list distinguishes the initial encounter from a further prohibited pair; a “ghost” in the name does not by itself forbid every occurrence. S has 車破輛 and G605 車破輪, a carriage breaking versus its wheel breaking. “A broken carriage” preserves the shared meaning without asserting an unsupported axle diagnosis. The Sha’s name 軒車 is retained as Carriage, not treated as a modern vehicle-specific prediction.

[^138]: **Wealth above, ghost within.** The subsequent five examples are parsed as natal phase + pair + that pair’s *nayin*: “Metal sees Jichou Fire; Wood sees Yiwei Metal,” and so forth. G605’s punctuation supports this reading. Without separating these units, the sequence can misleadingly look like repeated or doubled phase names.

[^139]: **Opening a Storehouse is qualified.** The quoted maxim “no one fails to develop” follows the prose’s restrictions: strong body, useful contents, proportionate clash, and no excessive damage or suppressing influence. The translation retains the maxim but does not elevate it into an unconditional instruction to seek clashes. “Three uses” means Wealth, Officer, and Seal.

[^140]: **The flourishing–Storehouse reversal.** S and G605 transmit the four compressed lines beginning 旺處生來墓庫絕. They contrast development at flourishing with development from a Storehouse and warn against excess. Their last line 終不撥 is rendered “never cast it off,” referring to unresolved calamity; no missing agent or precise timing formula is supplied.

[^141]: **River Chief and the southern clause.** 河魁 is Xu. Both witnesses have the difficult 南或加臨丑吉時, literally the south perhaps/also descending at auspicious Chou time. The translation stays close. Replacing 或 with 火, or supplying Bing/Ding as the subject, would make a plausible new formula, but the consulted witnesses do not securely establish it.

[^142]: **Ding and Ren taking Chen as Tomb.** Both S and G605 transmit 丁壬本取辰為墓. It cannot simply mean the ordinary same-phase Tomb of both Ding Fire and Ren Water; Chen is Water’s Tomb. The surrounding argument concerns stored Wealth and Officer, so it may be using their relational Storehouses, but the line does not make this explicit. Ding has not been silently changed to Gui.

[^143]: **Snail’s horn and fly’s head.** These images disparage tiny gains and petty competition. They are retained as literary images with their sense made clear here, not converted into additional named astrological stars. The preceding instruction to leave office when Wealth is excessive belongs to the Seal-centered part of the comparison.

[^144]: **Water both favored and disfavored.** S and G605 both give 金水行來名利厚 followed by 水鄉火地失堅牢: Metal-and-Water travel brings profit, yet Water and Fire ground undermine stability. The verse does not state the qualification that would reconcile these lines. Replacing the second 水 with 木 is tempting but unsupported by the consulted witnesses; the discrepancy is retained.

[^145]: **Following Officer or the forward course.** S has 喜順官, G605 喜順宮. The preceding 逆受 makes a contrast between backward and forward movement plausible, while S’s 官 explicitly names Officer. “Following Officer forward” preserves both implications without assigning an unmentioned stem or branch to the course. This is an uncertain reading, not a securely reconstructed prescription.

[^146]: **The Shen-course assertion.** 大運由申壽必終 is transmitted in both S and G605. No complete chart or additional qualifying condition accompanies this exceptionally sweeping death prediction. It is translated as written, not treated as a generally applicable result of a Wu day and Xu month or as evidence for predicting an actual person’s death.

[^147]: **One’s own Storehouse.** 自庫 distinguishes the natal influence’s own Storehouse from the Tomb of another operative influence, such as Officer or Seal. The text permits the former in old age while warning against the latter for an actively employed influence. It provides no numerical age at which “later life” begins here. These qualifications should remain beside the preceding verses that favor opening a Storehouse.

[^148]: **The divided heading.** S places 梟印 at the start of a second small-character line, before the alternative names. G606’s introductory list supports reading it as the last item in the aversions: Owl Seal, not a further name for Hurting Officer. 陽刃 and 羊刃 are related but competing labels elsewhere in the work; “Yang-Blade Sha” here reports an alternative name, not an identification of every Hurting Officer with the later Blade configuration.

[^149]: **“Complete injury” is a technical condition.** 傷盡 means that Officer does not remain as an operative competing influence within the arrangement being discussed. The prose gives two explicit structural definitions and immediately qualifies their value by body, Wealth, or Seal strength. It is not an instruction to harm anyone, nor does it mean that merely removing the written character 官 from a chart improves it.

[^150]: **Employing Wealth and employing Seal.** The passage changes the operative configuration as it moves between these two cases. Wealth drains the generated output onward; Seal supplies and restrains in a different arrangement. Consequently its preferences cannot be collapsed into one universal list. Later quotations allowing both Wealth and Seal are retained rather than deleted to make them agree with this more exclusive account.

[^151]: **Shi Mian and the rhetoric of blindness.** 師冕 names the musician Mian as an exemplar of blindness; the surrounding sentence explicitly concerns eye disease. It does not mean “teacher’s ceremonial cap.” The assertions about disability are preserved as features of the historical text, not endorsed as diagnostic or moral claims. S and G606 agree on the name and the surrounding warning.

[^152]: **The five Hurting-Officer pairings.** “Fire-and-Earth” means a Fire reference producing Earth as Hurting Officer, and so on; the first and second phases are not two arbitrary favorable elements. These arguments invoke warmth, drainage, containment, and cultivation in addition to the controlling cycle. Their very presence qualifies the maxim that Hurting Officer must never meet Officer.

[^153]: **Counterexamples are part of the argument.** The repeated Dingwei/Bingwu chart is explicitly poor despite complete injury and bodily strength. The autumn-Jia example, conversely, can retain office despite visible Ding. Wu 午 beneath Ren or Gui describes the arrangement of branch and stem, not the Earth stem Wu 戊. Both counterexamples are retained in full, including the text’s own course qualifications.

[^154]: **Different stems within the monthly branch.** The examples concern Ding within Wu 午, Bing within Si, and Ji within Wu 午, rather than treating the branch itself as a single fixed Ten-God label. A month can therefore supply one operative influence while another contained stem participates in a conflict. The literal pair Jiashen in the Yi example is preserved; it is not silently replaced by an exposed Geng stem.

[^155]: **An eight-row list, not a completed ten-stem table.** Both S and G606 supply Jia, Yi, Bing, Ding, Wu, Ji, Geng, and Gui, omitting Xin and Ren. The table contains exactly those eight transmitted entries. The missing cases could be inferred from familiar stem relations, but such inferences would be additions rather than translations and have not been inserted.

[^156]: **A difficult instruction about employing Officer.** S has 傷官用官在年月必要剝官運在日時不宜被傷; G606 transmits the same words, with different punctuation. The contrast between year/month and day/hour is retained. The first clause appears to require stripping the very Officer said to be employed. No secure repair is established by these witnesses, so the translation does not silently reverse 剝, remove 官, or manufacture an explanatory exception.

[^157]: **Character descriptions and physiognomy.** The passage combines descriptions of conduct with facial and bodily traits. These are the source’s judgments, not observations made by the translator or reliable grounds for assessing someone’s character from appearance. “Practitioner of the arts” here includes technical and divinatory occupations, not only the modern fine arts.

[^158]: **The Raven Terrace and the pillar assignments.** 烏臺 is the censorial institution evoked by the literary name “Raven Terrace,” not another natal star. The source assigns parents, brothers, offspring, and spouses respectively to year, month, hour, and day in this particular quotation. Elsewhere the assignments and emphases differ; the translation does not standardize them retrospectively.

[^159]: **Willow catkins and lotus coins.** 柳絮 suggests what scatters on the wind; 荷錢 evokes small, coin-shaped lotus leaves bearing rain. The images describe insecure accumulation and elusive gain. “Lotus coins” is not a currency or an extra technical Wealth indicator. The source’s concrete imagery is retained rather than replaced with a modern financial analogy.

[^160]: **Four Defeats.** 四敗 is the technical grouping Zi, Wu, Mao, and You, discussed in [juan three](juan-03.md), not four unsuccessful events. The final line’s claim about the absence of clash is preserved. It should be read beside, not substituted for, the preceding warnings about damaging clashes.

[^161]: **Companions welcomed and then forbidden.** The verse first permits Companions with Hurting Officer or Seven Killings, then warns against them without Officer and with an already flourishing body. The distinctions are strength and the available use, but not every transition is fully specified. “No Officer” translates 無官 as transmitted; it has not been changed to “no Wealth” to produce a simpler rule.

[^162]: **Rootlessness and a consequential Water/Wood variant.** In the adjacent Gui verse S reads 癸日無根水月中, “a rootless Gui day in a Water month”; G606 reads 木月中, “in a Wood month.” Wood would readily fit the Hurting-Officer context, whereas a Water month would normally strengthen Gui. S’s Water is retained and the discrepancy recorded here. Rootlessness elsewhere concerns effective support, not merely whether a stem is printed more than once. The preceding Metal-at-Wu verse is a separate case.

[^163]: **The final long verse.** S gives 月犯父子, father and son affected by offense in the month, even though an earlier quotation assigns brothers to the monthly pillar. The wording is retained. 斬絞徒流 names beheading, strangling, penal labor, and exile; the list is not reduced to a generic “bad luck.” Its violent predictions belong to the historical work, not to the translator’s assessment of any chart.

[^164]: **Counting to Food God.** “Third position” counts the starting stem as the first: Jia, Yi, Bing. Food God therefore has one intervening stem. 進神, 爵星, and 壽星 are alternative names in this passage; the use of “Advancing Spirit” does not automatically make it identical to every separately calculated 進神 elsewhere in the work.

[^165]: **Parent and child change with the relation.** Jia is the generating parent of Bing Food, while Bing generates Wu Wealth. “Mother and child” is a generic image for generating and generated qi; the text can also speak of father and son without changing the phase relationship. The language of effortless provision belongs to the image of a mature child supporting its parent, not a promise that this configuration produces income without work.

[^166]: **Food “becoming” Hurting Officer.** Repetition is said to turn Food God into Hurting Officer because excessive output drains the source; the stem’s formal yin–yang identity has not changed. The old illness terms 翻胃 and 噎食 are translated descriptively, not assigned a single modern medical diagnosis. Void, professional classifications, and the stated course conditions remain part of the passage.

[^167]: **Ren Food for Geng: evaluate the whole chain.** The text does not rate luck by Water alone: it follows Geng’s condition, Ren’s condition, and Jia Wealth’s condition through Shen, You, Xu, Hai, Zi, Chou, Yin, and Mao. This is why Hai and Zi can receive opposite judgments despite both belonging to Water. 甲冠帶 in Chou explicitly refers to Jia’s Cap-and-Belt stage. “Ten years” is supplied by the source itself, not calculated from a birth chart here.

[^168]: **Exceptions to Inverted Food.** These examples bring together stem combinations, branch Lu, and *nayin*. Thus Yisi “is also Fire” by *nayin*, not because Yi Wood has been reassigned to ordinary Fire. The claims that Geng does not fear Wu, Ji does not invert Xin’s Food, and Geng does not invert Ren’s Food are particular transmitted exceptions. They do not erase the earlier warnings about an exposed Indirect Seal controlling an operative Food God.

[^169]: **Xin borrowing Fire’s parental qi.** The paragraph invokes the Earth associated with Fire, including Ji’s Lu at Wu 午. Its statement that Ding “nourishes Xin” is not translated as a new direct generating relation Fire → Metal. The parent is borrowed through the associated Earth and the relevant positions. The derivation from Yin to Xin’s Lu is left in the source’s terms rather than expanded into an unprovided universal table.

[^170]: **A different derivation of “eating.”** Xiyin’s explanation uses transformed stem-pair phases: Jia–Ji Earth controls Bing–Xin Water, Water controls Wu–Gui Fire, and so on. Here 食 is explained through the controlling cycle, unlike the opening definition through a day stem’s generated offspring. Both explanations belong to the compilation and are translated distinctly rather than forced into a single derivation.

[^171]: **The six Bing encounters use more than the day stem.** With Jiazi as reference, Bingshen Fire by *nayin* can control Jiazi Metal and its Shen can break Jia’s Yin Lu. Bingchen’s Seal designation and Bingwu’s injury likewise belong to this older multi-layered account. “Same home,” “self-punishment,” and “breaking Fate” are preserved as the source’s classifications, not recalculated from a modern Day Master alone.

[^172]: **Grouped positions and official titles.** The source gives grouped phase territories, not an exhaustive table assigning each stem exactly one Birth and one Prosperity position. Si, Wu, Shen, and You in the Metal-Food group are therefore all retained. 兩制兩省 and 建節防團 evoke prestigious civil drafting/secretariat and military commands; the translation preserves those institutional distinctions without assigning modern governmental equivalents.

[^173]: **Returning nourishment.** 反哺 supplies the image of offspring feeding the parent. Here the source defines it as the lower positions feeding the upper, supplemented by Tianyi, Officer, Seal, and favorable sha. 鳳閣鸞臺 are literary names for exalted court institutions. They are not additional stars for which the reader must derive new positions.

[^174]: **Surplus Food versus excessive output.** The example of two or three Bing for one Jia seems to conflict with the earlier preference for a single Food God. This passage evaluates adequacy of provision against competing claimants, whereas the earlier one stresses depletion of the generating source. The compilation does not supply all the conditions needed to reconcile them; neither count is suppressed or promoted into an unconditional rule.

[^175]: **Devouring the Brain.** S names 吞啗腦子 and then gives 頭風腦漏, rendered descriptively. G606 has a different account distinguishing year and hour and speaks of injury to the head and face. S’s wording controls here. These old pathological and predictive expressions should not be converted into a diagnosis of a particular neurological condition.

[^176]: **An attested repair in Walking Alone.** S reads 清之則垣, literally an enclosure or wall in the clause about purity; G606 reads 清之則烜, “made clear, it is illustrious.” The translation adopts G606’s 烜 in brackets. S’s following 雜氣無用, “mixed qi without use,” is retained; G606 instead has 泄氣無用, “draining qi without use.” These are distinct variants, not a single wholesale replacement of the quotation.

[^177]: **Food Storehouse, Food Lu, and rank.** 食庫 and 食祿 concern Food God’s Storehouse and Lu positions in this context, not ordinary food storage or merely drawing a salary. 三監九卿 is retained as Three Superintendents and Nine Ministers: the phrase evokes high official rank, but this quotation does not specify a dynasty’s exact administrative arrangement.

[^178]: **Bells, cauldrons, baskets, and gourds.** 鐘銘鼎鼐 evokes public honor and ministerial power; 陋巷簟瓢 evokes poverty with simple provisions. The contrast retains the poem’s material imagery. The statements about one Food God versus two or three again require comparison with the surplus-and-competition passage above; they are not silently made consistent.

[^179]: **Devouring Sha absent: supported correction.** S has 柱中若要吞啗煞, “if the pillars require Devouring Sha,” contradicting both the favorable conclusion and the preceding account of Owl attacking Food. G606 has 若無, “if there is no.” The translation’s bracketed “no” follows that attested reading rather than a conjectural change made solely on doctrinal grounds.

[^180]: **A difficult line about Death, Extinction, and Indirect Seal.** Both S and G606 retain 死絕運臨偏印地 between favorable lines. It appears to introduce the very conditions the prose treats as dangerous, yet supplies no negative or explicit condition linking it to the closing blessing. The translation preserves the difficulty instead of inventing an omitted prohibition or promising that such a course is favorable.

[^181]: **Food and the named influences it encounters.** 食退, 食馬, 食貴人, and 食祿 extend the verb “feed” across technical influences or conditions. Their compressed wording has been retained. “Mist and rosy clouds” evokes withdrawal and reclusion; “founding a house elsewhere” renders 別立家 rather than asserting a specific marital event absent from the verse.

[^182]: **The four phases listed.** S reads 惟行水木土金佳, while G606 has 惟有水木土金佳. S’s 行 supports the translation’s reference to travel or courses. Both omit Fire. No fifth phase has been added, and this undifferentiated verse is not used to override the earlier, carefully qualified accounts of Fire Food God and its courses.

[^183]: **The secondary mother.** 庶母 is the family analogy for Indirect Seal, contrasted with the principal maternal relation; it is not automatically a modern stepmother. Indirect Wealth can restrain the Indirect Seal that attacks Food God, which explains its protective role in the verse. The autumn-grass and winter-frost image is retained as the source’s image of a threatened life.

[^184]: **Calling forth an absent Officer.** The “flying” clash is a technical inference: repeated branches are said to call forth an unoccupied opposite branch, whose contained Officer is then held by a further combination. It is not an actual extra pillar. 填實 means that the formerly absent position becomes expressly occupied, changing the configuration. S’s 干文本運 is difficult; G606 reads 干支本運, supporting the bracketed “stems and branches themselves.”

[^185]: **Four specified days versus the later relaxation.** The opening confines the configuration to Gengzi, Renzi, Xinhai, and Guihai; a later account relaxes the particular seated branch for Xin and Gui when Hai is numerous. Both formulations are preserved. The compiler’s closing observation that Renzi and Guihai are not themselves Hurting-Officer days is also essential: proximity to the Food/Hurting-Officer discussion does not make every configuration in it formally identical.

[^186]: **“Officer Star appears.”** 柱無羈絆官星現 occurs in S and G606 despite the prose’s prohibition on a visibly exposed Officer. In context, “appears” could mean that the absent Officer is successfully elicited, rather than printed in a pillar; the verse itself does not say so. The translation leaves the wording intact and does not insert an additional 無, “without.” Phoenix Pool evokes exalted court service.

[^187]: **A Wealth-course qualification not supplied.** The preceding verse has 關煞 in S and 官殺 in G606; bracketed “Officer” follows G606. This verse welcomes flourishing Wealth luck, while the section opens by excluding natal Wealth and later warns Renzi against Ding Wealth. Those statements concern different situations, but the verse does not enumerate the cases in which its promise holds. It remains a quoted assertion, not a universal replacement for the preceding conditions.

[^188]: **Xu drawing Wu and Yin together.** S has 戊要相牽午共寅; G606 reads 戌要相牽午共寅. The latter gives the branch Xu with Wu 午 and Yin, the Fire triple combination already named in the explanation. The translation adopts “[Xu]” on this textual support. In the following stanza, 三元 is retained as Three Origins and does not mean three additional pillars.

[^189]: **Reverse clash and its entanglements.** The source opens by saying that the hour is not considered here, but subsequently includes month-and-hour cases. This tension is preserved. Its repeated claim that Chen can detain Si also lies outside the elementary list of Six Combinations; neither S nor G606 warrants replacing Chen simply to fit that list. The directions of clash and the receiving combinations must be distinguished.

[^190]: **Only in the day: supported repair.** S reads 只月上有月內無, literally “only present in the month, absent within the month,” whereas G606 has 只日上有月內無, “only in the day, not in the month.” The bracketed “day” adopts G606. S’s subsequent 己亥 is kept as Jihai; G606 instead prints 巳亥. The passage explicitly allows a Hai encounter under specified seasonal conditions, rather than maintaining a blanket ban on every actual Hai.

[^191]: **Two days, six days, and the compiler’s verdict.** The alternate six-day account includes Bingyin, Bingxu, Dingwei, and Dingmao; the compiler then returns to Bingwu and Dingsi because these alone appear in the quoted poems. The translation preserves both the alternate account and the limiting conclusion. “Plain white clothes” signifies someone not yet holding official rank, not a prescribed garment or an additional chart indicator.

[^192]: **Heaven’s Kitchen, not Heavenly Clash.** S’s heading is 天衝食禄, but the paragraph repeatedly defines 天厨禄. G606 heads it 天廚食祿. “Heaven’s Kitchen: Food and Lu” follows the supported reading; both Chinese forms are shown in the heading. This is not an unrecorded new configuration. The explicit requirement that both the Food stem and its Lu branch appear is retained.

[^193]: **A year-derived, not day-derived formula.** The passage itself insists that its earlier verse proceeds from the year and cannot be reproduced by simply deriving the hours from a day stem. The translation keeps that warning. “Appropriate round” renders 本旬 within this account; no complete table of the remaining stems has been manufactured from the two examples.

[^194]: **The thirteenth position and the shared nest.** Counting Jiazi as one, Bingzi is the thirteenth position of the sexagenary sequence: twelve advances restore the branch while moving to the generated stem. This explains the otherwise obscure 十三位即同. The examples compare different pillars, not invariably the Day Master with the hour. The named officials’ charts are reported as transmitted examples, not independently authenticated birth records.

[^195]: **An absent combination, not an ordinary branch Six Combination.** The text explicitly says Gengshen combines with the absent Yimao. Geng–Yi is a stem combination; Shen–Mao is not one of the standard Six Combinations. This passage belongs to a special inferred-configuration account and has not been rewritten to imply otherwise. Its 寅字衝提 refers to Yin’s destructive clash in the configuration, even though Shen has just been specified as the hour rather than the monthly framework.

[^196]: **Seal both welcomed and excluded.** The prose expressly welcomes 印綬, while the poem says 要無官印, requiring Officer and Seal to be absent. S and G606 both retain the difference. It may distinguish helpful support from a Seal that destroys Food, but the text does not spell that out here. Likewise S favors autumn/winter with 日旺, a flourishing day, while G606 reads 食旺, flourishing Food. The main translation follows S rather than harmonizing the two.

[^197]: **Gao’s and Wang’s configurations: an attested subject restored.** S runs 高甲戊庚三奇為禄馬同鄉; G606 has 高甲戊庚三奇王祿馬同鄉. The latter separates Gao’s Three Wonders from Wang’s Lu-and-Horse Sharing a Home and makes sense of the comparison. The translation marks “[Wang has]” as a repair. The two full charts, Zhang Guangyuan’s differing month, and the compiler’s rejection of a rigid autumn/winter requirement are all preserved.

[^198]: **Red Luan here is a Food/Seal designation.** The four examples connect Food stems with their stated pairs: Bing–Wuxu, Xin–Guichou, Ren–Jiachen, Yi–Dingwei. This passage supplies no marriage-star calculation. Its evocative title should not be replaced by the rules of another configuration also called Red Luan.

[^199]: **Councillor Chen.** 陳朝議 supplies the surname Chen and a title, not a full personal name. No identification has been guessed. “Ink Pool” and “Gushing Spring” preserve the literary imagery of the heading; the source itself links the configuration with literary distinction and gives all six further pairings, which remain in the translation.

[^200]: **The three exceptional influences.** The prose derives Officer through Wu, Rank/Food through Yi, and Wealth through Bing. 三奇 here describes the complete conjunction of those useful influences; it should not automatically be converted to the fixed Jia–Wu–Geng or Yi–Bing–Ding stem trios. The hidden stems shared by Si and Shen and the secret combinations are doing different work. The final statement that Exclusive Seal is not itself Food or Hurting Officer is retained.

[^201]: **Yang versus sheep; competing Blade definitions.** This section insists on 陽, yang, and restricts Blade to the five yang stems, while the earlier [juan-three discussion](juan-03.md) preserves 羊, sheep, and other definitions. The compilation explicitly asks the reader to compare them. Its marriage analogy shifts perspective: Geng is termed 庚妻 in the opening, while a later quotation calls Yi Jia’s younger sister married to Geng. “Combines in marriage with Geng” retains the relation without imposing one consistent family diagram on both formulations.

[^202]: **An impossible literal hour pair.** S and G606 both give a Jia day with 乙卯, Yimao, in the hour as “True Blade.” Under the ordinary day-to-hour derivation used elsewhere in the book, a Jia day’s Mao hour is Dingmao, not Yimao. The phrase may isolate an ideal stem/branch Blade image rather than report a valid literal chart, or may be corrupt; neither possibility is established here. Yimao is retained. S’s following 己酉 is rendered Jiyou; G606 prints 巳酉. You supplies the relevant clash with Mao in either reading.

[^203]: **Tai Sui and Pardon’s Writ.** Calling Tai Sui an ominous spirit belongs to this warning and is immediately qualified by its auspicious associations. It is not the book’s sole definition of the annual influence. 赦文 is rendered “Pardon’s Writ”; the passage does not provide the calculation or date table needed to identify it, and none has been invented.

[^204]: **Severity does not exhaust the Blade account.** The harsh descriptions are immediately followed by a case in which the same classes of punishment and harm, properly situated and rescued, confer distinction. These are historical evaluative claims, not the translator’s assessment of people with particular facial traits, disabilities, or family backgrounds. 過房 means adoption or transfer into another family branch, not simply moving house.

[^205]: **The objection is to clashing Wealth, not Wealth as such.** The author himself challenges the blanket prohibition. Zi clashes with Wu 午, and Mao with You, but Earth Wealth for Jia and Metal Wealth for Bing do not inherently make those clashes. Si and Wu 午 can be discussed as Wealth territory through their associated Earth qi; they have not been relabeled as ordinary Earth branches. Ren’s Officer at Wu 午 is Ji Earth contained there, not Ding Fire itself.

[^206]: **Repairs in the non-Blade examples.** S’s 亥中午水, “Wu 午 Water within Hai,” is corrected to G606’s 壬水, Ren Water; S’s 巳中戌土 is corrected to G606’s 戊土, Wu Earth. Both changes are bracketed. The Yiyou/Gengchen explanation’s 乙坐庚下 remains awkward and is translated closely, not presented as a claim that the written year/day/hour order has changed. The examples include yin stems despite the opening restriction to yang stems; that inconsistency belongs to the transmitted account.

[^207]: **Killings in luck: the Seal exception.** The text first warns of new or repeated Killings and then explicitly permits Killings when Blade and Seal are already present. This is a change of supporting configuration, not permission to erase the unfavorable sentence. Natal presence, repeated arrival, and the available means of restraint must remain separate in reading this passage.

[^208]: **Three colored day animals.** The red Horse is Bingwu, the yellow Horse Wuwu, and the black Rat Renzi. The color supplies the stem’s phase while the animal supplies the branch. “Day Blade” here is therefore a specific set of three dates, not every day on which a separately derived Blade indicator occurs.

[^209]: **Self Blades and Flying Blades.** S explicitly gives three Self Blades and four Flying Blades, including yin-stem pairs, after having denied Blade to the five yin stems. The lists are preserved exactly. They should not be silently recalculated through a single modern definition or confused with the earlier three-Day-Blade set.

[^210]: **“Greatly auspicious” in a troubling context.** S and G606 both retain 遇此大吉 after disputes over wealth and brothers separating. The phrase may imply that separation itself is beneficial, or the text may be corrupt; the consulted witnesses do not establish a repair. The translation quotes it visibly rather than replacing 吉 with 凶. 敗財, Defeating Wealth, is here assigned specifically to yin stems meeting the corresponding yang stems.

[^211]: **A flourishing body with Seal.** The final 身旺又加印助必榮華而發福 is transmitted by S and G606, even though surrounding passages warn against excessive bodily strength and additional support. It could be a displaced or incompletely qualified maxim. The translation retains it and does not change 旺 to 弱 solely to produce theoretical agreement.

[^212]: **Companions as helper or rival.** 比肩 is read here through its effects upon the operative configuration: the same class can share an excessive burden or compete for scarce Wealth. The statements about brothers’ fortunes follow from that analogy, not an independent calculation of the brothers’ own charts. In the following verse S has 火土得衣糧, “Fire and Earth provide clothing and grain”; G606 ends 火土保安康, “Fire and Earth preserve well-being.” S is retained.

[^213]: **Heavenly Officer versus Indirect Officer.** S’s quotation from the Mysterious Mechanism reads 天官, Heavenly Officer; G606 reads 偏官, Indirect Officer. The latter fits the surrounding Killings-and-Blade discussion, but S’s wording is retained rather than silently identified with it. 閫外持權 evokes military authority outside the commander’s gate. The accompanying gloss explicitly identifies 生氣 as Seal-Ribbon, not an additional independently calculated star.

[^214]: **Reported examples, not medical evidence.** The statements about Lüzhu and the three charts lacking sight are the compiler’s assertions. The translation preserves all three four-pillar sequences and the source’s first-person “in my observation”; it does not claim independent verification. Likewise, the adjoining warnings of dismemberment and penal exile are historical predictions, not conclusions established by this translation.

[^215]: **Yama’s summons.** 閻羅 is Yama, the ruler of the dead in the imagery invoked here. 出引徵 evokes issuance of a summons to death. This preserves the religious image rather than introducing a further natal indicator or converting it into a specific medical cause.

[^216]: **The original foundation and later Wealth.** The verse distinguishes Wealth already rooted in the natal arrangement from Wealth arriving later into a configuration that has none. Its categorical claim of shortened life in the latter case is retained, but cannot be made a general prediction independent of the many exceptions elsewhere in the juan. The subsequent 氣神元旺 line is also compressed: the translation understands 無財被剋傷 as lacking Wealth that could be injured.

[^217]: **Contradictory maxims remain adjacent.** One verse fears punishment and clash with Blade and Killings; another explicitly welcomes breaking, harm, punishment, and clash. A further verse welcomes Wealth luck while warning against Wealth assembling in a particular year. The text gives no complete algorithm reconciling these claims. They are preserved as collected formulations, with the surrounding prose’s insistence on strength, restraint, and the operative configuration left in place.

[^218]: **The final Blade charts.** The first explanation is punctuated 子午雖衝，申子會，午不能衝；時官星制伏陽刃: Shen and Zi assemble despite the nominal Zi–Wu clash, followed by the hour’s Officer restraining Blade. It does not establish that every partial Shen–Zi assembly cancels every Zi–Wu clash. In the second example S’s 戊歸禄己 is read as Wu returning to Lu at Si, as the stated chart and Lu assignment require; Ji would be a stem, not the returning branch.

[^219]: **Established Lu now has a day/month reference.** The opening explicitly defines 建祿 by the day stem’s Lu in the monthly branch. This differs from the broader year-based and *nayin*-based Lu accounts preserved earlier, as the compiler himself states at the end. The warnings about ancestral property are also qualified within this very section by helpful Wealth and Officer in the year. They should not be detached from those exceptions.

[^220]: **Wood’s Extinction at Shen: attested correction.** S has 水絕之鄉, Water’s Extinction, while G606 reads 木絕之鄉, Wood’s Extinction. Wood accords with both the preceding residual Wood and the role of Wood as Geng’s Wealth; Water is conventionally at Birth at Shen in the forward cycle used here. “[Wood’s]” follows G606. The “three or four tenths” is the source’s qualitative estimate, not a measured probability.

[^221]: **Ten suitable hours, not ten exposed Killings stems.** All ten transmitted day/month/hour entries are included. Their use can depend on a branch’s contained stem: Renshen for Jia, for example, contains Geng Killings at Shen, although the exposed Ren is not Jia’s Killings. The closing injunction against excess and repetition belongs to the same instruction and must not be dropped from its practical reading.

[^222]: **The forty-eight-year example.** The full chart, Renyin course, Renshen year, and stated age forty-eight are reproduced from S; G606 agrees. No calendar conversion or independently verified birth/death chronology has been added. “Injury to Bing” refers to the Officer employed by Xin, explaining why the argument turns on the useful influence rather than bodily flourishing alone.

[^223]: **Water flourishing; and the compiler’s historical “recently.”** S’s 水取堤防破貴元 is difficult. G606 reads 水旺堤防破貴元, supporting the bracketed “flourishing”; 堤防 is understood here as the warning “guard against,” rather than as a claim that making embankments destroys the configuration. In the conclusion, 近 refers to what was recent from the compiler’s standpoint, not a modern change in practice. His grouping of Blade/Lu in the branches and Companion/Rob Wealth in the stems is retained together with his statement that Established Lu was not formerly treated as an independent configuration.
