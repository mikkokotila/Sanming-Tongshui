# Sanming Tonghui · Juan One
## A Comprehensive Gathering of the Three Fates

**Compiled by Wan Minying of the Ming dynasty.** From the imperially commissioned *Siku Quanshu*.[^01]

*Reading conventions.* **Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, and Water** name the Five Phases; *qi*, *yin*, *yang*, and *nayin* are retained where an English substitute would narrow the meaning. Stem–branch names are given in unaccented pinyin, with Chinese at their first systematic presentation. Angled brackets **〈 〉** distinguish the source's small-character commentary from the main text. Square brackets mark a translator's supplied clarification, not words present in the Chinese. The numbered endnotes distinguish explanation, textual variation, and conjectural correction.[^02]

## 1. Investigating the Beginnings of Creative Transformation
### 原造化之始

<!-- source: L31-L39 -->
Laozi says: “The nameless is the beginning of Heaven and Earth; the named is the mother of the myriad things.” And: “There is something formed in undifferentiated wholeness, born before Heaven and Earth.” Lie Yukou says: “What has form is born from what has no form. At the beginning of Heaven and Earth there were the Great Change, the Great Beginning, the Great Inception, and the Great Simplicity. The Great Change is that in which qi has not yet appeared; the Great Beginning is the beginning of qi; the Great Inception is the beginning of form; the Great Simplicity is the beginning of material substance. Qi, form, and substance, joined and not yet separated, are called undifferentiated wholeness.” The *Chronicle of the Ages* says: “Before there were Heaven and Earth, primal chaos was like a hen's egg. Within its dark confusion, the first shoots stirred; within its vast obscurity, life began to bud.” The *Treatise on Pitch-Pipes and the Calendar* says: “The Great Ultimate is primordial qi, containing the three as one.” The *Changes* says: “In the Changes there is the Great Ultimate. This generates the Two Modes; the Two Modes generate the Four Images; the Four Images generate the Eight Trigrams; the Eight Trigrams determine good and ill fortune.” The *Subcommentary on the Changes* says: “The Great Ultimate refers to primordial qi, mingled as one before Heaven and Earth were divided.” Master Mengquan says: “The Great Beginning is the beginning of pattern; the Great Void, the beginning of qi; the Great Simplicity, the beginning of images; the Great One, the beginning of number. The Great Ultimate is the beginning that comprises pattern, qi, images, and number together.”[^03]

These several accounts show that before undifferentiated wholeness had been divided, there was only one mingled qi: obscure, dark, and indistinct. Yet pattern was never absent from it. One with the Way, it is called the Great Ultimate. Zhuangzi places the Way before the Great Ultimate. What he calls the Great Ultimate is the condition in which the qi and form of Heaven, Earth, and humanity are already present but not yet differentiated; he then makes the Way something else, suspended in emptiness before it. He does not recognize that the Way is the Great Ultimate and the Great Ultimate is the Way. Spoken of as the unobstructed operation of pattern, it is called the Way; spoken of as pattern carried to its utmost, it is called the Great Ultimate. When were there ever two? Had Master Zhou not disclosed its mystery and Master Zhu unfolded and illuminated it, who would have understood that the Great Ultimate is pattern, intrinsically inseparable from qi?

The Great Ultimate is the fundamental reality of yin and yang, movement and stillness. It is inseparable from form and qi, yet has neither sound nor scent. Its transformations are inexhaustible, yet possess a constant measure. Thus movement and stillness are each the root of the other; yin and yang are distinguished, and the Two Modes are established. “Modes” here means things: no thing has ever lacked its counterpart, and no thing has ever stood alone. Heaven covers by means of qi and depends upon Earth; Earth supports by means of form and adheres to Heaven. Where there is pattern, there is qi: this is what is meant by yin and yang. Where there is qi, there is form: this is what is meant by Heaven and Earth. Heaven and Earth are not born from Heaven and Earth, but from yin and yang. Yin and yang are not born from yin and yang, but from movement and stillness. Movement and stillness are not born from movement and stillness, but from the Great Ultimate. The Great Ultimate is the subtlety of what is so of itself; movement and stillness are the workings through which it proceeds; yin and yang are the roots of what it generates. The Great Ultimate is the Way above form; yin and yang are the vessels within form. Movement and stillness have no first point; yin and yang have no beginning. Through these, creative transformation is established.

<!-- source: L41-L45 -->
〈Master He of Baizhai says: “Heaven is yang in movement. At what moment, precisely, does its movement reach an extreme and become still? Earth is yin in stillness. At what moment does its stillness reach an extreme and begin to move? Heaven cannot generate Earth; Water cannot generate Fire. Wise and foolish alike know this. Is it not a mistake, then, to say that yin and yang generate one another? Heaven and Earth, Water and Fire, are indeed inseparably united, yet they are also distinctly ordered and must not be confused. One may say that yin and yang depend upon one another, but not that they generate one another. One may say that each shelters the other's dwelling, but not that their mutual concealment generates them.” These words certainly show insight.〉[^04]

Before Heaven and Earth were established, the Way was their root. Once they were established, the pattern of the Great Ultimate was dispersed throughout the myriad affairs; from this the Five Phases arose. The Five Phases are one yin and yang: five distinctions, two underlying realities, with nothing left over. Yin and yang are one Great Ultimate: in subtle and coarse, root and branch, there is no separation into this and that. The material substance of the Five Phases is present in Earth; their qi moves through Heaven. In the order in which their substances are generated, they are Water, Fire, Wood, Metal, and Earth: Water and Wood are yang; Fire and Metal are yin. Considered together, qi is yang and substance is yin. Considered across these distinctions, movement is yang and stillness is yin. The transformations of the Five Phases are utterly inexhaustible, yet nowhere do they depart from the inherent nature of the Great Ultimate.[^04]

〈Master He of Baizhai says: “The Five Phases are one yin and yang; yin and yang are one Great Ultimate. Master Zhou certainly holds that the Great Ultimate does not lie outside yin and yang, nor yin and yang outside the Five Phases. But consider the matter now. Water is water; Fire is fire; Metal and Wood are the interacting transformations of Water, Fire, and Earth. Earth is the ground. Where, then, is Heaven? With Earth but no Heaven, can creative transformation be called complete? Suppose one says that Heaven itself is the Great Ultimate, and therefore Master Zhu explains the Great Ultimate by ‘the workings of Heaven above,’ and yin and yang by ‘the circulation of Heaven's Way.’ Yet the Changes says: ‘In the Changes there is the Great Ultimate. This generates the Two Modes; the Two Modes generate the Four Images; the Four Images generate the Eight Trigrams.’ Among the Eight Trigrams are Qian and Kun. It is therefore clear that Heaven and Earth are each partial embodiments of the Great Ultimate. To make Heaven the entire embodiment of the Great Ultimate, and Earth a partial embodiment of Heaven—is this not a very serious error?” His argument seems to have reason on its side.〉[^04]

<!-- source: L47 -->
As the Five Phases come into being, each possesses its own nature; as the four seasons circulate, each has its appointed order. Spring brings birth; summer, growth; autumn, contraction; winter, storage. Spring becomes summer, summer autumn, autumn winter, and winter returns to spring: the succession has no end. The Five Phases differ in substance and the four seasons in qi, yet none lies outside yin and yang. Yin and yang differ in position, movement and stillness in their times, yet none is separate from the Great Ultimate. As for what makes the Great Ultimate what it is, neither sound nor scent can be spoken of: such is the fundamental reality of nature. Each of the Five Phases has its own nature: this is what is meant by “each possesses a Great Ultimate.” The four seasons have their own succession: this is “the operation of one Great Ultimate.” The Five Phases and four seasons complete their circuits and begin again: this is “one Great Ultimate comprising the whole.” Here again we can see that nature is nowhere absent.

There is no thing under Heaven outside nature, and nowhere that nature is not present. This is why the Limitless, the Two, and the Five blend without a gap: the “subtle union.” The Limitless is pattern; the Two and the Five are qi. “True” speaks of pattern—the Great Ultimate's freedom from falsity. “Essence” speaks of qi—the nonduality of yin and yang and the Five Phases. To “congeal” is to gather: qi gathers and takes form. Nature governs; yin and yang and the Five Phases form the interwoven warp and weft, each gathering according to kind and taking shape. What is yang and vigorous becomes male: the Way of the father. What is yin and yielding becomes female: the Way of the mother. This is the first arising of human beings and other creatures through the transformation of qi. Once qi has gathered into form, forms unite and qi responds; thereafter, through the transformation of embodied forms, the myriad beings give birth and transform without end.[^05]

Bao Luzhai says: “Heaven and Earth mingle their qi and give birth to human beings and other creatures. Observe how they mingle and where qi arrives, and you can know whence each kind arises. When Heaven's qi mingles with Earth, it produces men among humans and males among animals. When Earth's qi mingles with Heaven, it produces women among humans and females among animals. Men and women, males and females, then unite in turn, giving birth and transforming without end. Once humans and other beings have arisen, their qi rises, descends, mingles, and responds with the qi of Heaven and Earth. Human beings receive the balanced qi of Heaven and Earth and respond to qi from every quarter. Other creatures receive its partial qi and each responds according to what it receives. Thus, by observing the mingling of Heaven's and Earth's qi, we can understand the first birth of humans and other creatures; by observing its responses, we can understand how they reproduce.”

Master Zhu says: “‘The Way of Qian becomes male; the Way of Kun becomes female’: bodies congeal at the beginning of creative transformation. ‘The two qi interact and respond, transforming and generating the myriad things’: this circulation follows thereafter. Such is the regular operation of pattern. But Jiang Yuan's birth of Ji and Jian Di's birth of Xie cannot be explained simply in terms of before and after: these are exceptional transformations of pattern.” Zhang Jiushao says: “Speaking of the first arising of humans and other beings when Heaven and Earth were first divided, transformation through qi preceded transformation through embodied form. This is what Master Zhang means by ‘the qi of Heaven and Earth gave them birth.’ Speaking of their first arising when an embryo forms and receives its shape, the gathering of essence and qi precedes the existence of the being. This is what Master Zhu means by ‘yin essence and yang qi gather to form things.’” Considered in this way, what human, what creature, what qi, what form could stand outside yin and yang?[^05]

Destiny is received from yin and yang at the beginning of life. It is not something a person can shift at will: it comes about without our making it so, and is not ours to command. Thus some are born to wealth, some to distinction; some to long life, some to an early death; some to poverty, some to low station. Some possess both wealth and rank and stand high above others; some suffer both poverty and low station and fall far below them. Some appear destined for long life yet die untimely; some appear destined for early death yet live to a great age. Is this the result of what they have accumulated through their actions, or of their inborn nature? If it results from accumulation, poverty may become wealth, low station distinction, and a short life a long one: what the ancients called “human effort can overcome Heaven.” If it results from inborn nature, those endowed with wealth and distinction end in wealth and distinction; those endowed with poverty and low station end in poverty and low station; those endowed with long or short life end accordingly: what the ancients called “destiny cannot be altered.”

If we speak of accumulation, we cannot attribute everything to destiny. If we speak of inborn nature, we cannot attribute everything to human action. Shall we entrust everything to accumulated action, when we do not know what destiny has allotted in wealth, rank, length of life, poverty, or low station? Shall we entrust everything to inborn nature, when none of these things can simply be awaited sitting still, and human action appears indispensable?

Someone asks: “Destiny is received at life's beginning—true enough. Why, then, do people born between Heaven and Earth sometimes possess the same Five Phases and Eight Characters, yet differ in wealth and poverty, rank and lowliness, longevity and early death?” The answer is this: when yin and yang interact, one receives the qi of the subtle union of truth and essence; it congeals into an embryo and becomes male or female, receiving in that instant the condition of qi in Heaven, Earth, and the parents. Those who receive its clear quality become intelligent and worthy; those who receive its turbid quality become foolish and unworthy. The intelligent and worthy consequently obtain something—wealth, distinction, or longevity. This is what is meant by virtue sufficient to secure blessings. The foolish and unworthy fail to exert themselves, grow daily more clouded and obscured, and cannot escape poverty, low station, and early death. This is what is meant by the lowest folly, which does not change.[^05]

Those who possess both wealth and distinction originally receive clear, light qi, are born when their endowment commands the season, and also enjoy free-flowing Wealth and Officer influences and flourishing Lu and Horse. Their luck cycles and allotted periods are most auspicious. Even if there is a little obscurity, their endowment is not seriously mixed or compromised. Those who suffer both poverty and low station originally receive heavy, turbid qi, are born out of season, and have punishments, clashes, and confused mixtures without anything harmonious or favorable. Even if calamity does not assail them, obstruction and lack of progress are hard to avoid. Yet there are also those who pass from wealth to poverty or poverty to wealth, from rank to lowliness or lowliness to rank, from the prospect of long life to early death or from the prospect of early death to long life. There are the worthy and intelligent who are poor and lowly, and the foolish and unworthy who are rich and distinguished. Human lives under Heaven differ beyond counting. This too results from the qi of the four seasons and Five Phases: its partiality or balance, gain or loss, approach or withdrawal, depth or shallowness.[^06]

Thus, although one's primordial qi was light and clear, if one was born at a time of decline and defeat and passes through luck cycles of rest and confinement, the rich lose their sources of wealth, the distinguished lose office and position, and those endowed for longevity die before their time. Conversely, although one's primordial qi was heavy and turbid, if one was born under a balanced and harmonious seasonal command and passes through flourishing and supported luck cycles, poverty does not remain poverty but becomes wealth; lowliness becomes distinction; a short-life endowment becomes longevity. Nevertheless, cultivation and conduct belong to the person: settled human effort can overcome Heaven. If destiny receives balance and harmony and one's nature is further enriched by accumulated goodness, how could the blessings end with oneself? It is fitting that children and grandchildren should also prosper and flourish. If destiny is one-sided and withered and one's nature is compounded by accumulated wrongdoing, the harm will not be confined to oneself; children and grandchildren will fall far below others. Is this not recompense? As the preceding discussion shows, although the matter is bound to destiny, it also depends on whether a person accumulates [goodness]. The Changes says: “A family that accumulates goodness is certain to have blessings left over; a family that accumulates what is not good is certain to have calamities left over.” Surely this is what it means.

Master Gengye says: “Heaven is simply one qi. Its transformation generates Water; turbid sediment in the Water accumulates to form Earth. As the Water subsides and Earth emerges, mountains and rivers take shape. The hard part of Earth becomes stone, and Metal arises there; its soft part produces Wood, and Fire arises there. The Five Phases are complete, the myriad things are born, and transformation has no end.”

Master Junchuan says: “At the beginning of Heaven and Earth there were only the two qi, yin and yang. Yang transformed into Fire; yin into Water. The sediment in Water congealed into the ground. Sediment forming the ground is already Earth: why wait until ‘Heaven's five’ before speaking of Earth's generation? Water, Fire, and Earth are the great transformations of Heaven and Earth; Metal and Wood arise from these three. The substance of metals and stones must accumulate over a long time before congealing; their generation must be understood in the same way as that of humans and other beings. Can one say that the qi of Metal generates human beings? Moreover, nothing between Heaven and Earth is other than the work of primordial qi. The nature and kind of each were already complete before the Great Inception. Metal has its own kind; Wood has its own kind; humans their kind; other beings their kinds. Each is complete in itself. They do not borrow from one another or encroach upon one another. Can one then say that the Five Phases successively generate one another?

“The Five-Phase specialists now say that Metal generates Water. Their kinds are wholly unlike, and the supposed principle reverses the proper order. They do not understand that Wood takes Fire for its qi, Water for its nourishment, and Earth for its dwelling: this is the supreme natural Way. But they say that Water generates Wood. Without Earth, where would Wood attach itself? With too much Water, Fire goes out; cut off from Earth, Wood dies. How can it grow? Master Zhou was misled by the Five-Phase specialists into saying that ‘the five qi spread in due order and the four seasons proceed.’ He did not recognize that the sun's advance and retreat produce heat and cold, and the balancing divisions of heat and cold produce the seasons. What has this to do with the distribution of five qi? Their assignments of Wood to spring, Fire to summer, Metal to autumn, and Water to winter are artificial conjunctions. Earth, having nowhere to go, is assigned to the four seasonal endings. They do not understand that Earth's qi is present everywhere within Heaven and Earth, on every day. Why should it circulate only at the end of each season's last month? If it is still present on that month's final day but vanishes on the first day of the next season, where does it go? When it returns, who commands it?

“‘Heaven's one generates Water’ is a saying from the apocryphal writings, yet Confucian scholars import it into the classics. Water and Fire are the subtle substances first generated by yin and yang: the first transformation is Fire, exemplified by the sun; the second is Water, exemplified by rain and dew. Is it acceptable to say ‘Heaven's one generates Water; Earth's two generate Fire’ in contradiction to the inherent subtlety of creative transformation?” His criticism of Master Zhu addresses three explanations of the Five Phases: by the qi circulating through the four seasons; by Heaven's odd and Earth's even numbers; and by the *Diagram of the Great Ultimate*, in which yang transforms, yin joins with it, and Water, Fire, Wood, Metal, and Earth are generated. He also criticizes the assignment of the Five Phases to the seasons: if, as the specialists say, each season is ruled by one phase alone, and spring is only Wood, who annihilates the qi of Water, Fire, Earth, and Metal? If autumn is only Metal, who holds back the qi of Water, Fire, Earth, and Wood? If Earth flourishes only at the four seasonal endings, who restrains its qi in the other months and prevents it from moving? And how can today be Wood, tomorrow Fire, the next day Earth, then Metal, then Water?[^07]

I consider Master Wang's argument reasonable, but not a fully comprehensive view. Luoluzi says: “If you assert that it exists, you establish being out of nonbeing; if you assert that it does not exist, Heaven displays images to reveal its patterns.” Heaven displays the sun and moon, the five planets, the Three Enclosures, and the Twenty-Eight Lunar Lodges. Observing celestial patterns and comprehending their connections, we assign names and terrestrial correspondences: these too are human acts. Yet meanings and images agree. In divining calamities and blessings, indications may refer to a particular kind of event or point toward a particular direction, and their fulfillment in a certain year, month, or day is as exact as matching the left half of a tally. However profound and remote Heaven's Way may be, it does not lie outside human affairs.[^08]

The yin–yang specialists distribute the ten stems and twelve branches among the Five Phases. The sun's return in relation to Heaven makes a year; the moon's conjunction with the sun makes a month. There are thirty days [to the schematic month] and twelve double-hours [to the day]. From the stems and branches of a person's year, month, day, and hour of birth, they establish the Four Pillars and infer the good and ill fortune of an entire life. This too follows the natural operation of pattern. Master Wang asks where Earth is if spring belongs to Wood. He fails to understand that the Five Phases' flourishing, support, death, rest, and confinement concern whether each is timely or untimely, active in command or not. They do not mean that because Wood flourishes in spring, Earth does not exist. The ten stems and twelve branches interweave into the sixty stem–branch combinations, complete their cycle, and begin again without contrived arrangement. Creative transformation is present in this very process. Assigning today to Wood and tomorrow to Fire does not make the sequence contrary to Heaven's natural Way.[^06]

He fails to consider that when humans establish [a correspondence], Heaven follows it; when humans stimulate, Heaven responds. This is precisely the meaning of naming celestial images and assigning their terrestrial fields: the Way in which Heaven and humanity are one. Look at a single day: morning, noon, late afternoon, and night each have their own warmth, coolness, cold, or heat. Metal, Wood, Water, Fire, and Earth are all complete within one day. So inseparable are the Five Phases. Why, then, should saying “today Wood, tomorrow Fire” be anything other than the natural Way of Heaven?

Moreover, the court compiles a calendar and distributes it throughout the realm. Within its three hundred and sixty-five days it records the annual positions of auspicious and harmful influences, each month's celestial movements and flourishing virtues, and, within each day, the black and yellow paths of ill and good fortune, what should be done and what should not. People meet blessings by following it and calamities by opposing it. Could this really be a fabrication without principle, imposed upon everyone under Heaven? Again, the art of reading a person's appearance observes the green-blue, yellow, red, white, and black of the complexion to determine calamity and blessing and their fulfillment in a certain year, month, day, or hour. Green-blue corresponds to Jia and Yi; yellow to Wu and Ji; red to Bing and Ding; white to Geng and Xin; black to Ren and Gui—with not the slightest discrepancy. The examination of illness works likewise, as may be seen in the *Basic Questions*. Thus, although the stems and branches mark the days, creative transformation does not lie outside them.[^05]

Again, a person's spirit and dreams foretell good and ill fortune. Those who interpret them may judge by meaning, by an object's image, by written characters, or by matching sounds. These are all human procedures, yet good and ill fortune cannot escape them. This particular person exists first, and then this particular dream; from the dream one seeks the person to whom it belongs. Even here, creative transformation is not separate. How much more must this be so of the stems, branches, and Five Phases! From the moment Heaven and Earth existed, this pattern existed; because this pattern existed, this person arose. Humanity and Heaven are one. To speak of Heaven apart from humanity, or humanity apart from Heaven, is false.

When Fuxi drew the trigrams, he looked upward and examined what was below, investigated what was distant and drew analogies from what was near. He apprehended the patterns of Heaven, Earth, humans, and other beings, and from this the Eight Trigrams were made. Today's investigators of yin and yang may exhaust the transformations of Heaven and Earth, search the subtleties of people and things, illuminate the past and examine what is to come, infer the hidden from the manifest, “join their virtue with Heaven and Earth, their brightness with sun and moon, their order with the four seasons, and their good and ill fortune with spirits and ghosts.” Yet can they dispense with the stems, branches, and Five Phases and discover some other creative transformation encompassing the vastness of Heaven, Earth, humanity, and the myriad beings? Master Wang knows to revere the Changes but refuses to trust the teachings of the yin–yang specialists. He knows that there is pattern, but not that there is number. Pattern and number are one; Heaven and humanity share one pattern. To make it spiritually effective and clear depends upon the person alone.

## 2. On the Generation and Completion of the Five Phases
### 論五行生成

<!-- source: L49-L51 -->
Heaven is high and boundless: the six qi revolve to form the four seasons. Earth is thick and profound: the Five Phases transform and generate the myriad things. Inexhaustible and unfathomable indeed! Yet the sages established methods of calculation because these processes cannot escape their numbers. Examine the grounds on which those numbers were established, and all arise from what is naturally so. Therefore the classics record them consistently, without divergence. Whether one calculates to reach their workings or investigates to penetrate their transformations, one never leaves the realm of number. “First, Water; second, Fire; third, Wood; fourth, Metal; fifth, Earth”—each designation has its origin.

Water occupies Zi in the north. Zi is the first beginning of yang; one is a yang number, and so Water is called one. Fire occupies Wu in the south. Wu is the first beginning of yin; two is a yin number, and so Fire is called two. Wood occupies the east; the east is yang. Three is odd, also yang, and so Wood is called three. Metal occupies the west; the west is yin. Four is even, also yin, and so Metal is called four. Earth corresponds to the southwest and the long summer; five is odd, also yang, and so Earth is called five. On this account, the numbers are assigned according to yin and yang.

Investigate the deeper meaning, however, and Water is generated at one. Before Heaven and Earth were divided and the myriad things completed, all first appeared in Water. Thus the *Spiritual Pivot* says: “The Great One is Water's exalted name: before Heaven and Earth, their mother; after them, the source of the myriad things.” Test this against present experience: before the seeds and fruits of plants are formed, the fetuses, eggs, and embryos of humans and creatures are all water. Should it not be first? As Water gathers and material form transforms, yin and yang qi must both be present within before completion is possible. Thus a thing that is small and bitter shows the first sign of Fire. When it ripens it becomes sweet, the flavor of Earth. Sweetness reaches its utmost and returns to blandness; blandness is the root.

Human beings receive the generative transformation of their parents' yin and yang. Therefore the two kidneys form first: the left belongs to Water, the right to Fire. This Fire is called the Gate of Life; Fire thus appears after, and because of, Water. Hence Fire is second. Although the seeds and fruits of plants differ in size, they all contain two parts joined together, like the human kidneys—another sign of yin and yang. Nothing can be transformed and born unless yin and yang unite in one body. Once they unite, there can be birth in spring and completion in autumn. Thus Wood is third and Metal fourth. Water needs a place to adhere; Fire, a place to be stored; Wood, a place from which to emerge; Metal, a place from which to be separated. None can be completed without Earth. Therefore Earth is fifth.[^09]

Wood is in the east, Metal in the west, Fire in the south, Water in the north. Earth occupies the center and lodges in the four corners, answering to the final month of each season and, in the human body, to the four limbs. Metal, Wood, Water, and Fire all depend on Earth for completion. Add Earth's five to complete their numbers: Water becomes six, Fire seven, Wood eight, Metal nine. Earth remains at its generating number of five and need not reach ten, for it does not await ten to be complete. Thus the numbers of generation and completion are all joined through five. From this the number of the Great Expansion is established. How could the myriad things escape their numbers?[^10]

Of the three yin and three yang, direct transformation follows the generating number of the root, while counterpart transformation follows the completing number of the manifestation. In the ordering of the five movements, excess uses the completing number and insufficiency the generating number. Each uses the number appropriate to generation or completion, more or less, to divine the governing influences and transformations of qi. These matters were again set forth in writings to make their applications clear.[^10]

Master Zhou says: “In the sequence of the Five Phases, considered as the generation of substance, Water is originally the moist qi of yang. At its first movement it is entrapped by yin and cannot fulfill its course; hence yin predominates in Water. Fire is originally the dry qi of yin. At its first movement it is constrained by yang and cannot extend freely; hence yang predominates in Fire. What generates is slight; what completes is abundant. Generation is the beginning of form; completion is its culmination. Each description, however, concerns a partial predominance. Thus [Water and Fire] have form without yet possessing fixed substance: their qi rises and descends, and [Earth] cannot restrain them. Wood arises as the moist qi of yang gradually increases, responds to yin, and unfolds; issuing outward, it becomes Wood. Its substance is supple and its nature warm. Metal arises as the dry qi of yin gradually increases, responds to yang, and contracts; congealing, it becomes Metal. Its substance is hard and its nature cold. Earth arises when yin and yang qi are both abundant, meet, press together, and congeal into substance.[^11]

“Considered as the movement of qi, one yin and one yang come and go, taking each other's place. Wood, Fire, Metal, Water, and Earth are merely distinctions of youth and age within that movement. Their sequence therefore proceeds from the young to the old; Earth shares in the flourishing of all four seasonal endings and occupies the central position. These five may seem unevenly distributed, yet as the means by which creative transformation produces and nourishes, they proceed together without contradiction. Substance is formed by the intermingling and congealing of yin and yang; qi is the unceasing circulation of their two poles. In speaking of substance, we say Water, Fire, Wood, Metal: yin and yang alternating, as in ‘east, west, south, north’—what is called paired opposition. In speaking of qi, we say Wood, Fire, Metal, Water: yin and yang succeeding one another, as in ‘east, south, west, north’—what is called circulation. Substance, once established, does not alter; qi transforms without end. This is what is meant by Change.”

Master Cheng says: “Movement and stillness are the root of yin and yang. The movements of the Five Phases, however, are staggered and unequal.” Master Zhang says: “Wood is said to bend and straighten because, once bent, it can extend again. Metal is said to yield to alteration because, once altered, it cannot restore itself. Water and Fire are qi; therefore Fire flames upward and Water moistens downward, accompanying yin and yang in their rising and falling, beyond Earth's power to restrain. Wood and Metal are Earth's flower and fruit. Their natures contain a mixture of Water and Fire. Thus Wood is a thing that grows when soaked in Water and burns in Fire without becoming mingled with it: it receives Earth's light, flowering quality at the meeting of Water and Fire. Metal receives Fire's essence in its dryness and Water's essence in its moistening. Water and Fire therefore attend one another without mutual harm; melted, Metal flows again without being consumed. It receives Earth's refined solidity at the boundary of Water and Fire. Earth is that by which things are completed in their beginning and completed in their end. It is the substance of the ground and the culmination of transformation. As for that through which Water and Fire rise and descend, Earth comprehends both within its substance and leaves neither out.” He also says: “Yang entrapped within yin becomes Water; yin attached to yang becomes Fire.”

Master Zhu says: “In the sequence of the Five Phases, Wood is the beginning, Water the end, and Earth the center. In the numbers of the River Chart and Luo Writing, Water is one, Wood three, Earth five. These are all yang generating numbers and cannot be exchanged. They can therefore take turns as chief and form the governing framework of the Five Phases. In terms of their virtues, Wood is the nature of emerging growth; Water, the substance of steadfast stillness; Earth, the mother that embraces and nourishes. Water contains the Five Phases because it flows and penetrates, present everywhere. Wood contains the Five Phases because they return to their roots, revert to their source, and are stored here. Earth, meanwhile, is where Water and Fire lodge and what Metal and Wood depend upon: occupying the center, it settles the four directions; as one body, it bears the myriad kinds.” He also says: “Water and Fire are clear; Metal and Wood are turbid; Earth is more turbid still.”[^12]

Master Shao says: “When Metal and Fire keep company, Metal flows; when Wood and Fire meet, Wood burns: each follows its kind. Water congeals when it encounters cold and dries up when it encounters Fire: it follows that which overcomes it.”

## 3. On the Mutual Generation and Control of the Five Phases
### 論五行生尅

<!-- source: L53-L55 -->
The principle by which the Five Phases generate and control one another is clear. The ten stems, twelve branches, five movements, six qi, and the years, months, days, and hours are all established from this and act through one another. In Heaven they are qi: cold, heat, dryness, dampness, and wind. On Earth they take form: Metal, Wood, Water, Fire, and Earth. Form and qi respond to one another, transforming and generating the myriad things. This is the great ordering of creation and generation. Trace its subtle workings to their source, and they may truly be called inexhaustible.

**Wood** governs the east and answers to spring. “Wood” is explained through “touching”: yang qi stirs into contact, breaks through the ground, and grows. Water flows toward the east and generates Wood. Wood sends its growth upward and casts its cover below: this is its natural substance. **Fire** governs the south and answers to summer. “Fire” is explained through “transforming” and “burning.” Yang is above, yin below; blazing forth in abundance, Fire transforms the myriad things. Drilling Wood produces Fire, for Wood generates it. Yet Fire has no fixed body of its own: its body is rooted in Wood. It emerges to respond to things and, when exhausted, returns within. This is its natural qi.[^13]

**Metal** governs the west and answers to autumn. “Metal” is explained through “restraining”: yin qi begins to check the myriad things and draw them inward. Sift sand and select the Metal: it is generated by Earth. Born within Earth yet distinct from Earth, such is its natural form. **Water** governs the north and answers to winter. “Water” is explained through “moistening”: yin qi moistens and sustains the myriad things. Water travels from west to east; Metal generates it. It flows in winding courses, follows the downward direction, and reaches its destination: this is its natural disposition. **Earth** governs the center, also occupies the southwest, and answers to the long summer. “Earth” is explained through “bringing forth.” It contains and releases the myriad things: what is about to live comes out; what is about to die returns. It is the home of all things. It therefore grows strong at summer's end, generated by Fire. Earth may overcome Water, and Water then returns to Earth: this is the natural meaning of their relation.

In mutual control, every child can avenge its mother. Wood controls Earth; Earth's child, Metal, controls Wood in return. Metal controls Wood; Wood's child, Fire, controls Metal. Fire controls Metal; Metal's child, Water, controls Fire. Water controls Fire; Fire's child, Earth, controls Water. Earth controls Water; Water's child, Wood, controls Earth. Their capacity to generate one another is their beginning; their capacity to control one another is their completion. Both arise from Heaven's nature. As the *Basic Questions* explains: Water generates Wood, and Wood in turn generates Fire; Wood's qi is thereby drawn away, so Water grows angry and controls Fire. A child suffers the draining of its qi, and its mother contends on its behalf. This has the same meaning as a mother being injured by the “ghost” that overcomes her, and her child coming forcefully to the rescue.[^14]

The strong can attack the weak: Earth meets Wood and is penetrated. The solid can overcome the insubstantial: Water meets Earth and is cut off. Yin can dissolve yang: Fire meets Water and is extinguished. The fierce can oppose the hard: Metal meets Fire and is impaired. The firm can restrain the pliant: Wood meets Metal and is felled. Thus the five circulate and turn in succession. Following the sequence, they generate one another; opposing it, they control one another. Each thereby fulfills its use and completes its Way.

## 4. On the Origins and Development of the Branches and Stems
### 論支干源流

<!-- source: L57-L59 -->
The stems are like a tree's trunk: strong, and therefore yang. The branches are like its boughs: weaker, and therefore yin. In antiquity, Pangu understood the Way of Heaven and Earth and penetrated the transformations of yin and yang, becoming the first sovereign of the Three Powers. After Heaven and Earth had separated, Heaven came first and Earth afterward; through the transformation of qi, human beings arose. Thus the Heavenly Sovereigns—thirteen persons of one lineage—succeeded Pangu in ruling. They were called the Celestial Spirits. Serene and uncontriving, they allowed the people's customs to transform of themselves. They first established the names of the stems and branches to determine the station of the year.[^15]

The ten stem names were **Yanfeng 閼逢, Zhanmeng 旃蒙, Rouzhao 柔兆, Qiangyu 彊圉, Zhuyong 著雍, Tuwei 屠維, Shangzhang 上章, Chongguang 重光, Xuanyi 玄黓, and Zhaoyang 昭陽**. The twelve branch names were **Kundun 困敦, Chifenruo 赤奮若, Shetige 攝提格, Chanyan 單閼, Zhixu 執徐, Dahuangluo 大荒落, Dunzang 敦牂, Xieqia 協洽, Tuntan 涒灘, Zuo'e 作噩, Yanmao 閹茂, and Dayuanxian 大淵獻**. Cai Yong's *Independent Judgments* says: “A stem is a trunk. There are ten names, also called the Ten Mothers: today's Jia 甲, Yi 乙, Bing 丙, Ding 丁, Wu 戊, Ji 己, Geng 庚, Xin 辛, Ren 壬, and Gui 癸. A branch is a bough. There are twelve names, also called the Twelve Children: today's Zi 子, Chou 丑, Yin 寅, Mao 卯, Chen 辰, Si 巳, Wu 午, Wei 未, Shen 申, You 酉, Xu 戌, and Hai 亥.”[^16]

The name “Heavenly Sovereigns” draws on the opening of Heaven at Zi; “Earthly Sovereigns,” on the opening of Earth at Chou; “Human Sovereigns,” on the birth of humanity at Yin. Thus the stem and branch names were first established in the time of the Heavenly Sovereigns. The Earthly Sovereigns fixed the three celestial luminaries, distinguished day from night along their courses, and made thirty days a month; then the stems and branches first acquired their paired assignments. Under the Human Sovereigns, rulers did not reign in name alone, nor ministers enjoy empty distinction. Government and instruction, sovereign and minister, took their beginning; the ordering of food and drink, men and women, began. Through the qi of Heaven and Earth's yin and yang, the distinction of mother and child was established, and the stems and branches first acquired their affiliations.

Fuxi looked upward at the images in Heaven, downward at the patterns on Earth, and between them at humanity and the myriad things. He first drew the Eight Trigrams to communicate the potency of the numinous and classify the dispositions of things. He made the stem-cycle calendar, and writing arose. When the Yellow Emperor received the River Chart, he saw the images of the sun, moon, stars, and celestial stations; then writings on the celestial offices first appeared. He commanded Danao to investigate the dispositions of the Five Phases and divine the direction established by the Dipper's guiding handle. Danao then first constructed the sixty stem–branch combinations and assigned their Five-Phase and nayin affiliations.

The *History of the Great* says: “Fuxi commanded the Hidden Dragon clan to divine with the stalks. They faced the sun and calculated with the counting stalks, determining hard and soft and establishing the Jiazi cycle to name the years and seasons. What was matched with Heaven became stems; what was matched with Earth became branches. Branches and stems were paired according to kind to provide the governing framework of the Four Images. Thus the genuine and the artificial responded to one another, and the stars followed their proper order.” By the Yellow Emperor's time, Danao was commanded to investigate the Five Phases and examine the Three Methods of the celestial writings. Expanding the ten stems and twelve branches into sixty, he took their incorporated sounds and fixed them as nayin: such as Jiazi and Yichou, Metal Within the Sea. Fenghou explained these to bring them into use, and the Three Fates were put into practice. Those specialists who claim that the Yellow Emperor assigned the ten heavenly-stem characters to the River Chart and the twelve earthly branches to the Luo Writing, that Guiguzi calculated nayin, and that Dongfang Shuo explained its images, have failed to find the source and speak without foundation.[^15]

Master Wang of Junchuan says: “When Danao made the Jiazi cycle, the possible names and numbers had no limit. He settled on these incidentally, for the convenience of marking time. If we insist on tracing them to an absolute origin, only the day when sun and moon first began to turn could properly be called Jiazi. When Heaven opened, Earth did not yet exist; how could there have been people? With no people, who could transmit a record of it? Do they claim to know it from the constancy of the twelve celestial stations? The movement of Heaven and Earth is like a ring without an end. When its motion completes one great cycle, does the turning millstone not begin again? A day completes twelve double-hours; does Heaven's movement not return to Zi? Before one great cycle, how do we know there was not another? Moreover, the degrees assigned to a calendrical epoch and the changing positions of the Ox and Dipper lodges have shifted greatly through precession. Later calendars each choose a basis to fit the current season. The methods of ancient calendars have disappeared with their ages. How can calculation recover an exact agreement? Those who now speak of Jia and Yi insist that an actual Wood qi governs them—today Wood, tomorrow Fire, the next day Earth. Is this not false?

“Someone may say that Danao observed the Dipper's handle when constructing Jiazi and must therefore have traced matters back to the beginning of Heaven and Earth, establishing a Jiazi year, Jiazi month, Jiazi day, and Jiazi hour as the calendrical epoch. They do not consider that observing the handle can determine only the twelve months. They say that at the first opening of Heaven and Earth, sun and moon were joined like paired jade discs and the five planets aligned like strung pearls, all beginning at the first point of the Ox lodge; from this they establish a winter solstice at midnight. But this is merely a fixed computational rule. Hence Mencius says that the solstices of a thousand years can be determined while sitting still. Examine the matter with present calendars: on their account Heaven and Earth would have opened several times already. Is this the beginning of Heaven and Earth, or merely the beginning of a revolution of sun and moon? Before writing arose, there was no record by which to examine Heaven's movements. How could anyone calculate them all?”

I consider that Master Wang's account does indeed show insight. Yet accomplished people of high understanding, ancient and modern, have investigated celestial numbers and examined yin and yang: by the numbers of Taiyi they infer the good and ill fortune of Heaven's movements; by Liuren, that of human affairs; by Qimen, that of terrestrial positions; and by the year, month, day, and hour, that of a person's entire life. Tiangang, Chunfeng, Yixing, Xuzhong, and others all achieved remarkable accuracy. How is this to be explained? Were Master Wang's account correct, none of these would deserve belief. Is that so? Can that really be so?[^17]

## 5. On the Meanings of the Ten Stem Names
### 論十干名字之義

<!-- source: L61-L63 -->
Heaven's qi begins with the stem Jia; Earth's qi begins with the branch Zi. This arises from the sages' investigation of the heavy and light workings of yin and yang. They assigned names to reveal each one's potency and established designations to express its activity. Zi and Jia were then joined to form a cycle. At the greater scale, one can calculate the years and comprehend a span of sixty; at the nearer scale, one can calculate the days and distinguish the twelve double-hours. The fullness and emptiness of the year's movements, the early or late arrival of seasonal qi, the birth and death of all things—by testing antiquity against the present, these may be understood. Nor is this all. Examine their finer details to know blessings and calamities before they bud; investigate their operation to discern the life and death toward which things move. How profound is the meaning contained in these subtle distinctions![^18]

Thus Jia and Yi belong to the east, Bing and Ding to the south, Geng and Xin to the west, Ren and Gui to the north, and Wu and Ji to the center: these are the positions of the Five Phases. **Jia 甲 and Yi 乙 occupy Wood**, receiving spring's command. In Jia, yang is within and yin still encloses it: grasses and trees first break through their armor and emerge. In Yi, yang has passed the middle but has not yet attained a fully upright direction; it still bends and curls. Another explanation says: “Yi means pressing through: all things loosen their husks and armor and push themselves outward.”[^13]

**Bing 丙 and Ding 丁 occupy Fire**, carrying out summer's command. In Bing, yang is above and yin below, yin within and yang without. At Ding, yang is in its strength and can stand directly against yin qi. Another explanation says: “Bing means bright: all things become brilliantly manifest, strong, and great.” **Wu 戊 and Ji 己 occupy Earth**, whose operation extends through the four seasonal endings. Wu is yang Earth: when things are born, they emerge from it; when cut down, they enter it. Ji is yin Earth: without contrived action, things reach their proper cessation. Another explanation says: “Wu means luxuriance; Ji means rising.” Earth operates at each season's end; all that holds flowering promise rises from a state of compression and bending.

**Geng 庚 and Xin 辛 occupy Metal**, carrying out autumn's command. In Geng, yin encroaches upon yang, replacing it and continuing the sequence. In Xin, yang is below and yin above: yin's encroachment upon yang reaches its utmost here. Geng means changing what is old; Xin means making new. Both belong to Metal. Metal's flavor is pungent, and things acquire flavor after reaching maturity. Another explanation says: “All things undergo a solemn transformation; blossom and fruit are newly completed.” **Ren 壬 and Gui 癸 occupy Water**, carrying out winter's command. Ren is explained through bearing a burden. It is the position where yang is born and conception occurs: all things are pregnant at Ren, sharing the meaning of Zi, the child. Gui means measuring. When Heaven's command reaches this point, all things close and store themselves, pregnant beneath the surface, measuring out the first stirring of their shoots. This is Heaven's Way; these names are therefore used to name the days. Thus the classic says: “Heaven has ten days; six completions of the days make one circuit of the Jia cycle.” In the numbers of Heaven and Earth, Jia, Bing, Wu, Geng, and Ren are yang; Yi, Ding, Ji, Xin, and Gui are yin. Each of the Five Phases has one yin and one yang; hence there are ten day-stems.

## 6. On the Meanings of the Twelve Branch Names
### 論十二支名字之義

<!-- source: L65-L69 -->
Clear yang becomes Heaven: the Five Phases manifest and the ten stems are established. Turbid yin becomes Earth: the eight directions are fixed and the twelve branches differentiated. As the movements shift and qi changes, each year's fullness and emptiness answer to their appointed cycle. As qi rises and descends, the transformations of every being can be anticipated. Thus the branches and stems, paired together, attain their subtle efficacy.

**Zi 子** is the northern position of extreme yin and cold Water, yet also the beginning of the first yang. When yin reaches its utmost, yang is born. What was conceived at Ren becomes the child at Zi. This is the station of the eleventh month. At **Chou 丑**, yin still holds fast and binds. Chou is also explained as yin and as assistance: at the junction of ending and beginning in the twelfth month, its name signifies a binding knot. **Yin 寅** is the first month. Yang is already above and yin below: people first perceive it. Therefore they observe the flying ashes in the pitch-pipes as a seasonal signal, marking the beginning of activity. Yin also means unfolding and a ford: the passage by which things proceed.[^19]

**Mao 卯** is the time of sunrise. Mao also means luxuriance: in the second month yang qi is abundant, and things multiply and flourish. At **Chen 辰**, yang has passed the midpoint. In the third month, all things stir and grow; Chen is also explained through “stirring.” **Si 巳** is the fourth month, pure yang without yin. From Zi to Si lie the yang positions, and here yang reaches its limit. Si also means rising: all things rise to their full extent. At **Wu 午**, yang has not yet bent back, but yin begins to arise and take command. Wu is also explained as growing and becoming great: by the fifth month all things are full, abundant, and grown.

**Wei 未** is the sixth month, when Wood has formed its seed and reached completion. Wei also means flavor: things become flavorful when complete, sharing the meaning of Xin. **Shen 申** is the seventh month's station: it extends what yang has done and brings it to an end. When yin reaches Shen, communication opens between above and below and people first perceive it. White dew and falling leaves are its signs; these allow yin's work to be described and brought to completion. Shen also means body: the bodies of things are now fully formed. **You 酉** is the time of sunset, the midpoint of [yin], the eighth month. You is also explained through tightening: all things draw together, contract, and gather inward.[^20]

In the ninth month, **Xu 戌**, yang is not entirely spent, but can no longer act; it hides within Xu. Xu contains the position of Qian and is therefore the Gate of Heaven. Xu also means extinction: all things decline and perish. The tenth month, **Hai 亥**, is pure yin. Hai also means prosecution: yin qi prosecutes and kills the myriad things. This is Earth's Way, and these names are therefore used for the months.

The stems beginning with Jia are Heaven's Five Phases, expressed as one yin and one yang. The branches beginning with Zi are Earth's Five Phases, expressed through its directions and corners. Thus Zi, Yin, Wu, and Shen are yang; Mao, Si, You, and Hai are yin. Earth occupies the four corners and rules at the end of each season. Of its four branches, Chen and Xu are yang, Chou and Wei yin. Hence the numbers of stems and branches differ. Taken together, ten are paired with twelve to produce sixty days. Repeating the six [decades] six times makes a year. Thus the classic says: “Heaven uses the division of six times six to complete one year.”[^21]

Chen Tuan says: “The heavenly stems begin with Jia and end with Gui: these are the River Chart's numbers of generation and completion. The earthly branches begin with Zi and end with Hai: these are the Luo Writing's odd and even numbers.” Yang begins with the hexagram Return; through six changes, Qian's yang becomes complete. Yin begins with Coming to Meet; through six changes, Kun's yin becomes complete. Combining the two sixes gives the twelve stations. Jia, Bing, Wu, Geng, and Ren are yang stems; Zi, Yin, Chen, Wu, Shen, and Xu are yang branches. Yi, Ding, Ji, Xin, and Gui are yin stems; Chou, Mao, Si, Wei, You, and Hai are yin branches. The method pairs yang stems with yang branches and yin stems with yin branches, as a tree has trunk and boughs. Beginning with Jiazi, arrange the six Jia and five Zi in succession, ending with Guihai.

Then use the stems' and branches' original numbers to calculate their completed totals. Add together the completed numbers of the relevant stems and branches, divide by five, and reduce whatever remainder is left to generate the sounds of the Five Phases. This is the nayin of the Six Jia. The sages extended it into practice, setting sixty fine directional divisions within the twenty-four positions. They made the ordinary Five Phases the governing framework of each sector and the Great Five Phases of the Six Jia its interweaving threads. They examined the qi of gestation, nurturing, decline, and death within the directional divisions, and determined the trigrams of isolation, emptiness, flourishing, and support. Within this system, Wu and Ji are the Tortoiseshell Void; Jia and Yi are the voids to be bridged and joined. Through these, yin and yang wax and wane. Whether establishing a burial, receiving qi, determining destiny, or using nayin, all take this as their basis.[^22]

## 7. A General Discussion of Nayin
### 總論納音

<!-- source: L71-L75 -->
I have examined the *Brush Talks* on the nayin of the sixty stem–branch combinations. It is based on the method by which the sixty pitch configurations rotate, each becoming the *gong* or governing tone. A single pitch-standard contains the five notes. Qi begins in the east and moves to the right; sound arises in the west and moves to the left. Yin and yang interweave and produce transformation. “Qi begins in the east” means that the four seasons begin with Wood and move rightward to Fire, then from Fire to Earth, Earth to Metal, and Metal to Water. “Sound begins in the west” means that the five notes begin with Metal and rotate leftward to Fire, from Fire to Wood, Wood to Water, and Water to Earth.[^23]

〈Nayin uses the same method as the Changes' incorporation of the stems: Qian incorporates Jia and Kun incorporates Gui, beginning with Qian and ending with Kun. Nayin begins with Metal—Metal is Qian—and ends with Earth—Earth is Kun. Of the Five Phases, only Metal, when cast into an instrument, makes its sound conspicuous. Therefore nayin puts Metal first. The *Comprehensive Discussions in the White Tiger Hall* says: “The bell is the sound of Dui.”〉

<!-- source: L77 -->
The rule of nayin is: “Marry a wife of the same kind; across eight positions, beget a child.” 〈These are words from the *Treatise of the Han History*.〉 It is the method by which the yang and yin pitch-pipes generate one another. Jiazi, Metal's middle position, marries Yichou in the corresponding position; across eight, it generates downward to Renshen, Metal's first position. Renshen marries Guiyou in the corresponding position; across eight, it generates upward to Gengchen, Metal's last position. Gengchen marries Xinsi in the corresponding position; across eight, it generates downward to Wuzi, Fire's middle position. Wuzi marries Jichou and generates Bingshen, Fire's first position. Bingshen marries Dingyou and generates Jiachen, Fire's last position. Jiachen marries Yisi and generates Renzi, Wood's middle position. Continuing leftward in this way, one arrives at Dingsi, the gong of Zhonglü, and the five notes complete a circuit. One then starts again from Jiawu, Metal's middle position, which marries Yiwei and across eight generates Renyin. The same method as for Jiazi continues until Guihai.[^23]

From Zi to Si is yang; therefore from Huangzhong to Zhonglü all generation is downward. From Wu to Hai is yin; therefore from Linzhong to Yingzhong all generation is upward. Upward and downward generation refers precisely to Heaven's qi descending and Earth's qi ascending. The Changes says: “Heaven and Earth communicate in Peace.” Its meaning is evident here. That each sequence generates only three positions also expresses the Three Origins. Thus the classic says: “Three complete Heaven, three complete Earth, three complete humanity.” The images of the Changes' lines take three as their basis. Laozi says: “One generates two; two generate three; three generate the myriad things.” There is a beginning, a middle, and an end: the whole is complete.

I have also examined the *History of the Great*, which says: “Jia and Yi are Wood; Chou and Wei are Earth; Zi is Water and Wu Fire. Of these six, not one is Metal. Yet when Fenghou combined them, he called Jiazi, Yichou, Jiawu, and Yiwei Metal. This arises from number.” What numbers join is where transformation emerges. Qian is Heaven and Kun Earth; together they make the hexagram Peace. *De* 德 serves as father and *hong* 紅 as mother; together they produce the sound *dong* 東. The stem is sovereign and the branch minister; their union produces nayin. Thus Jia and Yi are sovereigns, Zi and Chou ministers: Zi, Chou, Jia, and Yi together become Metal. The Five Phases throughout the world each possess their own qi and disposition, material capacities and positions. They may assist or control one another; they may already be fashioned into useful vessels or not yet fashioned; within flourishing they may encounter extinction, and within extinction receive qi. The result differs according to the particular pairing. Hence the numbers of Metal cannot all be treated alike, and there is a distinction between Metal Within the Sea and Metal Within Sand.[^24]

Another explanation says: “Jia and Yi are considered through the relation of control. Jia marries Geng, Yi marries Xin, and Jia and Yi thereby acquire Metal qi. Thus all Wood must receive a Metal gestation. Yang is born at Zi, where Water flourishes; therefore Jiazi and Yichou are the yang Metal within the sea. Yin is born at Wu, where Fire flourishes; therefore Jiawu and Yiwei are the yin Metal within sand. Zi begins yang; Wu begins yin. Place Jia upon Zi and Yi upon Chou, and count: on reaching Wu one obtains Geng; on reaching Wei, Xin. This is yang seeking yin. Place Jia upon Wu and Yi upon Wei, and count to Zi and Chou: again one obtains Geng and Xin. This is yin matching yang.” This too is a method of rotating the governing tone. Husband and wife, child and mother, assist and control one another, rise and descend in relation to one another; thus the signs of good and ill fortune become manifest. Among herbs are *shen* and *lei*: eaten separately they kill; eaten together they confer long life. Metal and tin are both soft; alloyed and refined together, they become hard. Such is indeed the operation of pattern.[^25]

The *Discourse on the Subtle Purport of the Six* says: “For nayin, at Zi and Wu count to Geng; at Chou and Wei, to Xin; at Yin and Shen, to Wu; at Mao and You, to Ji; at Chen and Xu, to Bing; at Si and Hai, to Ding. A count of seven is the qi of the White Sovereign in the west: the nayin is Metal. Three is the qi of the Vermilion Heaven in the south: Fire. Nine is the qi of Yang Nine in the east: Wood. One is the qi of the central ruler that comprehends all: Earth. Five is the qi of the Dark Ultimate in the north: Water.”[^26]

For example, in Jiazi and Jiawu count from Jia to Geng; in Yichou and Yiwei, from Yi to Xin. Each count is seven, so their nayin is Metal. In Bingyin and Bingshen count from Bing to Wu; in Dingmao and Dingyou, from Ding to Ji. Each is three, so their nayin is Fire. In Wuchen and Wuxu count from Wu round to Bing; in Jisi and Jihai, from Ji round to Ding. Each is nine, so their nayin is Wood. In Gengzi, Gengwu, Xinwei, and Xinchou, the count is one, so their nayin is Earth. In Bingzi and Bingwu count from Bing to Geng; in Dingwei and Dingchou, from Ding to Xin. Each is five, so their nayin is Water. This incorporates the numbers of the stems, which is why only the stems are counted, not the branches. From Bing to Geng means Bing, Ding, Wu, Ji, Geng: five. From Jia to Geng means Jia, Yi, Bing, Ding, Wu, Ji, Geng: seven. This is the meaning of the *History of the Great*'s account.

The *Leisure Records of the Hall of Auspicious Cassia* explains the nayin of the sixty combinations through the sounds of Metal, Wood, Water, Fire, and Earth. One and six are Water; two and seven Fire; three and eight Wood; four and nine Metal; five and ten Earth. Of the Five Phases, however, only Metal and Wood possess a sound of their own. Water, Fire, and Earth must borrow from another before producing sound. Water borrows from Earth, Fire from Water, and Earth from Fire. Thus Metal's sound is four and nine; Wood's, three and eight; Water's, five and ten; Fire's, one and six; Earth's, two and seven. Jiazi and Yichou total thirty-four: four is Metal's sound, so they are called Metal. Wuchen and Jisi total twenty-eight: eight is Wood's sound, so they are Wood. Gengwu and Xinwei total thirty-two: two is Fire, and Earth takes Fire for its sound, so they are Earth. Jiashen and Yiyou total thirty: ten is Earth, and Water takes Earth for its sound, so they are Water. Wuzi and Jichou total thirty-one: one is Water, and Fire takes Water for its sound, so they are Fire. All sixty combinations follow this rule. Strike two pieces of Metal or Wood together and they sound naturally. Water must be struck by Earth and Fire stirred by Water: sound arises through their overcoming one another. Earth must be fired into a vessel before it sounds. This too is a natural principle.[^27]

Another explanation says that the nayin of all sixty combinations arises from the five notes in an ordered sequence, as regular as pearls on a string. Jiazi comes first, and the five notes begin with gong. Gong is Earth, which generates Metal: therefore Jiazi is Metal, with Yichou following it as yin follows yang. Shang is Metal and generates Water: therefore Bingzi is Water, and Dingchou follows. Jue is Wood and generates Fire: therefore Wuzi is Fire. Zhi is Fire and generates Earth: therefore Gengzi is Earth. Yu is Water and generates Wood: therefore Renzi is Wood. Jichou, Xinchou, and Guichou follow their respective partners.

At Jiayin, nayin begins with shang. Shang-Metal generates Water, so Jiayin is Water. Jue-Wood generates Fire, so Bingyin is Fire. Zhi-Fire generates Earth, so Wuyin is Earth. Yu-Water generates Wood, so Gengyin is Wood. Gong-Earth generates Metal, so Renyin is Metal. The five Mao combinations each follow their partners. At Jiachen, nayin begins with jue. Jue-Wood generates Fire, so Jiachen is Fire. Zhi-Fire generates Earth, so Bingchen is Earth. Yu-Water generates Wood, so Wuchen is Wood. Gong-Earth generates Metal, so Gengchen is Metal. Shang-Metal generates Water, so Renchen is Water. The five Si combinations each follow their partners. Gong, shang, and jue may all begin a sequence; zhi and yu alone do not occupy the first place. Jiawu then follows the model of Jiazi; Jiashen that of Jiayin; Jiaxu that of Jiachen. The five Wei, five You, and five Hai combinations likewise follow their respective kinds.

Another explanation begins from the Great Expansion's fifty, of which forty-nine are used. Set out forty-nine counters, then use the Great Mystery numbers: **Jia, Ji, Zi, Wu = nine; Yi, Geng, Chou, Wei = eight; Bing, Xin, Yin, Shen = seven; Ding, Ren, Mao, You = six; Wu, Gui, Chen, Xu = five; Si and Hai alone = four.** Divide by five and use what remains as the nayin number: Water one, Fire two, Wood three, Metal four, Earth five. Then take the phase generated by that remainder. A remainder of one is Water, which generates Wood; two is Fire, which generates Earth; three is Wood, which generates Fire; four is Metal, which generates Water; five is Earth, which generates Metal.[^28]

For example, the four stem and branch positions Jiazi and Yichou total thirty-four. Subtracting these leaves fifteen. Remove two fives, or ten, and five remains: five is Earth, and Earth generates Metal. Hence Jiazi and Yichou are Metal. The four positions Bingyin and Dingmao total twenty-six. Subtracting these leaves twenty-three. Remove four fives, or twenty, and three remains: three is Wood, and Wood generates Fire. Hence Bingyin and Dingmao are Fire. The rest follow the same method.

Broadly speaking, the sixty combinations are the calendar; nayin is the pitch-system. Such is the distinction between stems and branches and their nayin. These are the natural numbers of Heaven and Earth. The River Chart gives generating numbers. Generation turns left: central Earth generates western Metal; western Metal generates northern Water; northern Water generates eastern Wood; eastern Wood generates southern Fire; southern Fire again generates central Earth. The Luo Writing gives controlling numbers. Control turns right: central Earth controls the Water of north and northwest; the Water of north and northwest controls the Fire of west and southwest; the Fire of west and southwest controls the Metal of south and southeast; the Metal of south and southeast controls the Wood of east and northeast; the Wood of east and northeast again controls central Earth. Nothing, however minute, lies beyond their reach. This is nayin's subtlety. It most certainly comes from the Yellow Emperor: of that there is no doubt.[^29]

Consider further why the Great Mystery numbers assign nine to Jia, Ji, Zi, and Wu. The myriad things are rooted in Heaven and Earth and move through the four seasons. In spring they grow in Gen; in autumn they wither in Kun. Emergence and return to storage never depart from Earth, and Earth is Kun and Gen. The Changes says: “Gen is the beginning of the generation of things; Kun is the end of their completion.” Jia heads the heavenly stems and Zi the earthly branches. These two principles circulate, and the first yang returns: thus Jiazi begins from the numbers of Heaven and Earth.

Zi is the first yang and belongs to Qian, the father's Way; Jia and Ren are assigned to it. Place Jia at Zi and count to Shen, where Ren appears: the count is nine. This is Qian's primal “use of nine.” Husband and wife are paired, so both Jia and Ji receive nine. Place Yi at Chou and count to Shen: eight; therefore Yi and Geng both receive eight. Place Bing at Yin and count to Shen: seven; therefore Bing and Xin both receive seven. Place Ding at Mao and count to Shen: six; therefore Ding and Ren both receive six. Place Wu at Chen and count to Shen: five; therefore Wu and Gui both receive five. In this way the heavenly stems begin from Qian.

Wu is the first yin and belongs to Kun, the minister's Way; Yi and Gui are assigned to it. Place Yi at Wu and count to Yin, where Gui appears: nine. Zi is the first yang and Wu the first yin, the Way of husband and wife; therefore both Zi and Wu receive nine. Place Chou at Yin and count to Wei: eight; therefore Chou and Wei both receive eight. Place Yin at [Shen] and count to Yin: seven; therefore Yin and Shen both receive seven. Place Mao at You and count to Yin: six; therefore Mao and You both receive six. Place Chen at Xu and count to Yin: five; therefore Chen and Xu both receive five. Place Si at Hai and count to Yin: four; therefore Si and Hai both receive four. The numbers stop at nine and do not speak of ten because ten starts the counting at one again; hence ten is called “one ten.” The Luo Writing's numbers begin at one and end at nine.[^30]

## 8. On the Images Chosen for Nayin
### 論納音取象

<!-- source: L79-L81 -->
In antiquity, the Yellow Emperor distinguished the light and heavy qualities of the stem–branch combinations and paired them into sixty, calling them the “Flowering Jiazi Cycle.” The word “flowering” is truly subtle. The sages borrowed meanings to convey an analogy; one must not cling rigidly to their literal images. Each of the twelve palaces from Zi to Hai has its affiliations with Metal, Wood, Water, Fire, and Earth. They begin with the first yang at Zi and end with the six yin at Hai. These Five Phases are the five planets in Heaven, the Five Sacred Peaks on Earth, the Five Constant Virtues in moral life, the five viscera in the human body, and the Five Phases within destiny. The stem–branch combinations thus answer to destiny, and destiny concerns the affairs of an entire life. In the nayin images, therefore, the sages used analogies like the events of a human lifetime.[^32]

How is this so? At **Zi and Chou**, yin and yang first conceive. The person is in the womb; other beings lie concealed in their roots. No boundaries have yet appeared. At **Yin and Mao**, yin and yang gradually open. The person grows; other beings split their husks and unfold their blossoms, like someone about to establish a place in the world. At **Chen and Si**, yin and yang qi is abundant and things flower in splendor, like a person of thirty or forty who has found a footing and begins to advance. At **Wu and Wei**, yin and yang are fully revealed and things have reached completion; a person reaches fifty or sixty, and wealth or poverty, rank or lowliness can be known, the rise and fall of a hundred affairs seen. At **Shen and You**, yin and yang become austere and cutting; things have been harvested, and the person draws inward like a tortoise. Each finds its stillness. At **Xu and Hai**, yin and yang close; the qi of things returns to their roots, and the person should rest. Each finds its place of return. Examine this succession of twelve positions, and the sixty combinations can be understood in order.

Why do **Jiazi and Yichou** take the image **Metal Within the Sea**? Their qi is enclosed and hidden: they have a name but no manifest form, like a person in the mother's womb. At **Renyin and Guimao**, Metal survives in the place of Extinction. Its qi is still soft and weak, thin as fine silk: hence **Gold Leaf Metal**. At **Gengchen and Xinsi**, Metal occupies the territory of Fire and Earth. Its qi has begun to emerge, but the Metal remains in the ore, lodging its form in the place of nurturing and birth and receiving the west's proper color: hence **White Pewter Metal**. At **Jiawu and Yiwei**, qi has matured and substance is naturally firm. Mingled with sand yet distinct from sand, lodged in Fire and refined by Fire, it is **Metal Within Sand**. At **Renshen and Guiyou**, qi is abundant and substance has reached its utmost; it is ready to exercise its gathering and cutting function, emerging as a keen-edged blade. Shen and You are Metal's proper positions; with Ren and Gui as stems, Metal and Water temper and whet it. Thus the image is **Sword-Edge Metal**, and Metal's function reaches its height. At **Xu and Hai**, Metal qi hides, and its bodily strength is spent. Forged into ornaments, its shape is complete; stored in the women's chambers, it has no further work to perform. Metal's function is finished: hence **Gengxu and Xinhai, Hairpin-and-Bracelet Metal**.[^33]

Why do **Renzi and Guichou** take the image **Mulberry Wood**? Their qi is curled and bent, their forms not yet extended. They occupy Water's territory in the months when silkworm activity has declined; mulberry and silkworm-thorn receive qi, and the image is taken from their seasonal generation. At **Gengyin and Xinmao**, qi already rides upon yang and gains the power of cultivation and growth. Yet its form is beneath Metal. Metal and white frost are inherently hard; Wood beneath them gains its vigor and is last to wither in the year's cold. The image takes its steadfast nature: **Pine-and-Cypress Wood**. At **Wuchen and Jisi**, qi is beyond measure, the season is right, branches and leaves are abundant, and a luxuriant forest forms. The image takes Wood's fullness: **Great Forest Wood**. At **Renwu and Guiwei**, Wood reaches Death at Wu and Tomb at Wei. Thus in high summer the willow's leaves wither, and its branches and trunk are weak. The image takes its pliancy: **Willow Wood**. At **Gengshen and Xinyou**, the ordinary phase is Metal but the nayin is Wood: the image is taken through mutual control. Among woods, the pomegranate alone has a pungent nature. At Shen and You, qi returns to stillness and severity; things gradually form their fruit. Wood occupying Metal's territory takes a pungent flavor: hence **Pomegranate Wood**. Other woods die at Wu, yet this Wood flourishes there: the image takes its exceptional nature. At **Wuxu and Jihai**, qi returns to concealment; yin and yang close. Wood's qi returns to the roots and lies hidden in Earth: hence **Level-Ground Wood**.[^34]

Why do **Bingzi and Dingchou** take the image **Streambed Water**? Their qi has not yet opened into free passage. High ground is no place for Water's flow; low, damp places are where it goes. Moving within the ground, it is called Streambed Water. At **Jiayin and Yimao**, qi emerges into yang brightness. Water's force depends on its source; pouring eastward, its momentum gradually increases: hence **Great Stream Water**. At **Renchen and Guisi**, its force reaches the southeast and its qi approaches Li's palace. Fire is bright and strong, while Water returns to its storehouse, filling each hollow before advancing: hence **Long-Flowing Water**. At **Bingwu and Dingwei**, qi is at the point of rising and descending, in the high, luminous position of Fire. Water pours down in abundant rain. Water that can bring relief amid Fire exists only in Heaven: hence **Celestial River Water**. At **Jiashen and Yiyou**, qi rests in quietness. Mother and child occupy the same position; Water emerges without end and can be drawn without exhaustion: hence **Well-and-Spring Water**. At **Renxu and Guihai**, the Gate of Heaven, qi returns to closure. Water has passed everywhere and no longer hurries; its force settles into tranquility. What comes is inexhaustible, yet what receives it never overflows: hence **Great Sea Water**.[^35]

Why do **Wuzi and Jichou** take the image **Thunderbolt Fire**? Qi stands at the first yang, while form occupies Water's position. Fire within Water could belong to none but the divine dragon: hence Thunderbolt Fire. At **Bingyin and Dingmao**, qi gradually shines forth and becomes manifest through fuel. Yin and yang are the smelter, Heaven and Earth the furnace: hence **Furnace Fire**. At **Jiachen and Yisi**, qi and form occupy flourishing ground, their force established on a high ridge. Brightness is transmitted and darkness succeeded; mother and child continue one another: hence **Lamp Fire**. At **Wuwu and Jiwei**, qi passes through yang's palace, and doubled Li meets itself. Numinous brightness mingles its light, radiating and flaming upward: hence **Fire in Heaven**. At **Bingshen and Dingyou**, qi rests and form conceals itself. Power veils its brilliance and withdraws like a tortoise into Dui's position; force is slight and substance weak, and the light does not reach far: hence **Fire Below the Mountain**. **Jiaxu and Yihai** are called **Fire on the Mountain** because the mountain conceals the form while its summit casts the light. Bright within and dark without, hidden rather than displayed, its flying radiance reaches Qian and returns to rest: hence Fire on the Mountain.[^36]

Why do **Gengzi and Xinchou** take the image **Wall-Plaster Earth**? Qi is closed and things are still concealed; form is covered and substance screened, with no exchange between inside and outside. Hence Wall-Plaster Earth. At **Wuyin and Jimao**, qi can complete things and its work nourishes them: issuing from the roots, strengthening calyx and blossom, it is **City-Wall Earth**. At **Bingchen and Dingsi**, qi receives yang. Emergence has passed, but full completion is still to come: hence **Sandy Earth**. At **Gengwu and Xinwei**, qi is receiving form and things become manifest along the road. There is form to give substance and a thing to display: hence **Roadside Earth**. At **Wushen and Jiyou**, qi returns to rest and things should gather inward, withdrawing like a tortoise into leisure: beautiful, but no longer busy. Hence **Great Post-Road Earth**. At **Bingxu and Dinghai**, qi becomes the storehouse of things and affairs reach a beautiful completion. Yin and yang have traversed the whole circuit, and their force finds an interval of repose: hence **Rooftop Earth**.[^33]

I observe that Roadside Earth is sown with the hundred grains. Wu and Wei—are these not the high summer season in which growth is nurtured? Great Post-Road Earth communicates with the four directions. Shen and You—is this not the principle of finding companions and obtaining benefit and free passage? City-Wall Earth takes its meaning from the work of embankments and defenses: kings and dukes rely on it to establish their states and protect the people. Wall-Plaster Earth reveals the use of coating and ornament: officials and commoners depend on it for their dwellings. Sandy Earth is the most moist of earths. Moist Earth gives life; although completion is still to come, it already has its use. Rooftop Earth is Earth whose work is accomplished. Accomplishment is still: it remains fixed in one place and does not move.

Earth occupies the center of the Five Phases, carrying out the command to bear and exercising the authority to nourish. Neither the Three Powers nor the Five Phases can dispense with it. High or low, it finds its proper position; through the four seasonal endings, it accomplishes its work. Metal gains from it a keen edge and heroic hardness; Fire, luminous brilliance; Wood, surpassing blossom and beauty; Water, restraint against overflowing waves; Earth itself, still richer harvests. Gathered without dispersal, it can become a mountain—and a mountain is height. Dispersed without gathering, it can become the ground—and the ground is a plain. Its uses are inexhaustible, its generation without limit. Great indeed is Earth's accomplishment!

I have also heard the calendar specialists explain the images as follows. **Jiazi and Yichou:** Zi belongs to Water, is a lake, and is where Water flourishes. Metal also reaches Death at Zi and Tomb at Chou. Water flourishes while Metal dies and enters its tomb: hence Metal Within the Sea. **Renshen and Guiyou:** Shen and You are Metal's proper positions, with Arrival at Office at Shen and Imperial Prosperity at You. Born and flourishing, Metal is truly hard; no hardness surpasses a sword's edge: hence Sword-Edge Metal. **Gengchen and Xinsi:** Metal is Nurtured at Chen and Born at Si. Its form and substance have only just emerged and cannot yet be hard and sharp: hence White Pewter Metal. **Jiawu and Yiwei:** Wu is where Fire flourishes, and flourishing Fire brings Metal to Defeat; Wei is where Fire declines, and declining Fire allows Metal to reach Cap and Belt. Having just passed from Defeat to Cap and Belt, it cannot yet cut and hew: hence Metal Within Sand. **Renyin and Guimao:** Yin and Mao are where Wood flourishes, and flourishing Wood weakens Metal. Metal also reaches Extinction at Yin and Gestation at Mao. It therefore lacks strength: hence Gold Leaf Metal. **Gengxu and Xinhai:** Metal reaches Decline at Xu and Sickness at Hai. In decline and sickness it is truly soft: hence Hairpin-and-Bracelet Metal.[^37]

**Bingyin and Dingmao:** Yin is three yang and Mao four yang. Fire has both suitable ground and the Wood of Yin and Mao to generate it. At this time Heaven and Earth open their furnace and the myriad things first arise: hence Furnace Fire. **Jiaxu and Yihai:** Xu and Hai are the Gate of Heaven. Fire illuminates that gate, and its light reaches the greatest height: hence Fire on the Mountain. **Wuzi and Jichou:** Chou belongs to Earth and Zi to Water. Water occupies its proper position, yet the nayin is Fire. None but the divine dragon could account for it: hence Thunderbolt Fire. **Bingshen and Dingyou:** Shen is the Door of Earth, and the west is the gate through which the sun enters. Arriving here, the sun hides its light: hence Fire Below the Mountain. **Jiachen and Yisi:** Chen is the meal-time hour and Si the time before noon. The sun approaches the middle of the sky; its brilliant force illuminates all beneath Heaven: hence Lamp Fire. **Wuwu and Jiwei:** Wu is where Fire flourishes, and the Wood within Wei generates it again. Fire's nature is to flame upward, and it also encounters a place of generation: hence Fire in Heaven.[^36]

**Wuchen and Jisi:** Chen is open country and Si six yang. Wood here has flourishing branches and abundant leaves; luxuriant Wood standing in open country is Great Forest Wood. **Renwu and Guiwei:** Wood dies at Wu and is entombed at Wei. Even though the Water stems Ren and Gui generate it, Wood in Death and Tomb remains pliant and weak: hence Willow Wood. **Gengyin and Xinmao:** Wood reaches Arrival at Office at Yin and Imperial Prosperity at Mao. Born and flourishing, it is unlike weak and pliant Wood: hence Pine-and-Cypress Wood. **Wuxu and Jihai:** Xu is open country and Hai the place of Wood's Birth. Wood growing in open country is more than a single root or trunk: hence Level-Ground Wood. **Renzi and Guichou:** Zi belongs to Water and Chou to Metal. Water is just generating Wood when Metal cuts it down, like mulberry and silkworm-thorn newly grown and immediately cut to feed silkworms: hence Mulberry Wood. **Gengshen and Xinyou:** Shen is the seventh month and You the eighth. At this time Wood is cut off, yet the pomegranate alone sets fruit: hence Pomegranate Wood.[^34]

**Gengwu and Xinwei:** the Wood within Wei generates the flourishing Fire at Wu. With Fire flourishing, Earth receives form here. Newly generated Earth cannot yet nurture things: it is like the earth beside a road. **Wuyin and Jimao:** the heavenly stems Wu and Ji belong to Earth; Yin is Gen, the mountain. Earth piled up becomes a mountain: hence City-Wall Earth. **Bingxu and Dinghai:** Bing and Ding belong to Fire, Xu and Hai to the Gate of Heaven. Since Fire flames upward, the Earth it generates is not below: hence Rooftop Earth. **Gengzi and Xinchou:** although Chou is Earth's proper home, Zi is where Water flourishes. Earth meeting Water becomes mud: hence Wall-Plaster Earth. **Wushen and Jiyou:** Shen belongs to Kun, the ground, and You to Dui, the marsh. The Earth of Wu and Ji added above ground and marsh is unlike other shallow, insubstantial Earth: hence Great Post-Road Earth. **Bingchen and Dingsi:** Earth is stored at Chen and reaches Extinction at Si, while the Fire of the stems Bing and Ding reaches Cap and Belt at Chen and Arrival at Office at Si. Though Earth is in Storehouse and Extinction, flourishing Fire generates it again: hence Sandy Earth.[^37]

**Bingzi and Dingchou:** Water flourishes at Zi and declines at Chou. Turning from flourishing to decline, it cannot become a great river: hence Streambed Water. **Jiashen and Yiyou:** Metal reaches Arrival at Office at Shen and Imperial Prosperity at You. As Metal is born and flourishes, Water is generated from it, but its force is not yet vast: hence Well-and-Spring Water. **Renchen and Guisi:** Chen is Water's storehouse and Si the place of Metal's Birth. Metal generates it, and Water's flourishing is already present. Stored Water meeting generative Metal has an inexhaustible source: hence Long-Flowing Water. **Bingwu and Dingwei:** Bing and Ding belong to Fire, and Wu is where Fire flourishes, yet the nayin is Water. Water issuing from Fire could exist nowhere but the Silver River of Heaven: hence Celestial River Water. **Jiayin and Yimao:** Yin is the northeastern corner and Mao due east. Water flowing due east follows its nature; rivers, ravines, pools, and marshes all join and return to it: hence Great Stream Water. **Renxu and Guihai:** Water reaches Cap and Belt at Xu and Arrival at Office at Hai, so its power is substantial; Hai is also a great river, unlike other waters: hence Great Sea Water. Though these explanations are somewhat forced, they illuminate the preceding account and help reveal how the ancients chose their images.

Let me offer a further account. The Five-Phase images distinguish yin and yang by paired oppositions and reveal transformation through beginning and end. **Jiazi–Yichou opposite Jiawu–Yiwei:** sea and sand distinguish Water from Earth, yin from yang. **Renyin–Guimao opposite Renshen–Guiyou:** gold leaf and sword edge distinguish Metal and Wood, hardness and softness. **Gengchen–Xinsi opposite Gengxu–Xinhai:** white pewter and hairpins and bracelets occupy the different directions of Qian and Xun, each fulfilling its own form and color. **Renzi–Guichou opposite Renwu–Guiwei:** mulberry and willow, one bent and one pliant, differ in form and substance. **Gengyin–Xinmao opposite Gengshen–Xinyou:** pine and cypress against pomegranate, one steadfast and one pungent, differ utterly in nature and flavor. **Wuchen–Jisi opposite Wuxu–Jihai:** great forest and level ground, one flourishing and one declining, occupy the distinct directions of Xun and Qian.

**Wuzi–Jichou opposite Wuwu–Jiwei:** thunderbolt and heavenly Fire—the thunder lashes its whip, while sun and moon shine together. **Bingyin–Dingmao opposite Bingshen–Dingyou:** furnace and foot of the mountain—when Fire flourishes, Wood burns; when Metal flourishes, Fire is extinguished. **Jiachen–Yisi opposite Jiaxu–Yihai:** lamp and mountain summit—one shelters its light and fears wind; the other casts its light and rests at Gen. **Gengzi–Xinchou opposite Gengwu–Xinwei:** wall plaster and roadside—form is distinguished as gathered or dispersed, kind as dead or living. **Wuyin–Jimao opposite Wushen–Jiyou:** city wall and great post-road—the directions of east, south, west, and north, with Kun and Gen in their proper positions. **Bingchen–Dingsi opposite Bingxu–Dinghai:** sand and rooftop—dryness and moisture serve one another, the beginning and end of transformation.[^38]

Whether considered as circle or square, nothing lies outside flourishing, support, death, rest, and confinement. Whether the analogy is taken from near or far, nothing escapes Metal, Wood, Water, Fire, and Earth. Assign the Five Phases through stems and branches; examine yin and yang to make beginning and end fully clear. What Heaven completes and what human effort supplies work together; Birth and Prosperity, Death and Extinction are grouped by kind. Ah! In the sixty combinations, the sages did no more than borrow images to illuminate principles. Yet the Five Phases' natures and dispositions, materials and substances, forms and colors, functions and uses are all traced to their finest detail, leaving no hidden store of creative transformation unexplored.

The Changes says: “To establish Heaven's Way: yin and yang.” The day-stems express Heaven's Way. From Jia to Gui the ten day names move in succession, revealing yin and yang. “To establish Earth's Way: soft and hard.” The stations express Earth's Way. From Zi to Hai the twelve stations take their turns, revealing softness and hardness. A sound produced alone is only a sound; combined and ordered, sounds become tones. Thus day-stems and stations are interwoven and the stems incorporated to form the five notes and derive the six images. The Three Powers are then complete and the Five Phases retain nothing concealed.

Take the **stem as Lu**, to determine rank and lowliness; the **branch as destiny**, to determine length or brevity of life; and **nayin as the body**, to examine flourishing and decline. When a person's Lu, destiny, and body are all flourishing or supported, the Three Powers possess qi: this indicates happiness and long life. If they fall into Death, Extinction, Rest, or Confinement, the Three Powers lack qi: the life will certainly be one of obscurity and hardship.[^39]

### 9.2. Following changes of scale and strength

<!-- source: L193 -->
Among these sixty combinations, what is great and powerful should not change into what is small and weak; what is small and weak seeks to become great and powerful. Does it not? Poverty and low station followed by wealth and distinction bring glory; wealth and distinction followed by poverty and low station bring humiliation. One must not see only the initial poverty and overlook the later distinction, nor see only the initial distinction and overlook the later poverty.

Suppose the birth year's phase is Wood, specifically Gengyin or Xinmao: we know that this Wood is great and strong. If no other Wood appears in the month, day, hour, or conception pillar, judge it as Pine-and-Cypress Wood. But if Willow, Mulberry, or Pomegranate Wood appears there, set aside the greater and follow the lesser: do not judge it simply as pine and cypress. Suppose, instead, the person is born in Renwu or Guiwei: we know that this Wood is small and weak. If no other Wood appears in the month, day, hour, or conception pillar, judge it as Willow Wood. But if Pine-and-Cypress Wood or Great Forest Wood appears, set aside the lesser and judge by the greater: do not judge it simply as willow.[^46]

The same applies to people born under Fire in Heaven, Sword-Edge Metal, Great Sea Water, or Great Post-Road Earth when another position in the month, day, hour, or conception pillar supplies a smaller, weaker nayin of the same phase. Conversely, someone born under Lamp Fire, Gold Leaf Metal, Well-and-Spring Water, or Sandy Earth may meet a greater, stronger nayin of the same phase in those positions. One may be led from the ordinary into the extraordinary, or begin with weight and end with lightness. In every case, judge by the transformation: do not cling to a single fixed aspect.

### 9.3. Expanded judgments on the sixty combinations

<!-- source: L195-L201 -->
**Jiazi 甲子.** This is Metal that yields to alteration, its qi dispersed. Support from Wushen Earth or Guisi Water is auspicious. Wushen is the position of Metal's Arrival at Office; Earth, moreover, flourishes at Zi and can assuredly generate and complete it. In Guisi, Metal is born at Si and Water flourishes at Zi: each nayin finds its proper destination. This is also called **Lu Returning to the Origin**. It avoids the Fire of Dingmao, Dingyou, and Wuwu. Yan Dongsou says: “Jiazi Metal is an Advancing Spirit. It receives the virtue of depth, inwardness, and an empty center, and is auspicious in all four seasons. When it fulfills a prestigious configuration and receives flourishing qi, it gives subtle mastery of an art or profession and the honor of taking first place.”[^47]

**Yichou 乙丑.** This is Metal in its own Storehouse. Fire cannot control it: Metal already hidden away is naturally free from such injury. When it is opened by a clash or break, distinction and glory invariably follow. It avoids only the Fire of Jichou and Jiwei. Yan Dongsou says: “Yichou is a Direct Seal and possesses great blessings and virtue. In autumn and winter it gives wealth, rank, and long life; in spring and summer there is ill fortune within the good. When it fulfills a configuration, it establishes merit and enjoys blessings; when accompanied by harmful influences, it tends to become an inauspicious conjunction.” The *Precious Mirror of the Jade Empyrean* says: “Jiazi and Yichou are Metal not yet fashioned into a vessel. Encountering Fire completes them; repeated encounters are auspicious.”[^48]

**Bingyin 丙寅.** This is brilliantly blazing Fire. Without Water to control it, there is danger of scorching and violent heat; yet Water must not be excessive. It especially loves Jiayin Water, which brings relief in the same branch position. This too is called Lu Returning to the Origin. The *Essential Discourse on the Five Phases* says: “Bingyin Fire contains numinous brightness and the qi of pure, open harmony, together with the life-generating virtue of all four seasons. When it fulfills a prestigious configuration, literary brilliance manifests, indicating the distinction of a leading place in the examinations.”

**Dingmao 丁卯.** This is Fire with hidden brightness. Its qi is weak and needs Wood to generate it. Meeting Water is inauspicious; Yimao and Yiyou Water are the most harmful. The *Essential Discourse on the Five Phases* says: “Dingmao is Fire in Bathing. It contains the qi of thunder's movement and wind's arising. Water's assistance allows it to attain its purpose; Earth bearing it gives it a deep foundation; Wood's support gives literary brilliance. But if Metal acts as its bellows and it also meets summer's command, it becomes violent and destructive.” The *Surviving Writings of Guiguzi* says: “Bingyin and Dingmao should be preserved and sustained in autumn and winter.” Its commentary explains: “Fire does not flourish in the west. Reaching autumn and winter, its force may not endure.”[^48]

**Wuchen 戊辰.** This is Wood beneath two Earths. Many Metals cannot control it, for Earth generates Metal and the relation of mother and child intervenes. Water generating it is favorable. The *Essential Discourse on the Five Phases* says: “Wuchen, Gengyin, and Guichou are three combinations whose numbers display firm virtue, clarity, and vigor. Born in spring or summer, they can stand apart, exert themselves independently, and achieve success by adapting to change. If they also encounter flourishing qi, their ambition rises into the clouds and towers above the ravines. Only autumn birth is avoided: though they cherish resolve and integrity, they remain bent down and cannot unfold.”

**Jisi 己巳.** This is Wood close to Fire. Metal is born here and does it no harm; nevertheless, Metal in Birth or Prosperity is avoided. Yan Dongsou says: “Jisi is Wood stirred by wind in Xun. Its roots are insecure and easily pulled up. Harmonize it with Metal and Earth; when its luck cycles turn southeast, it can become useful timber. Though yang without and yin within, if it has no other assistance its qi becomes empty and dispersed. Further controlled by the Metal ghost, it is Wood unfit for use.” Luoluzi says: “Jisi and Wuchen pass through Qian's palace and escape adversity.” The commentary explains: “Jisi and Wuchen stand for the Wood class. The west is the flourishing homeland of Metal, Wood's ghost; nayin Wood reaches Extinction there. This is called a meeting with adversity. But on passing into the palace of Qian and Hai, Wood receives Water and attains Birth; thus it escapes adversity.”[^49]

**Gengwu 庚午 and Xinwei 辛未.** These are Earth newly coming into being. Wood cannot control them. Only excessive Water is avoided, for it instead injures their qi. Abundant Wood, however, has a place to return, since Wood returns to Wei. Yan Dongsou says: “Gengwu, Xinwei, Wushen, and Jiyou are all Earth of substantial virtue: embracing, composed, and still, with harmonious qi that blends freely and an ample endowment of blessings and Lu. When they fulfill a configuration, they often hold regional office and accomplish works of wide benefit and generous care.”

**Renshen 壬申.** This is Metal at Arrival at Office. It benefits from Water and Earth; the Fire of Bingshen, Bingyin, or Wuwu brings calamity. Yan Dongsou says: “Renshen Metal bears the authority of a celestial general and draws upon the qi of Arrival at Office. In autumn and winter it wields the power of life and death; in spring and summer there is little good and much ill. When it fulfills a configuration, it exerts itself toward achievement and reputation; when it carries harmful influences, it makes severity and exploitation its specialty.”

**Guiyou 癸酉.** This is Metal firm and complete. Fire dies at You: what harm can it do? Only Dingyou Fire is avoided, for it controls Metal in the same position. Yan Dongsou says: “Guiyou is Metal flourishing in its own place, endowed with pure and unmixed qi. In spring and summer its nature is discerning and bright; in autumn and winter it is especially distinguished. When it fulfills a configuration, achievement, integrity, and steadfast conduct set it above others. With harmful influences, it is hard and forceful in youth, gradually attaining pure virtue after forty.” The *Precious Mirror of the Jade Empyrean* says: “Renshen and Guiyou occupy Metal's flourishing positions. They must not be made still more flourishing: excess strength injures things. Nor should they meet Fire, for Fire injures them themselves.”[^48]

**Jiaxu 甲戌.** This is Fire in its own Storehouse. It does not mind many Waters, but avoids Renxu alone. This is “receiving control within the Tomb,” whose trouble is difficult to escape. The *Essential Discourse on the Five Phases* says: “Jiaxu Fire is both Seal and Storehouse. It contains the hidden qi of utmost yang. In a prestigious configuration, it brings radiant wealth and distinction. Only summer birth is avoided: beware of ill fortune within the good.”

**Yihai 乙亥.** This is Fire with hidden brightness. Its qi is smothered and pent up rather than expressed. **Bingzi 丙子.** This is spreading, flowing Water. It does not fear many Earths, but especially dislikes Gengzi: this is meeting a ghost amid flourishing, and no ill omen is greater. The *Essential Discourse on the Five Phases* says: “Bingzi is Water flourishing in its own place, yang above and yin below, complete in essence and spirit. Those endowed with it have expansive natural gifts and profound understanding and capacity. In spring and summer it is qi that aids things, often accomplishing works of benefit and nourishment.”

**Dingchou 丁丑.** This is Water in which blessings gather. It most loves Metal's generation, and avoids Xinwei, Bingchen, and Bingxu, which punish or break it. The *Essential Discourse on the Five Phases* says: “Dingchou and Yiyou are, in their numbers, dispersed and weakened Water: yin strong and yang weak. **Wuyin 戊寅.** This is injured Earth, weakest of all. It needs Fire in Birth or Prosperity to sustain its qi. The *Essential Discourse on the Five Phases* says: “Wuyin and Bingxu both ride upon the substantial qi of Earth's virtue. One contains nascent Fire; the other, lodged Fire. This is called yang's numinous potency entering the center: combinations of blessing and felicity. In a prestigious configuration, they confer virtue surpassing the age and the highest distinction available to a subject. Princes and sons of noble houses are often born on these days. Even in an ordinary configuration, they indicate abundant blessings, great longevity, and ease from beginning to end.”[^05]

**Jimao 己卯.** This is Earth in its own Death, even more diminished. It values the Fire of Dingmao, Jiaxu, Yihai, and Jiwei, coming through combination to bring blessings. The *Essential Discourse on the Five Phases* says: “Jimao, Earth in its own Death, is established in Zhen. **Gengchen 庚辰.** This is Metal whose qi is gathered. It needs no Fire to regulate it: the vessel forms of itself. Strong Fire instead destroys the vessel; Fire in Sickness or Extinction does no harm. Yan Dongsou says: “Gengchen Metal possesses the virtues of firmness, vigor, depth, and substantiality, and the nature of intelligence and free understanding. In spring and summer, calamity and blessing lie hidden within one another; in autumn and winter, its excellence is fully developed. When it fulfills a configuration, it combines civil and military capacities; with harmful influences, it delights in manipulating military power.”

**Xinsi 辛巳.** This is Metal in its own Birth. Spirit and vitality are sufficient; substance and qi complete. Fierce heat may transform it without destroying it. It avoids the Fire of [Bingyin], Yisi, and Wuwu. Metal is born at Si, yet [under such conditions] cannot truly be born; it meets Defeat at Wu and Extinction at Yin, and its qi disperses. How could it bear further Fire in Birth or Prosperity? The *Essential Discourse on the Five Phases* says: “Xinsi Metal is the Academy in its own Birth, possessing the virtues of brilliant discernment and extraordinary beauty. In autumn and winter it receives full strength; in spring and summer there are seven parts ill to three parts good. In a prestigious configuration, it indicates outstanding learning and conduct, leading to refined distinction, with a constant desire to benefit others.” The *Precious Mirror of the Jade Empyrean* says: “Gengchen and Xinsi are Metal not yet made into a vessel and should encounter Fire. Xinsi is also in its own Birth; Si is Fire. Those endowed with it grow more radiant each day.”[^51]

**Guiwei 癸未.** This is Wood in its own Storehouse. Birth and Prosperity are favorable. Even Yichou Metal cannot clash with and break it: each returns to its root without encroaching on the other. **Jiashen 甲申.** This is Water in its own Birth. Its qi spreads and flows; it should have somewhere to return and also depends upon Metal's generation. It does not fear many Earths, but especially dislikes Wushen and Gengzi Earth. The *Essential Discourse on the Five Phases* says: “Jiashen Water is in its own Birth and contains the Academy of innate truth. When it enters a configuration, it indicates intelligence, learning, and inexhaustible subtle resourcefulness.”

**Yiyou 乙酉.** This is Water in its own Defeat. It borrows the assistance of abundant Metal: because its own qi is weak, it relies upon its mother to nourish it. It avoids the Earth of Jiyou, Jimao, Wushen, Gengzi, and Xinchou, which brings early death, destitution, and low station.

**Bingxu 丙戌.** This is Earth of strong blessings and substantial Lu. Wood cannot control it. It avoids Metal in Birth or Prosperity; if it meets abundant Fire, its distinction is beyond description.

**Dinghai 丁亥.** This is Earth at Arrival at Office. Wood cannot control it. Too much Metal is disliked; Fire must generate and rescue it for good fortune to follow. It avoids Jihai and Xinmao Wood. The *Essential Discourse on the Five Phases* says: “The two Earths Dinghai and Gengzi contain Metal within their numbers: firm within, harmonious without. Those endowed with them possess steadiness and resolve. If flourishing Water and Fire qi assists beneath the Earth, they can establish achievements and undertake affairs, daring in action, authoritative, and decisive.”

**Wuzi 戊子 and Jichou 己丑.** These are Fire within Water, also called the Divine Dragon's Fire. Only through meeting Water do they become distinguished: they are the sovereign Fire among the six qi. The *Essential Discourse on the Five Phases* says: “Wuzi contains the complete and substantial qi of spirit and radiance, bringing the blessing of preserving life through all four seasons. In a prestigious configuration, it produces great and noble people of capacious bearing, with wealth and distinction auspicious to the end.”

**Jichou 己丑, further.** This is the Fire of a celestial general, and also the original home of Heavenly Yi. It contains the qi of authority, blessing, radiance, and substantiality; its expression is steep and fierce. A prestigious configuration using it has the virtue of a commander, wins a leading name, and establishes merit. The *Classic Illuminating the Spirit* says: “Chou is Fire in Gestation and Nurturing; its qi gradually rises. With Bingyin or Wuwu Fire to assist, it can accomplish works that benefit others.”

**Gengyin 庚寅 and Xinmao 辛卯.** These are the Wood of the year's cold season. Snow and frost cannot change their constancy; how, then, could Metal control them? Geng and Xin already stand above them. Without borrowing further shaping or regulation, they naturally become useful timber. Yan Dongsou says: “Xinmao Wood flourishes in its own place. In spring and summer, its qi and integrity stand upright and it establishes achievements. Born in autumn, it becomes impetuous or rigidly aloof, is broken and frustrated, and cannot express its vigorous qi.”

**Renchen 壬辰.** This is Water in its own Storehouse, like a pool or marsh in which Water accumulates. It avoids Metal coming to cut it open and breach it. Meeting Renchen again is called self-punishment; another Chen combination is blameless. Abundant Water and Earth are both welcome, but the Water of Renxu, Guihai, and Bingzi is feared: its Birth and Prosperity are excessive, spreading boundlessly with nowhere to return. The *Essential Discourse on the Five Phases* says: “Renchen Water is a Direct Seal, containing the virtues of clarity, brightness, moisture, and fertility. Those endowed with it are embracing and generous, their mind and perception like a mirror. Received in spring and summer, it brings great blessings and wisdom; in autumn and winter, it tends toward deceit and lack of virtue.”

**Guisi 癸巳.** This is Water in its own Extinction, called a dried-up stream. If it meets the strong, substantial Earth of Bingxu, Dinghai, or Gengzi, its drying up is to be expected. But if Metal in a threefold combination, Birth, or Prosperity generates it, the spring wells up ceaselessly, filling each hollow before advancing. The *Essential Discourse on the Five Phases* says: “Guisi and Yimao are Water in its own Extinction and Death: utmost yin withdrawing into storage, conserving and nourishing its true essence until distinguished qi congeals. In a prestigious configuration, these commonly produce noble people of the subtle Way, long established in constant virtue and beneficial to others.”[^53]

**Jiawu 甲午.** This is Metal in its own Defeat, also called fiercely unyielding Metal. Meeting Fire in Birth or Prosperity allows it to become a vessel; the Fire of Dingmao, Dingyou, and Wuzi is avoided as inauspicious. The *Essential Discourse on the Five Phases* says: “Jiawu Metal is the leading qi of the Advancing Spirit and possesses the virtue of firmness and clarity. Autumn and winter are auspicious; spring and summer may be inauspicious. In a prestigious configuration it indicates success in the examinations and achievement in leading others. Out of season and bearing harmful influences, it becomes violent, harsh, and cruel, with little kindness or righteousness.” The *Classic Illuminating the Spirit* says: “Jiawu Metal suffers from excessive fierceness; restrained, it becomes deep and inward.” Its commentary explains: “Metal in sand and stone is hard ore. It welcomes the killing influence that restrains it: Fire alters it.” The *Surviving Writings of Guiguzi* says: “Jiawu loves its Officer-ghost.” Li Xuzhong says: “Jiawu Metal errs in fierceness; Renzi Wood errs in softness. If Renzi obtains Jiawu, or Jiawu obtains Renzi, yin and yang occupy their proper places and numinous brightness appears.”[^54]

**Yiwei 乙未.** This is Metal in an offset Storehouse. If Fire regulates it and Earth generates it, blessings become strong and qi gathers. It avoids the Fire of Jiwei, Bingshen, and Dingyou. The *Essential Discourse on the Five Phases* says: “Yiwei Metal is, in its numbers, Wood's Storehouse and also a celestial general. It possesses the virtues of pure humanity and substantial righteousness: nowhere is it inauspicious. In a prestigious configuration, it produces heroes extraordinary in their age, standing at the head of their peers. In an ordinary configuration, even with harmful influences, clashes, and infringements, it still produces a noble person among ordinary people, enjoying venerable longevity.”[^55]

**Bingshen 丙申 and Dingyou 丁酉.** These are Fire in its own Sickness and Death respectively. Their qi is extremely slight; only with Wood's help does it arise. They avoid the Water of Jiashen, Yiyou, Jiayin, and Yimao. Yan Dongsou says: “Bingshen, Fire in Sickness, takes Wood as the virtue of cultivated brilliance, Water as the nature of breadth and openness, and Earth as the foundation of blessings and wisdom. Metal alone makes it violent and oppressive: even with auspicious combinations, it changes into disharmonious qi.” The *Essential Discourse on the Five Phases* says: “Dingyou, Fire in its own Death, contains the qi of concealment, quietness, and stillness: harmonious without, firm within. A prestigious configuration using it commonly produces noble people possessed of the Way, with naturally virtuous conduct.”

**Wuxu 戊戌.** This is Wood within Earth. It avoids repeated Earth: with much Earth nayin, life is obstructed and difficult. Metal cannot control it, because Metal's qi disperses at Xu; meeting Metal can instead bring blessings. Abundant Water is beneficial, and strong Wood forms a prestigious configuration. Yan Dongsou says: “Wuxu Wood stands alone on a solitary root. Harmonize it with the flourishing qi of Water and Fire, and it possesses the virtues of brilliant discernment and mature excellence. In a configuration it advances through literary accomplishment and enjoys blessings and Lu from beginning to end. Yet because it rides upon the qi of a celestial general, it must pass through dangers and hardships without changing its integrity; only then will its later blessings appear.”

**Jihai 己亥.** This is Wood in its own Birth, with abundant roots. It does not fear many Metals, but dislikes Xinhai, Xinsi, and Guiyou Metal. With Yimao or Dingwei Water and Guiwei Wood, great distinction invariably follows. The *Essential Discourse on the Five Phases* says: “Jihai Wood is in its own Birth, displaying the virtues of outstanding talent and surpassing excellence. When received in a position of particular attainment, it commonly gives refined distinction and early advancement.” Yan Dongsou says: “Jihai Wood is refined and distinguished in season; out of season, it suffers toil and hardship.”

**Gengzi 庚子.** This is Earth of substantial virtue. It can control many Waters and does not fear other Wood, since Wood has no qi at Zi. Meeting Renshen Metal is called **Lu in the Luminous Position**: distinction is assured.[^47]

**Xinchou 辛丑.** This is Earth in which blessings gather. Many Woods cannot control it, since Chou is Metal's Storehouse and contains Metal: what injury can Wood do? The *Precious Mirror of the Jade Empyrean* says: “Gengzi and Xinchou Earth love Wood and dislike Water. Wood becomes their Officer; Water is unsuitable.” Yan Dongsou says: “The Earth of Xinchou and Jiyou contains Metal in its numbers. Its virtue is substantial, its nature firm: harmonious without mere conformity. Assisted above and below by flourishing Water and Fire qi, it displays authority, reputation, and great achievement, and is seen as courageous and decisive.”

**Renyin 壬寅 and Guimao 癸卯.** These are Metal in its own Extinction and Metal with dispersed qi respectively. Many Fires destroy their qi; only Water and Earth attending them are auspicious. The *Essential Discourse on the Five Phases* says: “Renyin and Guimao are insubstantial, thin Metal, possessing humanity's gentleness and righteousness's firmness. In autumn and winter they are vigorous and free of harm: an apparent ill omen becomes a good one. In spring and summer they are inauspicious within and auspicious without; good fortune is the forerunner of ill. In a prestigious configuration, resolve, integrity, and discernment shine; with harmful influences, violence prevents a good ending.” The *Collected Configurations of the Three Fates* says: “Guimao is Metal in its own Gestation. If it meets the Furnace Fire of Bingyin and Dingmao, that Fire is not a ghost: gestating Metal becomes a vessel within the furnace.”[^48]

**Jiachen 甲辰.** This is Fire in an offset Storehouse. Many Fires assisting it are auspicious: like qi seeks like and supplements what is lacking. Generation by Wuchen or Wuxu Wood makes a prestigious configuration. The Water of Renchen, Renxu, Bingwu, and Dingwei is avoided as most harmful. The *Essential Discourse on the Five Phases* says: “Jiachen is the Fire of a celestial general. It contains swift, keen, and fierce qi. In a prestigious configuration it gives exceptional attainment and leadership in letters. Autumn and winter are favorable; summer is not.”

**Yisi 乙巳.** This is Fire at Arrival at Office. Water cannot control it, because Water reaches Extinction at Si; Water's assistance instead makes it pure. Two or three Fires helping it are also favorable. The *Essential Discourse on the Five Phases* says: “Yisi Fire contains the qi of pure yang issuing through Xun, radiant and substantial. Spring and winter tend toward good fortune; summer and autumn toward ill.”

**Bingwu 丙午 and Dingwei 丁未.** These are the Water of the Silver River. Earth cannot control them; earthly Metal cannot generate Water in Heaven. Excessive Birth and Prosperity instead injures the myriad things; excessive Death and Extinction leaves it unable to generate them. The *Essential Discourse on the Five Phases* says: “Bingwu is Water of the greatest height, embodying the south's warm, substantial qi. Those endowed with it commonly possess the qi of the Way: open to transformation, keen and unusual, accomplished, and outstanding among their peers. Dingwei possesses the full numbers of the Three Powers and receives the qi of open rectitude. Those endowed with it have complete essence, spirit, and qi, lofty and subtle roots of character, and mastery of the Way of transformation.”

**Wushen 戊申.** This is Earth in great piled hills. Wood reaches Extinction at Shen and cannot control it. With abundant Metal and Water to assist, it forms a configuration of wealth, rank, honor, and glory.

**Jiyou 己酉.** This is Earth in its own Defeat, with insufficient qi. It borrows Fire's assistance; Dingmao and Dingyou Fire are auspicious. It must especially avoid Death and Extinction. Xinmao and Xinyou Wood are feared as bringing disaster, obstruction, and life cut short.

**Gengxu 庚戌 and Xinhai 辛亥.** These are firm, completed Metal. They should not meet Fire, lest it injure them; Water and Earth supporting them confer distinction. Yan Dongsou says: “Gengxu is Metal in Fire's Tomb. It has a fierce, self-reliant violence. In autumn and winter it may become deep and substantial; in spring and summer its actions give rise to regret and humiliation. A noble person wields military and penal authority; a lesser person indulges a savage and unyielding nature. Xinhai Metal receives Qian's vigorous, pure, clear, centered, and upright qi. Spring, autumn, and winter are auspicious; summer is seven parts good to three parts ill. A prestigious configuration using it embodies humanity and keeps righteousness. With punishments and killing influences, however, it indulges violence and covets achievement.”

**Renzi 壬子 and Guichou 癸丑.** These are Wood in its exclusive position and Wood in an offset Storehouse respectively. Meeting Death or Extinction brings wealth and distinction; Birth or Prosperity brings poverty and low station. Too much Water cuts life short; abundant Metal and strong Earth are favorable. The *Essential Discourse on the Five Phases* says: “Renzi is deeply yin Wood: yang weak, yin strong, pliant and unable to stand by itself. It then commonly belongs to immortals and extraordinary adepts, whose bearing is unlike that of ordinary people.” The *Classic Illuminating the Spirit* says: “Renzi Wood errs in excessive softness. If it is raised up, it becomes humane and brilliantly discerning.” The commentary explains: “Renzi Wood occupies Water's flourishing homeland. Borrowing a little moisture from Water within Zi, it grows, but is tender, brittle, and easily broken: Wood in its own Defeat. To ‘raise it up’ means to augment it with flourishing Fire qi and make it unfold in splendor; then it becomes humane, courageous, and discerning.”[^56]

**Jiayin 甲寅 and Yimao 乙卯.** These are Water in its own Sickness and Death respectively. Although sick or dead, Earth cannot control them: the stem and branch supply two Woods capable of restraining Earth. Meeting Renyin or Guimao Metal gives ample support. The *Essential Discourse on the Five Phases* says: “The two Waters Jiayin and Renxu are hidden and contrary, with yin overcoming yang, indicating deceit and harm to others. Only adjustment through Fire and Earth—diminishing and augmenting as needed—can make them into great vessels.”

**Bingchen 丙辰.** This is Earth in its own Storehouse, thick and strong. It loves Jiachen Fire and dislikes Wuchen Wood. Ordinary Wood cannot injure this Earth: Bing is Fire, and Chen is Fire's offset Storehouse, so Earth has already become a vessel. It dislikes only the Wood of Wuxu, Jihai, Xinmao, and Wuchen. The *Essential Discourse on the Five Phases* says: “Bingchen Earth is a Direct Seal, establishing the virtue of an auspicious meeting of the Five Blessings. Those endowed with it generally prosper greatly and accomplish much: if not distinguished, then wealthy. Those who suffer a clash, however, often become Buddhist or Daoist renunciants.”

**Dingsi 丁巳.** This is Earth in its own Extinction, yet not truly extinguished. One Earth lies beneath two Fires, in the home of its parents, receiving the favor of natural kinship; therefore it is not extinguished. Wood cannot control it, and more Fire is increasingly favorable. The *Precious Mirror of the Jade Empyrean* says: “Dingsi contains the flourishing number of the southeast's Fire virtue. Those endowed with it possess generosity, blessings, and longevity.”

**Wuwu 戊午 and Jiwei 己未.** These are Fire flourishing in its own place and Fire in an offset Storehouse respectively. They occupy Li's luminous direction and a place of flourishing and support; their qi is extremely strong. Other Waters do no harm, but the heavenly Water of Bingwu and Dingwei is avoided. Yan Dongsou says: “Wuwu, Fire flourishing in its own place, contains Li's luminous, upward-flaming qi. It handles things without feeling, and its actions oppose the multitude. Received in autumn and winter and assisted by flourishing Water and Earth qi, it becomes broad-minded and discerning, with firm and substantial blessings. In spring and summer, with Metal and Wood, its brilliance may rise swiftly, but the life is not enduring.” The *Essential Discourse on the Five Phases* says: “Jiwei is declining Fire, containing residual qi that stores treasure. In spring and summer, when its luck cycles enter a region of depth and concealment, it becomes discerning, keen, and swift, with profound and lasting blessings. Received in summer, it is not harmonious qi; received in autumn, it brings good fortune first and ill afterward.”[^57]

**Gengshen 庚申 and Xinyou 辛酉.** In these two Woods, Metal sits above Wood, which becomes a vessel through Metal. Further Metal is avoided, lest the vessel be destroyed. Jiashen and Yiyou Water allow them to fulfill a configuration. The *Precious Mirror of the Jade Empyrean* says: “Gengshen is Wood in its own Extinction: the soul roams and spirit transforms. Those born on this day are commonly no ordinary vessels. Even in an ordinary configuration their natural gifts are keen and unusual, and they are unconfined by the family mold. In a prestigious configuration they have heroic talent and establish achievements rare in an age. Xinyou is Wood displaced from its proper station, trapped in Metal's homeland. Those who receive it encounter many hardships in the world. Only pairing it with Guimao Metal lets hard and soft assist one another, making them stand above the crowd and secure a lofty examination rank.”

The *Classic Illuminating the Spirit* says: “The soul values wandering in Heaven. Therefore Gengshen Wood does not dislike Death or Extinction: it sits alone and keeps to Gengshen.” The commentary explains: “Gengshen is Wood in its own Extinction. Of the five viscera, Wood belongs to the liver, and the liver stores the soul. When Wood rests or is extinguished, the soul wanders in Heaven. Thus Gengshen Wood does not dislike Death or Extinction: its distinction lies in wandering in Heaven.” The *Surviving Writings of Guiguzi* says: “Xinyou awaits Birth and Prosperity.” The commentary explains: “Xinyou is Wood whose qi is extinguished; it seeks Birth and Prosperity in order to flourish.”[^58]

**Renxu 壬戌 and Guihai 癸亥.** These are Water in an offset Storehouse and Water at Arrival at Office respectively, called Great Sea Water. Their stems, branches, and nayin are [predominantly] Water. They avoid many Waters: even Renchen, Water's Storehouse, cannot contain them. They do not fear other Earth. Death and Extinction are auspicious; Birth and Prosperity bring flooding with nowhere to return. The *Precious Mirror of the Jade Empyrean* says: “Hai and Zi are Water's proper positions. Renxu's qi lies hidden and does not flow smoothly. Only adjustment by Fire and Earth can make it a great vessel. Guihai possesses a number of pure yang and inwardly embodies utmost humanity. Those endowed with it have an even, expansive natural disposition and vast aspiration, expressed in achievements that benefit and nourish. But harmful influences in the day and hour make for a violent and cunning person.”[^59]

### 9.4. Further principles and exceptions

<!-- source: L271-L279 -->
It is also said: “Bingxu Earth has abundant, substantial blessings because Fire gathers here.” Jiwei, Gengchen, Wuchen, and Dingchou follow the same principle. Jiwei is Fire, and Wei contains entombed Wood; Gengchen is Metal, and Chen contains entombed Earth; Wuchen is Wood, and Chen contains entombed Water; Dingchou is Water, and Chou contains entombed Metal. In each case parental qi has a place to nourish it. These five possess strong and substantial blessings. Even injury from a ghost above does no harm, because their qi is already complete. Li Xuzhong says: “Bingxu is especially remarkable: Xu is also Earth's own position, making it still more flourishing and powerful.”

Yisi and Wuwu are fiercely blazing Fire. In autumn and winter they act as virtue and are auspicious; in spring and summer they act as punishment and are inauspicious. In midsummer, violent heat erupts and immediately returns to dryness, ultimately burning away harmony and producing harm. Yisi is Fire at Arrival at Office, with a Wood above to generate it: its qi is abundant. Wuwu is Fire flourishing in its own place. Born in autumn or winter, it becomes warming qi and the virtue that assists things. Born in spring or summer, already flourishing Fire again obtains a yang position and acts inauspiciously. Midsummer birth produces the violent, harsh, cruel, and short-lived type.

Yimao, Guisi, Dingyou, and Yihai: although their Water or Fire is in Death or Extinction, it is instead clear, bright, subtle, and excellent. Fire in Death or Extinction is bright within and obscure without; its reflected light turns back upon itself. Water in Death or Extinction is still and transparent, able to reflect even beard and eyebrows. Hence they are clear, bright, subtle, and excellent. Li Xuzhong says: “Although the Water and Fire of these four positions are in Death or Extinction, they are clear, bright, subtle, and excellent. Examine the Heavenly Yi Noble Person, and this becomes evident.”

Renyin Metal serves its ruler without rebellion; Gengshen Wood, as a minister, does not become overbearing. The Five Phases correspond to the five notes. Gong-Earth is the ruler; shang-Metal the minister; jue-Wood the people. Excessive shang makes the minister strong; excessive jue makes the ruler weak. Therefore, within the five-note system, the four clear gong tones are regularly used to diminish shang and jue. Gengshen is jue-Wood in its own Extinction; Renyin is shang-Metal in its own Extinction. Their dispositions attain the Way of loyalty and compliance. Thus one serves without rebellion and the other ministers without overbearing strength. For this reason, the highest offices—from the Purple Tenuity, Phoenix Terrace, and Phoenix Pavilion upward—especially avoid lives in which Metal and Wood are in Birth and Prosperity. Such people either will not occupy those offices or cannot remain in them long. Censorial and remonstrating offices alone are suitable. If Metal and Wood are in Birth and Prosperity but are restrained and broken, however, the rule is different.[^60]

With Gengshen Wood and Yisi Fire, Earth and Metal are born yet are not born. With Bingwu Water and Guimao Metal, Wood and Water die yet do not die. Earth is born at Shen, but not at Gengshen; Water at Shen, but not at Wushen; Fire at Yin, but not at Jiayin; Metal at Si, but not at Yisi; Wood at Hai, but not at Xinhai. In each case the place of Birth instead brings control; those who receive it have shortened lives. [Water] dies at Mao, but not at Guimao; Earth dies at Mao, but not at Dingmao; Wood dies at Wu, but not at Bingwu; Metal dies at Zi, but not at Gengzi; Fire dies at You, but not at Xinyou. In each case the place of Death instead supplies generation; those who receive it enjoy long life.[^61]

Wuzi's stem and branch flourish in the north, Water's position, while its nayin is Fire. This is Fire within Water, which none but the divine dragon can possess. Bingwu's stem and branch flourish in the south, Fire's position, while its nayin is Water. This is Water within Fire, which none but the Celestial River can possess. A Wuzi person obtaining Bingwu, or a Bingwu person obtaining Wuzi, invariably attains distinction. Water emerges within Fire and Fire hides within Water: Water and Fire achieve completion together, and essence and spirit move with an efficacy extraordinary among humans.[^62]

Li Xuzhong says: “Bingwu is Water in Heaven, present in the Silver River: it is the Heavenly Empress among the twelve celestial stations. Those endowed with it are discerning, broad-minded, keen, unusual, and no ordinary people. Wuzi is Fire within Water, possessed by the divine dragon: it is the sovereign Fire among the six qi. Those endowed with it become numinously discerning of themselves, penetrate incipient workings, and anticipate events. Receiving both qi together is particularly subtle and extraordinary. Examine the remaining [qi] by this standard.”[^62]

Xinchou Earth does not dislike Wood; Wuxu Wood does not fear Metal. How are these cases distinguished? Chou contains Metal's Storehouse, so Wood does not act as its ghost. Xu contains Fire, so Metal instead suffers harm. In Wuxu Wood, two Earths stand above and a single Wood below. Buried within the two Earths, it has not yet sprouted and its form is unseen: Earth is strong and Wood weak. The other cases follow the same principle.

<!-- source: L281-L289 -->
Gengyin Wood and Dingsi Earth do not dislike their respective Metal and Wood ghosts. Metal reaching Yin's palace would ordinarily be Wood's ghost, but Metal is extinguished at Yin and therefore does not act as a ghost. Wood reaching Si's palace meets nascent Metal that controls it and therefore does not act as a ghost. But if Gengyin Wood meets Renshen Metal, it receives both clash and control. Apply this principle to the remaining cases.

Gengwu Earth rides upon the south's flourishing Fire to nourish its form; Wushen Earth is in its own Birth; Gengzi Earth is full in itself. They do not avoid the Wood ghost: Wood dies at Wu, is extinguished at Shen, and meets Defeat at Zi. Moreover, this Earth is strong in itself—what injury can it suffer? Apply the same principle elsewhere.

The four Metals Renshen, Guiyou, Gengxu, and Xinhai have strong qi and do not dislike their ghosts. Wuzi Fire does not fear its ghost either. Thunderbolt Fire within Water belongs to the divine dragon; only with Water can thunder sound. Yet the heavenly Water of Bingwu and Dingwei is avoided: it has the power to contend with it. Generally, injury to the natal stem and branch indicates insufficiency in the six faculties and a beginning without a completion—for example, Dingsi meeting Guihai or Renzi meeting Wuwu. Apply the principle to other cases.[^63]

Wuwu and Gengshen are extraordinary when either obtains the other. Gengshen Pomegranate Wood flourishes in summer and therefore welcomes Wuwu: its Fire Officer is flourishing, and the pomegranate's nature is in season. Wuwu is Fire at the extreme of flourishing; it welcomes Shen, where the Heavenly Horse appears and gives mutual support.

The **Spirit-Head Lu** are the Lu of the ten stems' exclusive positions: yin and yang in their proper stations, the numinous meeting of Heaven and Earth. They set out the true sources of the Eight Trigrams and unfold the successes and failures of the Five Phases. Hard and soft impel one another; presence and absence combine and transform. Thus Renzi Water answers to Kan in the north, and Bingwu Fire is truly Li in the southern palace. Bingwu obtaining Renzi therefore does not count as a break; Dingsi obtaining Guihai does not count as a clash. This is the source of Water and Fire's mutual assistance and the principle of husband and wife joining. Kan and Li are the workings of male and female essence and spirit. But Renzi obtaining Bingwu, or Guihai obtaining Dingsi, gives Fire and Water in the before-and-after relationship of Not Yet Across. It is not as favorable as Dingsi meeting Renzi or Bingwu obtaining Guihai.[^64]

Gengshen and Xinyou Metal answer to Dui in the west; Jiayin and Yimao Wood image Zhen in the east. Therefore Jiayin obtaining Gengshen does not count as punishment, nor Yimao obtaining Xinyou as meeting a ghost. This is the proper embodiment of Wood as wife and Metal as husband, revealing the numinous transformation of left and right. Wood governs the ethereal soul; Metal the corporeal soul. The two stand apart on left and right and do not unite. If they can unite completely, spirit's transformative generation proceeds without interruption. Likewise, Gengshen obtaining Yimao or Xinyou obtaining Jiayin does not count as the Primal Affliction: this is the operation of adaptation and transformation.[^64]

Wuchen and Wuxu Earth form a meeting of Kui and Gang. Like Qian and Kun's substantial virtue, they cover, bear, and contain life; they must not be treated as Reverse Echo. Wuchen and Wuxu do not count as a clash: Earth occupies its proper position and the stems preserve their primal meeting. Jichou and Jiwei are noble spirits maintaining loyalty and constancy. These four True Earths possess the Way by which all things begin and end. Who but a great and noble person could fully embody such virtue? Moreover, each Spirit-Head Lu has its own spirit presiding over it, moving left and right within the six directions, waxing and waning through changes of good and ill fortune.[^65]

**Jichou Earth** is the Heavenly Yi Noble Person; **Jiwei Earth**, the Great Constant Fortune Spirit, which resolves the harm of the hundred baleful influences. When obtained at the proper time for use, it gives the joy of unexpected wealth. **Wuchen** is Hooked Array; **Wuxu**, Empty Sky. These Earth spirits often indicate transfers and changes: occupying command in an outlying province, going forth to guard a frontier, and an unsettled course of service.

**Dingsi** is the Soaring Serpent spirit. Inauspiciously placed, it operates inauspiciously; auspiciously placed, it receives good fortune. It brings many worries of bewilderment and a witty, slippery disposition. **Bingwu** is the Vermilion Bird spirit, answering to the embodiment of yang brightness and giving elegant, richly ornamented writing. **Jiayin** is the Azure Dragon spirit: generous in giving, helpful to the multitude, and benefiting from all four directions. **Yimao** is the Six Harmonies spirit, governing emerging growth and splendor, gentle, yielding, and compliant with its companions.

**Renzi** is the Heavenly Empress spirit. It governs hidden beneficence and heavenly virtue, beautiful appearance, and considerable authority. **Guihai** is the Dark Warrior spirit, the final limit of yin and yang, containing hidden, latent qi. It follows downward like a flowing stream. Although it has great intelligence, it does not produce an imposing, loftily accomplished person. In a favorable course it is peaceful and secure; in an adverse course, treacherous and lawless.

**Gengshen** is the White Tiger spirit, favorable for military affairs but not for civil letters. Its nature embraces the Way and soars alone: good within and severe without, stern in appearance yet tender within, possessing humanity and righteousness, and fond of secluded places. **Xinyou** is the Great Yin spirit, holding the qi of austere cutting and a manner of purity and integrity. It gives literary skill, a sharp tongue, and talent rare in an age. Yet in every case, nearness or distance, rest or flourishing must also be considered to determine the disposition, calamities, and blessings of the person who encounters it.[^65]

## 10. The Thirty Nayin Images in Detail

### 10.1. Jiazi and Yichou — Metal Within the Sea
#### 甲子乙丑海中金

<!-- source: L297-L299 -->
Metal Within the Sea is treasure stored in the dragon's palace, a pearl conceived in the water-dragon's chamber. Although its emergence depends upon a void or clash, its becoming a vessel does not rely upon Fire's power. Thus Dongfang Shuo had good reason to name it after clams and pearl-bearing shells. The *Subtle Selections* has the configuration **Pearl Hidden in the Deep Sea**: Jiazi meeting Guihai. This uses no Fire and encounters a void. It also has **Pearl in the Shell Illuminating the Moon**: Jiazi meeting Jiwei. This seeks the transformation of combination and an exchange of Noble Person influences. Sea Metal has no visible form and cannot emerge without a void or clash; Yichou is Metal's Storehouse and cannot be cast without flourishing Fire.[^66]

For example, Jiazi meeting Wuyin or Gengwu is Earth generating Metal; Yichou meeting Bingyin or Dingmao is Fire regulating Metal. If the heavenly stems also meet the Three Wonders, such configurations invariably confer distinction. The old explanation says that both Jiazi and Yichou like Furnace Fire. Bingyin is strong Fire and does not want Wood's assistance; Dingmao is Fire in Defeat and does want it. Lamp Fire, Fire Below the Mountain, and Fire on the Mountain have slight natures and cannot refine Metal, so Wood's help is suitable. Fire in Heaven cannot control Metal: ordinary Fire must be borrowed, with Water also giving relief. Freedom from clash and break is favorable. Thunderbolt Fire controls it in the same branch position and indicates a dull, clouded nature; if the luck cycles repeat the offense, great misfortune follows.

Wood offers no effective transformation here, because this Metal is not yet a vessel and cannot restrain other things. But if the month and day contain Fire and the hour meets Wood, the Wood is judged as Wealth and is auspicious. Well-and-Spring, Streambed, Great Stream, and Celestial River Water should not be encountered without Fire. Sea Water, too, is said to be unsuitable. Yet the *Subtle Selections* judges it a prestigious configuration. Reasoning it through, Jiazi meeting Guihai encounters generation and moves toward Qian: what could be wrong with that?[^48]

It is said that if Roadside, Rooftop, Wall-Plaster, City-Wall, or Great Post-Road Earth appears in the day or hour, with no Fire regulation anywhere in the heavenly stems, earthly branches, or nayin, the person will be lowly and die in middle age. I do not find this invariably so: the two Earth pairs Gengwu–Xinwei and Wuyin–Jimao have an affectionate relationship in their transformations. Jiazi meeting Gengwu is Kan and Li assisting one another; meeting Xinwei gives an Officer Star carrying a Noble Person. Yichou meeting Gengwu gives an exchange of Officer and Noble Person. Jiazi meeting Wuyin encounters Birth in a place of Extinction; meeting Jimao gives a transformation through heavenly-stem combination. Yichou meeting Wuyin likewise encounters Birth in Extinction; meeting Jimao returns to Lu while carrying Wealth. With the other Earths, one must still distinguish the Lu, Horse, and Noble Person derived from the birth year before reaching a sound judgment.[^67]

Sandy Earth buries Metal and is indeed unsuitable; even Fire does not make it auspicious. Among Metals appearing in the pillars, Metal Within Sand and White Pewter are themselves unfinished, as is Metal Within the Sea: neither helps the other, so without Fire they are unsuitable. Sword Edge and Hairpin-and-Bracelet are finished Metal and give assistance when encountered. The *Subtle Selections* calls this **Leaving the Old Body and Transforming into Spirit**, indicating distinction. Gold Leaf Metal is also favorable. Jiazi meeting Jiazi, or Yichou meeting Yichou, means members of one kind supporting one another. A single Yin in the pillars is welcome; Wuyin is called **A Piece of Jade from Mount Kunlun**. Jiazi meeting Yichou is called **Stems and Branches Strung Like Pearls**. It cannot dispense with Fire's refining: this has the same meaning as the Metal Spirit configuration in the Ziping method.[^68]

### 10.2. Renyin and Guimao — Gold Leaf Metal
#### 壬寅癸卯金泊金

<!-- source: L301-L303 -->
Gold Leaf Metal embellishes cups and dishes and adds brilliance to palaces and rooms. To beat it thin, one must rely upon another Metal; to apply its colors, one must borrow Water's power. This Metal is exceedingly slight: without Wood it has nothing to adhere to. Of Woods, Level-Ground Wood is best. With it present, Fire should not appear; Fire indicates premature death. Meeting the sun makes it manifest by daylight. These two Fires are contrary and should not be seen together. Fire Below the Mountain or Fire on the Mountain, assisted by clear Water, is also auspicious. Furnace Fire alone is especially avoided, controlling it in the same branch position. This Metal is too thin to return to its original substance: early death is certain. The same judgment applies to the allotted periods and luck cycles.[^69]

Well-and-Spring, Streambed, and Celestial River Water are clear and welcome in the day and hour, but Wood must be present in the month's command for good fortune. Great Stream, Long-Flowing, and Great Sea Water are turbid. Stream and flowing Water indicate drifting and wandering; Great Sea Water without Wood as a foundation indicates violence and injury. Sword-Edge and Hairpin-and-Bracelet Metal can be ornamented, allowing this Metal to assist in the completion of a useful transformation; they are therefore welcome. Metal Within Sand, Metal Within the Sea, and White Pewter are of no benefit, though Fire's assistance can make them auspicious; without it, the outcome is inauspicious.

City-Wall and Wall-Plaster Earth provide a support upon which it can settle. City-Wall Earth commonly indicates living under another's roof; Wall-Plaster Earth with Wood gives distinction. Add Lamp Fire, whose radiance shines upon it, and authority and rank follow. Bingxu Earth contains Fire. If the stems and branches are not excessively fiery, this too is a prestigious configuration. In the *Subtle Selections*, a Metal life meeting Wuyin is **A Piece of Jade from Mount Kunlun**; Guimao meeting Jimao is **The Jade Hare Rising in the East**. These follow the same principle as the preceding Sea Metal configurations.[^70]

### 10.3. Gengchen and Xinsi — White Pewter Metal
#### 庚辰辛巳白鑞金

<!-- source: L305-L307 -->
White Pewter Metal is a piece of jade from Mount Kunlun, a precious object left upon the banks of the Luo. The light of sun and moon rests within it together; the qi of yin and yang gathers and congeals. Its form is luminous and its body pure: this is Metal's true color. This Metal chiefly welcomes refinement by Fire, specifically the blazing Fire of the Furnace. Yet when Gengchen encounters it without Water's relief, poverty and early death follow; Xinsi, by contrast, is judged distinguished. Si is Metal's place of Birth, and meeting Bingyin transforms into Water while encountering a Noble Person. Fire Below the Mountain gives early glory and distinction, but Water's assistance is still required.[^71]

Well-and-Spring and Great Stream Water both make prestigious configurations. Geng's Officer is Ding; Xin's Officer is Bing. Thus Geng meeting Dingchou possesses both Officer and Noble Person. For Xin, Bingzi is less clear than Guisi: [with Guisi] if not distinguished, then wealthy. Much Wood is of no benefit, because this Metal cannot control it. But if the pillars contain Fire without qi, Wood is needed to generate it; with Lu, Horse, and Noble Person, the result is auspicious. Of Earth, only what can grind and polish it into a vessel is auspicious; other Earth is useless. Metal Within the Sea and Metal Within Sand are avoided because they submerge it, though Fire in the day and hour brings glory. Clear Metal assisted by Water does not even need Fire; only clashes and punishments are feared. The *Subtle Selections* has **The Fierce Tiger Roaring into the Wind**: this Metal meeting Xinsi or Yisi in the day or hour.[^72]

### 10.4. Jiawu and Yiwei — Metal Within Sand
#### 甲午乙未砂中金

<!-- source: L309-L311 -->
Metal Within Sand has a hard form spread through the ground, a precious substance concealed in sand. Only through washing does it become a treasure; only through human work does it attain value. Without Furnace Fire this Metal cannot be regulated. Yet when Jiawu meets Bingyin, Fire is born within Yin and Yin and Wu join into a configuration. Without Earth in Birth among the pillars, it becomes parched. Add Wood's assistance, and illness or early death follows. Bingwu, though Water by nayin, is pure Fire in stem and branch; its further appearance is especially inauspicious.

Fire on the Mountain, Fire Below the Mountain, and Lamp Fire, generated by Wood, can regulate this Metal. Clear Water must also bring relief; then youthful glory and distinction are assured. Meeting the Dragon Fire of Wuzi or Jichou brings the subtle intercourse of Zi and Wu and the principle of Jia and Ji combining to transform: distinction follows. Mix in ordinary Fire, however, and it ceases to be exceptional. Of Waters, only clear, pure Well-and-Spring, Streambed, and Celestial River Water are suitable. Long-Flowing and Great Stream Water move rather than remain still; together they set the Metal afloat and leave it unsettled. Sea Water is especially avoided.[^73]

What direct relation does Wood have here? When Fire is weak, Wood's generation and support are welcome. Better still are exchanges of Lu, Horse, and Noble Person, attending and arching toward one another. If the pillars have no Fire, even one or two Woods bring danger. Jiawu meeting Jisi, however, is called **Gathering Fine Metal from the Yellow Desert**, a prestigious configuration. Metal arising in sand is auspicious when it obtains the proper transformation. Further Sandy Earth, however, threatens to bury it. Roadside and Great Post-Road Earth are also avoided, though Fire may make them tolerable. Of City-Wall Earth, Yiwei welcomes Wuyin: this is **Gathering Fine Metal from Green Sand**, a prestigious configuration. Bingxu Earth alone is welcome for the Fire Storehouse it contains. Other Metal is of the same kind; clear qi is most welcome and ranks highest. Metal Within the Sea and White Pewter are also acceptable with Fire's regulation.

### 10.5. Renshen and Guiyou — Sword-Edge Metal
#### 壬申癸酉劒鋒金

<!-- source: L313-L315 -->
Sword-Edge Metal is the White Sovereign exercising authority, its hardness born of a hundred refinings. Red radiance shoots toward the Dipper and Ox; the white blade congeals like frost and snow. This Metal's transformation cannot be brought forth without Water. Great Stream or Sea Water in the day and hour makes a superior configuration. Well-and-Spring or Streambed Water is favorable with Thunderbolt's assistance, or with Yimao's thunder. Even without thunder or lightning, it is still **Metal White and Water Clear**, especially auspicious in autumn birth. Long-Flowing Water in the day or hour, specifically Renchen, is **The Precious Sword Transforming into an Azure Dragon**. Guisi is also acceptable, though the sword then cannot undergo the same transformative change. Guichou, however, gives **The Sword's Qi Piercing the Dipper**, most auspicious.[^74]

Pine-and-Cypress and Willow Wood are also auspicious, though they bring many gatherings and separations. Great Forest and Level-Ground Wood are disliked because Earth interferes, indicating toil. Of Fire, the Divine Dragon is favorable when yin and yang meet: Renshen with Jichou, or Guiyou with Wuzi, makes a superior configuration. Fire in Heaven or Furnace Fire without Water's rescue cuts life short. Earth in general is inauspicious because it buries the blade. Wall-Plaster and City-Wall Earth alone can grind, temper, and sharpen its edge; these two are acceptable.

Metal welcomes its own kind. Renshen meeting Renshen, or Guiyou meeting Guiyou, with Wood for it to control, is called **Coiled Roots and Entangled Knots**: these distinguish the sharp instrument from the dull. Without Wood, illness is indicated. Among Sea, Sand, and White Pewter Metal, Yichou alone is auspicious. Hairpin-and-Bracelet Metal is already fashioned and is suitable. But unfinished Metal in the pillars adds nothing to the sword and is especially avoided; meeting it indicates a dull, frenzied disposition. [At Xu] the Metal becomes mixed; strong Fire in the hour is then appropriate. In general, Sword Edge is the keenest of Metals: it needs Water's moistening, not Fire's punishment. If Yin and Si appear and complete the three punishments [with Shen], great misfortune follows.[^75]

### 10.6. Gengxu and Xinhai — Hairpin-and-Bracelet Metal
#### 庚戌辛亥釵釧金

<!-- source: L317-L319 -->
Hairpin-and-Bracelet Metal adorns beauty and the hair, lending brilliance to smooth skin: a treasure nestled against red and green, a precious thing beside jade pillows and fragrant sleep. Kept within the women's chambers, this Metal wants only quiet Water. Well, Streambed, Great Stream, and Long-Flowing Water are auspicious, though too much sets it afloat. Sea Water brings poverty and early death. Celestial River Water does no harm to Xinhai. Bingwu is true Fire and is avoided by Gengxu, because Wu and Xu draw together into a Fire configuration and injure this Metal.[^76]

The sun's Fire makes it shine for a daytime birth; Lamp Fire makes it shine at night. Both are therefore suitable. But Jiachen and Yisi clash with Gengxu and Xinhai: cross-pairing yin and yang is the subtle arrangement. Wuzi–Jichou and Bingwu–Dingwei stand opposed; these two Fires must not be encountered together, or poverty or early death follows. Gengxu especially avoids Furnace Fire. Xinhai meeting it is somewhat auspicious because Bing and Xin combine into Water. Fire Below the Mountain and Fire on the Mountain are both unsuitable, though Water's relief can make them acceptable. With Water in the pillars, this Metal is **Placed Within Its Casket**; it is auspicious when accompanied by blessings and Noble Person influences.[^77]

Sandy Earth generates and nourishes it. With Streambed Water also assisting, glory, wealth, and distinction follow. Sword-Edge Metal is welcome, completing an effective transformation. Gold Leaf Metal adds brilliance to hairpins and bracelets, but a little Fire must assist. Apart from these two, other Metals are useless. If the life contains only Metal and Water without admixture, it is **Metal White and Water Clear**. Infer the result through a living, adaptable application of the method.

### 10.7. Wuzi and Jichou — Thunderbolt Fire
#### 戊子己丑霹靂火

<!-- source: L321-L323 -->
Thunderbolt Fire is a single thread of radiance, the command of the nine heavens: lightning flashes like golden snakes; clouds drive onward like galloping iron horses. This Fire must draw upon wind, Water, and thunder before it can transform. If even one of these is supplied among the Five Phases, free passage and success follow. Great Sea Water Guihai in the day or hour is **Leading the Ordinary into the Sacred**: best for Jichou, next best for Wuzi. Great Stream Water Yimao is **Thunder and Fire Transforming**, auspicious for Jichou but avoided by Wuzi. Chen and Si are wind; meeting them in the luck cycles is especially favorable. Water in Heaven is called **Already Across**, indicating good fortune. Those who encounter it possess numinous discernment and exceptional intelligence. Long-Flowing Water is useless. Although Streambed Water controls it in the same position, this is divine Fire and does not fear it; with wind, it also becomes conspicuous.[^78]

Among Woods, Xinmao has thunder, Great Forest has wind, and Level-Ground has the Gate of Heaven: these assist this Fire. Other Woods are useless. Roadside Earth with Xun is auspicious. Of Sandy Earth, Dingsi has wind; Jimao has thunder. With spring Water assisting, these too indicate distinction. Sword-Edge Metal with Water, Sea Metal with wind, and White Pewter with Streambed Water are all auspicious; other Metals are useless. Of Furnace Fire, Dingmao is auspicious. Wuzi meeting Bingyin is too dry, its nature violent, and indicates early death. Jichou may meet Bingshen, but Wuzi avoids it. Lamp Fire occupies Xun in the southeast and has wind: it is most suitable. Fire in Heaven, Wuwu or Jiwei, in the day or hour requires caution against punishment and control.[^79]

Further examination of the *Subtle Selections* finds **Fierce Wind, Thunder, and Rain**: Thunderbolt meeting Celestial River Water. It also has **Heaven and Earth Divided at the Center**: Wuzi meeting Wuwu; and **Thunder Finding Its Gate**: Wuzi or Jichou meeting Mao in the day or hour. These three configurations [including the pure Wuwu case] differ from the preceding account.[^80]

### 10.8. Bingyin and Dingmao — Furnace Fire
#### 丙寅丁卯爐中火

<!-- source: L325-L327 -->
For Furnace Fire, Heaven and Earth are the furnace and yin and yang the charcoal. It raises radiance through the universe and performs its smelting between Qian and Kun. This Fire flames upward and welcomes Wood's generation. Level-Ground Wood is best. Bingyin meeting Jihai is called **Heavenly Yi Noble Person**; meeting Wuxu is **Returning to the Storehouse**. Both are therefore auspicious; Dingmao ranks next. Yet Bingyin Fire is in its own Birth and can manage without Wood, whereas Dingmao Fire is in its own Defeat and without Wood is inauspicious. This Fire takes Metal for its use; only when Metal comes does the occasion for transformation arise. But Dingmao without Wood, meeting Metal as well, indicates a life of toil.

At Yin, much Wood makes Fire blaze. Without Water to control it, early death follows. Mao can meet three or four Woods without harm. Gengyin and Xinmao generate it in the same position; Renwu and Guiwei transform into true Fire. Yin meeting these in abundance commonly indicates violence, illness, or early death. If the heavenly or earthly components already contain controlling Water, a middling lifespan is possible. Dingmao is unharmed. Though this Fire welcomes Metal as Wealth, Sword-Edge Metal is only somewhat acceptable for Yin. Dingmao Fire is already in Defeat and reaches Death or Extinction at Shen and You: how can it control Metal? Poverty or early death follows. Sea, Sand, and White Pewter Metal are welcome only with Wood's generation; Hairpin-and-Bracelet and Gold Leaf are useless.[^81]

Before Earth appears, Metal and Wood should already be present. Earth is then welcome as a lodging for the Fire, keeping it from excessive dryness. City-Wall, Rooftop, and Wall-Plaster Earth are suitable because they are Earth already made into useful forms. When considering Water, Wood is first desired in the natal configuration; otherwise Water is welcome when Fire is abundant. The clear Water of Heaven is auspicious for a Yin life, but disliked in Mao without Wood. Of Great Sea Water, Bingyin meeting Renxu is a Blessing Storehouse; meeting Guihai is an Officer Star with combination, half inauspicious and half auspicious. Well, Streambed, Great Stream, and Long-Flowing Water are all inauspicious, unless Wood is present, in which case this judgment does not apply.

Fire meeting its own kind is auspicious if the day and month are otherwise weak or defeated. Thunderbolt Fire itself offers no benefit. But with Wood's generation and Sea Water in the day or hour, it is suitable; Water without Wood is inauspicious. Fire in Heaven requires Rooftop Earth as a shelter. Lamp Fire brings Xun's wind and also fans the **Lost Spirit** into activity: judgment must incorporate the whole transformation. Thus **Bingyin–Dingyou–Jiyou–Bingyin**, although its Fire lacks qi, does not fail to attain great distinction. But **Bingyin–Jiawu–Jisi–Bingyin** is drifting and unsettled.[^82]

### 10.9. Jiachen and Yisi — Lamp Fire
#### 甲辰乙巳覆燈火

<!-- source: L329-L331 -->
Lamp Fire holds its radiance in a golden bowl and breathes its splendor from a jade stand. It illuminates places the sun and moon do not reach and brightens hours when Heaven and Earth are not yet light. This is the Fire that lights the human world at night, taking Wood as its wick and Water as its oil. Meeting yin is auspicious; meeting yang is unfavorable. Repetition of Chen or Si in the day or hour is especially avoided. If the earthly branches clash, there is fear that wind will extinguish the lamp, indicating early death. Some define the yin interval as Xu, Hai, Zi, and Chou; others as Wei through Hai.[^83]

Among the Five Phases, Wood is its root, and all Wood is favorable. With Officer Star, Lu, and Noble Person mutually supporting it, and the stems transforming into Water, the result is especially auspicious. Assistance in the allotted periods and luck cycles indicates great distinction. Of Waters, Well-and-Spring and Streambed are true oil; Long-Flowing is borrowed oil. The *Subtle Selections* configuration **Adding Oil to a Dim Lamp** follows this principle. Great Sea and Celestial River Water cannot serve as oil and indicate an ordinary life. Generally, this Fire's encounter with Water requires Wood's support. It does not welcome Water in Birth or Prosperity: excessive Water floods it and instead becomes inauspicious.

Gold Leaf Metal in the natal configuration reflects its light and gives especially refined distinction, but Water and Wood must also assist before prominence is possible. Metal Within Sand and Hairpin-and-Bracelet Metal are both auspicious. Sword Edge alone gives **The Lamp's Blossom Brushing the Sword**, especially auspicious. White Pewter is unsuitable. With Earth, guard against control and break. Wall-Plaster Earth can provide a place to rest; Rooftop Earth can shelter and cover it. Both appearing in the day and hour indicate blessings and distinction. Sandy Earth with Wood also provides food and clothing. Other Earth is useless.

This Fire loves its own kind but fears wind. Thunderbolt is the Fire of the dragon spirit's transformations and necessarily brings wind: the lamp can scarcely survive. Fire in Heaven and Furnace Fire are most inauspicious when encountered. The *Subtle Selections* also gives **The Kui Star** and **Pointing South**: a Jiachen person meeting Wu or Wei in the day or hour. It has **Fire and Earth Entering the Hall**: because this Fire fears wind, Bingxu or Dinghai Rooftop Earth in the day or hour places the lamp inside a house. Adding oil makes the configuration especially distinguished.[^84]

### 10.10. Wuwu and Jiwei — Fire in Heaven
#### 戊午己未天上火

<!-- source: L333-L335 -->
Fire in Heaven warms mountains and rivers and illuminates the universe: yang virtue's radiance set in the sky, yin essence's brightness emerging from the sea. Wuwu is the sun and therefore hard; Jiwei is the moon and therefore soft. Some say that the summer sun is hard and the winter sun warm. That explanation is false. Both require Xu and Hai as the Gate of Heaven, Mao and You as their gates of emergence and entry, and the southeast as the ground of their course: then they are auspicious.[^85]

This Fire meeting Wood is called **Stirring and Breaking**. Wind and Water must be present in the day or hour to make it effective. Great Forest Wood has Chen and Si; Pine-and-Cypress and Pomegranate have Mao and You. Therefore these three Woods alone indicate distinction. Wu can still meet much Wood; Wei with three or four Woods indicates a life of toil. Metal can reflect this Fire's light but cannot control or assist it. Hairpin-and-Bracelet Metal has Xu and Hai; Gold Leaf has Yin and Mao: both are auspicious. Sword-Edge Metal is **Displaying the Light of Sun and Moon**, certainly indicating an examination degree in youth. Other Metals bring harm.

Of Waters, Streambed is suitable, but Wuwu must meet Dingchou and Jiwei must meet Bingzi: yin and yang must cross-pair to bring blessings and distinction. With Wood in the pillars also giving nourishment, both wealth and rank follow. Great Stream has Yimao and Well-and-Spring has [Yiyou], allowing emergence and entry through the proper gates: both are auspicious. Celestial River Water brings the mutual aid of rain and dew and is not judged as control in the same position. Wuwu meeting Dingwei is also auspicious; Bingwu, however, obscures the light.[^86]

This Fire welcomes Lamp Fire, but other additional Fire makes it too dry. Thunderbolt carries cloud and rain, depriving sun and moon of their brightness and indicating dimness and confusion. Of Furnace Fire, Wu avoids Bingyin: Wu is hard Fire, and Bingyin immediately supplies Fire's Birth position. Without clear Water to rescue it, penal punishment and a violent death are indicated. Dingmao is somewhat acceptable. Sandy Earth supplies Xun's wind; Roadside, City-Wall, and Rooftop Earth are all auspicious. Metal and Wood further assisting in the pillars make them especially favorable.

The *Subtle Selections* gives **Sun and Moon Separately Displaying Their Excellence**: Wuwu meeting Mao, and Jiwei meeting You. For Mao, Yimao and Xinmao are the principal forms, Jimao and Dingmao secondary. For You, Yiyou and Guiyou are principal, Jiyou and Dingyou secondary. **The Sun Rising over Fusang** is the Sun-Displaying-Excellence configuration with Si or Wu also in the day or hour. **The Solar Disc at the Gnomon** is Wuwu born in a Wu month and meeting a Si or Wu day. Wuwu meeting Wuzi is also **Kan and Li in Their Proper Positions**.[^87]

**The Moon Rising from the Vast Sea** is the Moon-Displaying-Excellence configuration with You paired specifically with Yi or Gui. **The Moon Illuminating a Cold Pool** takes Ren, Gui, Hai, or Zi with Water nayin as the pool; autumn birth is necessary for distinction. **The Moon's Cassia Giving Fragrance** is a Jiwei person with three or four Woods gathering and arching toward it. It is related to **A Single Branch in the Cassia Grove**, but the Cassia Grove values few, while Fragrance values many: each takes its meaning from a different aspect. Among the inauspicious configurations, **The Sun Losing Its Brightness** means that Wuwu cannot bear overflowing Water; it dislikes excessive Water. **The Moon Thinly Eclipsed** asks how Jiwei could bear too much Earth; it dislikes heavy Earth. Only by considering these together can the principle be fully understood.[^88]

### 10.11. Bingshen and Dingyou — Fire Below the Mountain
#### 丙申丁酉山下火

<!-- source: L337-L339 -->
Fire Below the Mountain glimmers among grasses and flickers within flowers: light threading the leaves of a cold wood, color touching a robe from behind a curtain. Dongfang Shuo therefore called it firefly light. The *Subtle Selections* has **Fireflies Illuminating Water**: with autumn birth, it confers distinction as a high minister or director. Thus this Fire welcomes Water: Hai or Zi among the earthly branches, or Water nayin, together with a Shen or You month. Another account says that Fire Below the Mountain chiefly loves Wood and a mountain, with wind to increase its brilliance and give distinction. This account no longer treats it as firefly light.[^89]

Great Forest Wood has Chen and Si, which are wind; Mulberry Wood has Guichou, which is mountain. Pine-and-Cypress and Level-Ground Wood are most auspicious; wind's further assistance indicates distinction. Too much wind, however, scatters it and indicates early death. Of Waters it loves Well-and-Spring and Streambed, with Wood assisting: high rank and eminence follow. Great Sea Water is unsuitable, though with a mountain it too can answer to a prestigious configuration. Yin and Mao are the eastern territory where Wood flourishes and Fire is born, but only Jiayin Water is auspicious. Yimao is Zhen and has wind; encountering it is unfavorable. Without [supporting] Fire or mountain, and with Thunderbolt Fire added, early death follows. Water in Heaven is a sudden downpour and is unsuitable for this Fire. If mountain and Water have already provided nourishment and support, however, it does no great harm.[^90]

Metal in the natal configuration is auspicious when clear and refined. Without Wood, repeated Metal is judged as stealing qi. Yichou is mountain and indicates distinction. Other Metals, if free of control and break and accompanied by Noble Person, Lu, and Horse, are judged simply as Wealth. Sandy Earth has Chen and Si, which supply wind. With Wood and mountain assisting, it indicates great distinction; without them, an empty reputation. Of Fires, it avoids the sun and Thunderbolt. Lamp Fire is Xun and indicates manifest brilliance. In general, where Fire is present among the Five Phases, Wood's support is necessary for good fortune. Judge favorable and unfavorable allotted periods by the same principles.

### 10.12. Jiaxu and Yihai — Fire on the Mountain
#### 甲戌乙亥山頭火

<!-- source: L341-L343 -->
Fire on the Mountain burns through the wild and sweeps the plain, spreading as far as the eye can see: faintly, a slanting radiance at Heaven's edge; dimly, the setting sun upon a mountain summit. This is the Fire of burning the wilderness in the ninth month, consuming the withered grasses. In general, it needs mountain, Wood, and wind. Of Woods it favors Great Forest and Pine-and-Cypress: Chen and Si supply wind, while Yin and Mao return to Lu. With Guichou also making Wood upon a mountain, distinction follows. Without a mountain, Wood has nothing to rest upon and Fire nowhere to be seen; even wind will not make it shine conspicuously. Other Woods are useless and are considered only through Lu and Horse.

Streambed Water is suitable and called **Mutual Communication in Peace**, indicating good fortune. Clear Well-and-Spring Water, assisted by Wood, is also auspicious. Of Great Stream Water, Jiaxu meeting Jiayin or Yihai meeting Yimao gives true Lu: both are auspicious. Water in Heaven should take the form of rain and dew. Fire at Wu or Wei obtains suitable ground; with clear Water also giving relief, it avoids dryness and brings blessings. Otherwise, early death follows. Great Sea Water controls it in the same position and is most inauspicious; with a mountain present, it is somewhat acceptable.

Metal in the day or hour is Wealth, but mountain and Wood must assist for good fortune; without them it is inauspicious. Of Earth, Sandy Earth alone has Xun to fan this Fire. Other Earth is of no benefit. In general, this Fire without Wood and with much Earth indicates a lowly life. Furnace Fire is too fierce; Thunderbolt brings violent harm; the sun causes obscurity; Fire Below the Mountain brings contention and punishment. None is suitable. If the natal configuration carries two or three Fires and the allotted periods encounter Wood, unforeseen calamity or early death follows. Above all, this Fire greatly fears punishment and clash.

### 10.13. Renzi and Guichou — Mulberry Wood
#### 壬子癸丑桑柘木

<!-- source: L345-L347 -->
Mulberry Wood is the nourishing foundation of colored silk, the root of gauze and brocade: the fluttering sleeves of scholar and commoner, the orderly robes of sage and worthy. It feeds silkworms and makes bows; its uses are very great. It most loves Sandy Earth as a foundation, for Chen and Si are also the positions of the feeding silkworm. Punishment, clash, and mutual break are unsuitable. Roadside and Great Post-Road Earth are next in favor; other Earth is of no benefit.

Of Waters, it loves the rain and dew of the Celestial River. Long-Flowing, Great Stream, Streambed, and Well-and-Spring Water can all support it, but Earth must first provide a foundation; Lu and Noble Person make the arrangement especially subtle. Great Sea Water sets it drifting without rest; without Earth, it indicates early death. Of Fires, Lamp Fire is most auspicious, again because Chen and Si are the silkworm's positions. Furnace Fire occupies Yin and Mao, Wood's flourishing territory. Fire in Heaven and Thunderbolt Fire have an affectionate relation of combination and transformation with this Wood's stems and branches, and the subtle intercourse of Kan and Li. All are auspicious, but the various Fires should not be encountered repeatedly together.

Of Metals, Metal Within Sand is first. Sword Edge can trim and shape this Wood and ranks next. Hairpin-and-Bracelet and Gold Leaf require Earth as a foundation; clash or break makes them inauspicious. Among Woods it welcomes Gengyin and Xinmao: the weak joins the strong and the small becomes great, a prestigious configuration, auspicious even without Sandy Earth. Level-Ground, Cypress, and Pomegranate are inauspicious without Earth. Great Forest occupies the southeast, the silkworm-feeding positions; Earth nourishing it indicates great distinction. Willow gives **Mulberry and Willow Forming a Grove**, also a prestigious configuration, but spring or summer birth is required for good fortune.[^91]

### 10.14. Gengyin and Xinmao — Pine-and-Cypress Wood
#### 庚寅辛卯松柏木

<!-- source: L349-L351 -->
Pine-and-Cypress Wood is splashed with snow and gathered about by frost, reaching Heaven and covering Earth. Wind shakes it into the music of reed pipes; after rain, it spreads like banners and pennons. This Wood lies beneath Metal but occupies due east, and is therefore supremely flourishing. It most welcomes a mountain for its foundation and Water for moisture. The rain and dew of the Celestial River can nourish it. Of Streambed Water, Dingchou belongs to mountain and can provide a foundation; Bingzi is less favorable. Great Stream Water includes Yimao's thunder, which can stir it into growth, but wind and lightning are disliked as causing breakage. Great Sea Water with a mountain is auspicious; Guihai is clear and pure and is auspicious even without a mountain.

If the pillars contain Level-Ground Wood together with Rooftop Earth, this Wood has already become beams and pillars and needs neither mountain nor Water. Without those two, it is luxuriant timber in the mountains and does need both. Of Fire, it especially avoids Furnace Fire, which is generated in the same position. Add wind-bearing Wood, and ashes scatter and smoke vanishes; without Water among the Five Phases, life is cut short. Fire on the Mountain, Fire Below the Mountain, the sun, and Lamp Fire must not offend it. A Yin person particularly avoids Wuwu and Bingyin: Wood should not rush south, and Yin and Wu join into a Fire configuration. Xinmao is unharmed. Thunderbolt Fire can encourage its growth, but ordinary Fire added in the luck cycles is inauspicious.[^92]

Roadside Earth seems insufficient to confer distinction, yet without dead Wood its blessings are genuine. Great Post-Road Earth without a mountain brings poverty and early death; adding Sea Water is especially inauspicious. Of Metal, Yichou is both Seal and mountain. Gold Leaf Metal flourishes in its own position and is auspicious. Sword Edge can cut and hew; with Wall-Plaster Earth to complete the arrangement and pine and cypress supporting one another, it indicates distinction. Great Forest has wind, and Willow joins Fire: these two Woods are especially avoided. Of Mulberry, Guichou is mountain and can assist. Pomegranate is Xinyou Metal transforming instead into dead Wood; with the proper transformation it is auspicious.[^93]

The *Subtle Selections* has **The Green Pine Displaying Its Excellence in Winter**: a person born under this Wood whose month, day, and hour belong to the three winter months attains distinction. There are also the configurations **The Day Joining Xinmao** and **The Month Meeting Gengyin**. Although these take Wuwu or Jiwei people born in summer or autumn as their examples, it is specifically these two Woods that confer distinction.[^94]

### 10.15. Wuchen and Jisi — Great Forest Wood
#### 戊辰己巳大林木

<!-- source: L353-L355 -->
Great Forest Wood has branches and trunks that shake the wind, boughs that hold up the moon: the virtue of towering above ravines and rising into Heaven, the power to reach the clouds and screen the sun. This Wood is born in the southeast, at the meeting of spring and summer, and grows into a forest. It depends entirely upon Gen's Earth as its source. Guichou is mountain: with no damage or collapse in the Three Fates, it gives the richest blessings, authority, and rank. Wuchen ranks first, Jisi second.

Roadside Earth bears it. Wuchen meeting Xinwei gives a Noble Person; Jisi meeting Gengwu gives Lu: both indicate blessings. Wall-Plaster or Rooftop Earth, together with Sword-Edge Metal, means that the forest's timber is taken for beams and pillars: a most auspicious completed configuration. Without this, it is luxuriant woodland in the mountains. Whether alive or dead, this Wood always wants Earth. A Ji person meeting Jia may be said to transform into Earth, but this is less subtle than a complete Earth configuration of Chen, Xu, Chou, and Wei. If the Wood is already dead beneath the mountain, Jiaxu or Yihai burning it indicates misfortune and early death. Lamp Fire is generated in the same position: Yisi is less auspicious than Jiachen. Thunderbolt and the sun can both foster its growth and are auspicious even in the luck cycles. Yet these two Fires should not appear together and contend for mastery; Earth must provide a foundation before they can be used.[^95]

Of Celestial River Water, Wuchen meeting Dingwei carries a Noble Person. Even without Earth or mountain it provides food and clothing: this is **The Numinous Raft Entering the Celestial River**, properly applying to autumn or winter birth, in Death or Extinction. Repeated Great Stream or Sea Water brings poverty and early death; a mountain makes it somewhat acceptable. The *Subtle Selections* has **The Azure Dragon Riding the Sea**: Wuchen meeting Guihai, conferring distinction. Of Streambed Water, Dingchou is most auspicious; Bingzi is less so. Metals in general are unsuitable. Sea Metal has Yichou as mountain; Sword Edge can be used with Rooftop or Wall-Plaster Earth as its basis. Other Metals are useless and indicate early death or low station.

Of Woods it welcomes Mulberry, Guichou alone being especially subtle. Level-Ground Wood still requires Roadside Earth: this is called **A Level Forest in Open Country**. Pine-and-Cypress occupies the east, a place of Birth and Prosperity. With Guichou in the pillars, obtaining Pine-and-Cypress gives **Dense Shade**, the finest arrangement.

### 10.16. Renwu and Guiwei — Willow Wood
#### 壬午癸未楊柳木

<!-- source: L357-L359 -->
Willow Wood sways gracefully along the Sui embankments and moves lightly in the Han gardens: ten thousand threads spun by no silkworm, a thousand ribbons sewn by no needle. Wu and Wei are Wood's Death and Tomb; Ren and Gui are its nourishment and moisture. For a foundation this Wood particularly loves Sandy Earth. Meeting Gen's mountain, it leans upon it and sways; Metal at Yin or Mao gives it suitable ground in the east. Xinchou has a mountain and is better than Gengzi. Wuyin is auspicious, but Jimao is better still. Bingchen and Dingsi, however, dislike the opposing clash of Xu and Hai.[^96]

Great Post-Road Earth with Chou is a post-road beside a mountain and is somewhat acceptable. Without Chou, this Earth alone indicates early death and low station. Roadside Earth occupies the same positions, again bringing Death and Tomb; appearing in the day or hour, it indicates a lowly, weak person. Of Rooftop Earth, Renwu meeting Dinghai is auspicious because Ding and Ren combine and transform; Bingxu is less favorable. Well-and-Spring, Long-Flowing, Great Stream, and Streambed Water are all auspicious, with the positions of combination and transformation especially favorable.

Bingwu and Dingwei have Bing and Ding as true Fire, while Wu and Wei are also Fire. This Wood reaches Death and Tomb at Wu and Wei, so Renwu meeting them is greatly inauspicious. Guiwei is somewhat more tolerable, and with other Water bringing relief there is no harm. This Wood already contains Fire at Wu and Wei; further Fire may stir it up and injure longevity. Of Lamp Fire, Yisi has wind that breaks the Wood and is inauspicious. Furnace Fire occupies Yin and Mao, where Wood flourishes, and is instead auspicious. With Thunderbolt Fire, Renwu meeting Jichou or Guiwei meeting Wuzi is a cross-pairing of yin and yang. Sandy Earth as a foundation then indicates distinction. But direct Zi–Wu or Chou–Wei opposition is not auspicious.

Metal meeting this Wood does not in itself produce a useful transformation. Hairpin-and-Bracelet and Gold Leaf, however, are welcome as completed objects. Although Sea, White Pewter, Sword Edge, and Sand Metal are avoided, the severity must be assessed through Lu, Noble Person, Virtue, and harmful influences. Pine-and-Cypress Wood gives **Leaving the Old Body and Transforming into Spirit**, indicating distinction. Of Mulberry, Guichou is mountain: with this support, it forms a grove and is auspicious. Gengshen and Xinyou meet Wood already in Death and Extinction and subject it to Metal's control: weakness encountering smallness, indicating a lowly person. Yet the *Subtle Selections* has **Red Blossoms and Green Willows**: this Wood meeting Pomegranate with spring or summer birth is not judged lowly. It also has **Willow Trailing Gold**: this Wood born in one of the three spring months, with a single Metal in the hour. Xinhai, Jiazi, Guimao, and Xinsi are especially subtle, because they are the Lu and Noble Person positions of Ren and Gui.[^97]

### 10.17. Gengshen and Xinyou — Pomegranate Wood
#### 庚申辛酉石榴木

<!-- source: L361-L363 -->
Pomegranate Wood has a nature pungent as ginger and flowers red as Fire. A few fruits hang clustered on the branch; within their chambers, many seeds gleam. Stem and branch are pure Metal, yet the nayin is Wood: this is Wood transformed from its ordinary nature. It may be moved into a pot and arranged as a miniature mountain. It therefore welcomes worked Earth as its foundation: City-Wall Earth is best, Rooftop Earth next. But yin and yang must cross-pair. Bing and Xin, Ding and Geng, then exchange Officers; Wu and Xin, Si and Yin exchange Seals, indicating good fortune.[^98]

Roadside, Wall-Plaster, Great Post-Road, and Sandy Earth are also auspicious with a mountain's assistance; without one, what use are they? Of Metals, Metal Within Sand is most auspicious. Gold Leaf has Water and Wood in its stems and branches but Metal as nayin; Pomegranate has Metal in stems and branches but Wood as nayin. Each leaves its original nature, exchanges with the other, and returns to flourishing: Wood flourishes at Yin and Mao, Metal at Shen and You. Each obtains its place. This is **Achievement Equal to Creative Transformation**, indicating great distinction. Of Sea Metal, Yichou is mountain and is auspicious with Water's further help; Wall-Plaster or City-Wall Earth is also acceptable. Sword Edge controls it in the same position and is most inauspicious. But if Sand Metal is already present and can restrain its harm, it does no injury.

Celestial River Water supplies nourishing rain and dew; clear Well-and-Spring, Great Stream, and Streambed Water irrigate it. Great Sea Water floods excessively, bringing poverty or early death; Gen's Earth makes it somewhat acceptable. The sun and Thunderbolt Fire are welcome but should not appear together. Furnace Fire occupies Yin and Mao, flourishing positions, and is intrinsically auspicious; additional Fire makes it inauspicious. If this Wood is born in the fifth month and the day and hour carry only one Fire, it is called **The Pomegranate Spouting Fire**, indicating distinction.

Mulberry, Great Forest, and Willow Wood are all welcome. Mulberry's Guichou supplies mountain; Great Forest's Wuchen allows it to leave its old form; Willow gives **Red Blossoms and Green Willows**. All indicate achievement and reputation. Pine-and-Cypress makes it strong; Level-Ground makes it great. With no other interfering elements, it is **Green Encircling and Red Surrounding**, also indicating wealth and distinction. With City-Wall Earth as its foundation and Water luck cycles assisting, it enjoys abundant blessings in ease for the longest duration.

### 10.18. Wuxu and Jihai — Level-Ground Wood
#### 戊戌己亥平地木

<!-- source: L365-L367 -->
Level-Ground Wood is a newly sprouting shoot, a branch just beginning to unfold. It depends upon rain and dew and dislikes accumulated snow and frost. It is luxuriant timber upon the ground, the Wood of human dwellings. Wuxu is the ridgebeam; Jihai the supporting beam. They most suitably meet one another in exchange, with Earth as their foundation. Roadside Earth makes the proper configuration; Zi or Wu makes it especially distinguished, because Zi and Wu are the upright pillars of Heaven and Earth. Rooftop, Wall-Plaster, and City-Wall Earth all draw support from this Wood. Transformations of elevation within the arrangement are especially auspicious. Sandy and Great Post-Road Earth are useless; appearing in the day or hour, they indicate disaster and early death.[^99]

Of Fires it loves the sun and Thunderbolt, which make it most conspicuous. Furnace Fire with Water brings blessings; Lamp Fire without wind is secure. Other Fires without Water are inauspicious. This is already fashioned Wood and should not meet Sword Edge, though other Wood assisting can make it acceptable. Gold Leaf adorns it with brilliance. If the heavenly stems combine, the earthly branches flourish, and Roadside Earth also supplies a foundation, great distinction follows. Other Metals are useless. Celestial River Water moistens it and is auspicious. Great Stream and Sea Water without a mountain are inauspicious. Well-and-Spring and Streambed are auspicious, especially Jiashen for combination and Dingchou for mountain.[^100]

Great Forest Wood has wind that shakes it and indicates a shortened life. Of Mulberry, Guichou is most auspicious. Renzi gives distinction when a Jihai person meets it, but a Wuxu person cannot bear it. Pine-and-Cypress supports Level-Ground Wood as beams and pillars; with Earth's further help, it indicates distinction. Generally, this Wood dislikes Metal and welcomes Water and Earth. Born in the three winter months, with Yin or Mao in the hour, it is **Spring Returning to the Cold Valley**, also judged distinguished.

### 10.19. Gengzi and Xinchou — Wall-Plaster Earth
#### 庚子辛丑壁上土

<!-- source: L369-L371 -->
Wall-Plaster Earth rests upon pillars and relies on beams, raising doors and establishing households. Its virtue is to keep out heat and cold; its work, to screen against frost and snow. This is the Earth of human walls. Without Level-Ground Wood, what could support it? Zi and Wu are the upright pillars of Heaven and Earth, and meeting them is especially auspicious and joyful. Any Wood can provide its principal support. Gengyin and Xinmao also serve as beams and pillars, but Shen or You clashing and breaking them, or Zi and Mao punishing one another, is a concern. Great Forest carries wind; without load-bearing Earth, and with ordinary Fire added, undertakings are difficult to complete and poverty, low station, and early death follow.[^101]

It loves Roadside Earth, called the load-bearer. Rooftop and City-Wall Earth can protect it and are both auspicious. Fire gives no useful transformation at all. Although the sun and Thunderbolt are said to illuminate it, obstruction and danger remain in the end. If the natal configuration already contains Wood, Fire assists the ghost, burning and controlling it. Encountering Fire again in the luck cycles indicates calamity and life cut short; Water in the pillars giving relief makes it somewhat tolerable. Of Waters, Jiashen is most auspicious and Yiyou next. Heaven's rain and dew are also auspicious. Great Sea Water sets things afloat: how can this Earth remain secure? Even with a foundation it is inauspicious.

Among Metals, Gold Leaf alone is loved. If Wood is already present in the life, distinction follows: a palace or great hall is completed, resplendent in gold and kingfisher blue—a display none but the imperial court would dare employ. Sword Edge injures it; other Metals are useless.

### 10.20. Wuyin and Jimao — City-Wall Earth
#### 戊寅己卯城頭土

<!-- source: L373-L375 -->
City-Wall Earth is the jade rampart of the heavenly capital, the golden wall of the imperial city: a dragon coiled for a thousand miles, a tiger crouching over the four corners. This Earth may be completed or uncompleted, and the two must be judged separately. Meeting Roadside Earth makes it completed Earth, with no need for Fire. Without Roadside Earth it is uncompleted and must have Fire. Generally, City-Wall Earth needs Wood. Of Willow, Guiwei is best and Renwu avoided. Of Mulberry, Guichou is first and Renzi second. Gengyin and Xinmao control it in the same position, making the city collapse and lose stability: how could it shelter people? If Wood appears without accompanying support, judge only through Noble Person, Lu, and Horse.

Water with a mountain gives conspicuous distinction: Jiashen and Dingchou are both auspicious. Celestial River Water's nourishment is also auspicious. Thunderbolt and Great Sea are avoided, though Renxu is not avoided. Combinations and transformations are judged auspicious. This Earth loves Roadside Earth, but guard against the various Fires. Great Post-Road Earth with a mountain must be judged distinguished; alone, without a foundation, it gives poverty, early death, solitude, and hardship. Among Metals, White Pewter alone is feared because of Xun: the two obstruct one another. Other Metals are useless and must also be judged through Noble Person, Lu, and Horse.[^102]

### 10.21. Bingchen and Dingsi — Sandy Earth
#### 丙辰丁巳砂中土

<!-- source: L377-L379 -->
Sandy Earth is deposited by returning waves and formed along the watery shoals: the palace where dragons and snakes coil unseen, the ground where hills and valleys change places. This Earth is clear and refined and particularly welcomes clear Metal to nourish it. With further pure, clean Earth, it indicates early distinction. Hairpin-and-Bracelet, Sand, Sword Edge, and Gold Leaf are four clear, refined Metals that assist it. A Bing person meeting Xinhai gives Bing and Ding entering Qian's gate, called **A Long Rainbow Riding the Sea**. There is also the interpretation **Stars Arching toward the North**: both are prestigious configurations. Water embracing the arrangement makes it supremely auspicious. Without Water, Fire in Heaven illuminating it in the day or hour is also acceptable.[^103]

For example, **Bingchen–Yiwei–Guiyou–Wuwu** has two Metals nourishing it but no Water at all. The sun's Fire illuminates it, and it is therefore distinguished. **Dingsi–Guimao–Jiwei–Renshen** likewise has two Metals nourishing it and Fire in Heaven shining upon it, and is distinguished. Yet Wuwu is too dry and Jiwei somewhat gentler: their indications for length of life differ. Clear Well-and-Spring and Streambed Water are auspicious. With Metal to nourish the Water, distinction follows. But if this Earth has already obtained Metal's nourishment and Sea Water appears in the day or hour, the transformation is spoiled. Guihai is light and clear; a Bing person meeting it, even in an allotted period or luck cycle, gains conspicuous glory. Other Water is useless.[^104]

Of Fire, it loves the sun, giving the configuration **The Vermilion Bird Soaring into the Sky**, which indicates distinction. Fire on the Mountain, Fire Below the Mountain, Furnace Fire, and Lamp Fire without Water's relief indicate a shortened life. Of Woods it loves Mulberry and Willow, because this Earth can grow them. Other Woods must be assessed through Lu, Horse, and Noble Person. If Wood in the day brings punishment, break, clash, or control, what useful transformation can its appearance accomplish? Its absence would be more auspicious.

Among the Five Phases, this Earth most avoids punishment by other Earth. Roadside Earth provides a place to rest; with Metal and Wood giving nourishment, it also indicates blessings and happiness. Great Post-Road Earth's traffic and movement are most unsuitable: even with Metal and Water, it is inauspicious. Other Earth is still less auspicious.

### 10.22. Gengwu and Xinwei — Roadside Earth
#### 庚午辛未路傍土

<!-- source: L381-L383 -->
Roadside Earth is the great ground joined to the highways, level fields extending over ten thousand stretches. The crops depend upon it for life; grasses and trees flourish through it. This is Earth warmed by Fire, temperate soil that fosters the myriad things. Water must therefore come first, for irrigation and moisture. In the ordering of the arrangement, transformation into Water is especially subtle. Metal further assisting allows the crops to ripen. Gengwu meeting Jiashen, or Xinwei meeting Yiyou, gives Lu; without clash or break, early distinction follows.

Water in Heaven supplies nourishing rain and dew. Gengwu welcomes Dingwei, Xinwei welcomes Bingwu: the subtlety lies in Officer, Noble Person, and Lu joining. Of Streambed Water, Gengwu meeting Dingchou gives Noble Person and Lu crossing in exchange; Xinwei meeting Bingzi transforms into Water and encounters generation. Great Stream Water Yimao is thunder, able to stir this Earth into growth; Yi and Geng also combine and transform. All therefore indicate good fortune. Long-Flowing and Great Sea Water cannot irrigate this Earth and are most avoided, indicating misfortune and early death.

Of Thunderbolt Fire, Gengwu meeting Jichou gives Noble Person and Lu interpenetrating; Xinwei meeting Wuzi gives **Seal and Noble Person Facing the Sun**. Both are auspicious. Fire in Heaven generates it in the same position, but excessive dryness leaves Earth unable to produce things. Water's moisture is required; Fire alone indicates early death. Furnace Fire is also dry and injures longevity. Lamp Fire requires Rooftop Earth before a useful transformation can occur: it is called **Passing Beyond the Ordinary into the Sacred**. Otherwise it too is inauspicious.

Wood can grow here. With Noble Person, Lu, and Horse it is auspicious; with punishments, harmful influences, clashes, and breaks it is inauspicious. Gengyin Wood alone is especially favorable. Great Forest is more than this Earth can bear. Of other Earth positions, Bingchen, Bingxu, Xinchou, and Xinwei are all auspicious. Gengwu meeting Xinwei, or Xinwei meeting Gengwu, is **The Noble Pairing of the Two Modes**: distinction invariably follows. Hairpin-and-Bracelet and Sand Metal can nourish clear Water; Metal and Water together are greatly auspicious. If Water is already present in the life but Metal is absent, meeting this Metal in the luck cycles also brings blessings.

Yichou Sea Metal may be considered as a mountain. With the clear Water of the Celestial River assisting, it is especially favorable for a Geng person. The *Subtle Selections* has **The Golden Horse Neighing into the Wind**: people born in Gengwu or Jiawu obtaining a Xinsi hour. **The Horse Transforming into a Dragon Steed** is a Wu-born person meeting a Chen hour. **The Fierce Tiger Roaring into the Wind** is a Geng or Xin person obtaining Xinsi or Yisi. All are judged distinguished; in these cases Water, Fire, Earth, and Metal seem not to impose the usual restrictions.[^105]

### 10.23. Wushen and Jiyou — Great Post-Road Earth
#### 戊申己酉大驛土

<!-- source: L385-L387 -->
Great Post-Road Earth is the broad highway, the level thoroughfare. Nowhere in the Nine Provinces lies beyond its reach; no journey among the myriad states fails to arrive by it. Its position belongs to Kun, its virtue to substantial bearing: Earth that turns Heaven and carries the sun, bears the seas and supports the mountains. To generate the myriad things, it takes Wood as its foundation. Wushen is Earth in Birth, its substantial virtue without bounds; it can nourish three or four Woods. Jiyou is Earth in its own Defeat: too much Wood steals its qi. Great Forest brings a clash within combination and indicates early death; other Woods are auspicious, with Lu and Noble Person also to be considered.[^106]

Well-and-Spring and Streambed Water are clear, distinguished, and not drying. Wushen meeting Dingchou or Yiyou, or Jiyou meeting Bingzi or Jiashen, gives Officer and Noble Person and is auspicious. Of Celestial River Water, Jiyou obtaining Bingwu or Wushen obtaining Dingwei also gives Noble Person and Lu, indicating blessings. Of Long-Flowing Water, Wushen meeting Guisi or Jiyou meeting Renchen is auspicious, though repeated Water brings unrest. Great Stream Water Yimao signifies the generating activity of Zhen in the east; a single occurrence is auspicious. Sea Water directly opposes and penetrates this Earth, which cannot overcome it; in the day or hour it indicates early death. A mountain lightens the harm. But Wushen meeting Guihai combines Wu and Gui, while Shen and Hai form **Earth and Heaven Communicating in Peace**: it is instead auspicious.

The sun and Thunderbolt Fire are called sacred Fire and can best stimulate this Earth to produce things. Water assisting indicates prominence. Other, ordinary Fires, further generated by Wood, make it too dry and indicate misfortune and early death. Of Earth, Roadside is most suitable. Rooftop, Wall-Plaster, and Sandy Earth bring obliteration even if Wood was previously obtained. City-Wall with Water is somewhat auspicious.

Metal in the life is auspicious when clear and refined. Of Hairpin-and-Bracelet, Xinhai, and of Gold Leaf, Renyin, are the desired forms. Wushen meeting them, with Water also assisting in the pillars, gives the great configurations **Earth and Heaven Communicating in Peace** and **Water Encircling, Mountains Enclosing**, indicating distinction. Jiyou meeting Gengxu or Guimao ranks somewhat lower. Sand Metal follows the same principle of transformation. Sword Edge must wait until Wood has a use; otherwise it offers no benefit.

### 10.24. Bingxu and Dinghai — Rooftop Earth
#### 丙戌丁亥屋上土

<!-- source: L389-L391 -->
Rooftop Earth is clay kneaded and shaped into [tiles], Water and Fire brought to completion together. It shelters against accumulated snow and frost and withstands the assault of wind and rain. This Earth is roof tile. Without Wood, it cannot be raised into place; therefore Wood is its foundation. Level-Ground Wood is best, Great Forest next. A transformation of the heavenly stems into Wood is also auspicious. Only clash and break are feared.[^107]

This is Earth already completed and should not meet Fire. Of Furnace Fire, Bingyin is most inauspicious and Dingmao somewhat acceptable. The sun and Thunderbolt may provide mutual assistance. Fire Below the Mountain or Fire on the Mountain, generated by Wood, brings calamity. Of Lamp Fire, Bingxu meeting Yisi is best; [Dinghai] meeting Jiachen is next. This is **Fire and Earth Entering the Hall**. But much Wood in the pillars makes even this inauspicious. Celestial River, Well-and-Spring, and Streambed Water are all suitable and auspicious. If Level-Ground Wood has already formed a configuration, great distinction follows. Great Stream or Long-Flowing Water without Wood often brings early death. Bingxu obtaining Guisi or Dinghai obtaining Jiayin, however, must be judged separately; examine what transformation the day and hour produce. Great Sea Water without a mountain should not be encountered.[^108]

Of Earth, Roadside is suitable: Bingxu obtaining Xinwei or Dinghai obtaining Gengwu is a cross-pairing of yin and yang. With Wood as the foundation, it indicates distinction. Wall-Plaster Earth is also suitable; other Earth submerges it. Without Wood, if the three punishments gather, even those two Earths are inauspicious. Of Sandy Earth, Dingsi alone does no harm.

Of Metals, Sword Edge and Hairpin-and-Bracelet are most auspicious. Ding meeting Renshen gives heavenly stems transforming into Wood and earthly branches in the clear, level arrangement of Qian and Kun. Bing meeting Xinhai gives heavenly stems transforming into Water and Bing entering Qian's gate. Both are configurations of great distinction. But Dinghai meeting Gengxu, or Bingxu meeting Guiyou, is not auspicious. Gold Leaf serves for ornament and is also auspicious. Other Metals are useless; assess them through Noble Person and Lu.

### 10.25. Bingzi and Dingchou — Streambed Water
#### 丙子丁丑澗下水

<!-- source: L393-L395 -->
Streambed Water sends small waves around the mountains and lifts rushing torrents white as snow: a current joining north and south, a vein between the facing positions of Kan and Li. This Water is clear and limpid and welcomes Metal's nourishment. Sand Metal and Sword Edge are most suitable. Of Hairpin-and-Bracelet, Gengxu is unsuitable for Dingchou because Chou and Xu punish one another. Xinhai meeting Bingzi, however, gives Bing and Xin transforming into Water and is especially distinguished. Other Metals are assessed through Lu and Noble Person: take their generative assistance and dislike their clashes and breaks.

A single Wood does no harm; two or three indicate toil. Noble Person, Lu, and Horse must also be considered. Earth in the life commonly makes the person turbid. If the heavenly component is Wood, or transforms into Water, the result is instead clear and auspicious. Sandy and Rooftop Earth have somewhat clear qi; Roadside and Great Post-Road are extremely turbid and indicate scattered wealth and arising calamity. A complete Earth configuration of Chen, Xu, Chou, and Wei is especially inauspicious, because Water is muddied and Earth confused.

The sun's Fire is fierce, but the distinctions of Already Across and Not Yet Across apply within the encounter. Thunderbolt contends with it in the same position and is most unsuitable. If the two Fires appear together without Metal's support, dissipation and sexual excess are indicated; with Metal, judge differently. Fire Below the Mountain and Fire on the Mountain are also auspicious, but their joint appearance in day and hour is disliked. Further Water in the life may instead produce drifting. Celestial River Water is **Leading the Ordinary into the Sacred**; Great Sea Water is **Blessings and Distinction Returning to Their Sovereign**. Both are auspicious. Dingchou meeting Renxu alone gives Chou–Xu punishment and a licentious Ding–Ren combination, indicating an unseemly reputation. Great Stream is too impetuous and Long-Flowing too restless; neither is auspicious.[^109]

In general, this Water should take Metal as its principal support, without Fire or Earth mixed in. Add Jiayin or Yimao Water, and the source is distant and the current clear: this is a truly noble person.

### 10.26. Jiayin and Yimao — Great Stream Water
#### 甲寅乙卯大溪水

<!-- source: L397-L399 -->
Great Stream Water drives startling waves against the banks and lifts terrifying billows into the sky. Its light embraces a breadth of ten thousand miles; its blue depths overturn the reflections of a thousand mountains. Above all, it needs a place to return and a source of nourishment. Meeting Kan gives it a place to return; obtaining Metal gives it nourishment. What it dislikes is Shen or You in the month, day, or hour clashing and stirring it, or Chen and Si blowing upon it: these indicate drifting and wandering.

Well-and-Spring Water is pure and still; Streambed has Chou as Gen; Celestial River moistens it; Great Sea receives it as a sovereign receives tribute. These four Waters are auspicious. Long-Flowing alone is unsuitable because it carries wind. This Water needs clear Metal for nourishment: Hairpin-and-Bracelet and Sand Metal are most suitable. White Pewter is also clear, but if Hairpin-and-Bracelet is present in opposing clash, the arrangement is unsuitable. Sea Metal offers no direct transformation, yet Jiazi belongs to Kan and Yichou is Gen, providing a place to return to the source; it is therefore auspicious. Gold Leaf is extremely slight and cannot generate it: how could conspicuous eminence result? Though Sword Edge can transform in the Great Stream, it avoids Mao's thunder and Xun's wind, which indicate an unsettled nature.[^110]

Earth of every kind is unhelpful. Rooftop and City-Wall obstruct the Water; Roadside is somewhat tolerable but not exceptional. Of Wall-Plaster, Xinchou alone is mountain. Of Great Post-Road, Jiyou alone has combination; Wushen clashes, while Gengzi punishes: these are not auspicious. The sun's Fire may lend illumination, but Thunderbolt is particularly avoided. The two Fires appearing together indicate poverty; a single occurrence is judged separately.

Wood meeting this Water merely suffers being set adrift. Mulberry alone supplies Renzi as Kan and Guichou as mountain, giving the distinction of **Water Encircling, Mountains Enclosing**. Specifically, Jiayin meeting Renzi is auspicious, and Yimao meeting Guichou auspicious. Other Woods are assessed through Lu and Noble Person; clashes and breaks are especially avoided.

### 10.27. Renchen and Guisi — Long-Flowing Water
#### 壬辰癸巳長流水

<!-- source: L401-L403 -->
Long-Flowing Water wells up without end and pours onward without exhaustion. Seeking the low, it must be received in the southeast; following its current, it naturally returns to Chen and Si. This Water welcomes Metal to generate and nourish it. White Pewter and Hairpin-and-Bracelet are wanted: their stems Geng and Xin are true Metal; Chen and Si generate in the same positions, while Xu and Hai are places of return to the source. Sword Edge is wholly Metal and Water; Gold Leaf has Water and Wood occupying the east. All are judged auspicious. Sea Metal and Sand Metal offer nothing useful.

Earth is disliked for obstructing and drying it, yet Earth also provides embankments. Among the six Earths, those with Geng, Xin, Bing, and Ding are auspicious; those with Wu and Ji are useless. Meeting Fire brings conflict, yet contains the subtlety of Already Across. Renchen welcomes Dingmao and Dingyou; Guisi welcomes Wuzi and Wuwu, because the heavenly stems combine and transform. Of Lamp Fire, Guisi especially welcomes Jiachen; of Fire on the Mountain, Renchen especially welcomes Yihai. The meanings of transforming into a dragon and returning to Lu make these especially favorable.

Wood may be set adrift, yet Mulberry's Guichou is mountain and Willow's Guiwei is garden. With this Water surrounding them from the year and hour, the result is **Water Encircling the Flowered Embankment**, a configuration of great distinction. Pine-and-Cypress and Pomegranate have Metal in the stems to generate it. Great Forest and Level-Ground are disliked for their Earth, but Guisi meeting Wuchen and Renchen meeting Jihai are both auspicious. Water is of its own kind. Streambed supplies the Ding–Ren transformation; Celestial River lends rain and dew. Well-and-Spring and Great Stream offer no benefit.

The sea is where all currents return. Renchen's dragon obtaining Guihai gives **The Dragon Leaping through Heaven's Gate**, auspicious with spring, summer, or autumn birth. **The Dragon Hidden in the Great Sea**, by contrast, requires winter birth. Metal should already be present in the pillars for the arrangement to be subtle. Renchen is also Water bearing its own Seal. Repeated Renchen brings punishment and therefore self-injury; Wuxu brings clash and therefore flooding, indicating misfortune. Guisi is Water in its own Extinction, called a dried-up stream. Meeting the Earth of Bingxu, Dinghai, Gengzi, or Xinchou makes its drying up expectable. If the pillars contain Metal in a threefold combination, Birth, or Prosperity to generate it, the result is auspicious.

### 10.28. Bingwu and Dingwei — Celestial River Water
#### 丙午丁未天河水

<!-- source: L405-L407 -->
Celestial River Water scatters across the six open regions and pours thickly over a thousand outskirts. Streaming down from the Silver River, it flies in fine drops from the azure heights. This is Heaven's rain and dew, upon which the generation of all things depends. Earth cannot control the Silver River's Water. Earth is therefore not avoided; it also receives the benefit of moisture. Earthly Metal can scarcely generate Water in Heaven, so Metal offers little increase, though it can contain and reflect its beauty. Excessive Birth and Prosperity becomes prolonged flooding and harms things; excessive Death and Extinction becomes drought and cannot produce them. Birth in one of the three autumn months is needed: timely receipt confers distinction.

This Water loves Long-Flowing and Great Sea Water. Bingwu should meet Guisi or Guihai; Dingwei should meet Renchen or Renxu. Cross-pairing yin and yang is especially auspicious. Great Stream's Yimao is thunder; Well-and-Spring's [Yiyou] gives a Noble Person. Both are auspicious. Of Fire, it loves Thunderbolt, the divine dragon's Fire. Assisting this Water, it transforms in an instant: clouds move and rain is bestowed. How could distinction fail to follow? Furnace Fire flourishes, and Great Sea Water flourishes. If the pillars obtain two Fires and two Waters, mutually assisting above and below, this is **Essence and Spirit Both Complete**, a configuration of great distinction. Lamp Fire has wind, and Fire on the Mountain has a Noble Person: both are auspicious, though other Water must also give relief. Fire in Heaven controls in the same position and is therefore avoided.[^86]

Of Woods, Pomegranate and Willow are both auspicious. Great Forest has Xun, Level-Ground has Hai: both are also auspicious. Pine-and-Cypress and Pomegranate with Bing–Xin combination and transformation are auspicious as well. The *Subtle Selections* has **The Numinous Raft Entering the Celestial River**: Wood in Death or Extinction, without roots and without Earth in the pillars to cultivate it, floats upon the Celestial River. Though Earth cannot control this Water, Gengwu and Xinwei in the pillars control in the same position, damming Earth and obstructing Water. If the Water is also born in winter, it freezes in pools and ponds and necessarily indicates turbidity and excess. Other Earths, such as Sandy and Rooftop, are auspicious; City-Wall and Great Post-Road are useless; Wall-Plaster's opposing branches also spoil the transformation.[^111]

Though Metal cannot generate this Water, Xinhai Hairpin-and-Bracelet belongs to Qian, Heaven. Since the Water is above Heaven, this one alone is most auspicious. Among other Metals, those with Geng, Xin, Ren, or Gui in the stems may be used; Jia and Yi offer no benefit. Lu, Horse, and Noble Person must also be considered. The *Subtle Selections* further calls a Water-born person obtaining Gengzi and Renzi **Clouds Rising and Rain Bestowed**. Spring birth gives drought, summer flooding, winter cold: only the three autumn months are most auspicious. With Jiachen, Yisi, Gengchen, or Xinsi, and Ren in the pillars as cloud and Chen as dragon, it is **Wind and Rain Becoming a Sustaining Downpour**. Born in winter, it becomes **Congealed Frost and Thin Dew**. Yin or Mao in the day or hour supplies gentle warmth that can thaw the freezing. These are all prestigious configurations.[^112]

### 10.29. Jiashen and Yiyou — Well-and-Spring Water
#### 甲申乙酉井泉水

<!-- source: L409-L411 -->
Well-and-Spring Water is a cold spring, clear and bracing, drawn upon for nourishment without exhaustion. Eight households dig it to drink together; the multitude relies upon it to sustain life. This Water is born from Metal and emerges through Wood. It therefore welcomes Metal as a blessing. Sand Metal contains Earth in its nature and is most suitable; clear and refined Hairpin-and-Bracelet ranks next. White Pewter clashes with Hairpin-and-Bracelet and should not appear in addition. Sword Edge's Shen and You are too flourishing, threatening a flood. Sea Metal offers no general use, but Yichou is Gen: a spring emerging beneath a mountain is also auspicious.[^113]

Without Wood this Water cannot be brought out—the analogy is a bucket. All Wood is therefore auspicious. Level-Ground and Great Forest require Sword Edge to cut and shape them before they can be used. Mulberry and Willow are useless. Pine-and-Cypress gives an exchange and return to Lu and is most auspicious. The Fires are auspicious when yin and yang cross-pair. Thunderbolt is called **Entering the Sacred**; the sun is **Manifest Illumination**. These two Fires are most auspicious when encountered, but they should not both be present: together they are inauspicious.

Of Earth, Roadside and Sandy are most auspicious. Rooftop has the Gate of Heaven, the source of Water's generation, and is also auspicious. City-Wall and Wall-Plaster have no particular involvement with this Water. Great Post-Road controls it in the same position: Water is covered by Earth, giving **The Well Cleared but Not Drunk From**. Wood must remove the obstruction before it is usable. Of Water, it loves Great Sea as **Leading the Ordinary into the Sacred**. Celestial River, Streambed, and Long-Flowing do no harm. Great Stream's Jiayin and Yimao are auspicious. Jiashen meeting Yiyou, or Yiyou meeting Jiashen, gives an exchange of Officer Stars and is most auspicious. Two Waters in the year and hour and two Woods in the month and day give **Water Encircling the Flowered Embankment**, a prestigious configuration.

### 10.30. Renxu and Guihai — Great Sea Water
#### 壬戌癸亥大海水

<!-- source: L413-L415 -->
Great Sea Water receives all the hundred rivers, vast and without shore. It embraces the immensity of Qian and Kun and raises and sinks the light of sun and moon. This Water may be clear or turbid, and the two must be distinguished. Renxu contains Earth qi and is turbid. Guihai is pure Water in stem and branch, with Water also as nayin, and is therefore clear. A Renxu person dislikes mountain, since Earth qi would become too strong; Metal must clear it before it is auspicious. Guihai most loves a mountain, for only then does Sea Water's nature settle.

Of Streambed Water, Dingchou is mountain. Celestial River and Sea communicate above and below. Wood in the pillars serving as a raft gives **Riding the Raft into the Celestial River**, a configuration of the highest order. Long-Flowing, Great Stream, and the other Waters ultimately all return to the sea. The sea refuses no small stream and can therefore become great. Renchen is **The Dragon Returning to the Great Sea**, especially auspicious. Here too, cross-pairing yin and yang and combination and transformation of stems and branches must be distinguished. Well-and-Spring Water is constrained and does not communicate with the sea; it is therefore unwelcome.

Among Metals, Sea Metal alone ranks first: Renxu and Guihai like Jiazi and Yichou. Sand Metal is also acceptable. Other Metals must be assessed through Noble Person, Lu, and Horse. Of Fire, Fire in Heaven is most auspicious, exchanging illumination with the sea. Of Thunderbolt, Jichou is mountain, and Wu and Gui combine and transform. If the pillars obtain a place where Wood and Fire flourish, it too is auspicious. Fire Below the Mountain, Fire on the Mountain, and Lamp Fire are unsuitable.

Of Woods, Renzi, Guichou, Renwu, and Guiwei are all auspicious. Great Forest has wind that stirs Water and unsettles its nature. Level-Ground bears heavily and finds a fitting relationship in the same positions. Pine-and-Cypress and Pomegranate, without Earth's restraint, drift without a resting place. Of Earth, it loves Roadside and Great Post-Road: these two alone can hold it firm and prevent its escape. Guihai meeting Wushen is the great configuration **Heaven's Pass and Earth's Axis**. With such substantial Earth in the day or hour, even wind and thunder do no harm. City-Wall's Jimao requires Gen's assistance to be auspicious. If Thunderbolt also appears, thunder and Fire transform and the sea surges violently, indicating poverty and hardship.[^114]

### 10.31. Why the Stems Take These Images

<!-- source: L417, first part -->
In assigning nayin images to the sixty combinations, some of the ten stems may become Metal and others may not. Although this depends upon the different numbers encountered, one must also understand the subtle principles by which the Five Phases transform according to kind and follow their kinds. **Jia, Yi, Geng, Xin, Ren, and Gui**—these three phase-pairs—may become Metal, but Bing, Ding, Wu, and Ji do not participate. Geng and Xin are originally Metal, naturally belonging to their own household. Ren and Gui are not Metal: why can they become it? Because Metal and Water generate in relation to one another, Water too can become Metal. Thus the world contains objects in which Water is blue-green and Metal bright. Jia and Yi Wood possess firm substance. That Wood can transform into stone provides a comparable example. These two pairs may therefore take Metal's image, like members of Metal's own household. Bing and Ding belong to Fire, whose dry hardness differs; Wu and Ji belong to Earth, whose softness and hardness differ. Both stand contrary to Metal and cannot be treated as Metal.[^115]

**Wu, Ji, Geng, Xin, Ren, and Gui** may become Wood, but Jia, Yi, Bing, and Ding do not participate. Bing and Ding are originally Fire and burn Wood, so naturally they cannot be taken. But Jia and Yi are originally Wood: why are they not taken? It is not generally understood that among the Five Phases, Metal returns to Metal, Water to Water, Fire to Fire, and Earth to Earth, while Wood alone changes. Wood borrows the natures of Fire, Water, and Earth, joining them provisionally to arise. When its sap-courses are cut off and it withers, it returns to Water; burned as Fire, it returns to Fire; decayed into Earth, it returns to Earth. Thus Wood itself is not used: this is leaving the old body and transforming into spirit. The numbers encountered in the revolving Six Jia and the principles obtained through the interweaving Five Phases can be grasped inwardly. All Wood is generated by Water; its sap is Water. Thus Ren and Gui can become Wood. Geng and Xin have a hardness like Jia and Yi. Therefore Jia and Yi can become Metal, and Geng and Xin can become Wood. The principle of their mutual exchange and communication is one and the same.

**Bing, Ding, Jia, Yi, Ren, and Gui** take Water's image. Ren and Gui are originally Water; Bing and Ding are its transformation; the sap of Jia and Yi is itself Water. Wu and Ji Earth control Water; Geng and Xin Metal dry it. Their qi differs, so they cannot be treated as Water. **Jia, Yi, Bing, Ding, Wu, and Ji** take Fire's image. Bing and Ding are originally Fire; Wu and Ji are its transformation; the living combustion of Jia and Yi is Fire itself. Ren and Gui Water control Fire; Geng and Xin Metal dry it. Their kinds differ, so they cannot be treated as Fire.[^115]

**Bing, Ding, Wu, Ji, Geng, and Xin** have Earth nayin. Wu and Ji are originally Earth; the Fire of Bing and Ding transforms into ash, and ash is Earth. Geng and Xin Metal are mingled within Earth, congealed from its essential qi. All can therefore become Earth. Ren and Gui Water moisten downward without congealing, while Jia and Yi Wood spread upward without stopping; naturally they cannot be treated as Earth.

Examine the images chosen: some are light, others heavy; some great, others small; some hard, others soft. Their qi and flavor, bodily substance, and functions all differ. These distinctions also involve the earthly branches' directional positions and their differing states of flourishing, support, rest, and confinement. What the heavenly stems encounter may follow the original image, a transformed image, or another image. Heaven and humanity together exhaust the principle; generation and control complete the meaning through one another. Thus Metal is distinguished as Sea, Sand, Gold Leaf, White Pewter, Sword Edge, and Hairpin-and-Bracelet, leaving nothing of Metal's images unexplored. Wood, Fire, Water, and Earth may be understood by analogy. Is this not why nayin's images express the subtlety of creative transformation?

Someone says: “Among the Five Phases, Water and Fire alone must not become excessively flourishing. Once excessive, they are beyond remedy. Therefore Bingwu and Dingwei are assigned Celestial River Water, because Water can restrain Fire. Wuwu and Jiwei are called Fire in Heaven: Wu and Ji cover them above, so Fire does not blaze out. Wu and Wei stand in the middle of Heaven. Bing and Ding belong to Fire, and both occupy the flourishing territory of Wu and Wei. Thus throughout the dynasties these two years have brought unrest.” This is reasoning from principle, not the original meaning of the chosen images; nevertheless, it should be known.[^116]

### 10.32. In Defense of Nayin

<!-- source: L417, remainder -->
Further consideration shows that nayin's imagery came from the Yellow Emperor, which is why the various specialists all take it as their basis. Since Xu Dasheng wrote the *Discourse Establishing the True*, with its remark that “before Lou Jing, it was not known that Metal lay within the sea,” the star specialists of the Yuan have gone on to say that “nayin vainly loses innate truth.” Thus today's interpreters of destiny discuss only the ordinary Five Phases and do not use nayin. How little they know of nayin's principles and the precision of its images—the very place where creative transformation is subtle! In discussing a person's destiny, one especially must investigate and embody this understanding to exhaust its finer meaning.[^117]

Today's yin–yang specialists discuss the Earlier-Heaven Old Five Phases but reject the Hongfan Five Phases, branding them the *Classic for Destroying the Barbarians* because they overturn yin and yang and reverse Water and Fire. Master Zhou of Taihe wrote the *Definitive Discourse on Yin and Yang* and attacked them deeply. Luo Qingxiao of Western Shu, in his *Distinguishing Doubts concerning Yin and Yang*, held that the Hongfan Five Phases came from the Eight Trigrams and could not possibly be dispensed with; he in turn condemned Master Zhou's view as false. Ah! Why is there no settled account from one generation to the next? In my view, the nayin Five Phases express the same meaning as the Hongfan Five Phases. One must not uphold one and discard the other. Those who discuss destiny should take the Five Phases as the warp and consult nayin as the weft. Only then may the principles of destiny's numbers be exhausted, leaving no hidden store of creative transformation unexplored.[^118]

The discourse continues: From the opening of Heaven and Earth, the names of the stems and branches were established. Tradition attributes them to the Heavenly and Earthly Sovereigns. Their interweaving into the sixty combinations, however, began when Fuxi made the Jia calendar. Once it was called the Jia calendar, years, months, days, and hours were all recorded by the sixty combinations. The beginning and end of Heaven and Earth, the movements of sun and moon, the cold and heat of the four seasons, and the transformations of yin and yang could not depart from it; [through three returns they were fixed]. When the Yellow Emperor assigned nayin images to the sixty combinations, each acquired its Five-Phase affiliation. The natures, dispositions, forms, substances, functions, and transformations of Metal, Wood, Water, Fire, and Earth were fully disclosed, and the Changes was already present within them.[^119]

Use this to measure the Two Modes, and Heaven and Earth cannot escape it; to infer the Three Lights, and sun, moon, and stars cannot alter it; to examine the four seasons, and cold and heat cannot depart from it; to divine human affairs, and good and ill fortune, calamity and blessing, longevity and early death, hardship and success cannot lie outside it. Creative transformation has no hidden circumstance left to conceal.

Today's Confucian scholars know only that Fuxi drew the Eight Trigrams, King Wen doubled them into sixty-four hexagrams, the Duke of Zhou composed the line statements, and Confucius composed the Appended Statements. Because the Changes passed through four sages before completion, they call it a classic, while dismissing the Five-Phase specialists as one of the miscellaneous schools. How thoughtless this is! Do they suppose that the Five-Phase specialists speak only of Birth and Prosperity while ignoring correct principle, entrust everything to Heaven's mandate while abandoning human affairs, and therefore fail to accord with the Way of the Changes?

Ah! The stems and branches come from high antiquity; the Jiazi cycle is rooted in Sovereign Fuxi; sounds and images were transmitted from the Yellow Emperor. These are several sages. Did they come after King Wen, the Duke of Zhou, and Confucius? If the stem and branch names had not been established when Heaven and Earth opened, they could not have been interwoven into the Jiazi cycle. Without the sixty combinations, the Five Phases could not have been interwoven. How could the calendar record and complete the year? Who would explain or know that a year has three hundred and sixty days, twelve months, thirty days to a month, and twelve double-hours to a day? The whole world would remain muddled, as though still in primal obscurity. How could one stand between the Two, participate in the Three Powers, and form an ordered world? This is precisely what people use daily without knowing it and follow throughout life without examining it.[^21]

Subtle though the Way of the Changes is, it does no more than take the images of Heaven and Earth establishing their positions, mountain and marsh communicating their qi, thunder and wind pressing upon one another, and Water and Fire not assailing one another, to draw the Eight Trigrams. Its principle does not lie outside the stems, branches, and Jiazi cycle, inventing something separate. Ah! The stems and branches interweave to make sixty; the Eight Trigrams interweave to make sixty-four. The Jiazi cycle incorporates sounds through number and takes images through principle: this is the proper working of the Five Phases, and the body of the Eight Trigrams is already complete within it. The Eight Trigrams, observing above and examining below, taking distant analogies from things and near ones from the body, become sixty-four hexagrams and three hundred and eighty-four lines—also the number of a year—and through them the operation of the stems and branches proceeds.[^120]

The stems and branches take Heaven and Earth as their foundation and form the warp; the Eight Trigrams articulate yin and yang and form the weft. Warp and weft interlace, coming and going, changing and transforming. The profound secrets of Heaven and Earth, the circumstances of ghosts and spirits, and the good and ill fortune of human affairs all lie within them. Their meaning is subtle indeed. How, then, can the world's Confucian scholars dismiss the Five Phases as a miscellaneous school?

## 11. On the Five Phases
### 論五行

<!-- source: L419-L421 -->
The Five Phases are what come and go between Heaven and Earth without exhaustion; therefore they are called “movements.” In the north, yin reaches its utmost and produces cold; cold produces Water. In the south, yang reaches its utmost and produces heat; heat produces Fire. In the east, yang spreads and issues forth, producing wind; wind produces Wood. In the west, yin halts and gathers inward, producing dryness; dryness produces Metal. At the center, yin and yang mingle and produce dampness; dampness produces Earth. Their mutual generation maintains their connections; their mutual control regulates them. This is called ordered relationship.

Fire is Greater Yang, its nature to flame upward. Water is Greater Yin, its nature to moisten downward. Wood is Lesser Yang, its nature to rise without a fixed stopping point. Metal is Lesser Yin, its nature to sink downward and come to rest. Earth has no constant nature: it depends on what it receives in the four seasons. The aim is for them to assist one another appropriately, without excess or deficiency. Each phase's nature fulfills its own use: Water's nature is wisdom; Fire's, ritual propriety; Wood's, humanity; Metal's, righteousness. Earth alone governs trustworthiness: weighty, substantial, broad, and all-embracing. Water attaches to it and moves; Wood rests upon it and grows. Without Earth, Metal has nowhere to emerge; without Earth, Fire has nowhere to return. One must reduce solidity to make passage possible and bring about openness to make illumination possible. Thus all Five Phases depend upon Earth.[^121]

Their forms and colors are Water black, Fire red, Wood green-blue, Metal white, and Earth yellow: these are the proper colors. In transformation, however, it is otherwise. In Birth and Prosperity they follow their proper colors. 〈In Birth and Prosperity, the proper qi is complete, so the proper color appears.〉 In Death and Extinction they follow their mother's color. 〈Water is Wood's mother: in Death and Extinction, Wood becomes black. Wood is Fire's mother: Fire becomes green-blue. Fire is Earth's mother: Earth becomes red. Earth is Metal's mother: Metal becomes yellow. When a phase dies or is extinguished, its qi returns to the root and the mother's color appears. A person groaning “Mother!” in suffering expresses the same principle.〉 At Formation and Cap and Belt they follow the wife's color. 〈In youth and strength, and again in declining age, one depends upon one's wife.〉 In Sickness and Defeat they follow the ghost's color. 〈The place of Sickness and Defeat is the ghost's flourishing homeland. Under control, qi returns to the ghost.〉 In Prosperity and Tomb they follow the child's color. 〈Prosperity transmits; Tomb gathers and stores. The color therefore rests with the child.〉[^122]

Their numbers are Water one, Fire two, Wood three, Metal four, Earth five. In Birth and Prosperity, double them; in Death and Extinction, halve them. Infer the result according to the principle. The myriad things bear yin and embrace yang, blending qi to make harmony. Excess and deficiency both depart from the Way. Therefore press down what is high to make it level; raise what is low to make it lofty. Add to what is insufficient; subtract from what is excessive. What matters is to strike the proper balance and return to the middle Way, free of the burden of too much or too little. This is the subtle meaning of Wealth, Officer, Seal, Food, Noble Person, and Traveling Horse. Luck cycles follow the same principle. Grasp this subtle meaning, and more than half the work of understanding destiny is done.[^123]

### 11.1. Metal

<!-- source: L423-L427 -->
〈**Favors:** Wood's image; generation by Earth; Void; forging and refining.〉

〈**Avoids:** flourishing Wood; flourishing Fire; Fire associated with Tomb or Defeat; cold Water; the melting away of Metal; punishment, harmful influences, control, and injury.〉[^124]

Metal takes utmost yin as its substance and contains within it the essence of utmost yang; only thus can it be hard and strong, unlike other things. Yin alone without hardness is ice and snow, which melt when they meet yang. Therefore unrefined Metal does not become a vessel; gathered Metal without Fire can scarcely gain the name of something freed from its crude state. Heavy Metal with light Fire makes the conduct of affairs difficult. Light Metal with heavy Fire is refined away into destruction. Metal at its utmost with abundant Fire makes the finest configuration. Complete Metal and Fire are called **Casting the Seal**; encountering Chou damages the mold. Much Metal and Fire is called **Riding the High Carriage**; Death or Decline makes it unfavorable.[^125]

Great Fire refining Metal brings fortunate achievement and reputation, but a swift retreat. Pure Metal gathering with Water encounters wealth and eminence with abundance to spare. Metal can generate Water, but flourishing Water sinks Metal. Earth can generate Metal, but when Metal is exalted, Earth is humbled. Metal without Water dries and withers; heavy Water sinks it into uselessness. Metal without Earth dies and is extinguished; heavy Earth buries it without display. Two Metals and two Fires are best; two Metals and two Woods provide sufficient Wealth. One Metal generating three Waters is too weak to bear the task; one Metal obtaining three Woods becomes dull and injures itself. Once Metal is completed, Fire is put out. Thus unfinished Metal wants Fire; finished Metal does not. Metal reaching Shen, You, Si, or Chou may be called completed. Its luck cycles favor the northwest, not the south.[^126]

**Spring.** Winter's residual cold has not entirely departed. Fire qi is valued for bringing it to glory. Its nature is soft and its substance weak; it wants substantial Earth's support. Strong Water adds cold and makes it difficult to exercise a keen edge. Flourishing Wood exhausts its strength and brings the danger of chipping and dullness. Other Metal's support and maintenance are most welcome. But companions without Fire leave it outside its proper kind and are not favorable.

**Summer.** Metal's nature remains soft and its form has not yet become firmly established. Death and Extinction are especially disliked. Much Fire, however, is not rejected. Strong Water nourishes the substance and brings auspiciousness. Wood helps the ghost and injures the self; Metal supports it and makes its essence strong. Thin Earth is most useful; thick Earth buries it and hides its light.

**Autumn.** Metal holds authority and receives the seasonal command. Fire forging and refining it makes material fit for bells and ritual cauldrons. Much Earth nurturing it instead produces stubborn, turbid qi. Water makes its spirit and vitality exceptionally refined; Wood allows it to display its power by carving and cutting. More Metal makes it harder, but excessive hardness must chip. Greater qi makes it more flourishing, but extreme flourishing is harmful.

**Winter.** Metal's form is cold and its nature chilly. Much Wood makes cutting and carving difficult; strong Water threatens to submerge it. Earth can control Water so that Metal's body is not cold. Fire comes to [assist] Earth, and mother and child accomplish their work. It welcomes Companions gathering qi and supporting one another; Officer and Seal warming and nourishing it are beneficial.[^127]

### 11.2. Wood

<!-- source: L429-L433 -->
〈**Favors:** carving and cutting; generation and support; assisting Fire; cultivation by Earth; ground of Birth and Prosperity; companions forming a forest.〉

〈**Avoids:** breaking in emptiness; drifting and falling; Void; shaking; Death and Extinction; withering; self-burning; exhaustion and leakage; damp decay.〉[^124]

Wood's nature is to rise without a fixed stopping point. When its qi is weighty, it wants Metal to put it to use. With Metal, Wood possesses the virtues both of height and of restraint. It also loves substantial Earth, in which its roots can coil deeply and securely. Too little Earth brings the danger of luxuriant branches above insecure roots. Wood depends upon Water for birth: a little moistens it; too much sets it adrift. Jiaxu and Yihai are Wood's source; Jiayin and Yimao, its homeland; Jiachen and Yisi, its birth. All are living Wood. At Jiashen and Yiyou, Wood receives control; at Jiawu and Yiwei, Wood dies in its own place; at Jiazi and Yichou, Metal controls Wood. All are dead Wood.[^128]

Living Wood wants Fire to display its excellence; Bing and Ding work in the same way. Dead Wood obtains shaping through Metal; Geng and Xin are necessarily beneficial. Living Wood meeting Metal injures itself; dead Wood obtaining Fire burns itself. Without wind, its unruly force stops; meeting Water, it returns to its source, its course exhausted. Equal Metal and Wood make the configuration **Carving the Wheel**. Born in autumn, however, it becomes **Damaging the Axe**: autumn birth avoids heavy Metal. Yin Wood with abundant Fire gives a glib tongue and skill in speech.

**Spring.** Residual cold remains. Wood welcomes Fire's warmth, freeing it from cramped bending. Water's support gives it the beauty of comfortable unfolding. Early spring should not have excessive Water: dense yin damages roots and withers branches. Late spring's yang qi becomes hot and dry; without Water, leaves [fall away] and roots dry out. Thus Wood and Fire together must achieve completion for a favorable result. Much Earth exhausts its strength; thin Earth makes Wealth abundant. Heavy Metal is avoided: injury, maiming, cutting, and felling leave no rest throughout life. Yet if Wood is flourishing, Metal is favorable and brings lifelong blessings.[^129]

**Summer.** Roots are dry and leaves parched. What was coiled has straightened; what was bent has extended. Abundant Water is wanted for its moistening power and truly cannot be lacking. Flourishing Fire is avoided for the danger of burning and is especially inauspicious. Earth is welcome when thin, not thick and heavy; thickness instead brings calamity. Metal is disliked in excess but must not be entirely absent, for without it there can be no carving or cutting. Repeated Wood merely forms a forest; repeated blossom ultimately bears no fruit.

**Autumn.** Qi gradually grows cold and desolate; form gradually withers and declines. In early autumn, Fire's qi has not departed, so Water and Earth are still welcome for nourishment. By mid-autumn, fruit is fully formed and hard Metal is wanted for pruning and shaping. After Frost's Descent, excessive Water is unsuitable, for it sets Wood adrift. At Cold Dew, flaming Fire is welcome, making Wood substantial. Much Wood gives the beauty of abundant timber; thick Earth exceeds its ability to bear responsibility.

**Winter.** Wood coils and bends within the ground. Much Earth is wanted to cultivate it; strong Water is disliked for destroying its form. Even much Metal cannot cut and fell it. Repeated Fire supplies warmth and accomplishes the work. This is the season of returning to the roots and recovering the life-mandate. Wood itself is sick: how could it assist? Death and Extinction alone are avoided; Birth and Prosperity alone are suitable.[^130]

### 11.3. Water

<!-- source: L435-L439 -->
〈**Favors:** clarity and purity; breadth and distance; generation; Fire's assistance; moistening downward; the northwest.〉

〈**Avoids:** Void; flooding; control and injury; much Wood; cold qi; drying up; Death and Extinction; parched, scorched Earth and the like.〉

Heaven inclines to the northwest: Hai is the direction from which Water issues. Earth sinks toward the southeast: Chen is the storehouse that receives Water. Flowing against its course as far as Shen, it makes a sound; therefore Water does not flow westward. Water's nature is to moisten downward. Following its course, it has capacity: proceeding forward through the twelve branches is called following, and indicates breadth of mind. With auspicious spirits assisting, it forms a prestigious configuration. Going against its course, it has sound: proceeding backward through the twelve branches is called reversing. When this fulfills a configuration, it indicates refined distinction and a resounding reputation. Punishment and clash are avoided because they cause the current to break its bounds. Its own Death and Extinction are welcome and auspicious.[^131]

Water's source does not end: it relies upon Metal's generation to flow far. When Water floods, it relies upon Earth's control to form embankments. Balanced Water and Fire unite in the beauty of Already Across; Water and Earth mixed together bring the misfortune of a turbid source. In all four seasons, excessive Fire is avoided, for Water suffers thirst. Heavy Earth is avoided, for Water cannot flow. Metal in Death is avoided, for when Metal dies, Water is confined. Flourishing Wood is avoided, for when Wood flourishes, Water dies. Shenzhi says: “A Water life that is agitated commonly becomes turbid and excessive. Women especially should avoid it.” A formula says: “Yang Water with a weak self is poor; yin Water with a weak self is distinguished.”[^132]

**Spring.** Its nature is overflowing and abundant. Further Water's assistance will necessarily give it the force to breach an embankment. Add strong Earth, and there is no fear of flooding. Metal's generation and support are welcome, but Metal should not be excessive. Fire is wanted for mutual completion, but not too much. Wood allows it to exercise its usefulness; without Earth, there remains concern over its scattering without bounds.

**Summer.** Water holds to its nature and returns to its source. At this time of drying up, it wants Companions. Metal generating and strengthening its body is welcome; flourishing Fire and excessive heat are avoided. Strong Wood consumes and steals its qi; strong Earth controls its current.

**Autumn.** The mother flourishes and Water is supported: lustrous within, shining without. Metal's assistance can make it clear and limpid; flourishing Earth is disliked for muddying it. Much Fire means abundant Wealth, but excess is unsuitable. Heavy Wood means the wife prospers; balance and harmony are beneficial. Repeated Water increases the danger of flooding. Repeated Earth, by contrast, can bring clarity and calm.

**Winter.** Water holds the seasonal command and exclusive authority. Fire adds warmth and removes cold. Earth gives its form a place of storage and return to transformation. Much Metal is instead called unrighteous; flourishing Wood is called affectionate. Excessive Earth controls Water to Death; when Water rises in flood, Earth is welcome as an embankment.

### 11.4. Fire

<!-- source: L441-L445 -->
〈**Favors:** gentle warmth; generation and assistance; Void; flaming upward; height and distance; Earth's illumination; Water's aid.〉

〈**Avoids:** confinement; impatient heat; chilly clarity; heavy Water; withered Wood; mixed companions; Wood in Defeat; Wood in dead Water.〉[^124]

Blazing true Fire holds its station in the south. There is no Fire whose principle is not to shine; yet radiance does not endure and must return to concealment, so no brightness lacks the prospect of going out. Fire takes Wood as its substance: without Wood, its flame does not grow. It takes Water as its use: without Water, it is too cruelly fierce. Thus too much Fire lacks substance, and excessive fierceness injures things. Wood can conceal Fire, which begins to be born at Yin and Mao. Fire is disadvantaged in the west and necessarily dies upon meeting Shen or You. Born in Li's position, it is decisive and effective; in Kan's palace, cautious, fearful, and observant of propriety.

Metal harmonized by Fire can be melted and cast; Water harmonized by Fire achieves mutual completion. Meeting Earth, Fire is not bright and commonly indicates obstruction. Meeting Water's flourishing place, it certainly gains glory. When Wood dies and Fire is empty, endurance is difficult: even early achievement and reputation cannot last. In spring, Wood is avoided for fear of burning; in summer, Earth for fear of obscurity; in autumn, Metal because flourishing Metal is difficult to control; in winter, Water because flourishing Water extinguishes Fire's form. Thus spring Fire wants brightness, not fierceness, which lacks substance. Autumn Fire wants concealment, not brightness, which is drying. Winter Fire wants generation, not killing, which darkens it.

**Spring.** The mother flourishes and the child is supported; their powers advance together. Wood's generation and support are welcome, but must not be too strong, or Fire becomes fierce. Water is wanted for mutual completion; its abundance is not feared, for abundance brings benefit. Much Earth obstructs and buries the light. Strong Fire injures through explosive dryness. Metal allows Fire to exercise its usefulness; even repeated Metal still permits fulfillment in wife and Wealth.

**Summer.** Fire's power exercises authority. Water controlling it prevents the harm of self-burning. Wood assisting it necessarily threatens life cut short. Meeting Metal, it becomes a skilled craftsman; obtaining Earth, it brings cultivation and harvest. Although Metal and Earth offer good benefits, without Water, Metal is parched and Earth scorched. Further Fire's assistance makes the excess unstable and dangerous.

**Autumn.** Fire's nature subsides and its body rests. Wood's generation brings the happiness of renewed brightness; Water's control makes extinction difficult to escape. Heavy Earth buries and extinguishes its light; much Metal damages its force. Fire meeting Fire gains radiance: even repeated Fire becomes increasingly beneficial.

**Winter.** Fire's substance is extinguished and its form lost. Wood's generation is welcome as rescue; Water's control brings calamity. Earth is wanted to restrain Water and bring glory; Fire companions are loved for their benefit. Metal is difficult to sustain as Wealth; without Metal, there is no injury through the wife. Though Heaven and Earth should overturn, Water and Fire are difficult to extinguish.[^133]

### 11.5. Earth

<!-- source: L447-L451 -->
〈**Favors:** generation and support; firmness and substantiality; openness and circulation; generating Metal.〉

〈**Avoids:** collapse; heavy Wood; much Water; Void; cold qi; heavy Metal; emptiness and shallowness.〉

Earth among the Five Phases is distributed through the four corners. Metal, Wood, Water, and Fire therefore rely upon it to take their images, and it has a use in every season. What it avoids is Fire's Death at You and Water's Prosperity at Zi. Earth relies upon Fire as its Seal: when Fire dies, Earth is confined. Earth loves Water as its Wealth: when Water flourishes, Earth becomes empty. Only with Metal and Fire does Earth become a great vessel. High Earth without distinction merely raises dust. Gathered Earth stagnates; scattered Earth is light.

Chen, Xu, Chou, and Wei are Earth's proper positions, but their division into yin and yang makes them different. Chen contains hidden Water; Wei, concealed Wood. They nourish the myriad things and accomplish their work in spring and summer. Xu contains stored Fire; Chou, hidden Metal. Autumn Fire and winter Metal act austerely to cut down the myriad things. Therefore Earth gathered at Chen and Wei is distinguished; gathered at Xu and Chou, it is not. Clearly Earth loves Chen and Wei, not Chou and Xu. If the Five Phases also have qi, a life meeting this arrangement possesses incomparable landed property and enduring wealth and distinction in later years.

If Earth is too solid, without Water it is not harmonious and pliant; without Wood it is not open and permeable. Fire above scorches it. Women's lives with this arrangement often do not bear and rear children. Earth flourishes in the four seasonal endings, but Xu Earth alone is confined and weak. Much Xu makes a person quarrelsome and sleepy. Chen and Wei people enjoy eating; Chou people are clear and restrained. Chou contains Gen Earth and Gui Water, which can moisten and enrich it. A life meeting this can stand out independently.[^134]

**Spring.** Earth's force is weak. Fire's generation and support are welcome; excessive Wood is disliked and flooding Water avoided. Companions' assistance is desired. Metal restraining Wood is auspicious, though too much Metal still steals Earth's qi.

**Summer.** Earth's force is dry and fierce. Abundant Water moistening it accomplishes the work. Flourishing Fire refining it into a red-hot state is avoided. Wood assisting Fire's blaze makes both generation and control unfavorable. Metal generating flowing Water benefits wife and Wealth. Companions bring obstruction and stagnation; if Earth is excessive, Wood's attack is welcome.

**Autumn.** The child flourishes and the mother declines. Much Metal consumes and steals Earth's qi. Strong Wood controls it into a pure and good condition. Repeated Fire is not rejected; overflowing Water is not auspicious. Companions can add strength, but by Frost's Descent their absence does no harm.

**Winter.** Earth is cold outside and warm within. Flourishing Water means abundant Wealth; much Metal means an excellent child; strong Fire brings glory; much Wood is blameless. Additional Earth's assistance is especially favorable. A strong self alone is desired for ample longevity.

Xu Dasheng says:

> Metal relies on Earth for birth; too much Earth buries Metal.  
> Earth relies on Fire for birth; too much Fire scorches Earth.  
> Fire relies on Wood for birth; too much Wood makes Fire fierce.  
> Wood relies on Water for birth; too much Water sets Wood adrift.  
> Water relies on Metal for birth; too much Metal makes Water turbid.

> Metal can generate Water; too much Water sinks Metal.  
> Water can generate Wood; strong Wood draws Water down.  
> Wood can generate Fire; too much Fire burns Wood.  
> Fire can generate Earth; too much Earth dims Fire.  
> Earth can generate Metal; too much Metal alters Earth.

> Metal can control Wood; hard Wood chips Metal.  
> Wood can control Earth; heavy Earth breaks Wood.  
> Earth can control Water; too much Water carries Earth away.  
> Water can control Fire; blazing Fire heats Water.  
> Fire can control Metal; too much Metal extinguishes Fire.

> Declining Metal meeting Fire must melt.  
> Weak Fire meeting Water must go out.  
> Weak Water meeting Earth must be silted up.  
> Declining Earth meeting Wood must be undermined.  
> Weak Wood meeting Metal must be cut and broken.

> Strong Metal obtains Water, and only then is its edge moderated.  
> Strong Water obtains Wood, and only then is its force released.  
> Strong Wood obtains Fire, and only then is its stubbornness transformed.  
> Strong Fire obtains Earth, and only then is its flame checked.  
> Strong Earth obtains Metal, and only then is its harmful excess restrained.

These words are brief, yet exhaust the matter.

[^01]: **Scope and title.** The controlling text is S, not the shorter unlabelled Wikisource version. The separate 四庫全書提要 is deliberately excluded; the juan's own opening, closing title, and catalog classification are retained. *Sanming tonghui* is the work's conventional romanization; `Sanming-Tongshui` remains the repository name requested by its owner. “A Comprehensive Gathering of the Three Fates” is an interpretive English title, not a claim that *ming* always means fatal predetermination. The numbered hierarchy, paragraphing, and inventory table are editorial reading aids. Completeness here means coverage of S's transmitted juan, not certification of a flawless historical text.

[^02]: **Key vocabulary.** *Qi* 氣 denotes the vital, material, and atmospheric processes through which this text explains change; “energy” alone would be too narrow. *Li* 理 is translated “pattern,” sometimes “principle” where the argument requires it. The Five Phases are modes of operation as well as named substances, not simply five chemical ingredients. Capitalization helps distinguish technical relationships from ordinary objects without denying the source's deliberate movement between them. Angled brackets retain small-character source commentary; square brackets expose supplied clarification or emendation. Original Chinese headings remain available for alignment.

[^03]: **Quotations within the compilation.** The opening draws together Laozi, Lie Yukou, calendrical writings, the *Changes*, and later commentators. The two sayings attributed to Laozi correspond to chapters 1 and 25 of the received text, but are translated here as quoted by S, rather than replaced with a modern translation of a different recension. The same principle governs later quotations: attribution means “this compilation attributes these words to this authority.” It does not certify that every quotation is verbatim or that its named source survives in precisely this form. “The three as one” is left without an imposed identification.

[^04]: **The critic is not suppressed.** The small-character passages explicitly criticize the account surrounding them. Their arguments must not be mistaken for a single, harmonized cosmology. S varies 栢齋 and 栢齊 in the designation rendered Baizhai. In the second objection, the unpunctuated sequence 金木水火土之交變也 is read “Metal and Wood are the interacting transformations of Water, Fire, and Earth”; reading all five names as a flat list obscures the subsequent question about Heaven. This punctuation is interpretive. G593 contains additional material in this area; it has not been imported to enlarge S.

[^05]: **Historical claims and ethical assumptions.** The chapter's explanations of conception, bodily endowment, illness, sex, moral character, wealth, and longevity belong to its historical cosmology. Faithfully translating them does not establish them as biological or medical facts, nor make poverty evidence of vice or wealth evidence of virtue. The argument itself alternates between inherited endowment, circumstance, and deliberate cultivation. Jiang Yuan's and Jian Di's conceptions are invoked as exceptional traditional narratives, not ordinary reproductive mechanisms. Diagnostic and prognostic statements throughout this translation are the source's claims, not present-day medical advice.

[^06]: **Strength is contextual.** 旺、相、死、休、囚 are rendered flourishing, support, death, rest, and confinement. They describe a phase's relation to seasonal command, not its literal existence or nonexistence. Likewise, Lu 祿, Horse 馬, luck cycles 運, and allotted periods 限 belong to the techniques being discussed. Their reference point must be established locally. The chapter frequently works from the birth year and nayin; replacing every such reference with a modern Day Master would change the method. The author's reply to Master Wang explicitly insists that an out-of-season phase has not ceased to exist.

[^07]: **Master Junchuan and Master Wang.** The text subsequently identifies the critic as 浚川王氏, “Master Wang of Junchuan.” The translation preserves this designation rather than adding a biographical identification not established by the present collation. His objections concern both the physical generation cycle and the numerical allocation of seasonal qi. The long rebuttal following them belongs to the compiler's voice. “Heaven's five,” “Heaven's one,” and “Earth's two” refer to the generating numbers explained in the next section; they are not dates or numbered celestial objects.

[^08]: **A supported reading.** S has 觀天王會通, literally an awkward sequence involving a “heavenly king.” G593 reads 觀天文會通. The translation adopts 文, yielding “observing celestial patterns and comprehending their connections.” The surrounding discussion of stars, naming, and terrestrial correspondences supports this reading. The Three Enclosures and Twenty-Eight Lodges are named structures of the traditional sky. Matching the left half of a tally is the author's image of exact correspondence; it is not an independent demonstration that the predictions described were accurate.

[^09]: **Body and number.** The account of the two kidneys, one Water and one Fire, and the Gate of Life 命門 is translated as a premodern correspondence scheme. It should not be read as an anatomical distinction between the functions of the left and right kidney. Similarly, seeds and embryos being “water” is part of the argument for Water's numerical priority. The attribution to the *Spiritual Pivot* is retained as S gives it; this edition has not silently substituted a passage from another received medical text to regularize that attribution.

[^10]: **Generating and completing numbers.** The exposition assigns Water, Fire, Wood, Metal, and Earth the generating numbers 1–5; addition of central five completes the first four as 6–9. Its insistence that Earth need not reach ten is retained, even though ten appears elsewhere in the numerical scheme. The subsequent language of three yin and three yang, roots and manifestations, and excessive or insufficient movements belongs to a six-qi/five-movements framework. It is not a direct instruction to count planets, modern calendar months, or the five visible phase labels in a chart.

[^11]: **A conjectural repair.** In the long quotation, S reads 上不得而制, “above cannot restrain [them].” The translation supplies “[Earth] cannot restrain them,” conjecturally reading 土 for 上. The discussion contrasts mobile Water and Fire with substances confined by Earth, and the later quotation explicitly says 土不得而制焉. This internal parallel makes the correction plausible, but it is not presented as a confirmed reading from an independently inspected facsimile. “Generation” and “completion” in the same passage distinguish a substance's first formation from its mature predominance.

[^12]: **A difficult allocation retained.** S attributes the returning of things to their roots and storage to Wood in the sentence beginning 木之包五行. The translation retains Wood rather than silently reallocating the clause to Water, even though storage is assigned to Water elsewhere. The passage collects several explanatory schemes, and a transposition remains possible. Without a securely established corrective witness, apparent conceptual neatness is not sufficient grounds for rewriting it. The distinction between paired opposition and circulation likewise belongs to the source's argument, not to two interchangeable compass lists.

[^13]: **Explanatory wordplay.** 木/觸, 火/化, 金/禁, and the explanations of the stem names are traditional sound- and sense-associations. They are translated as the author's explanations, not asserted as modern historical etymologies. Such passages use a character's pronunciation, shape, and conventional significance together. In 庚乃陰干陽, 干 is a verb, “encroach upon”; the phrase does not reclassify the yang stem Geng as a yin stem. “Green-blue” renders 青 without forcing its older range into a single modern English color category.

[^14]: **Mother, child, and ghost.** These are relational terms: what generates the reference phase is its mother; what it generates is its child; what controls it is its ghost 鬼. The same phase can therefore play different roles in different comparisons. “Avenging the mother” personifies the controlling cycle; “the mother contending for the child” explains a different relation in the same network. Translating every 鬼 as a supernatural apparition, or every instance as the modern Seven Killings alone, would erase these distinctions.

[^15]: **Accounts of origin.** The chronology of Pangu, the Three Sovereigns, Fuxi, the Yellow Emperor, Danao, and Fenghou is the compilation's traditional history of the arts. The translation does not convert that account into independently verified dates or historical priority claims. It also preserves the author's criticism of rival attributions to Guiguzi and Dongfang Shuo, even though Dongfang Shuo is later cited for individual images. The discrepancy should not be hidden by removing one of the attributions or making every legendary figure merely an unnamed metaphor.

[^16]: **The archaic year names.** The first list corresponds in order to Jia through Gui; the second to Zi through Hai. Their Chinese forms are retained because historical readings and spellings vary. 單閼 is read *Chanyan*, not by combining the characters' most familiar modern pronunciations. 閼逢 is also encountered as *Yanfeng*; the Taiwan Ministry of Education's [historical dictionary entry](https://pedia.cloud.edu.tw/Entry/Detail?title=閼逢) gives *è féng* and records alternative spellings. The translation's unaccented pinyin is a reading aid, not a reconstruction of ancient pronunciation. Note especially the different characters behind stem **Wu 戊** and branch **Wu 午**.

[^17]: **Several arts, not one algorithm.** Taiyi 太乙, Liuren 六壬, and Qimen 奇門 are presented as separate applications to celestial change, human affairs, and terrestrial positioning. Their juxtaposition does not make them interchangeable with the Four Pillars. The named practitioners are retained in the forms used by S—Tiangang, Chunfeng, Yixing, and Xuzhong—rather than supplied with unverified biographies. The author's rhetorical claim that their judgments were remarkably accurate remains an attributed claim, not a result demonstrated by the present translation.

[^18]: **Routine normalization and explicit repairs.** S frequently interchanges 己, 已, and 巳, and occasionally corrupts a stem or technical label. Where a fixed stem–branch name makes the intended form unambiguous, the English normalizes it; the Chinese source file remains unchanged. More substantive repairs are separately noted. Here S's 干甲相合 is read 子甲相合 with G593: Zi and Jia are paired. G594 also supports 短夭, “Short Life,” against S's 短天, and 立地之道 against 立池之道. These are local corrections, not permission to regularize every unusual doctrine.

[^19]: **Seasonal reckoning.** The branch months are the traditional numbered months in the scheme being described, not fixed Gregorian months. The account of ashes responding within pitch-pipes belongs to the source's method of observing seasonal qi; it is not a modern measuring procedure recommended here. A branch-hour is one of twelve divisions of the day, conventionally a double-hour. The text's calendrical symbolism should not be converted into exact present-day clock times without the separate rules and location needed for such a calculation.

[^20]: **You and the midpoint of yin.** S calls You the “midpoint of yang” 陽正中 even though the immediately surrounding passage describes sunset and the eighth month; G593 repeats the difficulty. The translation's “[yin]” is therefore a conjectural correction, not an attested variant. It fits the local progression from the first yin at Wu toward complete yin at Hai. The original 陽 remains recoverable here, and a reader preferring diplomatic literalism may restore “yang” without altering the Chinese source.

[^21]: **Schematic calendar numbers.** Thirty days to a month and 360 to a year form the text's regular computational model. Elsewhere this same juan speaks of 365 days. Neither statement has been forced into the other. The twelve months, twelve branch-hours, six decades of the sexagenary cycle, and repeated cycles operate at different scales. Their presence is not evidence that an actual astronomical year contains exactly 360 days, or that historical calendar makers were unaware of seasonal adjustment. The translation preserves the argument without supplying an absent calendrical derivation.

[^22]: **The passage turns toward siting.** The twenty-four positions, sixty finer divisions, burial orientation, and reception of qi extend beyond natal reading into spatial practice. 龜甲空亡 and 接續空亡 are rendered “Tortoiseshell Void” and “voids to be bridged and joined,” retaining their specialized character without pretending that this brief passage supplies a complete operational method. “Nayin” here participates in the arrangement of directions as well as dates. The reader should not automatically transfer every spatial instruction to a natal pillar.

[^23]: **Sound and rotation.** 宮、商、角、徵、羽 are *gong, shang, jue, zhi,* and *yu*, the five named tones; “governing tone” describes gong's organizing role without equating it rigidly with a Western tonic in every use. “Across eight” is an inclusive cyclic relation within the traditional generative scheme, not simply an instruction to add eight in ordinary arithmetic or to jump a modern octave. Huangzhong, Zhonglü, Linzhong, and Yingzhong are pitch-standard names. The translation retains the author's upward/downward generation and yin/yang assignments as given.

[^24]: **A phonetic example, not a mystical equation.** 德紅合而為東 illustrates *fanqie*: the initial of 德 (*de*) combines with the rhyme and tone supplied by 紅 (*hong*) to indicate 東 (*dong*). “Father” and “mother” therefore have a concrete phonetic function in this analogy. Translating the characters only as “virtue,” “red,” and “east” would conceal the point. The example then supports the author's comparison between two components producing a third result in nayin. It is an analogy of composition, not an identity between modern Mandarin and the historical sound system.

[^25]: **Two different “marriages.”** Jia marrying Geng and Yi marrying Xin belongs to this passage's explanation through control; it is not the familiar five stem combinations beginning Jia–Ji. The text later uses those combinations as well. Keeping the two arguments distinct prevents a false correction. The herbs 莘 and 藟 are left as *shen* and *lei*: their identification is not securely established here. The claimed poisonous and life-extending effects, and the accompanying alloy analogy, are transmitted examples, not recipes or experimentally validated instructions.

[^26]: **The stem-count method is reproducible.** Count inclusively around the ten stems from the relevant stem to the endpoint assigned to its branch: Geng for Zi/Wu, Xin for Chou/Wei, Wu for Yin/Shen, Ji for Mao/You, Bing for Chen/Xu, and Ding for Si/Hai. The resulting odd numbers signify 1 Earth, 3 Fire, 5 Water, 7 Metal, and 9 Wood. This is a separate presentation from the later forty-nine-counter method. Confusing their intermediate numbers makes the worked examples appear more contradictory than they are.

[^27]: **An unexplained numerical discrepancy.** In the account attributed to the *Leisure Records of the Hall of Auspicious Cassia*, S gives Wuchen–Jisi a total of twenty-eight. Under the Great Mystery weights explicitly supplied later, the total is twenty-three: 5 + 5 + 9 + 4. Both three and eight belong to Wood in the sound-number explanation, so the stated phase remains the same. The translation retains twenty-eight rather than silently assuming that two separately quoted procedures must use an identical intermediate calculation. This discrepancy is not counted as a successfully verified example.

[^28]: **Checking the forty-nine-counter method.** Add the weights of the two stems and two branches, subtract their total from 49, and reduce the result to 1–5, treating a multiple of five as five rather than zero. Then take the phase generated by the remainder's phase: 1→Wood, 2→Earth, 3→Fire, 4→Water, 5→Metal. For Jiazi–Yichou, 49−34=15, giving five and therefore Metal. For Bingyin–Dingmao, 49−26=23, giving three and therefore Fire. This procedure was checked against all thirty pairs in the translated inventory and reproduces them all. That verifies this arithmetic, not the text's predictions.

[^29]: **Do not replace the directional scheme.** The Luo Writing passage assigns Water to north and northwest, Fire to west and southwest, Metal to south and southeast, and Wood to east and northeast. These are not the ordinary phase directions stated earlier. They belong to the numerical rotation being explained and are retained rather than “corrected” to the familiar east-Wood/south-Fire sequence. The surrounding insistence on the Yellow Emperor's authorship is the compiler's conclusion, not an inference established by the arithmetic.

[^30]: **The derivation of the weights has its own problems.** In the first copy of the paragraph, S reads 以寅加甲; the repeated copy and G593 read 以寅加申. The translation adopts “[Shen]” in the first occurrence. Both S and G593, however, retain 丑加寅至未得八, whose apparent forward count from Yin to Wei is six, not eight. That sentence remains unrepaired; reversing or relocating its terms would be conjectural. At the end, G593's 從四起數 supports reading S's 從四九數 as “begins its count at four.” These difficulties concern the proposed derivation, not the successfully checked weights themselves.

[^31]: **Lacuna and repetition.** S inserts the small-character marker 闕, “lacuna,” and then repeats a substantial stretch of the preceding argument about the Great Mystery numbers. Both the marker and the repeated passage are translated in full. G593 presents the numerical discussion without this long duplicate. That suggests a transmission or transcription problem, but the present comparison cannot locate the stage at which it arose. Removing the repetition would make a smoother reading text at the cost of no longer fully representing the chosen source.

[^32]: **Images are analogies.** 花甲 is rendered “Flowering Jiazi Cycle” here to preserve the author's emphasis on 花. His explicit warning against clinging to literal images is essential: the fetal, youthful, mature, and declining stages provide a model for the twelve positions. The Three Powers are Heaven, Earth, and humanity. References to five planets, sacred peaks, virtues, viscera, and phases establish a network of correspondences; they do not imply that the English word “element” adequately describes every member of that network.

[^33]: **Names that should not be homogenized.** S writes 金泊金; “Gold Leaf Metal” understands 泊 in the sense conventionally written 箔, also found in the comparison material. 白鑞 is rendered “White Pewter,” preserving 鑞, not silently substituting 蠟, “wax.” The English names identify the images used by this text, even where a contemporary school prefers a different label. Likewise 大驛 is a great post-road or postal thoroughfare, 壁上 the Earth of plastered walls, and 屋上 the Earth of roofing. Those material distinctions drive the ensuing judgments.

[^34]: **Symbolic botany.** 桑柘 includes mulberry and *zhe*, the silkworm-thorn, rather than naming only one tree. “Mulberry Wood” is the short heading; the fuller distinction is retained where relevant. Statements about willow leaves, the pomegranate's pungency, seasonal fruiting, and the endurance of pine and cypress function within the source's analogical botany. They should not be silently rewritten to match a present-day horticultural account. The chapter itself offers more than one explanation of the same tree image, sometimes with conflicting details; see note 52.

[^35]: **High ground and the course of Water.** The opening Streambed explanation contains an image-only historical glyph in the source's phrase about high terrain. “High ground” translates the phrase's contextual function without replacing the source glyph in the Chinese file. The six Water images are distinguished by containment, emergence, sustained flow, celestial descent, springs, and final reception. “Return to the source” can therefore describe a technical position or a cosmological completion, not necessarily a river flowing physically back uphill.

[^36]: **Lamp Fire is not renamed Buddhist Fire.** S uses 覆燈, so this translation uses “Lamp Fire,” not the different spelling 佛燈. The initial image explanation places its illumination near midday, while the detailed essay calls it specifically a light of the human night. That tension is retained. G's parallel calendar explanation also varies the wording concerning the sun's course. Neither this variation nor contemporary terminology licenses replacing all occurrences with one supposedly uniform account. “Fire in Heaven” likewise later distinguishes sun and moon, not just the sun.

[^37]: **The twelve vitality stages.** The main sequence is Birth 長生, Bathing 沐浴, Cap and Belt 冠帶, Arrival at Office 臨官, Imperial Prosperity 帝旺, Decline 衰, Sickness 病, Death 死, Tomb 墓, Extinction 絕, Gestation 胎, and Nurturing 養. The chapter also uses Defeat 敗, commonly associated here with Bathing, and Storehouse 庫, related to Tomb but not identical in emphasis. These capitalized labels are technical states, not automatic predictions of biological death or disease. The text's phase-based and nayin applications must be distinguished from later stem-specific versions of the sequence.

[^38]: **The paired survey is not completed by invention.** The source compares the six Metal, six Wood, six Fire, and six Earth images in opposing pairs, but does not supply a matching Water paragraph at this point. None has been invented. Its concluding reference to the six images 六象 is preserved without forcing it into a fully specified taxonomy absent from the passage. The movement among material images, directions, and stages of qi is intentional, even where the concise parallelism leaves the connection implicit.

[^39]: **The “Three Fates” are explicitly defined here.** The stem is taken as Lu to determine status, the branch as *ming* to determine life's length, and nayin as the body to examine flourishing and decline. Whatever later systems do with these terms, this is the local definition governing the argument. Much of the ensuing material begins with the birth year's stem–branch and compares the remaining positions. A translation that silently makes every judgment relative to the day stem would not preserve this older architecture. “Body” here also has a technical scope wider than physical anatomy alone.

[^40]: **How to read the indicator column.** Names such as Flat Head 平頭, Hanging Needle 懸針, Broken Character 破字, Missing Character 闕字, Crooked Leg 曲脚, Deaf–Mute 聾啞, Nine Uglies 九醜, and Short Life 短夭 are inherited indicator labels. They are not translator diagnoses or descriptions of the reader. Other recurrent names include Canopy 華蓋, Roadblock 截路, Eight Exclusives 八專, Great Defeat 大敗, Advancing/Retreating Spirit 進神/退神, Hidden Spirit 伏神, Crossing Spirit 交神, and Joy Spirit 喜神. The table preserves distinctions between Fortune Star 福星 and Fortune Spirit 福神, Staff 棒杖 and Beating Penalty 杖刑. It records what S lists; it does not supply missing activation rules from another school.

[^41]: **Uncertain divisions in the inventory.** S's compact annotations are not consistently punctuated. In Jiazi's entry, 進神喜 is provisionally read “Advancing Spirit (auspicious),” not made into an otherwise unexplained new indicator. Wuzi's 天伏神 is retained as “Heavenly Hidden Spirit,” with the Chinese exposed because the segmentation remains uncertain; G594 repeats the sequence. 水祿馬庫 is similarly preserved as “Water/Lu/Horse Storehouse” rather than split into confidently independent labels. These local uncertainties matter more than cosmetic uniformity.

[^42]: **Dingchou's Spirit.** S lists 退神, “Retreating Spirit,” and G594 agrees. W instead has 進神, “Advancing Spirit.” The translation follows S. This is an example of a real variant that has not been resolved by silently selecting the more favorable designation. Elsewhere the inventory and extended judgments may use different indicator vocabularies; the table is not intended to replace the fuller discussion.

[^43]: **Several inventory variants.** For Xinsi, S and G594 describe “newly born” Metal, 金之生者; W reads 金之幽者, suggesting hidden Metal. S is retained. For Jichou Fire, S gives lightning 電, whereas G594 gives thunder 雷; again S is retained. Dingsi Earth's unusual 沮涚 appears as 沮涗 in G594; “waterlogged ground” is a contextual rendering of the damp or sodden Earth image, not a securely reconstructed technical name. The source's variant graph is recorded rather than normalized into a familiar but unattested label.

[^44]: **An extra 木 in Gengzi's annotation.** S reads 喜木及金木德合, and G594 preserves the same sequence. The English provisionally treats the second 木 as a duplicated word after “Wood and Metal,” then reads 德合 as the next indicator. This is conjectural punctuation and omission of an apparent repetition, not an emendation confirmed by G. A strictly diplomatic rendering would leave “Wood” standing again before “Virtue Combination.” The Chinese source remains untouched.

[^45]: **Dingwei: firelight or waterlight?** S describes Dingwei Water as 火光, “firelight,” and W agrees. G594 instead reads 水光, “waterlight.” The latter is plausible, but the translation retains S's striking phrase and records the alternative here. Since the preceding Bingwu entry uses the moon's disc and the detailed Celestial River essay uses rain and dew, simply replacing the word with “moonlight” would go beyond either attested reading.

[^46]: **Changing the governing image.** The instruction to follow the greater or lesser Wood compares the birth year's nayin with those of the month, day, hour, and conception pillar 胎. It is not a direction to replace the day stem or alter the date. The social analogies of rising from poverty or falling from wealth explain a change in the scale of the image. Their interpretive role is preserved without turning the passage into a quantitative rule that counts Wood characters alone.

[^47]: **Lu compounds are not interchangeable.** 朝元祿 is rendered “Lu Returning to the Origin”; 明位祿, “Lu in the Luminous Position.” Their explanations here depend on particular phase and positional relationships. Lu is therefore left in pinyin rather than invariably translated “salary,” “prosperity,” or “day-stem rooting.” The source also distinguishes true Lu, Lu Storehouse, broken Lu, and Lu joined with Horse. Those distinctions should remain visible until the relevant method supplies their exact operation.

[^48]: **Disagreement is part of the text.** The extended entries quote several authorities whose judgments do not always coincide. Jimao Earth, for example, is said by one account to fear Wood and by another not to fear it. Metal may be declared complete and hostile to Fire in one passage, yet require Fire's shaping in another. Some differences are conditional; others remain unresolved. The translation preserves the names and boundaries of the quotations rather than manufacturing one consistent doctrine or presenting every quoted rule as the compiler's unqualified conclusion.

[^49]: **Why a controller may fail to control.** The Jisi and Gengyin discussions distinguish a phase's nominal controlling relationship from its actual condition at a particular branch. Metal may be present yet unable to injure Wood because it is weak, extinguished, diverted, or itself checked. “Qian's palace” points here toward Hai and the northwestern sector. The argument must be followed through its stated positional conditions; “Metal controls Wood” alone does not reproduce it.

[^50]: **Yihai's corrupted Wood name.** S's list of supportive Woods ends 發未, which is not a stem–branch combination. G594 reads 癸未, Guiwei, agreeing with the Willow Wood pair already identified in the chapter. The translation adopts Guiwei. This is an attested repair of a name, not a doctrinal adjustment. The other named Woods in the list—Jihai, Xinmao, Jisi, and Renwu—are retained.

[^51]: **Xinsi and Bingyin.** S lists 丙辰, Bingchen, among the Fires harmful to Xinsi, but Bingchen's nayin is Earth. G594 reads 丙寅, Bingyin, which is Furnace Fire and matches the following discussion of Metal's Extinction at Yin. The translation therefore prints “[Bingyin].” The Academy 學堂 in the same entry is a technical auspicious designation, not evidence that the person must literally attend a particular institution. The quoted authorities' differing attitudes to Fire are retained; the correction fixes the named pair, not every tension in the entry.

[^52]: **Willow's summer leaves.** In the earlier image explanation, S says the willow's leaves wither in high summer; in the later quotation it says they are dense then. Both are translated as transmitted. It would be easy to make the botany consistent by changing one phrase, but the present witnesses do not justify such confidence. The important technical distinction is also preserved: Wood in its own Death or Storehouse can be assigned good qualities, so these labels cannot be read as universally unfavorable.

[^53]: **Threefold combination and usable strength.** 三合 names a three-branch grouping; 生旺 identifies generative or flourishing condition. The passage invokes these as ways Metal can restore apparently exhausted Water. It does not say that the word “Metal” anywhere in the chart is sufficient. “Dried-up stream” is the source's image for Water in its own Extinction, while the actual judgment remains conditional on the other positions.

[^54]: **Officer and ghost.** 官鬼 here links two ways of naming what restrains the reference phase. Jiawu's fierceness can make restraint beneficial; consequently “ghost” does not automatically mean an evil result. The pairing of Jiawu Metal with Renzi Wood is explained through contrasting hardness and softness. It is not a free-standing claim about the romantic compatibility of people born in those years, and the text supplies no warrant for applying it without the rest of the configuration.

[^55]: **Offset Storehouse.** 偏庫 is translated “offset Storehouse” to retain its distinction from the unqualified or “own” Storehouse without pretending the chapter supplies a complete definition. It should not be automatically equated with the later “Indirect Wealth” or “Indirect Seal” categories merely because they also contain 偏. In Yiwei's entry, the quotation speaks of Wood's Storehouse “in its numbers” while the nayin remains Metal. That shift of analytical level is preserved.

[^56]: **Death can be favorable.** Renzi and Guichou Wood are explicitly said to prosper in status at Death or Extinction and to fare poorly in Birth or Prosperity. This is not an accidental English reversal: it belongs to the source's distinction between a phase label and a balanced configuration. The later “raising up” of Renzi explains how Fire can make tender Wood unfold. The text's use of humanity, courage, and spiritual cultivation remains evaluative language within its own correspondence system.

[^57]: **Jiwei's seasonal contradiction.** The quotation first favors Jiwei received in spring and summer under a particular course of luck, then calls summer receipt inharmonious, and gives autumn a favorable beginning but unfavorable end. The repeated summer 夏 is retained rather than conjecturally changed to winter. A distinction between the natal season and subsequent luck may explain part of the passage, but does not remove every difficulty. The reader should not treat this sentence as a clean four-season table.

[^58]: **Soul, liver, and Wood.** The explanation of Gengshen links Wood with the liver and the ethereal soul 魂; the corporeal soul 魄 later belongs to Metal. These are terms of the text's body–cosmos model, not claims about an experimentally identified substance leaving an organ. Gengshen's favorable Death/Extinction and Xinyou's desire for Birth/Prosperity are both retained. Their juxtaposition is another warning against imposing one universal strength rule on every nayin pair.

[^59]: **Guihai's “pure yang.”** S and G594 both say 癸亥具純陽之數, “Guihai possesses a number of pure yang,” although Gui and Hai are conventionally yin within the stem–branch distinction used earlier. The translation does not silently change 陽 to 陰. The writer may be referring to another numerical level, or the text may be faulty. In the preceding sentence, the claim that both pairs have Water throughout their components is qualified as “[predominantly]” because Xu is elsewhere explicitly Earth. The original generalization is recorded here rather than concealed.

[^60]: **Musical government.** The identification of gong with the ruler, shang with the minister, and jue with the people supports a historical analogy between tuning and political hierarchy. The warnings about high office express the source's theory of appropriate subordination, not a modern assessment of political fitness. Palace and office names are rendered as literary designations rather than assigned unverified equivalents in a later bureaucracy. The passage's exception for censorial and remonstrating service is important and has not been omitted.

[^61]: **“Wood dies at Mao” is probably corrupt.** S and G594 both read 木死卯而不死於癸卯, followed shortly by the separate and expected statement that Wood dies at Wu. The five-member parallel sequence requires Water in the first clause: Water dies at Mao, yet Guimao's Metal nayin can generate it. The translation therefore prints “[Water].” This is a strongly motivated internal conjecture, not a correction verified in G. The broader point is that a branch's conventional stage can be modified by the particular stem–branch's nayin.

[^62]: **Keep the analytical levels separate.** Wuzi is situated in Water's northern position but has Fire nayin; Bingwu has Fire in its ordinary stem–branch components but Water nayin. The passage deliberately uses that reversal to explain Fire within Water and Water within Fire. Correcting Bingwu everywhere to “Water,” or Wuzi everywhere to “Fire,” would destroy the argument. “Essence and spirit” 精神 likewise carries the traditional correspondence of Water and Fire here, rather than merely the modern sense of mental alertness.

[^63]: **The six faculties.** 六根 is rendered “six faculties,” retaining the traditional expression rather than reducing it to six anatomical organs. The prediction of their insufficiency is the source's, not a medical inference. “Natal stem and branch” 本命干支 generally points here to the natal reference pair under discussion, often the year; the translation does not silently substitute “Day Master.” The examples Dingsi–Guihai and Renzi–Wuwu combine branch opposition with changes in the relevant phase relationships.

[^64]: **Spirit-Head Lu changes the frame.** 神頭祿 uses the phases of the exclusive stem–branch positions: thus Bingwu is Fire in this discussion, even though it is Celestial River Water by nayin. The transformations between Kan and Li, Metal and Wood, husband and wife, and the two souls are not a second list of nayin names. Already Across 既濟 and Not Yet Across 未濟 also depend on the ordering of the relationship; the text does not treat reversing the two parties as automatically equivalent. Its exceptions to clash, punishment, and Primal Affliction are method-specific.

[^65]: **The twelve presiding spirits.** The passage assigns Heavenly Yi, Great Constant, Hooked Array, Empty Sky, Soaring Serpent, Vermilion Bird, Azure Dragon, Six Harmonies, Heavenly Empress, Dark Warrior, White Tiger, and Great Yin to the named positions. Empty Sky translates 天空 in the explicit list. The preceding discussion's language of emptiness and change should be read alongside that list, not replaced by an unrelated universal “void” rule. Descriptions of temperament, military or literary aptitude, and office are the historical system's characterizations. The final instruction to consider proximity and strength qualifies the whole catalogue.

[^66]: **Emergence is not the same as refining.** The Sea Metal essay distinguishes hidden treasure becoming accessible through Void or clash from unfinished Metal becoming an implement through Fire. Its pearl configurations exploit that distinction. “Pearl Hidden in the Deep Sea” and “Pearl in the Shell Illuminating the Moon” are configuration names, not instructions to place objects in a ritual arrangement. The cross-pairing of Noble Person influences belongs to the named dates; the prose does not spell out a complete independent algorithm for all such exchanges.

[^67]: **The author's correction of a rule is retained.** After reporting that certain Earth encounters without Fire imply low status and death in middle age, the compiler explicitly says he has not found this invariably true. He then gives countervailing combinations and insists on the birth year's Lu, Horse, and Noble Person. These are not translator qualifications added to soften a prediction. They are part of the source's own critical practice and should remain visible to anyone extracting rules from the chapter.

[^68]: **Named configurations and figurative transformation.** 崑山片玉 is rendered “A Piece of Jade from Mount Kunlun”; 干支連珠 becomes “Stems and Branches Strung Like Pearls.” 脫體化神, “Leaving the Old Body and Transforming into Spirit,” marks a change of effective form or quality within this technical imagery. It need not imply literal bodily transmigration. The comparison to the Ziping Metal Spirit 金神 is retained as a comparison made by the text, not an assertion that the two configurations have identical conditions in every later manual.

[^69]: **Gold leaf and Fire.** The essay first prohibits Fire where thin Metal adheres to Level-Ground Wood, then permits certain forms of illumination. The distinction between destructive heating and reflected light accounts for part of the contrast, but the prose is not a fully explicit decision tree. Its particularly severe warning against Furnace Fire is retained. “Returning to the original substance” is the source's image of what thin worked Metal can no longer safely undergo, not a modern claim about the impossibility of melting gold leaf.

[^70]: **The Jade Hare.** The “Jade Hare Rising in the East” is a named auspicious configuration built around Gui-Mao and Ji-Mao, not an additional animal sign introduced into the sixty-pair table. Likewise the decoration of walls, vessels, and buildings keeps the reader within the material logic of leaf metal. The chapter's named prestigious configurations can override an earlier generic warning; the translation leaves that hierarchy of specificity intact rather than making every occurrence of Earth uniformly good or bad.

[^71]: **White Pewter's stage of workmanship.** The opening describes a luminous but not finally worked substance. Its need for Fire therefore differs from the completed Sword Edge or Hairpin-and-Bracelet images. The claim that Xinsi meeting Bingyin transforms into Water refers to the additional stem-combination reasoning, not to changing the official nayin name of either pair. The account of Gengchen's vulnerability and Xinsi's advantage is retained even where a modern reader might expect two members of one nayin pair to behave identically.

[^72]: **Officer relations in this paragraph.** Geng's Officer is identified as Ding and Xin's as Bing; these are controlling stem relationships. The following preference for Guisi over Bingzi is transmitted as an additional judgment, not derived in full by the author. The Tiger Roaring into the Wind configuration is named here and recurs later under Roadside Earth. Repetition across different essays is preserved: it need not mean that the name has only one possible entry point or that its conditions can be detached from the local discussion.

[^73]: **The same Fire can act differently.** Jiawu's encounter with Bingyin is assessed through Fire's Birth at Yin and the Yin–Wu relationship, not merely through the fact that Metal usually benefits from refining. The Water nayin of Bingwu does not erase its fiery stem and branch. “Dragon Fire” refers to Wuzi–Jichou Thunderbolt Fire. In reading these examples, distinguish the named nayin, the ordinary stem phase, the branch's phase, and the relationship formed between positions; all four are active in the prose.

[^74]: **Sword and dragon.** The sword's transformation into an azure dragon and its qi reaching the Dipper are literary configuration images. The English preserves them rather than replacing them with generic “career success.” Gui-Chou in the second image is deliberately different from Gui-Si in the preceding sentence. Dou 斗 here belongs to the traditional celestial vocabulary; the opening 斗牛 names the Dipper and Ox regions, not a bullfight or two zodiac animals. The precise named combinations are retained alongside the imagery.

[^75]: **The unfinished-Metal warning is generic in S.** After allowing completed Hairpin-and-Bracelet Metal, S warns against 未成之金, “unfinished Metal,” without naming another pair in that sentence. The translation likewise does not insert one. The short following clause 戌則金混雜 is compressed; “[At Xu] the Metal becomes mixed” exposes the implied location without inventing a missing stem. The final three-punishments warning involves Shen with Yin and Si; it is more specific than an unrestricted ban on any Fire anywhere in a Sword-Edge chart.

[^76]: **The ornament's setting matters.** References to the women's chambers, jade pillows, and red and green adornment belong to the literary setting of finished jewelry. They are not added romantic decoration. Quiet Water is preferred because the governing image is completed beauty preserved and reflected, not raw ore awaiting refinement. The different judgments for Gengxu and Xinhai follow their distinct stems and branches even though both are Hairpin-and-Bracelet Metal by nayin.

[^77]: **Cross-pairing is not repetition.** The essay distinguishes opposite branches from advantageous exchanges of yin and yang; it does not say that every pairing among Chen, Si, Xu, and Hai is equally favorable. “Placed Within Its Casket” translates the image of finished Metal being contained or concealed. It should not be converted into an instruction to put a physical charm in a box. As elsewhere, the text assumes additional rules for Lu and Noble Person that this juan mentions but does not fully teach.

[^78]: **Thunderbolt Fire is deliberately exceptional.** Water, wind, and thunder are not treated as ordinary extinguishing agents here: they allow the dragon's Fire to manifest. The cross-pairings Jichou–Guihai and Jichou–Yimao therefore cannot be inferred from a simple Fire-versus-Water opposition. “Leading the Ordinary into the Sacred” and “Thunder and Fire Transforming” are names for these configurations. The literary vocabulary of divine transformation is retained without treating it as an instruction to evoke weather or supernatural beings.

[^79]: **A change of Earth type within a compressed sentence.** Dingsi supplies wind as Sandy Earth; Jimao supplies thunder through Mao, but is City-Wall Earth in the established nayin list. The sentence shifts between these examples rather than reclassifying Jimao as Sandy Earth. The translation separates the clauses accordingly. The subsequent permissions for Furnace Fire and Fire Below the Mountain are specific to named stems and branches; they do not cancel the essay's later warnings about excessive or competing Fire.

[^80]: **A favorable configuration beside an unfavorable rule.** The prose warns about Wuwu and Jiwei, yet the quotation from the *Subtle Selections* gives Wuzi meeting Wuwu the distinguished name “Heaven and Earth Divided at the Center.” The author's closing remark explicitly observes that these named configurations differ from the preceding account. That observation is translated, not harmonized away. The bracketed reference to the pure Wuwu case simply identifies the example under discussion; it does not supply a new condition from another edition.

[^81]: **Furnace Fire: Yin is not Mao.** Bingyin is described as Fire in its own Birth and Dingmao as Fire in its own Defeat. The former can become excessive with more Wood; the latter may need Wood's support before it can regulate Metal. Their common nayin label therefore does not justify treating their requirements as identical. The later reference to Shen and You concerns the Fire's condition at those branches, not a change in the official nayin assignment of Dingmao itself.

[^82]: **The worked examples are preserved in order.** The two four-pair sequences are Bingyin–Dingyou–Jiyou–Bingyin and Bingyin–Jiawu–Jisi–Bingyin. The text contrasts their outcomes rather than deriving every step. They are presented in the usual pillar sequence without adding a person's identity, a Gregorian birth date, or missing biographical evidence. “Fire lacks qi” is the source's evaluation within the whole arrangement; it does not mean that the written Fire-related components are absent. Simple character counts would not reproduce the comparison.

[^83]: **Two definitions of the yin interval.** The Lamp Fire essay reports Xu–Hai–Zi–Chou as one range and Wei through Hai as another. Both are retained. The source is discussing favorable darkness and the lamp image, not providing a single universal conversion to modern civil time. The threat of wind refers to the branch and directional correspondences as well as the literal image of a vulnerable flame. Repeated Chen or Si is specifically mentioned and should not disappear in a generalized paraphrase about “too much Fire.”

[^84]: **Kui Star and Pointing South.** 魁星 and 指南 are transmitted configuration names. “Kui Star” keeps the association with distinction and leadership without inserting an unverified astronomical identification; “Pointing South” retains the directional image. The text's use of Wu and Wei in connection with Jiachen does not by itself authorize merging Lamp Fire with Fire in Heaven. “Fire and Earth Entering the Hall” instead returns to the protective architecture of the lamp: Rooftop Earth gives it shelter, and added oil sustains it.

[^85]: **Sun and moon are distinct here.** Wuwu is identified with the sun and Jiwei with the moon. The author expressly rejects an alternative that distinguishes a hard summer sun from a warm winter sun. A translation that calls both pairs simply “Sun Fire” would lose this argument and the lunar configurations that follow. The Gate of Heaven and the gates of emergence and entry are technical directional images; they are not a claim that the physical sun rises and sets at the same compass bearing throughout the year.

[^86]: **Yiyou, not an impossible pair.** In the relevant Well-and-Spring references, S has 巳酉 or 已酉 where 乙酉 is required. Only Yiyou is both a valid sexagenary pair in this context and one of the two Well-and-Spring combinations already named. The translation prints “[Yiyou]” at the affected passages. This is a name repair supported by the chapter's own complete list, not a new preference invented for the English edition. Yin–yang cross-pairing elsewhere in the same essays is retained exactly as the named examples specify.

[^87]: **Solar configuration names.** Fusang 扶桑 is the traditional tree of the sunrise in the image “The Sun Rising over Fusang.” “The Solar Disc at the Gnomon” translates 日輪當表, keeping the measuring-post image rather than reducing the title to “strong Fire.” The examples differentiate birth pair, month, day, and hour; those distinctions are part of the conditions. Kan and Li in their proper positions refer to the Water–Fire arrangement, not to a judgment derived from the nayin label alone.

[^88]: **Lunar cassia and number.** 桂 in these configuration names carries the literary associations of the moon and distinguished attainment. “The Moon's Cassia Giving Fragrance” values several Woods; “A Single Branch in the Cassia Grove” values few. The source explicitly contrasts the two rather than providing one rule that more Wood is always better. The unfavorable names “The Sun Losing Its Brightness” and “The Moon Thinly Eclipsed” are likewise technical images, not predictions of astronomical eclipses on a person's birthday.

[^89]: **Two accounts of Fire Below the Mountain.** One treats it as firefly light and therefore welcomes Water; the other treats it as Fire supported by mountain, Wood, and wind. The author explicitly notices that the second is no longer a firefly account. Both are retained, including their different practical conditions. The opening images of light among leaves and upon clothing belong to the source's miniature, flickering scale; translating them as a general forest fire would erase the contrast with the following essay.

[^90]: **An awkward negative retained.** In this passage S reads 若無火無山, “if there is no Fire and no mountain,” before adding the warning about Thunderbolt Fire. The translation supplies “[supporting]” only to indicate the likely distinction between a useful Fire presence and the destructive Fire introduced afterward. It does not change 火 to 水 or 木. Celestial River Water is then described as a sudden downpour, but the text immediately allows a previously established mountain-and-Water arrangement to mitigate the harm.

[^91]: **Mulberry's practical and literary uses.** The opening connects the Wood with silk clothing, social order, silkworm feeding, and bow-making. These uses explain the preference for the silkworm positions Chen and Si; they are not incidental decoration. “Mulberry and Willow Forming a Grove” remains conditional on spring or summer birth in the quoted rule. The same Wood appearing under a different seasonal or positional arrangement is not thereby assigned the same result.

[^92]: **The endurance of pine and cypress.** Snow and frost supply the literary image of constancy, while the technical analysis still asks for roots, mountain, moisture, and suitable regulation. The warning that Wood should not rush south is part of the specific Furnace-Fire discussion, not a universal ban on every southern correspondence. The essay also distinguishes timber already functioning as beams from a living tree in a mountain forest. That change of state alters what further support is wanted.

[^93]: **Metal becoming Wood.** The reference to Xinyou Metal “transforming into dead Wood” shifts from its ordinary Metal components to its Wood nayin. It is not a misprint requiring Xinyou's nayin to become Metal. The warning about additional Pomegranate Wood must be read with that transformation and with the condition of the Pine-and-Cypress reference. Similarly, a Wood type may supply a mountain through a particular member of its pair rather than because every instance of that Wood is literally mountainous.

[^94]: **Day and month also mean sun and moon.** In the configuration names 日會辛卯 and 月逢庚寅, 日 and 月 resonate with both calendrical day/month and the solar/lunar images developed under Wuwu and Jiwei. The source explicitly refers back to people of those two Fire pairs. The translation keeps the compact titles and records the double sense here instead of treating them as unrelated general rules for any Xinmao day or Gengyin month.

[^95]: **Root, fuel, and worked timber.** The Great Forest essay can speak of Wood rooted in a mountain, dead Wood below the mountain, and timber fitted into buildings. Its needs differ accordingly. The reference to Ji meeting Jia and transforming into Earth is the source's stem-combination explanation, not a statement that the mere presence of Jia anywhere guarantees a completed transformation. “Azure Dragon Riding the Sea” is an expressly named exception to a more general warning about excessive Water and is retained as such.

[^96]: **Willow's setting.** The Sui embankments and Han gardens evoke the willow's cultivated, graceful presence. No additional historical event or garden identification has been inserted. Its dependence on support and the danger of wind continue the image of flexible rather than massive Wood. The several Metal and Earth examples work through particular stem–branch pairs: the English therefore preserves their names rather than replacing the passage with a generic list of favorable elements.

[^97]: **An exception is not a contradiction to erase.** The Willow essay initially treats Pomegranate as a weak or unfavorable encounter, then cites “Red Blossoms and Green Willows” as distinguished under spring or summer birth. The seasonal condition and named configuration explain why the later statement cannot be reduced to the earlier generic rule. “Willow Trailing Gold” similarly requires a spring birth and a single Metal in the hour; the four named Metal pairs are preserved, not expanded into a blanket permission for abundant Metal.

[^98]: **A compressed exchange formula.** S reads 丙辛丁庚互官戊辛巳寅互印. The translation renders the stated pairs but does not pretend the sequence is a fully explicit derivation of conventional Officer and Seal rules. In particular, 戊辛巳寅 joins stems and branches in a very compressed notation. Its interpretation remains provisional. The surrounding claim is clear: opposite-polarity encounters with particular worked Earths support the potted-tree image. “Achievement Equal to Creative Transformation” subsequently concerns the exchange between Gold Leaf Metal and Pomegranate Wood, not all Metal–Wood encounters.

[^99]: **Level-Ground Wood can already be architecture.** Wuxu is called the ridgebeam and Jihai the supporting beam. The need for Earth and the preference for roofing or wall materials therefore follow the image of a structure, not solely a living tree. Zi and Wu as the upright pillars of Heaven and Earth add a cosmological analogy to the architectural one. The translation retains both scales. “Cold Valley Returning to Spring” later reintroduces seasonal growth without erasing the earlier worked-timber account.

[^100]: **Combination and mountain.** The preference for Jiashen and Dingchou is explained respectively through combination and the mountain correspondence of Chou. These are additional grounds for judgment, not new nayin names. In the same essay, Jihai can receive Renzi where Wuxu cannot: the difference is attached to the individual pair, despite their shared Level-Ground label. “Spring Returning to the Cold Valley” requires winter birth with Yin or Mao in the hour; the condition is not supplied merely by finding Wood somewhere in the chart.

[^101]: **Wall plaster needs a support.** The Wood beneath plaster and the Earth carrying the structure are distinct images. The source's warning about Fire assisting the “ghost” operates within that arrangement rather than making all Fire universally harmful to Earth. Gold Leaf's favorable role is decorative: a completed building becomes resplendent. The distinction between an ordinary dwelling and imperial magnificence belongs to the source's social imagery; the translation does not turn it into a present-day rule about property ownership or permission to use particular colors.

[^102]: **A completed wall differs from an unfinished one.** Roadside Earth is used to distinguish Earth already completed from Earth still needing Fire's work. The short White Pewter warning invokes Xun and mutual obstruction but does not explain every intervening step. It is retained without an invented mechanism. The final instruction to assess Noble Person, Lu, and Horse makes clear that the nayin image alone is not offered as the entire judgment.

[^103]: **The names in the Sandy Earth essay.** 駕海長虹 is “A Long Rainbow Riding the Sea”; 星拱北 is “Stars Arching toward the North.” The first is tied to the text's explanation of Bing and Ding entering Qian's gate. These are kept as configuration titles, not expanded into unsupported astronomical or meteorological claims. The apparent breadth of the phrase “Bing and Ding” in an example introduced by a Bing person is retained rather than silently rewritten into a new rule.

[^104]: **The two Sandy Earth examples.** The source gives Bingchen–Yiwei–Guiyou–Wuwu and Dingsi–Guimao–Jiwei–Renshen. Each contains two Metal nayin positions and a Fire-in-Heaven position, matching the author's stated comparison. The difference in longevity is attributed to Wuwu's greater dryness and Jiwei's relative gentleness. No biographical identification or observed lifespan is supplied by the text, and none has been added. These examples illustrate the author's reasoning; they are not independent empirical tests of it.

[^105]: **Special configurations beside ordinary rules.** The Golden Horse Neighing into the Wind, the Horse Transforming into a Dragon Steed, and the Fierce Tiger Roaring into the Wind are reported as prestigious arrangements. The author's final sentence observes that the ordinary restrictions among Water, Fire, Earth, and Metal seem not to apply in these cases. That reservation is retained. It would misrepresent the passage either to delete the exceptions or to use them to abolish all the preceding conditional rules.

[^106]: **What a great post-road is doing in the image.** 驛 evokes the network of official posts and travel routes; the essay expands it into the ground that bears movement across the realm. “Great Post-Road Earth” preserves that infrastructure rather than suggesting a modern hotel or a single station building. The distinction between Wushen's own Birth and Jiyou's own Defeat governs how much Wood each can sustain. “Substantial bearing” also echoes Kun's receptive, load-bearing virtue.

[^107]: **Tiles: a conjectural correction made visible.** S opens the Rooftop essay with 埏埴為林, literally forming worked clay into a “forest.” G595 and W also preserve 林, so neither confirms a correction. The following description of roof tile, its firing, and its dependence on wooden support strongly suggests 瓦 or an equivalent lost reading. The translation therefore gives “[tiles]” explicitly as a contextual conjecture. It does not claim that the historical page image has been checked and found to read 瓦.

[^108]: **Dinghai, not Dingmao.** In the Lamp Fire comparison within the Rooftop essay, S and G595 read 丁卯見甲辰. W reads 丁亥見甲辰. The latter agrees with the Rooftop pair Bingxu–Dinghai and with the parallel comparison to Bingxu immediately before it. The translation adopts “[Dinghai]” and records both readings. This supported local repair does not make W the controlling text elsewhere; its shorter scope and other divergent readings remain distinct from S.

[^109]: **The Ding–Ren combination's moral label.** The source calls this an 淫合 and associates it with an unseemly reputation when combined with Chou–Xu punishment. The translation preserves the historical judgment rather than endorsing it as a reliable statement about anyone's sexuality or conduct. The later requirement that a truly clear configuration contain no admixture of Fire or Earth belongs to the particular “clear current” image; it should not be turned into a universal prescription for all Water lives.

[^110]: **Water's return and nourishment are separate.** A place of return, a source in Metal, and freedom from disruptive clash or wind are three distinct conditions in the Great Stream essay. Gold Leaf Metal is judged too slight to nourish effectively even though its nayin is Metal. Sword Edge may transform beneficially but is vulnerable to additional positional disturbances. These qualifications are why the English retains named types instead of reducing the paragraph to “Metal helps Water.”

[^111]: **Heavenly Water and Earth.** The Celestial River essay begins by saying that Earth cannot control this Water, then warns against particular Earths that obstruct it in the same positions or under winter conditions. The text does not reconcile these statements through one fully articulated rule. Both are retained. “The Numinous Raft Entering the Celestial River” specifically requires Wood without roots or cultivating Earth in the arrangement described; the translation does not add a literal boat or a ritual practice.

[^112]: **Weather as a configuration image.** Clouds, dragon, wind, rain, frost, and dew here name interactions among stems, branches, seasons, and nayin. The winter configuration's need for Yin or Mao is explained as warmth that thaws freezing. The contrast between spring drought, summer flooding, winter cold, and autumn suitability is the source's symbolic seasonal judgment, not a weather forecast for a place or year. The two named rain configurations retain their stated stems rather than being generalized to all Water.

[^113]: **The shared well and the unused well.** The eight households evoke the well's communal provision. The later phrase 井渫不食, rendered “The Well Cleared but Not Drunk From,” is an allusion to the *Changes*' Well image: usable nourishment remains unreceived. It explains obstruction and unrealized use, not a modern water-quality test. Wood's role includes the drawing vessel, which is why the translation supplies “the analogy is a bucket” rather than treating Wood solely as something watered by the spring.

[^114]: **The sea's clarity and its boundaries.** Renxu contains Xu Earth, whereas Guihai is Water throughout its ordinary components as well as its nayin. This distinction explains why the essay welcomes mountain for one but warns of too much Earth in the other. “Heaven's Pass and Earth's Axis” is a configuration name for the specified Guihai–Wushen encounter, not a statement about the physical rotation axis of the planet. The final warning about thunder and Fire transforming is retained alongside the earlier favorable mountain-and-Water arrangement.

[^115]: **Which stems can yield which nayin.** The discussion is about the distribution of phase labels across the sixty paired positions, not the ordinary phase of a stem taken alone. Thus Jia and Yi never head a Wood-nayin pair in this list, although they are ordinarily Wood; Geng and Xin can head Wood pairs although ordinarily Metal. The author's explanations through hardness, sap, ash, and congealing are analogical rationales for that distribution. Phrases such as Metal “drying” Water or Fire are retained without being recast as chemical mechanisms.

[^116]: **The unrest claim is attributed, not tested.** The statement about dynastic disorder in Bingwu and Dingwei years belongs to an unnamed explanation quoted by the compiler. He immediately says it is reasoning from principle rather than the original meaning of the images. The translation preserves both claim and qualification. No modern historical examples, political predictions, or claims about the current sexagenary cycle have been added; the passage does not provide the evidence needed for such conclusions.

[^117]: **The polemic against abandoning nayin.** Xu Dasheng's *Discourse Establishing the True* and the saying attributed to Yuan-period practitioners are reported through this compilation. Their wording is translated as given rather than reconstructed from an independently established critical edition. Lou Jing 婁景 remains the named figure in the quotation; no speculative identification has been supplied. The compiler's point is methodological: ordinary Five Phases alone, in his view, do not exhaust the earlier system. His disagreement is part of the book, not an editorial verdict against contemporary practice.

[^118]: **Hongfan Five Phases and a hostile title.** 洪範五行 is retained as “Hongfan Five Phases,” distinct from the ordinary Five Phases and the “Earlier-Heaven Old Five Phases” named in the debate. “Classic for Destroying the Barbarians” translates the hostile designation 滅蠻經 as reported, not a title endorsed by the translator. S and G595 give 周視 for the Taihe writer; the translation uses “Master Zhou of Taihe” rather than attaching an uncertain modern biography to the name. The warp-and-weft conclusion calls for combination of methods, not their conflation.

[^119]: **A difficult short clause.** 三反以定 is retained as “[through three returns they were fixed].” The relation of this clause to the preceding sequence of cosmic and calendrical cycles is not fully clear. The brackets signal a provisional syntactic rendering, not a recovered missing sentence. It has not been expanded into an invented astronomical calculation. The remaining argument—that the calendrical cycle provides a common framework for celestial, seasonal, and human affairs—is explicit in the source.

[^120]: **Sixty combinations, eight trigrams, sixty-four hexagrams.** The unpunctuated sequence is read as the completion of the sixty combinations followed by the Eight Trigrams, not as a new system of sixty-eight trigrams. The source's 384 lines are the six lines of each of sixty-four hexagrams; its further association with a year's number is retained without forcing 384 to equal the schematic 360 mentioned earlier. The four-sage account of the *Changes* is the compiler's received attribution, not a modern critical chronology inserted by the translation.

[^121]: **Five Phases and five virtues.** The chapter links Water with wisdom, Fire with ritual propriety, Wood with humanity, Metal with righteousness, and Earth with trustworthiness. “Humanity” translates 仁 as a virtue, not the human species; “ritual propriety” translates 禮 more precisely here than simple politeness. The explanation of 行 as unceasing coming and going motivates “Phases” and “movements.” These correspondences are the text's organizing categories, not a claim that physical substances possess moral character.

[^122]: **The color rules are not a seamless table.** S first associates Birth and Prosperity with the phase's own color, then later associates Prosperity and Tomb with the child's color. Both statements are retained. The mother's-color commentary lists several examples but does not explicitly complete every possible pairing; none has been silently added. “Formation and Cap and Belt” follows the compact 成形冠帶 phrase without inventing an extra standardized stage. The wife's and child's colors are relational categories within the model, not literal predictions of family members' appearance.

[^123]: **Number and balance.** Doubling a phase's number in Birth/Prosperity and halving it in Death/Extinction is a separate rule from the nayin algorithms earlier in the chapter. The source does not here provide a complete worked application. Wealth 財, Officer 官, Seal 印, and Food 食 are kept as technical relationship names; their exact reference point depends on the method in use. The decisive instruction is to reduce excess and supply deficiency. This qualifies, rather than abolishes, the many earlier categorical-sounding judgments.

[^124]: **The compressed preference lists.** The lists of what each phase favors or avoids are transmitted without fully reliable punctuation. Their English divisions are therefore interpretive, especially Metal's 墓敗等火水寒 and Fire's 木枯雜黨木敗死水之木. The latter is left close to its wording as withered Wood, mixed companions, Wood in Defeat, and Wood in dead Water, rather than rewritten into a polished modern rule. In Wood's favorable list, the Earth-cultivation reading 土培 is supported by G595 and by the following discussion. These lists should be read with their explanatory paragraphs, not in isolation.

[^125]: **Casting the Seal and Riding the High Carriage.** 鑄印 and 乘軒 are configuration names built from Metal and Fire imagery. The statement that Chou damages the mold belongs to the quoted rule; no additional unstated mechanism has been invented to explain it. “Seal” in a configuration's literary title should not automatically be reduced to the technical Seal relationship everywhere. The chapter moves between the casting of a physical implement, social distinction, and phase relationships, often within one sentence.

[^126]: **Completed Metal and the numbers of components.** Shen, You, Si, and Chou are named as positions in which Metal may be considered completed; the 己/巳 confusion is normalized to Si in the English. The examples with two Metals, two Fires, three Waters, or three Woods are proportional comparisons in the author's reasoning, not a fully specified weighting model for a modern chart calculator. The shift from raw material to a finished implement explains why Fire can be welcomed first and rejected afterward.

[^127]: **A supported correction in winter Metal.** S reads 火來取土, an awkward “Fire comes to take Earth.” G595 reads 火來助土, “Fire comes to assist Earth.” The translation adopts “[assist],” which also fits the following statement that mother and child accomplish their work. 比肩 is rendered “Companions,” not literally “shoulder to shoulder.” Officer and Seal here refer to relations appropriate to the Metal under discussion; the wording is not a promise that those named influences are beneficial regardless of season and strength.

[^128]: **Living and dead Wood do not identify nayin alone.** Jiaxu–Yihai, Jiayin–Yimao, and Jiachen–Yisi are called living Wood in this section even though several of their nayin labels are Fire or Water. The author is considering Wood through the Jia/Yi stems and its condition at the branches. These are therefore not obvious typographical errors to be replaced with Wood-nayin pairs. The distinction between living growth and dead timber then explains the different uses of Fire and Metal.

[^129]: **Two difficulties in spring Wood.** S's 葉摘根乾 is rendered “leaves [fall away] and roots dry out”; 摘 ordinarily suggests picking or plucking, so the supplied English is contextual, not a securely established replacement character. G595 repeats 摘. The subsequent 木火二物既濟 also appears in both S and G595. Although the preceding sentences discuss the need for Water, the translation retains “Wood and Fire” rather than silently changing 木 to 水. The text's difficulty remains visible to a reader comparing the Chinese.

[^130]: **Autumn and winter Wood.** In the abundance-of-timber sentence, S reads 木多, “much Wood,” while G595 reads 水多, “much Water.” S is followed. The phrase 土厚無已任之才 is syntactically rough; “thick Earth exceeds its ability to bear responsibility” conveys the apparent contrast without pretending to recover an exact lost graph. In winter, “Wood itself is sick” means the seasonally weakened reference phase cannot automatically be helped by equally weakened companions. The final preference for Birth/Prosperity is retained beside that qualification.

[^131]: **Cosmogeography is not modern hydrology.** Heaven tilting northwest, Earth sinking southeast, and Water not flowing westward form the source's cosmological explanation of direction. They are not asserted here as universal observations about real rivers. The ensuing forward and backward sequences concern movement through the twelve branches. “Capacity” and “sound” preserve the source's parallel contrast between a broad, receptive current and one whose resistance produces a name or resonance. The text then adds different fortune conditions to those two modes.

[^132]: **Gendered and strength-based formulas.** 陰人 is rendered “women” in this context, following the passage's historical contrast, not as a disparaging modern label. The statement that weak yang Water is poor and weak yin Water distinguished is transmitted as a compact formula without its full application rules. It should not be universalized into a judgment about every person assigned yin or yang Water. The same caution applies to temperament and bodily claims elsewhere in the chapter; they are not clinical or moral assessments of the reader.

[^133]: **Cosmic Water and Fire versus a particular Fire.** The closing assertion that Water and Fire are difficult to extinguish even if Heaven and Earth overturn shifts scale from an individual seasonal configuration to the enduring constituents of the cosmology. It does not retract every preceding reference to Fire's Death or Extinction. Wealth and wife remain linked in the historical relationship terminology; the warning about injury through the wife is preserved as the source's claim, not endorsed as evidence about women or marriage.

[^134]: **The closing generalizations and the final corrective.** Fertility, temperament, appetite, sleep, landholding, and longevity are all discussed through the source's branch and phase correspondences. These statements are translated, not validated as medical, psychological, or socioeconomic facts. The final twenty-five clauses attributed to Xu Dasheng systematically show how generation and control can reverse their apparent benefit when strength is excessive or deficient. Their parallel structure is retained as verse-like blocks. The juan ends with this principle of proportion, not with a claim that any single phase or indicator is universally fortunate.
